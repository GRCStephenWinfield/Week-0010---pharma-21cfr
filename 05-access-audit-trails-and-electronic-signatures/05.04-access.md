## 1. What this chapter settles

Three provisions of Part 11 bear on who may get into a system and on what happens to the credentials that
let them: **`21 CFR 11.10(d)`, `21 CFR 11.10(g)` and `21 CFR 11.300`.** This chapter says what each of them
requires, in the regulation's terms, and says what none of them requires.

**The last of those is the reason the chapter is long.** Access control is the area of Part 11 where the
industry's beliefs are furthest from the text, and the specific belief this chapter has to dispose of is
that Part 11 imposes a password expiry interval. **It does not, anywhere.**

**And the third provision does not reach as far as it is routinely made to reach**, which is settled here
before anything is built on it:

> **`21 CFR 11.300` governs identification codes and passwords where they are used as the components of
> an electronic signature — its introductory text says so.** On the 42 systems that execute no electronic
> signature it is not engaged, and the account controls there rest on `21 CFR 11.10(d)` and `(g)` and on
> the predicate rules. **Helix nonetheless applies the same account controls estate-wide, as its own
> choice, and does not report doing so as compliance with `21 CFR 11.300`.**

The chapter also records the `OBS-4` link — the shared account on a packaging line system — **and draws no
disposition from it.** That is Phase 08's.

**It states no result.** How the sixty-one systems stand on `(d)`, `(g)` or any other paragraph is
`05.08-the-position.md`'s and `logs/control-position-register.md`'s.

## 2. `21 CFR 11.10(d)` and `(g)` — what they ask

`21 CFR 11.10` opens with an obligation on **persons**, not on products. Verbatim:

> *"Persons who use closed systems to create, modify, maintain, or transmit electronic records shall employ
> procedures and controls designed to ensure the authenticity, integrity, and, when appropriate, the
> confidentiality of electronic records, and to ensure that the signer cannot readily repudiate the signed
> record as not genuine."*

**Procedures and controls, employed by persons.** That phrasing is why there is no such thing as Part 11
compliant software: the duty is on the regulated entity's use of its system, and a vendor cannot discharge
it on the firm's behalf. `01.03 §5` owns the paragraph and its eleven sub-paragraphs.

| ¶ | What it requires, in the sources' own words | What it does **not** reach |
|---|---|---|
| **`(d)`** | **Limiting system access to authorised individuals** | It says nothing about how the limitation is achieved — no technology, no directory, no authentication mechanism and no interval |
| **`(g)`** | **Authority checks** — that only authorised individuals may use the system, *"electronically sign a record"*, access *"the operation or computer system input or output device"*, alter a record, or perform the operation at hand | It is not a duplicate of `(d)`. `(d)` is about the door; **`(g)` is about what a person who is through the door is permitted to do** |

**The distinction between the two paragraphs is worth holding, because a firm that has solved `(d)` often
believes it has solved `(g)`.** A system on which every account belongs to a named, authorised individual
satisfies the question `(d)` asks. If every one of those individuals can approve a batch record, the
question `(g)` asks has not been reached. **Limiting who may enter and limiting what each of them may do
are two controls, and the regulation gives them two paragraphs.**

## 3. `21 CFR 11.300` — the controls over identification codes and passwords

`21 CFR 11.300` sits in Subpart C, and **its introductory text states the condition on which everything
under it operates.** Verbatim:

> *"Persons who use electronic signatures based upon use of identification codes in combination with
> passwords shall employ controls to ensure their security and integrity. Such controls shall include:"*

Five lettered requirements follow, and each is stated below in the regulation's own terms.

| ¶ | What it requires, in the regulation's own words |
|---|---|
| **`(a)`** | **Maintaining the uniqueness of each combined identification code and password**, such that no two individuals have the same combination of identification code and password |
| **`(b)`** | Ensuring that identification code and password **issuances** are *"periodically checked, recalled, or revised (e.g., to cover such events as password aging)"* |
| **`(c)`** | Following loss management procedures **to electronically deauthorise** lost, stolen, missing or otherwise potentially compromised tokens, cards and other devices that bear or generate identification code or password information, **and to issue temporary or permanent replacements** *"using suitable, rigorous controls"* |
| **`(d)`** | Use of transaction safeguards **to prevent** unauthorised use of passwords and/or identification codes, **and to detect and report** in an immediate and urgent manner any attempts at their unauthorised use *"to the system security unit, and, as appropriate, to organizational management"* |
| **`(e)`** | **Initial and periodic testing** of devices, such as tokens or cards, that bear or generate identification code or password information, to ensure that they function properly and **have not been altered** *"in an unauthorized manner"* |

**The chapeau is not decoration, and the industry gloss that `21 CFR 11.300` governs the credential while
`21 CFR 11.200` governs the signature is not what the section says.**

> **`21 CFR 11.300` governs identification codes and passwords where they are used as the components of
> an electronic signature — its introductory text says so.** On the 42 systems that execute no electronic
> signature it is not engaged, and the account controls there rest on `21 CFR 11.10(d)` and `(g)` and on
> the predicate rules. **Helix nonetheless applies the same account controls estate-wide, as its own
> choice, and does not report doing so as compliance with `21 CFR 11.300`.**

**What `21 CFR 11.200` requires of a signature — and that two distinct identification components is not
multi-factor authentication — is `05.07-the-continuing-component.md`'s, and this chapter does not restate
it.**

## 4. The interval that is not a requirement

**"Part 11 requires ninety-day password expiry" is false, and this chapter says so.**

`21 CFR 11.300(b)` requires that issuances be *"periodically checked, recalled, or revised (e.g., to cover
such events as password aging)"*. **That is the whole of the text on the subject, and it specifies no
interval anywhere in Part 11.**

Read what the clause actually does and does not do.

**It names three acts, not one.** *Checked*, *recalled*, *revised*. A firm that reviews its issuances
against its leaver and mover records has checked them. A firm that withdraws a credential has recalled it.
A firm that forces a password change has revised one. **Password ageing is offered as an example of an
event the arrangement should cover** — the regulation's own *"e.g."* — **and an example is not the
requirement.**

**It requires periodicity and does not set one.** *Periodically* means at intervals; it does not name an
interval, and no other section of Part 11 names one either. **Any interval Helix operates is a company
procedural decision.**

**And the decision, once written, is enforceable against Helix.** `21 CFR 211.22(d)` requires the quality
control unit's responsibilities and procedures to be in writing and provides that *"such written procedures
shall be followed."* For procedures owned outside the quality control unit, `21 CFR 211.100(b)` and
`21 CFR 211.160(a)` are what make a written procedure enforceable, **and which provision bites depends on
which procedure it is** — `04.11 §4` owns that argument. **A firm that writes ninety days because it
believes the regulation requires ninety days has invented an obligation and then made it real.** The
obligation is real either way; what is false is the belief about where it came from.

**Two adjacent claims fall with the same reasoning, and both are on this repository's standing list.**
**Part 11 requires no multi-factor authentication** — `21 CFR 11.200(a)(1)` requires *"at least two distinct
identification components such as an identification code and password"*, which an identification code and a
password satisfy. **And Part 11 requires no biometrics**; `21 CFR 11.200(b)` makes them a separate
permitted route and never an obligation.

**What follows for the register is a rule about attribution rather than about controls.** Where Helix
operates an interval, a lockout threshold, a complexity rule or a re-authentication timeout, **the control
is Helix's and the register records it as Helix's.** None of them is entered as compliance with
`21 CFR 11.300(b)`, and **none of them is what `21 CFR 11.300(b)` asks for**: what it asks for is that the
issuances be checked, recalled or revised, and a system that expires passwords on a schedule has not
thereby checked whether the accounts it holds still belong to people who work at Helix.

## 5. What access control at Helix is assessed against

The framework's evidence set for `(d)` and `(g)` is Phase 04's — `04.05 §5` — and this phase assesses
against it. **What the assessment looked at, and what it did not, is stated here so that a determination in
the register can be checked by somebody who did not make it.**

| Looked at | Why |
|---|---|
| The account population on each system, against the individuals it is said to belong to | `(d)` — **limiting system access to authorised individuals** is a statement about who holds an account, and an account nobody can attach to a named individual defeats it |
| Whether accounts are shared, generic, functional or vendor-held | The same question, and the point at which `(d)` and attribution part company — `§6` |
| The permission or role arrangement, and what each role may do | `(g)` — authority checks, including **who may electronically sign a record** |
| Whether an account review takes place, on what basis, and against what source of truth | `21 CFR 11.300(b)`'s *checked, recalled, or revised* on the systems where the credential is a signature component; **on the rest, a Helix control recorded as Helix's** — `§3` |
| Whether a procedure requires each of the above | **The second and third of the three determinations turn on it** — `05.03 §3` |

| Not looked at, and stated so | Why not |
|---|---|
| Whether any control performs as specified | **A control assessment is not validation** — `05.01 §4`, `ADR-0034` |
| The password interval, complexity rule or lockout threshold as a compliance question | **Part 11 sets none of them.** They are recorded as Helix's own controls where they exist |
| Any individual's account, conduct or work | **No document in this repository assesses any individual's work** |
| Whether the arrangement would satisfy an inspector | **The programme forecasts no inspection outcome** |

**The access review record template in `templates/` is the form the review is recorded on**, and it is an
empty form: **no template in this repository contains a result.**

## 6. The `OBS-4` link, recorded

**`OBS-4` on the Form FDA 483 issued at Ridgemont on 2026-02-13 records, in Helix's own summary of it, that
a user account shared by more than one operator was in use on a packaging line system, with the consequence
that entries made through it could not be attributed to a named individual.** `01.02 §4` owns the summary
and it is not restated further here.

**The link is recorded because this is the phase whose subject matter includes it, and nothing is drawn from
it.**

**Two distinctions have to survive the recording, and both were fixed before this phase.**

**A shared account does not make a system open.** Access to such a system is still controlled by Helix's own
people, and `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)` turns on who controls access. **What a shared account defeats is
attribution** — the ability to say which individual did what — which is a different requirement arising
from different text. `02.07 §6` owns that distinction and this chapter carries it without variation.

**Attribution is a CGMP requirement before it is a Part 11 requirement.** FDA's position on shared
credentials in the December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance is grounded in Parts 211 and 212.
`21 CFR 211.188(b)(11)` requires identification of the persons performing and directly supervising or
checking each significant step — **within a batch production and control record**; `21 CFR 211.194(a)(7)`
requires *"The initials or signature of the person who performs each test and the date(s) the tests were
performed"* — **initials suffice, the person need not be an analyst, and the date is part of the
requirement** — and `(a)(8)` requires the initials or signature of a second person showing that the
original records have been reviewed. **Remove Part 11 and every one of those obligations stands
untouched.**

**One nuance the programme records because it is easy to overstate the rule: FDA does permit shared,
read-only accounts for viewing data, where the user cannot modify data or settings.** Whether any particular
arrangement at Helix falls into that category is a question about that arrangement, and the register records
what was read.

**And here the chapter stops.** **No disposition of `OBS-4` is drawn, in either direction.** Nothing here
says the observation's subject matter is present elsewhere in the estate, nothing here says it is not,
nothing here closes it, and nothing here converts it into a deviation, a finding or a control gap. **A Form
FDA 483 is not a legal finding and not a final agency determination** — `01.02 §2` — and **the closure
position on any observation is Phase 08's.** `ADR-0006` keeps the 483's own numbering and forbids the
programme from giving an inspectional observation an identifier of its own.

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no control position.** Nothing here says whether `21 CFR 11.10(d)`, `(g)` or any other
  paragraph is met on any system — `05.08-the-position.md` owns the determinations.
- **It designs no access arrangement.** No account is created, removed, merged or split by this phase, and
  **the Data Integrity Lead does not administer accounts, configure systems or approve changes** —
  `01.07 §6`.
- **It sets no password interval, no complexity rule, no lockout threshold and no re-authentication
  timeout**, as a requirement or as a recommendation. **Part 11 sets none of them.**
- **It states no signature position.** `21 CFR 11.200` and what a signature must manifest are
  `05.07-the-continuing-component.md`'s and `05.06-signatures-and-the-human-readable-form.md`'s.
- **It draws no disposition from `OBS-4`** — Phase 08 — and reaches no conclusion about any packaging line
  system, any shared account elsewhere in the estate, or any individual.
- **It raises no CAPA and no deviation.** **A control gap is not a deviation.**
- **It states no validation outcome**, and **a control assessment is not validation** — `ADR-0034`.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome.
- **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted.
