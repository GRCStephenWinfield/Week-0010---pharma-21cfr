## 1. What this chapter settles

**Most of the paragraphs Helix is weakest on are paragraphs FDA has stated an intention not to enforce.**
`05.08-the-position.md` publishes where the failures sit and how they divide against the discretion set;
that is its figure and this chapter cites it rather than restating it.

**This chapter is about what that fact means, and it means nothing about the position.**

The chapter exists because the sentence it refuses is the one that will be offered, in good faith, by
somebody reading the register for the first time: *most of this is in the part FDA said it would not
enforce, so it is less serious than it looks.* **That reading is a misreading of a guidance document, and
this chapter says why in the terms the guidance itself uses.**

`01.04 §3` owns the general argument that enforcement discretion is not exemption, and this chapter does not
restate it. **What is argued here is narrower and belongs to this phase: what the discretion set does and
does not do to a control position that has already been stated.** `ADR-0035` records the decision.

## 2. What is under enforcement discretion, precisely

FDA's August 2003 guidance *"Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and Application"* is
final and in force. It announced the narrow interpretation of Part 11's scope and the enforcement discretion
set below, **while stating that part 11 remains in effect.**

| Part 11 requirement | Subject |
|---|---|
| **`21 CFR 11.10(a)`** and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Validation of computerised systems** |
| **`21 CFR 11.10(e)`** and **`(k)(2)`**, and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails** |
| **`21 CFR 11.10(b)`** and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Generating copies of records** |
| **`21 CFR 11.10(c)`** and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Protection of records to enable accurate and ready retrieval throughout the retention period** |
| All Part 11 requirements, for systems **operational before 1997-08-20** that meet the guidance's stated conditions | **Legacy systems.** **No Helix system has been assessed against those conditions** |

**The set reaches `(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)`, and `(k)` in part — the guidance names `(k)(2)`, the
second of `(k)`'s two subparagraphs, and says nothing about `(k)(1)`.** `21 CFR 11.10(k)` has two limbs:
`(k)(1)` requires adequate controls over the distribution of, access to, and use of documentation for
system operation and maintenance, and `(k)(2)` requires revision and change control procedures *"to
maintain an audit trail that documents time-sequenced development and modification of systems
documentation"*. **It is the second the discretion set names, and `(k)(1)` is not in the set at all.**

**Two boundaries have to be stated with it, because both are routinely lost.**

**The corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` are not engaged for any Helix system at this vantage**,
because **all sixty-one are closed systems** on the test the regulation sets, which is who controls access —
`ADR-0011`, `02.07 §6`. The limb is recorded for completeness and does no work here.

**And everything else in Part 11 is enforced, by FDA's own statement.** Verbatim: *"We intend to enforce all
other provisions of part 11 including, but not limited to, certain controls for closed systems in
§ 11.10."* And of that set: *"We expect continued compliance with these provisions, and we will continue to
enforce them."* **That set includes limiting system access to authorised individuals, operational system
checks, authority checks, device checks, the education, training and experience determination, written
accountability policies, controls over systems documentation, and all of the electronic signature
requirements — `21 CFR 11.50`, `21 CFR 11.70`, `21 CFR 11.100`, `21 CFR 11.200` and `21 CFR 11.300`.**

## 3. What enforcement discretion is

**It is a stated intention of an agency.** It is not a legal exemption, it is not a safe harbour, and it
confers no rights.

The guidance says so on its own face. It is marked **Contains Nonbinding Recommendations**, and it carries
FDA's standard disclaimer, verbatim: *"It does not create or confer any rights for or on any person and
does not operate to bind FDA or the public"*.

**Three consequences follow and each of them is enough on its own.**

**An intention is held today and may be revised.** Nothing in the 2003 guidance commits FDA to holding it,
and nothing in it gives any firm a right to rely on it.

**It does not amend the Code of Federal Regulations.** `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)` are still
printed in the regulation exactly as they were in 1997. **The substantive control requirements in
§§ 11.10, 11.30, 11.50, 11.70, 11.200 and 11.300 read as originally promulgated**; the part's amendments
since then are confined to scope exclusions added at `21 CFR 11.1(f)`–`(p)`, which touch no drug CGMP
record, and an amendment at `21 CFR 11.100(c)(1)` (88 FR 13018, 2 March 2023) **permitting the
certification to be submitted in electronic or paper form and replacing the mailing address with a pointer
to FDA's web page**. Neither touches a control requirement.

**And it is FDA's statement about FDA's own conduct, not a statement about Helix's systems.** Whether an
audit trail obscures a previously recorded value is a fact about a configuration. It is the same fact
whatever any agency intends to do about it.

**The house sentence: "A programme that reads enforcement discretion as permission has read the wrong
document."**

## 4. What survives it

**The predicate rules survive entirely, and the 2003 guidance says so in four separate places.**

> *"Although persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements for validation
> (e.g., 21 CFR 820.70(i)), this guidance should not be read to impose any additional requirements for
> validation."*

**The predicate-rule example FDA gives is a device provision; for Helix the applicable provision is
`21 CFR 211.68`.**

> *"Persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements related to documentation of,
> for example, date, time, or sequencing of events, as well as any requirements for ensuring that changes
> to records do not obscure previous entries."*

> *"Persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements for record retention and
> availability …"*

> *"We will enforce all predicate rule requirements, including predicate rule record and recordkeeping
> requirements."*

**The second of those carries a clause this phase depends on and this repository had been dropping.** In
the same passage in which FDA states discretion over `21 CFR 11.10(e)`, it says that the predicate-rule
requirements for **ensuring that changes to records do not obscure previous entries** still bind. **The
no-obscuring obligation therefore does not sit inside the discretion set at all: what FDA said it would
not enforce is the Part 11 paragraph, and the obligation the paragraph expresses survives on the
predicate-rule side, where FDA has announced no discretion whatever.** `05.10-the-inversion.md` owns what
that means for the estate and this chapter states none of it; what belongs here is the reason **`CG-15` is
not softened by anything in this chapter**, and the reason is FDA's own sentence.

**For a drug manufacturer the two that matter most in this phase are `21 CFR 211.68` and
`21 CFR 211.100(b)`, and both are regulations rather than guidances.** FDA has announced no discretion over
either and could not exercise discretion over either without saying so.

| Where Part 11 is under discretion | What stands unmoved on the predicate-rule side |
|---|---|
| **`21 CFR 11.10(a)`** — validation | **`21 CFR 211.68(a)`** requires equipment including computers, if used, to be **routinely calibrated, inspected or checked according to a written program designed to assure proper performance**, with written records of those checks maintained; `(b)` requires appropriate controls over computer or related systems **to assure that changes in master production and control records or other records are instituted only by authorized personnel**, requires input/output verification, and requires a backup file |
| **`21 CFR 11.10(e)`** — audit trails | **`21 CFR 211.68(b)`**'s "appropriate controls" clause is expressly and only **to assure that changes in master production and control records or other records are instituted only by authorized personnel**; **`21 CFR 211.100(b)`** requires production and control activities to be **documented at the time of performance**; `21 CFR 211.188` and `21 CFR 211.194(a)` require the record content that makes change visible |
| **`21 CFR 11.10(b)`** — copies | **`21 CFR 211.180(c)`** requires all records required under Part 211, or copies of them, to be **readily available for authorised inspection during the retention period at the establishment where the activities occurred** |
| **`21 CFR 11.10(c)`** — protection through retention | **`21 CFR 211.180`** sets the retention requirements and **`21 CFR 211.68(b)`** sets the backup requirement |

**Every row that shows discretion on the left shows an unmoved obligation on the right.** The asymmetry is
`01.04 §3`'s and is cited here rather than re-argued.

**One clarification the table would otherwise hide.** The predicate rule and the Part 11 paragraph are not
the same requirement expressed twice. `21 CFR 211.100(b)` requires an activity to be documented at the time
it is performed; `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires that a change to that documentation not obscure what it said
before. **They overlap in subject matter and neither is a restatement of the other**, so a system that fails
`(e)` has not automatically failed `21 CFR 211.100(b)`, and a system that satisfies `21 CFR 211.100(b)` has
not thereby satisfied `(e)`. **This chapter draws no predicate-rule conclusion about any system**, and none
is drawn anywhere in this phase.

## 5. The position is stated identically inside and outside it

**This is the sentence the chapter exists for.**

**The register does not mark a position differently because FDA said it would not enforce the paragraph.**
There is no column recording whether a cell sits inside the discretion set, no annotation on the cells that
do, no separate section for them, no softened vocabulary, and no ordering that puts them last.
`logs/control-position-register.md` records eleven determinations per system and **a reader cannot tell from
a cell whether its paragraph is inside the set or outside it.**

Three reasons, each sufficient alone.

**The determination is about Helix and the discretion is about FDA.** *Not met* on `21 CFR 11.10(e)` records
that the control the paragraph describes is not present on a system on 2027-01-29. **Nothing about FDA's
stated intention changes what is on the system**, and a register that annotated the cell would be recording
a fact about an agency in a field reserved for a fact about a configuration.

**A qualifier does not survive the journey.** Phase 04 refused a provisional control position for exactly
this reason: **a labelled value enters a summary, a slide and a report, and the qualifier survives none of
those journeys** — `ADR-0029`. A discretion annotation would travel the same route, and it would travel it
faster, because it is the annotation everybody wants. **Once *Not met (under enforcement discretion)*
exists as a value, the question that would have asked what it would take to close the gap stops being
asked.**

**And dividing the eleven by FDA's posture is a division `ADR-0029` already refused.** That decision
declined to state a position on the paragraphs outside the discretion set while staying silent on those
inside it, because doing so would produce a position on some and silence on others **for a reason about
FDA's posture rather than about Helix's systems.** The same reasoning forbids the mirror image: stating a
position on all eleven and then marking some of them as mattering less.

**What the register does instead is the control gap register's job.** `logs/control-gap-register.md` records,
for each gap, **whether it sits inside enforcement discretion** — as a fact about the regulatory landscape,
recorded where a reader can find it, next to the cause and the closure condition. **The fact is published.
It is simply not allowed to modify a determination.**

## 6. The four moves a hostile reader will make

Each of these is a reasonable move, each is answered, and none of the answers is that the reader has the
facts wrong.

**"Most of the failures are in the discretion set, so the position is better than it looks."** The premise
is `05.08-the-position.md`'s and it is accurate. The inference does not follow. **A control that is absent
is absent.** What the discretion set changes is FDA's stated intention about enforcing one paragraph of one
part; it changes nothing about the configuration, and it leaves `21 CFR 211.68` and `21 CFR 211.100(b)`
exactly where they were.

**"FDA has not enforced these paragraphs since 2003, so the risk is theoretical."** This is an argument
about likelihood, and **no entry in this programme's registers carries a likelihood, an impact or a score.**
It is also an argument about somebody else's future conduct, which the programme does not make: **it will
not forecast the outcome of any inspection**, and a prediction that a provision will not be enforced is a
forecast wearing different clothes.

**"Then Helix should fix the paragraphs outside the set first."** That is a sequencing proposal, and
sequencing is not this phase's. **This phase states no remediation plan, no priority order and no CAPA**,
and `05.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md` says so. It is also worth noticing what the proposal assumes:
that the closure cost of a gap tracks the discretion set. **Nothing in the register suggests it does**, and
Phase 04 found the opposite shape when it observed that the gaps cheapest to fix were the ones hardest to
find — `04.08 §6`.

**And: "the position is weakest exactly where FDA is least interested, which is a coincidence worth
explaining."** It may be, and this phase does not explain it. **An explanation would be a cause, and a cause
asserted at this vantage would be a guess wearing the clothes of a conclusion.** Nothing in this chapter
bears on `IS-01`, which stays open and stays unweighted, and whose second half is Phase 08's.

## 7. `ADR-0035` — discretion changes nothing about the position

**`ADR-0035`, taken at `DEC-517` on 2027-01-27 by Terrence Abbatiello, records that enforcement discretion
changes what FDA said it would enforce and changes nothing about the control position, which is stated
identically inside and outside it.**

Three rules follow.

**No determination is annotated, qualified, softened or re-ordered by reference to the discretion set**, in
the register, in any chapter, in any governance record or in any report.

**The discretion set is published as a fact and never as a mitigation.** It appears in this chapter and in
the control gap register's own column, with the guidance's status stated alongside it: **final, in force,
nonbinding, and not an exemption.**

**And no closure condition is relaxed because a paragraph sits inside it.** A gap on `21 CFR 11.10(e)` has
the same closure condition it would have had if FDA had never issued the 2003 guidance, **because the
condition describes what would have to become true on the system.**

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no count.** Where the failures sit, and how they divide against the discretion set, is
  `05.08-the-position.md`'s.
- **It states no control position for any paragraph on any system.**
- **It reaches no predicate-rule conclusion.** Nothing here says that Helix has met or failed
  `21 CFR 211.68`, `21 CFR 211.100(b)` or any other provision of Part 211. **Those provisions are cited to
  locate an obligation in the text, not to assert that Helix meets it.**
- **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind**, and characterises nothing as a violation of anything.
- **It forecasts no enforcement action and no inspection outcome**, and does not predict what FDA will or
  will not do about any provision. **The programme will not forecast the outcome of any future FDA
  inspection.**
- **It proposes no sequencing, no priority, no remediation plan, no CAPA and no deviation.** **A control gap
  is not a deviation.**
- **It states no validation outcome**, and **a control assessment is not validation** — `ADR-0034`.
- **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted.
