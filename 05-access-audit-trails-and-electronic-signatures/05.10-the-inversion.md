## 1. One idea

**Twenty-three of the sixty-one systems carry a reason-for-change field, which Part 11 does not require.
Fourteen of those twenty-three do not meet `21 CFR 11.10(e)`'s requirement that record changes not obscure
previously recorded information, which it does.**

**Effort spent on a control that is not required, absent on one that is.**

`ADR-0036`, `DEC-519`, taken by Joachim Reuter on 2027-01-28: **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change
field, and holding one is never reported as compliance with anything.**

## 2. What the text says, and what it does not

`05.05-audit-trails.md` quotes `21 CFR 11.10(e)` whole and owns the reading of it. **This chapter takes two
clauses from it and does not re-argue either.**

**What the paragraph requires.** *"Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information."*
**That is a no-overwrite requirement.** The prior value must remain discernible after the change. It is a
requirement about what survives a change, and it is the requirement fourteen of the twenty-three do not
meet.

**What the paragraph does not require.** **There is no textual requirement anywhere in Part 11 for a
reason-for-change field.** Not in `21 CFR 11.10(e)`, not in any other paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10`, not in
`21 CFR 11.30`, and not in Subpart C. The paragraph reaches *"operator entries and actions that create,
modify, or delete electronic records"* and requires the date and time of them to be recorded
independently; **it says nothing about why anybody did anything.**

**The same is true of read and view events**, and for the same reason: the three verbs are create, modify
and delete, and reading is not among them.

**Where Helix implements either, it is Helix's decision and this repository says so.** A reason-for-change
field is a perfectly good control. It is useful to a reviewer, it is expected by other regulators' guidance
and by other firms' auditors, and a firm may have excellent reasons for wanting one. **What it is not is a
Part 11 requirement**, and **no document in this repository describes a reason-for-change field, or a
read-event log, as a Part 11 requirement.**

## 3. The twenty-three, and the fourteen

**Every figure is a count of `logs/control-position-register.md §2` and of the field enumeration at
`EV-527`.**

| Cut | Value |
|---|---|
| Systems in scope | **61** |
| **Systems holding a reason-for-change field** | **23** |
| **Of those 23, systems whose `21 CFR 11.10(e)` position is Not met** | **14** |
| Of those 23, systems whose `21 CFR 11.10(e)` position is Met | **9** |
| **Check** | **14 + 9 = 23** |
| Systems failing `21 CFR 11.10(e)` in total | **29** — `logs/control-position-register.md §4` |
| Of the 29, systems holding no reason-for-change field | **15** |
| **Check** | **14 + 15 = 29** |

**The fourteen, named.** `SYS-012`, `SYS-013`, `SYS-014`, `SYS-015`, `SYS-016`, `SYS-017`, `SYS-018`,
`SYS-020`, `SYS-023`, `SYS-024`, `SYS-027`, `SYS-034`, `SYS-045` and `SYS-053`.

**They fail `21 CFR 11.10(e)` in two different ways and both are recorded in the gap register.**

**Eight of the fourteen — `SYS-012` to `SYS-018` and `SYS-020` — are `CG-14`.** The audit trail's scope is
configurable at administrator level and a change to that configuration is not itself recorded. Every one of
those eight holds a reason-for-change field, so an operator amending a value is required by the product to
type a reason, into a trail whose extent somebody can alter without leaving a mark.

**Six of the fourteen — `SYS-023`, `SYS-024`, `SYS-027`, `SYS-034`, `SYS-045` and `SYS-053` — are
`CG-15`.** A change to a stored value replaces the previous value in the record the reviewer sees.
**Those six are the sharpest form of the inversion in the estate: the field capturing the reason for the
change is retained, and the value the change was made to is not.** A reviewer opening such a record finds
an explanation of a change and no way to see what was changed.

**The `OBS-2` link is recorded and no conclusion is drawn from it.** `SYS-023` and `SYS-024` are the
chromatography data systems and are among the six. **The chromatography-specific work is Phase 06's**,
`MS-10`, and nothing in this chapter bears on it or may be cited in support of any conclusion about it.

**And nine of the twenty-three meet `21 CFR 11.10(e)`.** They hold a reason-for-change field and their
record changes do not obscure previously recorded information. **They are not the finding and they are
named as a class so that the chapter is not read as saying that a reason-for-change field causes a
failure.** It does not cause one. It conceals one, which is §4's subject.

## 4. Why the field conceals the absence

**Because the field is the thing everybody looks at.**

A reviewer opening a changed record sees a change, a signer, a timestamp and a reason. **That is a complete-
looking picture and three of its four elements are exactly what a reviewer wants.** The fourth — the value
before the change — is the one the regulation actually requires, and it is the one whose absence is invisible
in a view that shows what is there rather than what is not.

**An auditor asks a question of the same shape.** *Does the system capture why a change was made?* is a
natural question, it is the question other firms are asked, and on twenty-three systems the answer is yes.
**The next question — and is the previous value still discernible? — is a different question, and nothing in
the estate required anybody to ask it.** It is the same mechanism Phase 04 published as its twelve, on a
different object:
`../04-the-validation-framework-and-policy-architecture/04.08-where-the-obligations-land.md §5`,
`ADR-0028`. **Cited, not re-argued.**

**And there is a third layer, which is where the effort went.** Configuring a reason-for-change field is
work. Somebody specified it, somebody enabled it, somebody wrote the picklist of reasons, somebody trained
the operators to complete it and somebody reviews what they typed. **All of that effort was spent on a
control Part 11 does not require, on fourteen systems where the control Part 11 does require is absent.**
That is not a criticism of anybody who did the work: the field is genuinely useful and the requirement it
was believed to answer is one the whole industry cites. **It is a description of where the industry's
attention goes, observed on one estate and counted.**

## 5. Why it happens, and it is not carelessness

**The reason-for-change field has become a Part 11 signature in the way a logo becomes a brand.** It is on
every vendor's Part 11 feature list, in every consultant's readiness checklist and in most firms'
audit-trail review templates. A firm that has one looks compliant to anybody scanning for the marks of
compliance. **A firm that has one and cannot show a previous value looks exactly the same.**

**The no-obscuring requirement is harder to see because it is a requirement about an absence.** *Record
changes shall not obscure previously recorded information* is satisfied by something continuing to be
visible, and continuing visibility is not a feature anybody demonstrates. There is no screen for it. There
is no field to configure. **The only way to test it is to change a value and then go looking for the old
one**, which is what this phase did, and which nothing in Helix's estate had required anybody to do.

**Guidance pulls the same way, and this repository is careful about whose guidance it is.** **Part 11
imposes no reason-for-change requirement.** FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance does
recommend change justification in a CGMP context — *"Documentation should include change justification for
the reprocessing"* — and `21 CFR 211.100(b)` and `21 CFR 211.160(a)` require deviations from written
procedures to be *"recorded and justified"*. **None of those is `21 CFR 11.10(e)`, and holding a
reason-for-change field is not compliance with it.** The inversion stands: the field Helix built serves a
CGMP expectation, and the requirement it does not meet is the one `21 CFR 11.10(e)` actually states. FDA
uses plain ALCOA and maps the attributes to the CGMP
predicate rules, citing Part 11 nowhere — `ADR-0019`, and `03.04` owns footnote 5. **The four additional
attributes are MHRA's, MHRA itself refers to the shorter acronym and states there is no difference in
expectations regardless of which is used, and it does not reject them.**

## 6. What this finding is not

- **It is not a recommendation to remove any reason-for-change field.** Nothing in this phase recommends
  anything. The twenty-three fields are Helix's own controls and **withdrawing a control because a
  regulation does not compel it is the same error as claiming compliance because a regulation does not
  mention it.**
- **It is not a statement that any system's audit trail is worthless.** Fourteen systems fail the
  no-obscuring requirement; the trails on them still record entries and actions, and on eight of them the
  failure is about who can change the trail's scope rather than about the trail itself.
- **It is not a new determination.** The fourteen are already Not met on `(e)` in
  `logs/control-position-register.md` and are already inside `CG-14` and `CG-15`. **This chapter counts an
  overlap and creates nothing.**
- **It is not a deviation, a CAPA or a remediation plan.** **A control gap is not a deviation**, and no
  procedure was breached — `05.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md`.
- **It is not a validation outcome.** **No package executed, no protocol run, no test result** —
  `ADR-0034`, `IS-34`.
- **It is not a chromatography determination**, notwithstanding that two of the six are the chromatography
  data systems. **Phase 06**, `MS-10`.
- **It bears on `OBS-6` nowhere.** `OBS-6` concerns a change to an approved value in the electronic batch
  record and is one of the three observations in this programme's scope. **`SYS-001` is not among the
  fourteen and not among the twenty-nine**, and this chapter states no position on the observation, its
  remediation or its disposition. `ADR-0006` keeps the 483's numbering and this programme keeps one set of
  books.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.** **An open gap is Helix's own
  assessment of its own position. It is not a violation, not a breach, not a non-compliance, not a finding
  and not an inspectional observation.**

## 7. What would close it

**Stated as the condition, not as a plan.** No date, no cost, no sequence, no priority.

| For | The condition |
|---|---|
| The six at `CG-15` | **The previous value retained in a form the reviewer sees**, so that a record change does not obscure previously recorded information |
| The eight at `CG-14` | **The audit trail's configuration fixed and placed under change control**, with changes to it recorded |
| The twenty-three | **Nothing.** The field is Helix's own control and no requirement attaches to it. What is required is that it never be reported as compliance with `21 CFR 11.10(e)` or with any other provision — `ADR-0036` |
| The estate | **A procedure requiring the no-obscuring property to be tested by changing a value and looking for the previous one**, so that the test that found this is required rather than remembered |

**The third row is the one `ADR-0036` is about.** The other three rows describe work; that one describes a
form of words. **A firm that answers *do you have a reason-for-change field?* with a yes has answered a
question about a control it chose**, and the answer becomes misleading the moment it is offered as an
answer to a question about `21 CFR 11.10(e)`.

**`IS-32` carries the twenty-three and the fourteen**, owned by Joachim Reuter, raised 2027-01-28, open.
Nothing is closed by this chapter.
