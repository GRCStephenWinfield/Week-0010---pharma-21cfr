## 1. What this chapter settles

**`21 CFR 11.10(e)` is one of the most quoted paragraphs in this industry and one of the least read.** It is
three sentences long. This chapter quotes it whole, reads it clause by clause, and then spends most of its
length on what it does not say — because the things attributed to it that are not in it are the things
Helix's estate has been built around.

**Two of them matter most and both are in this repository's standing list.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` **says
nothing whatever about read or view events**, and it **imposes no reason-for-change requirement**. What it
does require is that record changes not obscure previously recorded information, and that the audit trail
be retained for at least as long as the record it describes.

`ADR-0036` records the consequence: **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, and holding one is never
reported as compliance with anything.**

**This chapter states no result.** How the sixty-one systems stand on `(e)` is `05.08-the-position.md`'s,
and the systems that hold a reason-for-change field are `05.10-the-inversion.md`'s.

## 2. The paragraph, whole

**`21 CFR 11.10(e)`, verbatim and entire:**

> *"Use of secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails to independently record the date and time
> of operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic records. Record changes shall
> not obscure previously recorded information. Such audit trail documentation shall be retained for a
> period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records and shall be available for
> agency review and copying."*

**That is the whole paragraph.** Three sentences. Four requirements, and they are set out separately below
because each of them is a different obligation about a different thing.

**One fact about the vocabulary has to arrive before the reading.** **Part 11 does not define "audit
trail".** `21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and no more, and *audit trail*, *metadata*, *data
integrity*, *static record* and *dynamic record* are none of them. **`21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires the use of
audit trails and describes their properties without ever naming the thing.** The definition the industry
uses comes from FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance, which defines an audit trail as *"a
secure, computer-generated, time-stamped electronic record that allows for reconstruction of the course of
events relating to the creation, modification, or deletion of an electronic record"*. `01.03 §3` owns the
point and it is not restated further.

## 3. What it requires, clause by clause

| Clause | What it obliges | What it does not reach |
|---|---|---|
| *"Use of secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails"* | The trail is **generated by the computer**, is **secure**, and carries a **time stamp**. A log a person writes is not this | It names no format, no storage location, no field set and no technology |
| *"to independently record the date and time of operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic records"* | **Independently** — the record of the action is not the action's own output. **Three verbs: create, modify, delete** | **Reading is not among them.** `§4` |
| *"Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information."* | **A no-overwrite requirement.** After a change, **the prior value must remain discernible** | **It is not a reason-for-change requirement.** `§4` |
| *"Such audit trail documentation shall be retained for a period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records"* | **The audit trail inherits the retention period of the record it describes. It does not have one of its own** | It sets no period. **The period comes from the predicate rule** — `21 CFR 211.180`, Phase 07 |
| *"and shall be available for agency review and copying."* | **The obligation runs to FDA.** The trail must be producible and copyable for the agency | **It imposes no internal review obligation at all** — `§5` |

**The second sentence is the one this phase reads hardest, because it is the one an estate can fail
quietly.** *Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information* is a requirement about what
survives a change, not about what is captured at the moment of it. **A system that records that a value was
changed, by whom and when, but overwrites the old value, has obscured previously recorded information** —
and it has done so while producing an audit trail entry that looks like compliance.

**And the retention clause is a relative requirement, which is why it cannot be answered on its own.** The
audit trail's period is *at least as long as* the record's, and **no retention period is recorded against
any of the thirty-four in-scope record types** — `IS-14`, `DP-18`, Phase 07. `IS-05` records that Helix
cannot state, for any system, how long audit trail data is retained relative to the retention period of the
underlying record. **This phase does not set a retention period and does not close either entry**, because
setting one is Phase 07's and neither is answerable without it.

## 4. What `21 CFR 11.10(e)` does not say

**It says nothing about read or view events.** The paragraph reaches *"operator entries and actions that
create, modify, or delete electronic records"*. **Three verbs, and viewing is not among them.** No section
of Part 11 requires a read event, a view event, a query, a print or an export to be logged. **Where a
system at Helix logs any of them, that is Helix's arrangement or the supplier's default, and the register
records it as such and never as compliance with `(e)`.**

**And it imposes no reason-for-change requirement.** The second sentence requires that record changes not
obscure previously recorded information. **That is a no-overwrite requirement. There is no textual
requirement anywhere in Part 11 for a reason to be captured**, on any record, on any system, at any time.

**The two claims are not equal in consequence, and the difference is the phase's.** A firm that logs read
events has spent money on something Part 11 does not ask for and has broken nothing. **A firm that holds a
reason-for-change field and reports it as its audit trail control has substituted a field the regulation
does not require for a property the regulation does require** — and the substitution is easy to make,
because a reason-for-change dialogue is visible, is demonstrable to a visitor, and feels like the audit
trail working. **`21 CFR 11.10(e)` asks whether the previous value is still discernible. A reason field
does not answer that question, and a system can hold one while overwriting the value it asks a reason for.**

**`ADR-0036` fixes the reporting rule:** a reason-for-change field is recorded as a Helix control where it
exists, is never described as a Part 11 requirement, and **is never reported as compliance with
`21 CFR 11.10(e)` or with anything else.** `05.10-the-inversion.md` owns what the estate looked like when
that rule was applied to it, and this chapter does not report it.

**One more absence, for completeness.** `21 CFR 11.70` requires signatures to be linked to their records
*"to ensure that the signatures cannot be excised, copied, or otherwise transferred to falsify an electronic
record by ordinary means."* **It names no mechanism. There is no hashing requirement at `21 CFR 11.70` and
no digital-signature requirement for a closed system** — `01.03 §8` owns the list and
`05.06-signatures-and-the-human-readable-form.md` owns the signature sections.

## 5. Where the review obligation actually comes from

**`21 CFR 11.10(e)` imposes no internal review obligation at all — no periodic review, no second-person
review, no review at any frequency.** The only review it names is the agency's.

**The review obligation comes from the predicate rules**, and `01.04 §7` owns that argument. In outline:
laboratory records carry the second-person review at `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` — *"the initials or signature of
a second person showing that the original records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and
compliance with established standards"*; production and control records carry quality unit review and
approval at `21 CFR 211.192`. **FDA's construction, in the December 2018 guidance, is that the audit trail
is part of the record being reviewed**, so the obligation reaches it through the existing review requirement
rather than through a new one — *"Personnel responsible for record review under CGMP should review the audit
trails that capture changes to data associated with the record as they review the rest of the record …"*,
the guidance's own list of predicate-rule citations elided.

**On frequency, the conditional form is the whole of the point and it is never published without its
condition.**

> **The review frequency is inherited from the review frequency CGMP specifies for the underlying data;
> where CGMP specifies none, the firm determines it, and only there does risk enter.**

FDA's own words are *"If the review frequency for the data is specified in CGMP regulations, adhere to that
frequency for the audit trail review."* **The default is therefore inheritance rather than periodicity** —
review at batch release, review when the laboratory record is reviewed — and risk-based periodicity applies
**only** where CGMP is silent about the underlying data. **"Audit trails must be reviewed quarterly" is not
what FDA says.**

> **No primary source names a stand-alone periodic audit trail review frequency.** FDA names the
> frequencies to be **inherited** — review after each significant step under `21 CFR 211.188(b)`, and
> review before batch release — and leaves the firm to determine one only where CGMP specifies none for
> the underlying data. **This phase names none.**

**`IS-04` stays deliberately unresolved** — `CAL-06`'s cadence is undefined precisely where CGMP does not
specify a frequency for the underlying data, and nothing in this phase resolves it, including by
implication. **`IS-06` — whether the audit trail associated with a result forms part of the original record
reviewed under `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` — is a question this phase's evidence bears on**, and
`05.13-phase-summary-and-transition.md` records what it does and does not do with it.

## 6. The `OBS-2` link, recorded

**`OBS-2` on the Form FDA 483 concerns audit trail review in the QC laboratory**: in Helix's own summary,
laboratory records generated on the chromatography data system were reviewed and approved without the
associated audit trail forming part of that review. `01.02 §4` owns the summary.

**The regulatory anchor is the predicate rule and not Part 11.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires audit trails to
be generated, retained and available for agency review and copying; **it imposes no review obligation at
all.** The review obligation for laboratory records is `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`'s.

**The link is recorded and nothing is drawn from it.** Nothing here concludes anything about the
chromatography data systems, about the laboratory record review procedure, or about whether the
observation's subject matter is present elsewhere. **The chromatography estate is Phase 06's, `MS-10`; the
closure position on any observation is Phase 08's**; and `GOV-10 §6` directed that no document draw the
connecting sentence before those phases. **That direction binds this chapter.**

## 7. What the assessment of `(e)` looked at

Stated so that a determination in the register can be checked by somebody who did not make it, and so that
the boundary between what was read and what was concluded is visible.

| Looked at | The clause it goes to |
|---|---|
| Whether the trail is computer-generated, secure and time-stamped, and whether it is separable from the record's own output | *"secure, computer-generated, time-stamped"* and *"independently record"* |
| Which operator actions the trail captures, tested against **create, modify and delete** | *"operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete"* |
| **Whether the value recorded before a change remains discernible after it** | *"Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information."* |
| What the trail's retention arrangement is, **and that it cannot be resolved against a record retention period that does not exist** | *"retained for a period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records"* |
| Whether the trail can be produced and copied | *"available for agency review and copying"* |
| Whether a procedure requires each of the above | **The three determinations turn on it** — `05.03 §3` |

**Not looked at, and stated so:** whether the trail performs as specified, which is validation and not a
control position — `ADR-0034`; whether any entry in any trail is accurate, which is a statement about a
record and not about a control; and **whether any reason-for-change field is well designed**, which is a
question about a control Part 11 does not require.

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no control position.** Nothing here says whether `21 CFR 11.10(e)` is met on any system —
  `05.08-the-position.md` owns the determinations and `05.10-the-inversion.md` owns the reason-for-change
  population.
- **It configures no audit trail.** No setting is changed, enabled or disabled by this phase.
- **It sets no audit trail review frequency as a requirement**, and **no primary source names a
  stand-alone periodic one.** FDA names the frequencies to be **inherited** — review after each
  significant step under `21 CFR 211.188(b)`, and review before batch release — and leaves the firm to
  determine one only where CGMP specifies none for the underlying data. **This phase names none**, and
  `IS-04` stays deliberately unresolved.
- **It sets no retention period, backup rule or archive rule** — `IS-05`, `IS-14`, `DP-18`, Phase 07.
- **It draws no conclusion from `OBS-2`** — Phase 06 for the chromatography data system, Phase 08 for the
  closure position — and it converts no inspectional observation into anything.
- **It describes no reason-for-change field as a Part 11 requirement, and reports none as compliance with
  anything** — `ADR-0036`.
- **It states no validation outcome**, and **a control assessment is not validation** — `ADR-0034`.
- **It raises no CAPA and no deviation.** **A control gap is not a deviation.**
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome. **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted.
