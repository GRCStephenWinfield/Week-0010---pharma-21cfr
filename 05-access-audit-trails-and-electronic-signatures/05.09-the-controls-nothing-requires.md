## 1. One idea

**Two hundred and eighteen control positions are met by something that nothing requires.**

They are not gaps. Every one of them is a control that is in place, working, and doing exactly what the
paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` it answers to describes. **What is absent is not the control. What is absent is
a sentence obliging anybody to keep it.**

**A control nobody requires can be withdrawn without a deviation, because no procedure would be
breached.** That is the whole of this chapter and everything below is its consequences.

`ADR-0031`, `DEC-508`, taken by Terrence Abbatiello on 2027-01-06: **Met but not required is a
determination in its own right.** `05.03-the-three-determinations.md` owns the argument for the
determination's existence; this chapter owns what the two hundred and eighteen instances of it mean.

## 2. What withdrawal without a deviation actually means

Take an audit trail on one of the twenty-six systems where `21 CFR 11.10(e)` is met and nothing requires
it. It is running. It records the date and time of operator entries and actions that create, modify or
delete records, its record changes do not obscure previously recorded information, and it is retained
against the record it describes.

**Now suppose somebody turns it off.**

**No procedure has been breached, because no procedure required it to be on.** There is therefore no
deviation to raise, because a deviation is a departure from a requirement and there was no requirement.
There is no change control trigger, because the quality system's change control fires on changes to
controlled things and the configuration was not one. **There is no record of the withdrawal anywhere,
except in the system itself if the system happens to record its own configuration changes — and on the
systems in `CG-14` it does not.**

**The next thing that happens is nothing, and that is the point.** Nobody is told. The next periodic review
finds a system without an audit trail and has nothing to compare it against. The next validation package,
whenever one is written, specifies the evidence Phase 04's framework requires and finds none. **And a firm
asked how a control it had was lost cannot answer, because losing it broke no rule.**

**Contrast the same event on one of the six systems where `21 CFR 11.10(e)` is met and required.** Turning
the audit trail off breaches the sentence that requires it. That is a deviation, it is raised, it is
investigated, and `21 CFR 211.22(d)` supplies the teeth — the quality control unit's responsibilities and
procedures shall be in writing and *"such written procedures shall be followed."* **The control is not
better on those six systems. It is more durable, which is a different property and is the one that survives
staff turnover, a product upgrade and a busy afternoon.**

**So the second determination is not a softer version of the first.** A position that is Met but not
required is a position that is true today and has no mechanism holding it true. **It is not a control at
all in the sense a quality system uses the word. It is a configuration that happens to be right.**

## 3. Where the 218 sit

**Every figure is a count of `logs/control-position-register.md §2`.**

| ¶ | Subject | **Met but not required** | Met and required | Not met |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(a)` | Validation | **0** | 37 | 24 |
| `(b)` | Accurate and complete copies | **30** | 12 | 19 |
| `(c)` | Protection of records throughout the retention period | **35** | 9 | 17 |
| `(d)` | Limiting system access to authorised individuals | **5** | 44 | 12 |
| `(e)` | Audit trails | **26** | 6 | 29 |
| `(f)` | Operational system checks | **33** | 20 | 8 |
| `(g)` | Authority checks | **17** | 33 | 11 |
| `(h)` | Device checks | **46** | 10 | 5 |
| `(i)` | Education, training and experience | **5** | 47 | 9 |
| `(j)` | Written accountability policies | **7** | 48 | 6 |
| `(k)` | Controls over systems documentation | **14** | 36 | 11 |
| **Total** | | **218** | **302** | **151** |

**The distribution is not random and it is not surprising once it is seen.**

**The paragraphs with the most unrequired controls are the paragraphs that describe properties of how a
system was built.** Device checks, operational system checks, the ability to produce a complete copy, the
protection of a record in a store: every one of those is a thing an engineer either did or did not do when
the system was configured, and none of them is a thing an operator performs. **A quality system writes
procedures for acts people perform.** Nobody writes a procedure requiring an interface to keep checking the
validity of its source, because nobody performs that act; the interface does.

**The paragraphs with the fewest are the paragraphs that describe acts.** Limiting access to authorised
individuals, education and training, written accountability policies — five, five and seven unrequired
positions respectively — are all things somebody does, repeatedly, and Helix has procedures for them
because it has always had procedures for them.

**`(a)` has none at all**, and it is the one paragraph in the register where the control and the
requirement are the same object: a validation package exists because the computerised system validation
procedure requires one.

**`(e)` is the row to read twice.** Twenty-six audit trails are running that nothing requires, against six
that something does. **The single most-cited Part 11 control in this industry runs, on this estate, on
twenty-six systems under no requirement and on six under one.**

**Sixty of the sixty-one systems carry at least one Met but not required position.** One does not:
`SYS-001`, on which all eleven positions are Met and required. **That is the shape of the finding at system
level — not a handful of neglected corners, but one system in sixty-one whose eleven-paragraph position
would survive somebody reconfiguring it without asking.**

## 4. This is Phase 04's twelve, seen at control level

**Phase 04 found twelve obligations landing in a procedure that exists, covers the subject matter and does
not require the act**, and recorded them as a distinct class of gap from the five landing in no procedure at
all. `ADR-0028`, `DEC-417`, and
`../04-the-validation-framework-and-policy-architecture/04.08-where-the-obligations-land.md §5` owns the
argument. **It is cited here and is not re-argued.**

**The 218 are the same phenomenon observed from the other end.** Phase 04 walked from an obligation to the
procedure estate and asked whether any sentence required the act. Phase 05 walks from a control that is
running and asks whether any sentence requires it to keep running. **When the answer is no, Phase 04 records
a procedure gap and Phase 05 records a control that is met and not required. They are two views of one
absence.**

**And the property Phase 04 identified transfers exactly.** The twelve are invisible to the question *is
there a procedure?*, because the answer is an accurate yes. **The 218 are invisible to the question *is the
control in place?*, because the answer is an accurate yes.** Both questions are the natural ones. Both are
asked by internal audit, by a third party, by management review and by a firm's own management. **Both
return a true answer and stop.**

**The second question — *and does anything require it?* — is a question somebody has to know to ask, and
knowing to ask it requires already suspecting the answer.** That is why the determination exists as a
determination and not as a footnote on a two-value scale: **a class that has to be counted separately is a
class somebody has to look at separately.**

**One thing does not transfer, and it is worth being honest about it.** Phase 04's twelve are cheap to fix
and expensive to find. **The 218 are cheap to find, now that the register exists, and are not cheap to
fix** — closing a Met but not required position means writing or amending a sentence in a controlled
procedure, and doing that two hundred and eighteen times is not a document exercise, because every sentence
Helix writes is a sentence Helix can be found not to have followed. **Which provision holds Helix to a
given procedure depends on which procedure it is**: `21 CFR 211.22(d)` for the quality control unit's own,
`21 CFR 211.100(b)` for written production and process control procedures, `21 CFR 211.160(a)` for
laboratory control mechanisms.
`../04-the-validation-framework-and-policy-architecture/04.11-what-a-framework-cannot-fix.md` owns that
exposure and `PR-32` carries it.

## 5. What this is not an argument for

**It is not an argument that the 218 controls should be withdrawn**, and nothing in this phase suggests it.
They are doing useful work and several of them are doing work the predicate rules require whether or not
Part 11 is in the picture.

**It is not an argument that Helix should write two hundred and eighteen sentences.** A programme that
responded to this chapter by requiring every control it happens to have would produce a quality system it
could be found not to have followed in two hundred and eighteen new places, at no gain in what anybody
does. **The nine SOPs Phase 04 approved are not yet applied to anything**, and adding to them before
applying them would be the same mistake at greater scale — `PR-32`, `DP-22`.

**It is not an argument about any individual's work.** A control that is present because somebody
configured it carefully is a control somebody configured carefully. **The absence is a sentence in a
procedure, not an act by a person**, and no document in this repository assesses any individual's work.

**It is not a validation outcome, a deviation, a CAPA or a remediation plan.** **No validation package has
been executed, no protocol has been run and no test result exists**, and **a control gap is not a
deviation** — which matters twice over here, because **the 218 are not even gaps.** Nothing is absent from
them except a requirement. `05.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md` says all of this as a numbered chapter.

**And it is not a re-scoring or a re-banding of anything.** Phase 03's registers and Phase 04's rigour
register are inputs and are unchanged.

## 6. What the determination is for

**A determination earns its place by changing what somebody does with the number, and this one does three
things.**

**It stops the 520.** Reporting five hundred and twenty met positions out of six hundred and seventy-one is
arithmetically correct and materially misleading, and the only defence against it is a register in which
the two components are held apart and cannot be added by somebody working quickly —
`logs/control-position-register.md`, `PR-35`.

**It gives the periodic review something to compare against.** A review that finds a control absent can
only call it a change if something said it should be present. **On 218 positions, nothing does**, and a
review has no baseline to work from. Recording which positions those are is what turns *the control has
gone* from an observation nobody can act on into an entry with a prior state.

**It tells anybody sizing the work what kind of work it is.** The 151 Not met positions want engineering,
configuration and in some cases a different product. **The 218 want sentences**, and sentences are written
by a quality unit that already has nine procedures approved and unapplied. **Those are different queues
drawing on different capacity**, and a phase that merged them into a single count of *things to fix* would
have hidden that.

**`IS-29` carries the 218**, owned by Priyanka Venkataraman, raised 2027-01-06, open. **Nothing is closed by
this chapter**, and `IS-29` does not close by the 218 being reduced — it closes when the quality system says
which controls Helix requires of itself, which is a decision nobody has taken.
