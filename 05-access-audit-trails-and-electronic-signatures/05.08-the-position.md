## 1. What this chapter reports

**Six hundred and seventy-one control positions have been determined and this chapter is what they say.**

The unit is a paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` on a system — `ADR-0030`, argued at `05.02-the-grid.md`. The three
determinations are Met and required, Met but not required and Not met — `ADR-0031`, argued at
`05.03-the-three-determinations.md`. **Neither is re-argued here.** What is here is the result, the cut by
paragraph, the cut by discretion, and the four things the result is not.

**The register is the authority and this chapter is a reading of it.** Every figure below is a count of
`logs/control-position-register.md §2` and none is entered independently.

## 2. The three determinations, counted

| Determination | Positions |
|---|---|
| **Met and required** | **302** |
| **Met but not required** | **218** |
| **Not met** | **151** |
| **Total** | **302 + 218 + 151 = 671** |

**Five hundred and twenty positions are met. 302 + 218 = 520.**

**And two hundred and eighteen of the five hundred and twenty are met by a configuration that nothing in
Helix's quality system requires.** That is the phase's first finding, it is carried by **`IS-29`**, and
`05.09-the-controls-nothing-requires.md` owns what follows from it. This chapter states the number and
stops.

**The number to resist is 520.** A report saying that five hundred and twenty of six hundred and
seventy-one positions are met is arithmetically true, is the sentence a steering committee will want, and
**describes a position Helix does not have** — because two hundred and eighteen of those met positions rest
on a configuration that can be changed tomorrow without breaching anything. **`ADR-0031` exists to stop the
two figures being added**, and `PR-35` carries the standing risk that they are added anyway.

**Not met by paragraph, as one line.** **`(a)` 24 · `(b)` 19 · `(c)` 17 · `(d)` 12 · `(e)` 29 · `(f)` 8 ·
`(g)` 11 · `(h)` 5 · `(i)` 9 · `(j)` 6 · `(k)` 11 = 151.**

**Four rows are worth reading rather than scanning.**

**`(e)` is the worst row in the register on both axes.** Twenty-nine Not met is the largest failure count of
any paragraph, and six Met and required is the smallest requirement count of any. **On twenty-six systems an
audit trail is running that no procedure requires anybody to keep running**, and on twenty-nine there is
either no audit trail, or one whose record changes obscure previously recorded information, or one that is
not retained for a period at least as long as that required for the subject records.
`05.05-audit-trails.md` owns what `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires; this chapter owns where it stands.

**`(a)` is the only paragraph with no Met but not required position at all.** Where validation is in place,
the computerised system validation procedure requires it to be in place. That is the cleanest row in the
register and it is clean for a structural reason: **validation is the one control at Helix that has always
had a procedure behind it.** The twenty-four failures are twenty-four systems that never entered that
procedure's reach, and seventeen of them are the seventeen holding no validation package at all —
`IS-23`, and Phase 04 counted them.

**`(h)` is the row that looks best and means least.** Fifty-six positions are met and forty-six of them are
met by something nothing requires. Device checks — determining, as appropriate, the validity of the source
of data input or operational instruction — are largely a property of how interfaces were built, and an
interface built carefully by somebody who has since moved on is a control with no owner and no sentence
behind it.

**`(j)` is the row that shows what a written policy is worth.** Forty-eight positions are Met and required,
the highest count in the register, because a written accountability policy is by its nature a procedure and
a procedure requires itself. **The six failures are not failures of technology at all.**

**And `(j)`'s column has to be read for what it says.** **`21 CFR 11.10(j)` is conditioned on electronic
signatures by its own words** — it requires written policies holding individuals accountable and responsible
for actions initiated under their electronic signatures. On the 42 systems that execute none, the
determination records **the existence and adherence of the written accountability policy in the estate that
would govern a signature on that system**, not a signature control operating on it. **The cell is a real
determination about a real policy; it is not a claim that a signature control is in place where no signature
is executed**, and a reader must not take it as one. **All six `(j)` failures fall on systems that do
execute signatures** — `CG-29` and `CG-30` in `logs/control-gap-register.md`.

## 4. The cut by system

**Every system carries eleven determinations and none carries fewer.** A system's position is its row, and
this phase publishes no single band, score, tier or index per system and never will —
`logs/control-position-register.md §6`.

| Cut | Value |
|---|---|
| Systems | **61** |
| Systems with no Not met position | **5** — `SYS-001`, `SYS-008`, `SYS-043`, `SYS-047`, `SYS-054` |
| Systems carrying seven Not met positions, the most in the register | **4** — `SYS-011`, `SYS-021`, `SYS-022`, `SYS-055` |
| **Systems carrying at least one Met but not required position** | **60 of the 61** |
| Systems on which all eleven positions are Met and required | **1** — `SYS-001` |

**The last two rows are the same fact stated twice, and it is the fact of the phase.** On sixty of the
sixty-one systems in this estate, at least one paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is met by something no procedure
requires. **One system in sixty-one has an eleven-paragraph position that would survive somebody
reconfiguring it without asking.**

**The Not met cells by system owner.** Joachim Reuter **72** across 27 systems · Dr Sunita Raghunathan
**42** across 18 · Bernard Kwiatkowski **19** across 7 · Amara Sissoko **13** across 5 · Terrence
Abbatiello **5** across 4. **72 + 42 + 19 + 13 + 5 = 151.**

**That cut is a fact about where systems sit and is not a statement about any individual.** It is published
because a cut withheld is a cut somebody reconstructs badly, and `PR-41` carries the standing risk that it
is read inside a function as a criticism of the function. **No document in this repository assesses any
individual's work.**

## 5. Inside and outside enforcement discretion

**Enforcement discretion reaches `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)`, and `(k)` in part — the
guidance names `(k)(2)`, the second of `(k)`'s two subparagraphs, and says nothing about `(k)(1)`**,
together with the corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30`. Those four paragraphs across sixty-one
systems are **4 × 61 = 244 positions**.

| Cut | Positions |
|---|---|
| **Not met inside the four** — `(a)` 24 · `(b)` 19 · `(c)` 17 · `(e)` 29 | **89** |
| **Not met outside them** — `(d)` 12 · `(f)` 8 · `(g)` 11 · `(h)` 5 · `(i)` 9 · `(j)` 6 · `(k)` 11 | **62** |
| **Check** | **89 + 62 = 151** |

**The paragraphs Helix is weakest on are the paragraphs FDA said it would exercise enforcement discretion
over.** That sentence is true, it is uncomfortable, and it is published rather than buried.

**It is not a mitigation and this chapter draws nothing from it.** `05.11-discretion-changes-nothing.md`
owns the argument and it is not restated here; what this chapter owns is the count. What must be said in
the same breath as the number is what the number is not: **enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention
about what it will enforce. It is not an exemption, it is not a safe harbour, and it confers no rights.
`21 CFR 211.68` and `21 CFR 211.100(b)` survive it entirely.** **A programme that reads enforcement
discretion as permission has read the wrong document.**

**And the sixty-two are the answer to anybody who reaches for the eighty-nine.** Sixty-two Not met
positions sit in paragraphs discretion does not reach at all, across access, operational checks, authority
checks, device checks, training, accountability policies and systems documentation. **`IS-33`** carries the
whole of it.

## 6. The 151 grouped

**The hundred and fifty-one Not met positions group into thirty-four control gaps, `CG-01` to `CG-34`**,
each a paragraph and a set of systems sharing one cause — `logs/control-gap-register.md`. The grouping adds
no determination and removes none; it is a way of reading the failures so that what would close them can be
seen.

**Fifteen of the thirty-four sit inside the four paragraphs discretion reaches and nineteen sit outside.
15 + 19 = 34.**

**Five causes account for ninety of the hundred and fifty-one positions between them, across sixteen of the
thirty-four gaps**, and every one of them is a fact about how the estate was assembled rather than about how
it is operated: systems that never entered a lifecycle; platforms scoped as
infrastructure; configurability that is not itself controlled; shared credentials; and products that can
show a record and cannot produce it. The gap register sets them out; this chapter does not duplicate them.

**Not one of the thirty-four is closed at this vantage.**

## 7. What this position is not

- **It is not a validation outcome.** **No validation package has been executed, no protocol has been run
  and no test result exists.** Assessing that a control is present is not validating it, and neither
  substitutes for the other — `ADR-0034`. **The evidence Phase 04's framework requires is still owed in
  full**, for all eleven paragraphs on all sixty-one systems — **`IS-34`**.
- **It is not a compliance statement about any system.** A row of eleven determinations is not a verdict,
  and **no system in this estate is described anywhere in this phase as compliant or as non-compliant.**
  There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated software.
- **It is not a set of deviations.** **A control gap is not a deviation** — on most of these rows no
  procedure was breached because no procedure required the control.
  `05.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md` says so as a numbered chapter.
- **It is not a re-scoring of anything.** Phase 03's **121 pairs, 74 `DR` and 47 `ND`, High 9, Moderate 31
  and Low 34** are unchanged. Phase 04's **complexity and reliability High 11, Medium 27 and Low 23**, its
  **38 obligations**, its **21 · 12 · 5** landing and its **44 packages and 17 with none** are unchanged.
  **Not one rating, band or determination has moved and none can move here.**
- **It is not an ordering.** No system and no gap goes anywhere before another because of a count.
  **The register states positions; it does not queue them.**
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.** **An open gap is Helix's own
  assessment of its own position. It is not a violation, not a breach, not a non-compliance, not a finding
  and not an inspectional observation.**

## 8. What a reader may legitimately take from it

Stated positively, once, so that the chapter is not only a list of refusals.

- **That a position now exists for every paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` on every one of the sixty-one
  systems**, taken on a method fixed before the first system was looked at — `GOV-21`, `EV-503`.
- **That the position distinguishes a control that is required from a control that merely exists**, which
  is a distinction no previous phase in this programme could make and which no single number can carry —
  `ADR-0031`.
- **That the hundred and fifty-one failures are published with a cause and a named individual owner
  apiece**, rather than as a count — `logs/control-gap-register.md`, `ADR-0008`.
- **That the eighty-nine inside enforcement discretion are published as eighty-nine**, in the same words
  and on the same method as the sixty-two outside it — `ADR-0035`.
- **That two findings under Subpart C sit alongside the register and outside it** — the twelve at
  `05.06-signatures-and-the-human-readable-form.md` and the seven at `05.07-the-continuing-component.md` —
  **because `21 CFR 11.50` and `21 CFR 11.200` are different sections with a different population and were
  not folded into a grid built for `21 CFR 11.10`.**

**None of those is a statement about whether Helix's records can be relied on.** They are statements about
what Helix now knows about its own controls, which is a smaller and more useful thing.
