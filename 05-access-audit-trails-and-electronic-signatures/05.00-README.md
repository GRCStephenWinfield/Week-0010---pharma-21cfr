## 1. What this phase is

Phase 04 published a validation framework that requires evidence for all eleven paragraphs of
`21 CFR 11.10`, `(a)` to `(k)`, for every system in scope, and then refused to say whether any of them was
met. **`ADR-0029` made the refusal explicit and handed the question here.**

**This is the first phase in the programme that states a control position. `IS-28` closes here.**

It is also the first phase whose subject matter is what the systems actually do rather than what Helix has
written down about them. Phases 01 to 04 produced determinations, assessments, registers and an instrument.
This phase points the instrument at the estate and records, for each paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` on each
system, whether the control is there.

**And it is the phase most likely to be over-read**, in two directions at once. A control position is not a
validation outcome, and the evidence Phase 04 requires is still owed in full —
`05.01-what-a-control-position-is.md`, `ADR-0034`. A control gap is not a deviation, and nothing in this
phase raises one — `05.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md`.

## 2. Read this first

Five things, and the rest of the phase follows from them.

**The unit is a paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` on a system.** Eleven paragraphs across sixty-one systems is
**671 control positions**. Never the system alone, because a system meets some paragraphs and not others;
never the paragraph alone, because a paragraph is met on some systems and not others. **A determination
made at either of the coarser units is a determination that averages a population it has not looked at.**
`05.02-the-grid.md` owns the argument and `ADR-0030` records the decision.

**There are three determinations and the middle one is the phase's method contribution.** *Met and
required* · **Met but not required** · *Not met*. A control that is present because somebody configured it
that way, and that no procedure requires anybody to keep, **can be turned off tomorrow without a
deviation, because no procedure would be breached.** That is Phase 04's procedure-gap argument seen at
control level — `04.08-where-the-obligations-land.md §5` owns it — and it is the reason a two-valued scale
was refused. `05.03-the-three-determinations.md`, `ADR-0031`.

**A control assessment is not validation, and neither substitutes for the other.** Validation asks whether
a system does what it is specified to do and keeps doing it; a control position asks whether a control is
there now. **The framework Phase 04 approved requires evidence for eleven paragraphs on sixty-one systems
and none of it has been produced.** This phase does not discharge that requirement and does not reduce it.
`05.01-what-a-control-position-is.md`, `ADR-0034`, `IS-34`.

**`21 CFR 11.50` is tested against every human readable form of the record, not against the screen**, and
`21 CFR 11.200(a)(1)(i)`'s continuing component is the password and not the user identification code.
Nineteen of the sixty-one systems execute electronic signatures.
`05.06-signatures-and-the-human-readable-form.md` and `05.07-the-continuing-component.md` own the
regulation-to-result work and the results; `ADR-0032` and `ADR-0033` record the decisions.

**And enforcement discretion changes what FDA said it would enforce and changes nothing about the
position.** It reaches `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)`, and `(k)` in part — the guidance names
`(k)(2)`, the second of `(k)`'s two subparagraphs, and says nothing about `(k)(1)` — plus the
corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30`, and, for systems operational before 20 August 1997 that meet
the guidance's conditions, all Part 11 requirements. **No Helix system has been assessed against those
conditions.** **It is FDA's stated intention, not an exemption and not a safe harbour; `21 CFR 211.68` and
`21 CFR 211.100(b)` survive it entirely; and the register states a position identically inside and outside
it.** `05.11-discretion-changes-nothing.md`, `ADR-0035`.

## 4. What Phase 05 deliberately does not establish

None of the following exists at the vantage, and nothing in this phase states, implies or forecasts any of
it.

- **No validation outcome for any system.** **No validation package has been executed, no protocol has been
  run and no test result exists.** **A control assessment is not validation**, and the evidence Phase 04
  requires is **owed in full** — `ADR-0034`, `IS-34`.
- **No CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no remediation cost.** **A control gap is not a
  deviation** — `05.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md`, and the vocabularies are Phase 01's, at
  `01.12 §6`.
- **No chromatography-specific determination** — Phase 06, `MS-10`. The `OBS-2` link is recorded at
  `05.05-audit-trails.md` and **no conclusion is drawn from it**.
- **No disposition of `OBS-4`.** The link is recorded at `05.04-access.md`. **What an inspectional
  observation's closure position is, is Phase 08's**, and this phase does not reach it.
- **No retention, backup or archive rule.** `IS-05` and `IS-14` remain open and `21 CFR 211.180` governs
  the question — Phase 07, `MS-11`.
- **No periodic review, no internal audit result, no third-party audit result and no inspection outcome of
  any kind.** Phases 08 and 09. **Vashti Okonjo & Partners has audited nothing.**
- **No audit trail review frequency stated as a requirement.** The frequency is inherited from the review
  frequency CGMP specifies for the underlying data; **where CGMP specifies none, the firm determines it,
  and only there does risk enter.** **No primary source names a stand-alone periodic audit trail review
  frequency.** FDA names the frequencies to be **inherited** — review after each significant step under
  `21 CFR 211.188(b)`, and review before batch release — and leaves the firm to determine one only where
  CGMP specifies none for the underlying data. **This phase names none**, and `IS-04` stays deliberately
  unresolved.
- **No re-scoring of Phase 03 and no re-banding of Phase 04.** Those registers are inputs. No pair's
  determination moves and no rigour band changes.
- **No answer to `IS-01`**, and **no promotion of its fourth candidate explanation** by statement, by
  ordering or by tone — `ADR-0012`, `02.11`. **`IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted.**
- **No legal conclusion of any kind**, and no forecast of what any future inspection will find.

**A control position is not validation evidence, and a control gap is not a deviation.**

## 10. Standing framing

These positions hold across all nine phases and are restated here so that a reader arriving at Phase 05 has
them.

- **There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated
  software.** A product has **features that support** compliance; compliance is a property of Helix's
  validated system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated by its trained people. **Outsourcing
  to a service provider transfers no responsibility.**
- **Part 11 applies to records, not to systems**, and this repository never says "a Part 11 system" without
  saying which records make it one — `ADR-0001`, `01.03 §2`.
- **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention. It is not a legal exemption and not a safe harbour**,
  and `21 CFR 211.68` stands whatever FDA does about Part 11: `21 CFR 211.68(a)` requires equipment
  including computers, if used, to be **routinely calibrated, inspected or checked according to a written
  program designed to assure proper performance**, with written records of those checks maintained; `(b)`
  requires appropriate controls over computer or related systems **to assure that changes in master
  production and control records or other records are instituted only by authorized personnel**, requires
  input/output verification, and requires a backup file — `ADR-0003`, `ADR-0035`.
- **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by
  the Quality Management System Regulation, and does not apply to Helix.** Adopted voluntarily as good
  practice, **labelled as adopted every time** — `ADR-0005`, `ADR-0026`.
- **GAMP 5 Second Edition, ISPE, July 2022, is good-practice guidance and never a requirement**, and **no
  software-category count and no enumeration of the categories is published in this repository.**
- **FDA uses plain ALCOA, and ALCOA does not map onto `21 CFR 11.10`** — FDA's own footnote 5 maps the
  attributes to the CGMP predicate rules and cites Part 11 nowhere, `ADR-0019`. **The four additional
  attributes are MHRA's**, MHRA itself refers to the shorter acronym and defines all four, and **no
  originator is named for either set.**
- **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, no read-event logging, no cryptographic hashing under
  `21 CFR 11.70` and no password interval under `21 CFR 11.300(b)`. It requires no multi-factor
  authentication** — `21 CFR 11.200(a)(1)` requires two distinct identification components, which is not
  the same thing.
- **A closed system is defined by who controls access** — `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`. Hosting is not the test, and
  **all sixty-one are closed** — `ADR-0011`.
- **`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and Part 11 contains ten sections.** There is no
  `21 CFR 11.60`, no `21 CFR 11.400` and no Subpart D. Citations in this repository are exact and can be
  looked up.
- **A Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding.** No document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of
  any kind, and **never forecast what a future inspection will find.** **An open gap is Helix's own
  assessment of its own position** — not a violation, not a breach, not a non-compliance, not a finding and
  not an inspectional observation.
- **Applicability is not integrity, a rating is not a validation status, a requirement is not a control,
  and a control position is not validation evidence.**

## 11. How to read this phase

Read **`05.03`** if you read only one chapter. Three values instead of two is a small-looking method choice,
and it is the choice that makes the largest group in the register visible at all.

Read **`05.02`** for the unit, and **`05.01`** for the boundary between this phase and Phase 04 — the one
place where a reader who is pleased with a result is most likely to draw the wrong conclusion from it.

Read **`05.04`**, **`05.05`**, **`05.06`** and **`05.07`** for the regulation, paragraph by paragraph, and
**`05.08`** to **`05.10`** for what the estate looked like when each was applied to it. Read **`05.11`**
with particular attention: it answers the objection a hostile reader will raise first, and the answer is
not that the objection is wrong about the facts.

Read for the arguments rather than the citations. Where a chapter cites `21 CFR 11.10(e)` or
`21 CFR 11.300(b)`, it is locating a requirement in the text — **not** asserting that Helix meets it.
