## 1. What this chapter settles

Every cell of the grid takes one of three values, and **the middle one is this phase's method
contribution**. It is also the only part of the method a reader is likely to think unnecessary before
seeing what it produced.

The obvious instrument has two values. A control is present or it is not. **That instrument would have
been complete, defensible, quick to apply, and would have concealed the largest single group in the
register.**

This chapter says what the three values are, what *required* means, why the middle value exists, and what it
is not. **`ADR-0031` records the decision.**

**It states no result.** How the six hundred and seventy-one determinations fall across the three is
`05.08-the-position.md`'s, and what the middle group means for the estate is
`05.09-the-controls-nothing-requires.md`'s. This chapter cites both and reports neither.

## 2. The three values

| Value | What it records | What it takes to enter it |
|---|---|---|
| **Met and required** | **The control described in the paragraph is present on the system, and a sentence in a procedure requires it to be** | The configuration or arrangement, read and recorded with its basis; **and the procedure and the sentence in it, both named** |
| **Met but not required** | **The control is present, and nothing in the quality system requires it to remain so** | The configuration or arrangement, read and recorded; **and a search of the procedure estate that found no sentence requiring it** |
| **Not met** | **The control described in the paragraph is not present on the system at this vantage** | What was looked at, what was absent, and the `CG` identifier the position groups into |

**The three are exhaustive and mutually exclusive.** Every cell takes exactly one and no cell is left blank
— `ADR-0030`, `05.02 §7`.

**There is no fourth value, and three candidates for one were refused.** No *partly met*, because a control
that is present for some records on a system and not others is a fact the basis field records and not a
fourth determination. No *in progress*, because a control that is being built is not present. And **no *not
determinable***, which needs saying because Phase 03 has one and this phase does not. Phase 03's
determination that an attribute could not be determined recorded **the absence of evidence about a record**
— `ADR-0018`, and it was refused a score for exactly that reason. A control, by contrast, is either there
to be seen or it is not: **where the assessment could not establish a control's presence, the cell reads
*Not met*, because the position Helix can defend is the one it can evidence.** The basis field records what
was looked at, and the assumption that the account, configuration and manifestation reads would have found
a control that was present is stated in the assumptions register rather than left implicit.

## 3. What "required" means, and it is a narrow question

**The second and third columns of the table above turn on a question about documents, not about systems.**
The control is present in both the first and second values; what differs is whether anything obliges Helix
to keep it that way.

**A procedure was recorded as requiring the control only where a sentence in it obliges somebody to
maintain the control the paragraph describes.** That is Phase 04's reading rule, applied to a different
object, and `04.08 §2` owns it. Three things were not treated as a requirement, and each was refused for
the reason Phase 04 gave.

**Subject-matter coverage is not requirement.** A procedure that governs the electronic batch record system
does not thereby require that system's audit trail to retain the prior value of a changed field. It is the
procedure the requirement would live in; it is not a procedure that imposes it.

**A general obligation is not a specific one.** A procedure requiring systems to be *appropriately
controlled* was not read as requiring any of the eleven paragraphs. **A sentence broad enough to cover
anything requires nothing in particular, and a requirement nobody can fail is not a requirement.**

**And silence is not permission.** Where a procedure neither requires the control nor addresses it, the
reading recorded *does not require it* and stopped. **A procedure that is silent on a question is not
neutral about it** — the position Phase 01 fixed at `01.07 §2`.

**One consequence has to be stated plainly because it is counter-intuitive.** *Met but not required* is not
a judgement about the person who configured the control, and it is frequently a record of somebody having
done more than they were asked to. **The determination is about the quality system, not about the
engineer.** No document in this repository assesses any individual's work.

## 4. Why the middle value exists

**A control that is present but that no procedure requires is a control that can be turned off tomorrow
without a deviation, because no procedure would be breached.**

That is the whole of the argument and the rest of this section is its consequences.

**Consider what a two-valued register would have said.** Both of the first two values would have been
recorded as *Met*. A reader — a steering committee, an auditor, a future programme — would have seen a
control present and reasonably concluded that the position was held. **It is not held. It is merely true.**

**Nothing keeps a merely-true control in place.** A configuration is changed by an administrator with the
rights to change it. A supplier's upgrade resets a setting to its default. A system is migrated and the new
platform is configured to match what the last one did, as far as anybody remembered what it did. **In each
case the control disappears and no procedure has been departed from**, so no deviation is raised, no
investigation is opened, no change control is triggered on the ground that a required control has been
removed, and **nothing in Helix's quality system produces a record that anything happened at all.**

**The failure is silent, and that is what distinguishes it from the third value.** A *Not met* position is
visible: it has a cause, a `CG` identifier and a closure condition. A *Met but not required* position looks
like success on the day it is recorded, and its failure mode produces no artefact anybody will see.

**And the asymmetry runs the other way too, which is the part that makes the value worth having.** The
route from *Met but not required* to *Met and required* requires no system change and no purchase. **It
requires a sentence.** The control already exists; what is missing is the document that obliges Helix to
keep it. That is the cheapest closure route in this phase, and it is invisible to any instrument that
records only whether a control is present.

## 5. The same shape Phase 04 found, one level down

**This is Phase 04's procedure-gap argument seen at control level, and `04.08 §5` owns it.** That chapter
found twelve obligations landing in a procedure that exists and does not require the act, and argued that
such a gap is worse than an absent procedure because **an auditor who asks "is there a procedure?" gets a
yes.** The argument is not restated here.

**What this phase adds is the object.** Phase 04 was reading obligations against procedures — a
document-to-document question, answered by reading. **Phase 05 is reading a configuration against
procedures, and the configuration is already doing the thing the procedure does not require.** The gap is
therefore invisible to a second question as well as the first:

| The question asked | Against a Phase 04 procedure gap | Against a *Met but not required* position |
|---|---|---|
| *Is there a procedure?* | Yes — and the enquiry stops | Yes — and the enquiry stops |
| *Is the control in place?* | Not asked; the obligation was the object | **Yes — and the enquiry stops again** |
| *Does anything require the control to stay in place?* | The question `ADR-0028` forced | **The question this determination forces** |

**Two yeses are harder to get past than one.** A position that answers affirmatively to both of the
questions an ordinary enquiry asks is a position nobody has a reason to look at twice, and it is the
position this value exists to make visible.

**Helix has watched the mechanism operate before.** `IS-03` records that the register of computerised
systems carried no attribute identifying which systems held records required by a predicate rule, and that
the same gap was raised inside the audit history at `IA-2025-01/F3` and closed there against the procedure
that existed — `04.08 §5` sets out what that audit could and could not record. **Describing a mechanism is
not assigning a cause**, nothing in this chapter bears on `IS-01`, and `IS-01` stays open and stays
unweighted.

## 6. What the middle value is not

**It is not a lesser form of *Met*.** The control is present. A system carrying *Met but not required* on
`21 CFR 11.10(d)` limits access to authorised individuals today exactly as thoroughly as a system carrying
*Met and required*.

**It is not a half-failure and it is not half of anything.** The three values are named and are never
converted to numbers. **They are not averaged, not weighted, not summed across a row into a figure for a
system or down a column into a figure for a paragraph, and never expressed as a proportion** — `05.02 §6`
owns that prohibition. **A value that can be halved is a value somebody will eventually add up.**

**It is not a deviation, a finding, a CAPA or an inspectional observation.** It is Helix's own assessment
of its own position at a vantage — `05.01 §3`, and `05.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md` carries the
inventory.

**It is not a criticism of the procedure estate.** A procedure that does not require a control was written
to require what it was written to require, at a time when nobody had determined which systems held Part 11
records — the position `02.10` established and `IS-10` carried. **No document in this repository assesses
any individual's work.**

**And it is not an instruction to write a procedure.** Whether Helix writes the sentence, and where, is a
decision for the quality system through change control. **This phase records the position and proposes no
remediation plan**, because it states none: no CAPA, no deviation, no remediation and no cost.

## 7. `ADR-0031` — "Met but not required" is a determination in its own right

**`ADR-0031`, taken at `DEC-508` on 2027-01-06 by Terrence Abbatiello, records that a control that is
present and that no procedure requires is entered as a determination in its own right, and is never merged
into *Met*.**

Three rules follow.

**The three values are the whole of the value set**, and no report may compress them into two. **A summary
that says how many positions are *Met* has re-merged the two the decision separated**, and a phase whose
method depends on a distinction cannot allow its own reporting layer to remove it.

**Every *Met and required* position names the requiring sentence.** Not the procedure alone — the sentence.
**A citation to a document is what makes the first value indistinguishable from the second when nobody
checks**, and the check is the point.

**And the two values carry different closure routes and neither is a remediation plan.** A *Not met*
position groups into a control gap with a cause. A *Met but not required* position has no gap identifier,
because nothing is missing from the system; what is missing is a sentence, and `05.09-the-controls-nothing-requires.md`
owns what that means.

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no result.** How the six hundred and seventy-one fall across the three determinations is
  `05.08-the-position.md`'s, and the consequences of the middle group are
  `05.09-the-controls-nothing-requires.md`'s.
- **It states no validation outcome**, and **a control assessment is not validation** — `05.01 §4`,
  `ADR-0034`.
- **It raises no CAPA and no deviation, and proposes no remediation and no remediation cost.** **A control
  gap is not a deviation.**
- **It writes no procedure and amends none.** Where a sentence would move a position from the second value
  to the first, that is a change to the quality system through change control and not an act of this phase.
- **It sets no audit trail review frequency and no retention period** — `05.05-audit-trails.md` and Phase
  07.
- **It re-scores nothing and re-bands nothing**, and no Phase 03 or Phase 04 determination is edited by it.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, assesses no
  individual's work, and forecasts no inspection outcome.
- **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted.
