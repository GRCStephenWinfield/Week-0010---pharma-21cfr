# 09.08 — The Four That Go Nowhere

| Owner | Lydia Marchetti-Nwosu — Programme Manager |

*Four of the forty-nine open entries were raised by the programme, could not be resolved by it, and cannot be handed to anybody. All content is fictional and illustrative.*

---

## 1. The distinction this chapter turns on

**There are two ways an entry can lack a destination and they are not the same, so the register records
which applies to each of the four.**

**The first is that nobody has been assigned.** A post exists, a remit reaches the subject matter, and the
assignment has not been made. **That is an omission, and it is repairable by somebody making it.** **The
twelve entries whose destination is a function are of this kind**: `DP-48` asks for names, the names could
be given, and nobody has given them.

**The second is that nobody at Helix has the remit, the authority or the funding to take it.** **That is
not an omission and no assignment repairs it.** **An entry handed to somebody who cannot act on it is an
entry with a name written beside it and nothing else** — and writing the name would make the register read
better and describe the world worse.

**All four below are of the second kind, and each is of it for a different reason.** **`ADR-0060` requires
the register to publish that rather than fill the cell**, and `handover-register.md` does.

**This chapter proposes nothing.** **No CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan, no cost, no
recommendation, no successor arrangement and no request.** It states four positions.

---

## 1.1 Three sit under a provision, and one sits under none

**`IS-12` sits under `21 CFR 211.180(c)`**, which has three sentences: all records required under Part 211,
or copies of them, shall be readily available for authorised inspection during the retention period at the
establishment where the activities described in the records occurred; those records or copies shall be
subject to photocopying or other means of reproduction as part of the inspection; and *"[r]ecords that can
be immediately retrieved from another location by computer or other electronic means shall be considered as
meeting the requirements of this paragraph."* **The third sentence is the only saving available for records
not held at the establishment, it is conditional on immediate retrievability, and `IS-12` records that
nobody has established where the records are — which is a prior question to whether anything about them
could be retrieved at all.** **`IS-60` and `IS-62` sit under
no provision at all**: they are facts about a programme, and no regulation reaches a programme. **`IS-66`
sits under all of them and none of them**, which is what makes it the last entry rather than one of the
forty-eight.

**Naming the provision does not give an entry a destination.** Three of these four could be quoted at a
regulation all day and would still have nobody to hold them. **What gives an entry a destination is a
named individual with the authority and the funding to act on it, and that is the thing these four do not
have** — `ADR-0060`.

---

## 2. `HO-04` — `IS-12`. Where the records of the nine removed entries now are

**Nine entries were removed from the register of computerised systems in Phase 02, with evidence, and the
removal was recorded.** **They carry no `SYS` identifier**, because `SYS-001` to `SYS-222` number the
systems that exist. **They are on no row of the systems register, no row of the record-type register, no
row of the control position register, no row of `periodic-review-register.md` and no row of
`decay-register.md`.** **That is correct in every one of those registers, and it is also the reason nothing
will ever look for them.**

**The entry asks where the records those arrangements held now are. Nobody has established it.**

**There is no destination because there is no system.** **Every named system owner at Helix owns systems**
— the five owners across the GxP-relevant estate own the systems the register lists — **and nobody owns a
system Helix no longer runs.** No line function's remit reaches records held by an arrangement that has
been removed: **the IT organisation runs the estate that exists; the quality unit's review obligations
attach to records it can identify; and the record-level determination applies to record types on systems in
the inventory.** **The removal was correct and it moved the subject matter outside every remit at the same
time.**

**Raised 2026-06-12. Open at every vantage since. Nobody has been assigned, and there is nobody whose remit
reaches it.**

---

## 3. `HO-43` — `IS-60`. The pattern in the charter score

**Three of the four objectives about the programme's own artefacts are met. Not one of the four requiring
the estate to change is met.** `09.03-what-the-score-is-shaped-like.md` states what that does and does not
mean and this chapter does not restate it.

**The entry's destination would have to be somebody with the authority to change the estate and the funding
to do it.** **`DP-51` names the ask: funding, and an authority to commit it, for work outside the
programme's own artefacts.**

**No such person exists at Helix at this vantage.**

**The programme had a sponsor and the sponsor had an envelope, and both end today.** **The charter may be
varied only by a further minuted decision of the sponsor** — and **a charter that has closed cannot be
varied into a successor.** **No successor programme is chartered. No envelope exists after 2027-04-30. No
line manager holds a budget for estate change on this scale**, and the five estate-side asks the entry
reduces to — seventeen validation packages, nine shared accounts, two procedure revisions, sixteen retention
relations with no second term, two auditable event classes the product cannot record — **are each somebody's
subject matter and none of them is anybody's funded authority.**

**Above the sponsor there is the Board's audit committee, and this repository does not name its
non-executive membership.** **That is deliberate and it is `GOV-05 §2`'s rule**: *"Nothing in this minute
concerns any individual's conduct or performance, and the record is written so that it could not be read as
though it did."* **A register that cannot name an individual cannot record one as a destination.**

**Raised 2027-04-21. This is not an entry nobody has been assigned. It is an entry nobody at Helix has the
authority or the funding to take.**

---

## 4. `HO-45` — `IS-62`. That four of the forty-nine have no destination

**The entry states the register's own condition: thirty-three named, twelve to a function, four nowhere.**

**There is no destination for a fact about the absence of destinations.** **Handing it to a named individual
would assert that somebody now holds the four** — which is precisely what is untrue, and **the entry would
then be false on its own subject matter.** **Handing it to a function would be the defect `ADR-0060`
exists to prevent, applied to the entry that records the defect.**

**It is one of the four it counts.** **That is arithmetic rather than a device**, and it follows from what
the entry is: **a register that records its own gaps produces one more gap, and the register does not
subtract it to make the total look better.**

**Raised 2027-04-27. It closes if the condition changes for each of the four separately, and not
otherwise.**

---

## 5. `HO-49` — `IS-66`. The question the programme leaves

**The entry states what Helix now knows, what knowing it has changed, what the programme was funded to do,
and what nobody has been funded to do.**

**It is addressed to nobody by construction.** **`09.13-close-out.md` carries its words and they are not
repeated here.**

**Giving it a destination would be the one act of tidying the register refuses.** A name beside it would
assert that somebody at Helix holds the question of what happens next, **and the whole of the entry's
content is that nobody does.** **There is no post at Helix whose remit is the last of its four clauses**,
and inventing one on paper at close-out would be `PR-26`'s failure mode in its purest form: **a thing
treated as closing because somebody wrote a document.**

**Raised 2027-04-30. It does not close, and the register does not invent a closure condition in order to
have written one down.**

---

## 6. What this chapter is careful not to be

**It is not a criticism of anybody.** **No individual is named as having failed to take an entry**, and no
individual is assessed. **The two entries that turn on authority turn on the absence of a post, not on the
conduct of anybody in one.**

**It is not an argument that the four should have been handed to somebody anyway.** **A destination
recorded where none exists is worse than a blank**, because it stops anybody looking — the same reason
`commitment-register.md` prints *No completion criterion* rather than leaving a cell empty, and the same
reason the decay register prints **None** against the evidence index.

**It is not a request, a proposal or a plan.** **`DP-49` and `DP-51` record two asks and neither states an
amount, a scope of works, a date or a recipient.** **A dependency on nobody is a task**, and these two are
dependencies with no source at all, **which is a worse thing than a task and is recorded as such.**

**And it reaches no conclusion of any kind about Helix's compliance, about what any inspection would find,
or about whether Helix is ready for one.** **Four entries with no destination is a fact about a register at
a vantage.**
