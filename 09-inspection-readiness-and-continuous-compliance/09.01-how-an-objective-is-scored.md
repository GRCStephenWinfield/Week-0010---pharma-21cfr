*The measure names an artefact, and the artefact is the only thing the programme is permitted to look at when it scores itself. All content is fictional and illustrative.*

---

## 1. One idea

**An objective is scored from the artefact its measure names, and from nothing else.**

That is `ADR-0058`, taken by the Programme Manager on 2027-04-14 at `DEC-903` and authorised at `GOV-41`,
**before any objective was scored.** The sequence is recorded because it is the only thing that makes the
method checkable rather than believable: **a method settled after the results are known is a method chosen
for the results it produces.**

**This chapter argues the method and states no score.** The scores are at
`09.02-the-charter-scored.md`, one objective at a time, and at `logs/objective-score-register.md`, which is
authoritative. **The shape they make is `09.03-what-the-score-is-shaped-like.md`'s and is not touched here.**
Nothing in this chapter says how any objective came out.

It also carries the decision that governs the register at the same moment — **`ADR-0063`: the programme
closes with its open entries open, and nothing is closed at close-out for the sake of closing it.** `§6`
argues it. **The two decisions are the same refusal applied to two different objects**, which is why they sit
in one chapter.

## 1.1 And no predicate rule requires any of this

**Nothing in `21 CFR Part 11`, and nothing in Parts 210 and 211, requires a firm to run a programme, set
objectives for it, or score them.** A charter is Helix's own instrument and the measures in it are Helix's
own words, which is why `ADR-0058` reads them the way `ADR-0052` read the response commitments: **against
themselves, and against nothing borrowed.**

What the predicate rules reach is what happens once Helix adopts something. **`21 CFR 211.100(b)` requires
that written production and process control procedures be followed in the execution of the various
production and process control functions and be documented at the time of performance, and that any
deviation from the written procedures be recorded and justified. `21 CFR 211.22(d)` requires that the responsibilities and procedures applicable to the quality
control unit be in writing and that *"such written procedures shall be followed."*** **An objective is not
a procedure. A procedure written to satisfy one is.**

## 2. What a measure names

**Each of the charter's objectives carries a measure, and each measure names a thing.** Not a state of mind,
not an impression and not a general condition of the organisation — **a document, a register, a record set
or a report, in a state the measure describes.** The charter fixed that on 2026-03-06 and it has never been
varied: `01.06 §3` records that **a measure is readable from evidence when a person holding the artefact can
score it without asking anybody how they feel about it.**

**The charter went further and stated it as a negative, which is the sentence this method exists to
honour:**

> *"No objective in this set is measured by an opinion. There is no objective whose measure is a regulator's
> view, an auditor's view, a consultant's assessment of maturity, or a score on a scale somebody sells."*

— and it closed by requiring that each objective be *"…scored from an artefact that either exists or does
not."*

**A negative claim of that kind is easy to write at kickoff and hard to keep at close-out.** At kickoff there
is nothing to score, so nothing is at stake in refusing an opinion. **At close-out the opinions are
available, they are informed, and several of them are more flattering than the artefacts.** The charter's
authors knew that, which is why the measures name artefacts rather than describing outcomes: **an objective
whose measure names a thing cannot be scored by describing a situation.**

**The measure is therefore transcribed and not summarised.** Every measure scored in this phase was copied
word for word from the published charter and checked against it by somebody other than the scorer, at
`EV-906`. **A measure restated in the scorer's own words is a measure the scorer has quietly adjusted**, and
the adjustment is invisible because it looks like clarity. **`01.06 §9` answers who may vary a measure during
the programme with one word and a reason**, and the same reasoning forbids the variation arriving in the
form of a paraphrase on the last day.

**And the artefact is identified before it is read.** The scoring form records which artefact the measure
names, where it is held and what state the measure requires it to be in, and it records those things first.
**An artefact identified after the reading is an artefact chosen to fit the reading** — the same defect as a
method chosen after the results, in a smaller place where nobody would look for it.

## 3. Why effort, progress and intent are not evidence

**They are all reports about work rather than the work's product, and a measure that names an artefact is
asking for the product.**

That is the whole of the argument, and it is worth taking the three separately because they fail for
slightly different reasons.

**Effort is a fact about what people did, and the measure asks what exists.** Effort is real and is often
very large: an objective can absorb the greater part of a year, consume the time of several named
individuals, produce a method, change how a function talks about its records, and stop short of the artefact.
**Every one of those is true and none of them is the artefact.** A measure that could be satisfied by effort
would be a measure satisfied by working on something, **which is a condition almost nothing fails.**

**Progress is a comparison between two states of the world, and only one of them is observable.** To say that
an objective has progressed is to say that its position is better than it was — which requires a record of
where it was, taken at the time, on the same basis. **The programme has such a record for very little**, and
where it does not, *progress* is a comparison against a remembered baseline. **A comparison against a
remembered baseline is an opinion with a direction attached**, and the charter's negative claim reaches it.

**Intent is a fact about the future, and the register is a record of the present.** An objective that will be
met, that is scheduled to be met, that would have been met but for something, or that everybody agrees ought
to be met, **is an objective that is not met.** The repository has refused the same move about the estate
since Phase 01 — **an arrangement is not a review, a configuration is not a demonstration, a grant decided
is not access performed** — and refusing it about the programme's own objectives is the same refusal, applied
where it costs the programme rather than somebody else.

**There is a fourth candidate and it is the one that would have been hardest to resist, because it is
genuinely expert: the view of somebody who knows.** An advisory firm that has worked alongside the programme
for more than a year, an auditor, or the objective's own owner can each say, with real authority, how far an
objective got. **The charter's negative claim removes all of them by name**, and it removes them for a
reason that has nothing to do with their competence: **an opinion cannot be checked by a later reader
holding the artefact.** A score taken from an artefact can be disagreed with by anybody who reads the same
artefact. **A score taken from an expert view can only be disagreed with by finding a different expert**,
and at that point the programme is scored by whoever is asked.

**None of this says the effort did not happen or that the intent is not sincere.** It says that the register
is not the place where either is recorded. **The score records the artefact; what would change the score is
recorded beside it, in its own column, once**; and the reason an objective stands where it stands is
`09.03-what-the-score-is-shaped-like.md`'s subject and is argued there at length. **The information is not
lost. It is kept out of the score.**

## 4. What "from nothing else" excludes, and what it does not

**The rule is narrower than it sounds in one direction and wider in another, and both are worth stating.**

**It does not forbid consulting anything.** A scorer reading a register may need a definition, a decision
reference, an earlier register the artefact derives from, or the method note that says how a column was
populated. **All of that may be read**, and the scoring form records both that it was read and that it did
not enter the score. **Understanding an artefact is not scoring from something else.**

**What it forbids is an input.** Nothing other than the artefact, in the state the measure describes,
determines whether the score is met or not met. **Where an artefact is silent, the score is taken on the
silence** — because a measure asking whether an artefact says something is not satisfied by somebody
knowing the answer. **A negative that nobody can test is not a determination; it is a silence**, and so is a
positive.

**And where the measure names a population, the score is taken across the whole population.** Not across the
part that was examined, not across the part that was reachable, and not across the part that supports the
better answer. **Scoring a subset and reporting the result as the score is a partial score with the partial
label removed**, which is the defect `§5` is about, arriving through a side door.

**One consequence is uncomfortable and is accepted.** The rule makes the score sensitive to how the charter
happened to word a measure. A measure that names an artefact Helix controls is easier to satisfy than one
that names a state of the estate, **and the difference is not a difference in how hard anybody worked.**
That is not a defect in the method; **it is the finding**, and it is the whole of
`09.03-what-the-score-is-shaped-like.md`.

## 5. There is no partly met, and what that costs

**The score has one of two values. There is nothing between them, and no qualifier attaches to either.**

**The cost is real and it is paid in information.** Two objectives recorded as not met can be in entirely
different conditions. One may want a signature on a document that is drafted, reviewed and with an approver.
The other may want a change to the estate that nobody has begun, nobody is funded to make, and nobody has the
authority to require. **A binary score records both with the same word and tells a reader nothing about the
distance between them.** Whoever inherits this register is entitled to know which of it is nearly done, and
the score does not tell them.

**The alternative was available, it was argued at length, and it was not a soft option.** A middle value can
be defined as strictly as a binary one — admitted only where the artefact exists, is under document control,
and is in the described state for an enumerated and countable part of the population the measure covers.
**On that definition the middle value is read off the artefact exactly as the outer values are, and the usual
objection that partial scores invite judgement does not obviously survive.** `ADR-0058` puts the case in
full, together with the fairness argument that a binary score attaches the same word to an owner who
delivered nearly everything as to one who delivered nothing.

**It was refused for one reason above the others, and the reason is about what happens after the register is
published.**

> **A binary score loses information and buys something worth more: nobody can argue a score up.**

**The mechanism is worth being explicit about.** With two values, the only question anybody can raise is
whether the artefact is in the state the measure describes — a question with an answer, about a thing that is
named, which a later reader can settle by opening the artefact. **With three, every score that would have
been the lower one becomes a conversation.** The conversation is not dishonest; it is a genuine disagreement
about where a boundary falls. **But it is conducted at close-out, by the people who own the objectives, in a
week when the whole organisation would like the phase to read better than it does**, and the enumerated
definition does not survive it — the argument simply moves one step back and becomes an argument about what
the population is.

**And the cost is recoverable while the property is not.** Every score in this phase is published beside the
artefact it was taken from and beside a statement of what would change it, and `09.02-the-charter-scored.md`
argues each one at length. **A reader who wants to know how close an objective came can read that.** A reader
who wants to know whether it was met reads one word — **and no amount of reading, arguing or retelling
changes the word.**

**Two smaller consequences follow and are accepted.** **No aggregate is produced from the scores** — no
total, no index, no proportion, no percentage, no trend and no scorecard — because an aggregate is a middle
value computed after the fact from values that refused to have one. **And nothing is subtracted from *not
met* for the work that went into it, or added to *met* for the ease of it.** An objective that was easy and
was met is met. **The programme does not get credit for difficulty, and it does not lose credit for the
absence of it.**

## 6. The programme closes with its open entries open

**`ADR-0063`, taken by the Vice President, Quality on 2027-04-29 at `DEC-915`, at the final Steering
Committee minuted at `GOV-45` — the sitting at which that committee dissolved.**

**It is the same refusal as `§5`'s, applied to the register instead of to the score**, which is why it is
argued here rather than in a chapter of its own.

**The register's own rules have always answered most of it.** An entry closes when the condition that made
it an entry no longer holds, with the evidence named, the closure dated and the decision referenced.
**Closure names the evidence. "Believed complete" is not a closure condition** — `01.12 §9`. **What
close-out adds is a case the rules did not anticipate**: closure not because the condition ceased, but
because the programme did.

**The sweep that was refused is a respectable instrument.** Review every open entry against what is now
known, and close those whose subject matter has been superseded by a later determination, absorbed into
another entry, or made academic by something that has happened since — each closure recorded with its
reason. **It produces a shorter register, and a shorter register is one a successor might actually work
through.** `ADR-0063` argues the case at length, including its strongest form, in which a closure is
permitted only where a named later artefact demonstrably supersedes the entry.

**That strongest form is what refuses it.** Where a named later artefact demonstrably supersedes an entry's
subject matter, **the register has always permitted the closure and did not need close-out to authorise it**
— such an entry is already closed. **What the sweep adds is the set of entries where the supersession is
arguable**, and it is proposed precisely because that set is not empty. **A rule whose only work is done on
arguable cases is a rule for deciding arguable cases in one direction.**

**And there is a second reason, which is about who is left.** Every previous closure in this repository was
taken at a vantage with further vantages after it: a monthly review at `CAL-01`, a quarterly report at
`CAL-04`, a later phase reading the register as an input. **A closure taken on the last day is reviewed by
nobody, ever.** It is the one class of closure the register's own machinery of correction cannot reach.

> **A close-out that tidies is a close-out that lies to whoever inherits it.**

**Three consequences, stated as consequences.** **No entry closes because it has been handed over** — a
handover moves a question to somebody who may answer it; it does not answer one, and
`09.07-the-handover.md` owns that. **No entry closes because nobody will pick it up** — an entry with no
destination is not an entry with no subject matter, and the absence of a holder is information the entry now
carries. **And no status is invented to stand in for closure**: there is no *transferred*, no *superseded*,
no *carried forward* and no *deprecated*, because each of those is a closure with a euphemism attached and
**the euphemism does not survive a summary.**

**What closes in this phase closes on the ordinary conditions and each is argued.**
`09.13-close-out.md` carries them and `logs/raid-log.md` is authoritative. **This chapter states which
conditions a closure must meet and no closure at all.**

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **No score.** Not for any objective, not in any form, and not by implication from anything argued here.
  The scores are `09.02-the-charter-scored.md`'s and `logs/objective-score-register.md`'s.
- **No pattern, shape, grouping or reading of the scores.** That is
  `09.03-what-the-score-is-shaped-like.md`'s and it is not previewed here.
- **No count of anything** — not of objectives, artefacts, budget lines, register entries or closures.
- **No compliance conclusion and no readiness position.** **Readiness is the ability to answer and is not a
  compliance position** — `ADR-0059`, argued at `09.05-readiness-is-the-ability-to-answer.md`. **Neither
  "Helix is ready" nor "Helix is compliant" is asserted anywhere in this phase.**
- **No legal conclusion**, and no characterisation of the Ridgemont inspection, or of any condition observed
  at it, as a violation of anything. **A Form FDA 483 is not a final agency determination.**
- **No closure of any inspectional observation and no representation to FDA** — `ADR-0051`, `ADR-0064`.
- **No re-determination of any Phase 05 control position, no re-scoring of any Phase 03 pair, no re-banding
  of any Phase 04 obligation and no re-derivation of any Phase 07 period** — `ADR-0049`. Every earlier
  register is an input to this phase and is never edited by it.
- **No retention period for any record type** — `ADR-0044`, `ADR-0050`.
- **No CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan, no remediation cost, no sequence, no priority and no date.**
- **No forecast of what any inspection, audit, periodic review or independent assessment would find.**
  **None of those has been performed.**
- **No candidate explanation of `IS-01`**, which closed at Phase 08 on what the programme can establish and
  expressly not on why. **Its refusal is permanent and was expressly not handed to this phase.**
- **No statement about any individual.** A score is a statement about an artefact. **No document in this
  repository assesses any individual's work.**
