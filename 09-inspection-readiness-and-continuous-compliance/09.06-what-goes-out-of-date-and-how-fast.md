*Twelve artefacts, twelve decay mechanisms, and eleven holders. All content is fictional and illustrative.*

---

## 1. Why an artefact carries a decay mechanism and a holder

**Every artefact this programme built describes a state of the world at a date.** **None of them describes
a state of the world after that date**, and none of them contains anything that would tell a later reader
which of its rows have stopped being true.

**The rule is `ADR-0062`'s** — `DEC-913`, taken by Priyanka Venkataraman on 2027-04-28: **every artefact
carries what makes it go out of date and who holds it, and an artefact with no holder is published as
having none.** **`09.01-how-an-objective-is-scored.md` owns the method question of what an artefact is
scored from; this chapter takes the artefacts as given and asks what happens to them next.**

**The register is `logs/decay-register.md` — `DK-01` to `DK-12` — and it is authoritative.**

**Three columns and each does a different job.**

**What makes it go out of date** names the event, not the passage of time. **An artefact does not decay
because a year passes; it decays because a system is upgraded, a procedure is revised, a product enters a
market, a person leaves a post or a record type stops being maintained electronically.** **Naming the event
is what makes the decay observable**, and `ADR-0015` said so about one register in Phase 02: **a trigger set
with no floor is a control that reports success by being silent.**

**How fast** describes the rate at which that event occurs, and nothing else. **It is not a review
frequency, not a cadence, not a due date and not a commitment.** `CAL-07` and `RV-01` to `RV-61` are the
periodic review arrangement, they are Phase 08's, and **no row of the decay register sets, varies or
supplements either.**

**Holder after 2027-04-30** carries a named individual, a function, or **None**. **It carries nothing
else.** **A vacancy is recorded as a vacancy, not covered by a function** — `ADR-0008` — and the same rule
applied one step further gives the third value: **where nobody has accepted an artefact, the register says
so rather than writing a department name into the cell.** *"It is never converted back to a department
name."*

---

## 1.1 The paragraph that depends on an artefact staying current

**`21 CFR 11.10(c)` is *"Protection of records to enable their accurate and ready retrieval throughout the
records retention period."*** **The obligation runs to the end of a period**, and Phase 07 established
that for fourteen of the thirty-four in-scope record types **no predicate rule sets one**. An artefact
that goes out of date does not breach `(c)` on its own — `(c)` is about records and not about registers —
**but a register nobody maintains is a register that stops describing the estate it was built to
describe, and the determination it carries is the thing every later determination rests on.**

**That is why each of the twelve rows below carries a decay mechanism and a holder rather than a date** —
`ADR-0062`. **A date tells a reader when to look. A mechanism tells them what to look for.**

## 3. The decay mechanisms, and the three shapes they take

**The register states each mechanism per row. Three shapes recur, and naming them is worth one paragraph
each because the shape predicts what a holder would have to watch.**

**The estate shape.** `DK-01`, `DK-03`, `DK-04`, `DK-05`, `DK-08` and `DK-10` decay when the estate moves —
a system entering or leaving, a configuration change, an account change, a version upgrade. **The estate
moved nine times during the programme by removal alone, and the walk of June 2026 found seventeen systems
nobody had entered.** **`DK-04` is the fastest of these and it is already ninety-one days behind** —
`09.11-the-position-is-ninety-one-days-old.md`, `IS-64`.

**The regulation-and-procedure shape.** `DK-07` decays when a predicate rule is amended or when a named
individual determines a period for one of the fourteen record types no predicate rule supplies one for.
**The first is very slow and `CAL-12`'s quarterly horizon scan is the instrument for it; the second is
instantaneous and `DP-38` has not moved.** `DK-02` decays when a pair changes, which is likewise abrupt.

**The vendor shape, and it is the one where decay can change a finding rather than merely age it.**
`DK-06` records that `AE-08` and `AE-16` cannot be enabled by any setting available to Helix **on the
product version Helix runs**. **A vendor release could make that sentence false.** **It could also leave it
true and change something else nobody would look at**, because Helix does not track the release schedule of
the product and no Helix record reports it. **`AS-43` — the vendor statement Helix has not tested — is the
whole of the ground for the current position**, and `PR-07` carries the risk of a vendor being treated as
supplying compliance rather than features that support it. **A product has features that support
compliance; compliance is a property of Helix's validated system in its intended use.**

**And one mechanism belongs to no shape, because it is a mechanism of not being read.** **`DK-09`, the
commitment register, went thirteen months without being opened** — 2026-03-06 to 2027-03-30. **An artefact
nobody reads does not go out of date so much as stop being consulted**, and the two are indistinguishable
from the outside: **in both cases somebody asks a question the artefact could have answered and does not
get the answer.** **That failure mode is the reason `OS-08` is Not met**, and it is the only decay
mechanism in the register that has already demonstrated itself in full.

## 4. The two with a function

**`DK-05`, the control gap register, and `DK-08`, the backup and restore register.**

**Neither is unheld because nobody could be found. Each is unheld because the enquiry at `EV-928` returned
a function and no name.**

**`DK-05` needs a holder who can decide what Helix intends to require of itself.** The register's second
decay mechanism is not an estate event at all: **it is a decision by the quality unit about the two hundred
and eighteen controls that are present and required by nothing** — `IS-29`, `DP-27`, **which has been open
since Phase 05, is larger than the rest of the quality unit's queue together, and against which no sentence
has been written.** **The holder of that register would be holding a decision nobody has taken.**

**`DK-08` needs a holder across an operational estate that no one person runs.** The backup arrangements
span jobs, schedules, retention windows, storage platforms and two data centres; **the register's cells were
taken from enquiries answered by the people who operate the arrangements** — `AS-49` — **and the function
that could have tested them has read access on none of the sixty-one.** **`IS-47` records the ownership
shape directly: three of the four systems constituting the retention estate are owned by the Chief
Information Officer, none by the quality unit, and one entered the inventory by walk.**

**In both cases the register records the function and records that no individual within it has accepted the
artefact.** **`DP-48` asks for the names — fourteen of them, across the two decay rows and the twelve
handover entries whose destination is a function — and none exists.** **A function cannot supply a name on
its own behalf.**

## 5. The one with none, and it is the evidence index

**`DK-12` is `logs/evidence-index.md` and the eight blocks before it, `EV-101` to `EV-940`.**

**It is the artefact every other artefact in the register cites.** `DK-01` to `DK-11` each name `EV`
identifiers in their rows and their closures; **this is where those identifiers resolve.** **`IS-01`'s
closing note names eleven of them. `IS-58`'s closing note names five. Every closure in this repository
names its evidence, because *believed complete* is not a closure condition** — and **every one of those
names is a pointer into a file that, after today, nobody holds.**

**Its decay mechanism is the sharpest of the twelve and the least visible.** **An artefact moves, is
superseded, is renamed, or ceases to be retrievable — and the index does not know.** It records where a
thing was at the vantage it was indexed. **Nothing fires when the thing moves.** **There is no trigger, no
floor and no owner**, which means the register's *how fast* column reads **immediately and silently**: it
is the only artefact of the twelve whose subject matter is other artefacts, **and the only one with nobody
to notice.**

**The holder column reads None, and it reads None rather than reading anything else for a stated reason.**
**`ADR-0062` requires an artefact with no holder to be published as having none.** **Writing *the quality
unit* into that cell would have made the register look complete and would have been false**, and a holder
column completed for the sake of completion is worse than an empty one, **because it stops anybody
looking.** **`DP-49` records the ask — a named individual who accepts `DK-12` — and records that no source
is identified**: the programme manager holds it to this vantage and holds nothing after it, no line
function's remit includes an index of programme evidence, and no post was created for it. **`DP-49` is the
only dependency in the log with no source at all.**

**The evidence index states this about itself, at `evidence-index.md §0`, in its own first paragraph.**
**It is stated once, there and here, and it is not remarked on further.** **`IS-61` carries it.**

## 6. What a decay register is not

**It is not a maintenance plan.** The *first action owed* column states what would have to be done for an
artefact to describe the world again. **It is owed by nobody on any date, and no row assigns work to
anybody.** **No row states a CAPA, a deviation, a remediation plan or a cost.**

**It is not a review schedule, and it must not be read as one.** **`RV-01` to `RV-61` are the periodic
review arrangement**, they carry a frequency and a first due date per system, **and reviews performed
against them number none** — `IS-57`. **No row of the decay register is a review commitment, and no row
implies that one exists.** `PR-62` carries the risk that an arrangement is reported as the thing it
arranges for, and **the same risk applies here in a second form: a decay rate is not an undertaking to
refresh anything.**

**It is not a statement that any artefact is wrong.** **An artefact that has gone out of date was true at
the vantage it was published.** **A determination nobody maintains is a determination that was true once**
— `ADR-0015` — and that is a statement about maintenance, not about the determination.

**And it is not a re-determination.** **Nothing in this chapter or in the register re-determines a Phase 05
cell, re-scores a Phase 03 pair, re-bands a Phase 04 obligation, re-derives a Phase 07 period or re-takes a
control position.** **Every one of the twelve is handed on exactly as its owning phase published it.**

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **No holder for `DK-12`**, and none is proposed. **No name is invented for `DK-05` or `DK-08` either.**
- **No review frequency, no cadence, no due date and no commitment.**
- **No retention period for any record type**, and no bound on one.
- **No score, band, rating, priority or aggregate.** No row carries a likelihood or an impact.
- **No CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no cost.**
- **No compliance conclusion, no legal conclusion, no readiness claim and no forecast.**
- **No closure of any observation and no representation to FDA.**
- **No result of any periodic review or independent assessment**, and **no access described as performed.**
- **No candidate explanation of `IS-01`.**
