---

## 1. What is being handed over, and what a handover is

**Forty-nine issues stand open at the close-out vantage.** **Sixty-six exist, seventeen are closed, and
`logs/raid-log.md §0` prints the arithmetic.** **The forty-nine are the population of
`logs/handover-register.md`, one row each, in `IS` order**, and that register is authoritative.

**A handover is one act and it is narrow: a named individual states, on a date, that an entry is theirs
after 2027-04-30.** **It is not a resolution.** **It is not agreement that the entry is soluble.** **It is
not a commitment to a date, a statement that resources exist, or an undertaking to do anything by any
time.** **No row of the register carries a target date, and none ever will**, because the programme that
could have set one ends at this vantage.

**The rule is `ADR-0060`'s** — `DEC-908`, taken by Gideon Halvorsen on 2027-04-21 and minuted at `GOV-42`:
**an open entry is handed to a named individual or it is recorded as having no destination.**

---

## 2. A function is not a destination

**This is the argument the register turns on, and it is stated once.**

**A function cannot accept.** **Acceptance is an act by somebody, on a date.** A function has no date on
which it did anything, because a function is not the kind of thing that does something on a date; **people
in it do.** **A record that says an entry was accepted by the quality unit is a record of nobody having
accepted it, written so that the cell reads full.**

**A function cannot be asked a question and answer it.** **The value of a destination is that a later
reader can go to it and find out where the entry stands.** Going to a function means finding somebody in
it, which means finding out who holds the entry, **which is the thing the register was supposed to
record.** **A destination you have to look up is not a destination.**

**A function cannot be held to a date.** **A commitment with no individual behind it has no failure mode**:
nobody has missed anything, because nobody undertook anything. **`IS-51` records the same defect on the
other axis — a commitment with an owner and a date and no test — and the two together describe the whole of
what makes an undertaking unenforceable.**

**This is `ADR-0008` applied to an entry rather than to a control.** *"Every control, every procedure,
every record and every obligation in this programme has a named individual owner. No control is owned by a
team, a function, a department or a committee."* **A team owner is a plausible-looking absence of an
owner** — and **so is a team destination.** **The regulation's own unit of accountability is a person**:
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` speaks of *"…the initials or signature of a second person…"*;
`21 CFR 211.188(b)(11)` requires the identification of the persons performing and directly supervising or
checking each significant step in the operation, or, where a significant step is performed by automated
equipment under `§ 211.68`, *"…the identification of the person checking the significant step performed by
the automated equipment"*; `21 CFR 11.10(j)` requires the establishment of, and adherence to, **written
policies that hold individuals accountable and responsible for actions initiated under their electronic
signatures, in order to deter record and signature falsification**; `21 CFR 11.100(a)` requires an
electronic signature to be **unique to one individual**.
**A register whose unit is a function has already lost that correspondence.**

**So the register records the function where a function is all there is, and records No in the acceptance
column beside it.** **The No is arithmetic and not a judgement about anybody in the function.** **It is
printed rather than left blank, because a blank invites completion** — the same reason `commitment-register.md`
prints *No completion criterion* rather than an empty cell.

**And the alternative was available and was refused.** **Recording a function as the destination and
treating receipt as acceptance** would have produced a register in which forty-five of forty-nine entries
had a destination and forty-five had been accepted. **It would have been tidier and it would have been
false**, and `ADR-0060`'s refused-alternative section argues it at length.

## 4. The twelve that go to a function, and the ten that go to one function

| Function | Entries | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **The quality unit** | `HO-01`, `HO-13`, `HO-14`, `HO-17`, `HO-29`, `HO-30`, `HO-31`, `HO-33`, `HO-38`, `HO-48` | **10** |
| **The validation function** | `HO-22` | **1** |
| **The change control function** | `HO-37` | **1** |
| | **Total** | **12** |

**Ten of the twelve go to the quality unit, and that is the second finding of the register rather than its
tidiest column.**

**The quality unit is a real body with a statutory position and it is not a placeholder.** **`21 CFR
211.22` requires a drug manufacturer to have one, gives it the responsibility and authority to approve or
reject, and at `21 CFR 211.22(d)` requires that its responsibilities and procedures be in writing and
followed.** **It existed before this programme and it will outlast it**, and **its approval and rejection
authority is not delegable to this programme.** That is precisely why ten entries point at it: **each of
the ten asks for something only the quality unit can give.** A retention period determined and recorded as a
decision. A sentence in a procedure. A statement of what Helix intends to require of itself. Ownership of a
retention arrangement it does not own. Names against seven calendar entries that outlive the programme.

**And ten entries pointing at one function, none of them accepted by any individual within it, is not a
handover.** **`DP-07`, `DP-11`, `DP-22`, `DP-27`, `DP-35`, `DP-38`, `DP-45`, `DP-47`, `DP-48` and `DP-52`
draw on the same quality unit capacity**, and `DP-27` — the decision on the two hundred and eighteen
unrequired controls — **is still larger than the rest together, has been open since Phase 05, and no
sentence has been written.** **A destination that receives ten entries none of which anybody has accepted
has received nothing.**

**`HO-22` and `HO-37` are the other two and each is unheld for a reason of its own.** **`HO-22` — `IS-34`,
the validation evidence owed in full — has a named lead and no capacity**: `DP-05` and `DP-21` ask for
execution resource contracted and sized against sixty-one validation plans, `AS-32` assumes it is available
and has never been tested because nothing has drawn on it, **and a lead cannot accept an entry whose
discharge requires a resource nobody has released to him.** **`HO-37` — `IS-53`, the registers that cannot
evidence a remediation — needs a route that sits in change control, outside every register this programme
built**, and `DP-47` asks for it. **`DP-48` asks for the twelve names and none exists.**

## 5. What acceptance rests on

**`AS-62` states it and it is not buried.** **The thirty-three acceptances at `EV-919` are records that
each individual stated, on a date, that the entry is theirs.** **What was read is the acceptance. It was
not, and could not be, the use of it.**

**If the assumption fails, the handover is a record of thirty-three signatures and not of thirty-three
destinations**, and the 33 / 12 / 4 becomes a count of paperwork. **It is `AS-54`'s shape at one further
remove** — and `AS-54`'s subject matter, the five commitment owners' own trackers, is `IS-52`'s, **which
closed at this vantage on five statements of exactly the same kind.** **The programme has now twice
recorded a position from a statement it could not test**, and it says so both times.

**Nothing in the register or in this chapter states that any of the thirty-three will act.** **`PR-67`
carries the risk that a function destination is reported inside Helix as handed over**, and the same
pressure applies to a named one: **an entry accepted is an entry with a destination, and nothing more.**

## 6. The four that go nowhere

**Four entries have no destination at all**: `HO-04` (`IS-12`), `HO-43` (`IS-60`), `HO-45` (`IS-62`) and
`HO-49` (`IS-66`).

**`09.08-the-four-that-go-nowhere.md` takes them one at a time and this chapter states only that they
exist, that they are four, and that the register records why for each.** **`IS-62`** carries the fact.

**One thing belongs here rather than there, because it is about the register and not about the four.**
**`IS-62` is itself one of the four it counts.** **That is arithmetic and not a device**: an entry that
records that four entries have no destination is an entry about the absence of destinations, and there is
no destination for it. **Handing it to somebody would assert that the four now have a holder, which is
exactly what is untrue.**

---

## 7. What the handover does not do

- **It closes nothing.** **No entry is closed by being accepted**, and **`ADR-0063` records that nothing
  was closed at close-out for the sake of closing it.**
- **It sets no date.** No row carries a target, a due date or a milestone.
- **It resolves no dependency.** **`DP-43` is the only dependency this phase discharged**, and it was
  discharged by five statements and not by the handover.
- **It states no CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no cost.**
- **It closes no inspectional observation and describes none as closed**, and **it makes no representation
  to FDA** — `ADR-0064`.
- **It reports the result of no periodic review and of no independent assessment.**
- **It re-determines, re-scores, re-bands and re-derives nothing.**
- **It describes no access as performed.** `HO-39` records that the grant at `DEC-911` is decided and
  dated, that `IS-55` stays open, and that the published position is unmoved — `ADR-0061`.
- **It names no candidate explanation of `IS-01`.**
- **It reaches no compliance conclusion, no legal conclusion, no readiness claim and no forecast.**
