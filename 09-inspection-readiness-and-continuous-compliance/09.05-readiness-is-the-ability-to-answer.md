# 09.05 — Readiness Is the Ability to Answer

*Being able to answer a question and having a good answer to it are two different properties, and only one of them is this programme's to report. All content is fictional and illustrative.*

---

## 1. One idea

**Inspection readiness is the ability to answer. It is not the quality of the answer.**

That is `ADR-0059`, taken by the Head of Quality Assurance on 2027-04-19 at `DEC-905`, on the questions
recorded at `EV-907` three days earlier. **The distinction is the whole of the chapter, and everything else
in it is a consequence of taking it seriously.**

**This chapter states no score and no register figure.** What Helix can now answer is a set of artefacts;
what the artefacts say is `09.02-the-charter-scored.md`'s and `logs/objective-score-register.md`'s, and how
old the estate-wide position is is `09.11-the-position-is-ninety-one-days-old.md`'s. **None of it is restated
here.**

**And the chapter reaches no conclusion about Helix's compliance with anything**, states no position on
whether Helix should expect any particular outcome from any inspection, and forecasts nothing. **The phrase
this chapter exists to prevent does not appear in it.**

## 2. What Helix could not say in February 2026

**In February 2026 Helix could not say which of its records were Part 11 records, which systems held them,
or what its control position was.**

**The first of the three was an issue in its own right, raised at the inaugural Steering Committee on
2026-03-13 and worded without cushioning:**

> **`IS-02` — "No written record exists of which Helix records are Part 11 records.** No determination has
> been made, at any point, by anybody. Every scoping statement in the estate therefore rests on an
> unrecorded assumption."

**The second and third followed from it, because they could not be answered before it was.** A system is in
scope because of the records it holds — `ADR-0001` — so a firm that has not determined which records are in
scope cannot say which systems are, **and a control position is a position taken against a paragraph of a
regulation on a system that is in scope for a reason nobody had written down.** `IS-03` recorded that the
register of computerised systems carried no attribute identifying which systems held records a predicate rule
requires. **The estate was not unmanaged. It was undetermined**, and the two are different.

**Phase 01 put the question in one sentence and split it:** *"Which records actually are Part 11 records —
and did anybody ever write that down?"* **Phase 02 answered the first half.** The second half was `IS-01`'s,
it stayed open across six phases, and it closed at Phase 08 on what the programme can establish and expressly
not on why — **which is Phase 08's and is not reopened here.** **Nothing in this section bears on `IS-01`,
supports any candidate explanation of it, or may be cited in support of one**: what is recorded here is the
state of Helix's record in February 2026, **and a fact about the state of a record is not an explanation of
anything.**

**None of this is a criticism of anybody at Helix, and the record says so in the same place it says the
rest.** `02.10` established that no written determination existed anywhere **and that no procedure required
one to exist.** A company cannot be faulted for failing to maintain a record its own procedures never asked
for. **What can be said, and is, is that in February 2026 the questions had no answers**, and that this is a
fact about the state of the record rather than about anybody's diligence.

**What changed in February 2026 was not what applies to Helix.** `01.02 §7`: **"An inspection reveals a
state of affairs; it does not create an obligation."** `21 CFR 211.68` applied before the inspection exactly
as it applied after it, and Part 11 reached the same records. **What changed was what Helix knew, and what
Helix had committed to** — *"That is the whole of the difference, and it is not nothing."*

## 3. What Helix can answer now, and what the answering consists of

**Helix can now answer all three, and the answering consists of artefacts rather than of assurances.**

**Which records are Part 11 records** is answered by the record-type determination and the systems register
Phase 02 published and Phase 09 has not touched: a determination per record type, under document control,
each with a named determiner, a date and a quality unit reviewer, with a review trigger and an annual floor
attached — `ADR-0015`. **Which systems hold them** is derived from that determination rather than entered
directly, which is why the answer to the second question changes when the answer to the first does. **What
the control position is** is answered by the Phase 05 control position register, taken paragraph by paragraph
against every system in scope on a method fixed before the first system was looked at.

**Three properties of that answering matter more than the fact of it.**

**Each answer names a determiner and a date.** **An answer with no date has no age**, and age is what gets an
answer looked at. Every determination in the repository can be asked when it was made and by whom, and the
answer is in the artefact rather than in somebody's recollection.

**Each answer states what it rests on, including where that is an assumption.** The registers carry their
assumptions as `AS` entries rather than burying them, and the widest of them — that those who supplied the
facts described the position as it is rather than as procedure says it should be — **has been open and
stated at every vantage from the first to the last.**

**And each answer is checkable against something.** A reader who doubts a determination can open the record
type, read the predicate rule cited against it, and disagree. **That property is what distinguishes an answer
from an assertion**, and it is the property the programme was funded to create.

**Many of the answers are that the position is not what it should be.** That is `09.02-the-charter-scored.md`'s
subject and is not restated here — **but the fact that unflattering answers are among them is not incidental
to this chapter; it is the point of it.** **A firm that can answer a question with a bad answer is in the
condition this chapter describes.** A firm that can answer it with a good one is in a different condition,
and nothing in this phase claims Helix is in the second.

## 4. Readiness and compliance are different objects

**Readiness is a property of the answering. Compliance is a property of the thing being asked about.**

**They come apart in both directions, which is what shows they are different objects rather than degrees of
the same one.**

**A firm can be able to answer and the answer can be bad.** It holds the determination, the register and the
evidence index; it can produce them, dated and owned; and what they say is that a control is absent, a
procedure is silent, or a comparison has never been made. **The answering is complete and the position is
poor.**

**And a firm can be unable to answer while the position is perfectly good.** An estate can be well
configured, well operated and well maintained by people who know what they are doing, with no register
anywhere that records it. **Nothing about the records makes the systems worse.** What it makes worse is the
firm's ability to say anything true about them — which is why `02.10`'s finding was about the absence of a
written determination and not about the state of the estate.

**Compliance, in any event, is not the sum of a set of register positions.** It is a property of a validated
system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated by its trained people. **A control being present
is not the same as the system being validated, the procedure being followed, the people being trained or the
records being reliable** — and no phase of this programme has established a validation outcome at all. **A
conclusion about compliance drawn from control positions would be asserting a totality out of one of its
parts**, which is what `ADR-0057` refused in Phase 08 and what nothing here reverses.

**The word *ready* is the hazard, and it is a specific one.** The word already exists in the organisation's
vocabulary with the other meaning attached — the meaning it carries when somebody asks whether the site is
ready for an inspection and expects an answer about how the inspection will go. **A phase that uses the word
without fixing which meaning it carries has handed the reader the other one**, and the other one is a
forecast about somebody else's judgement.

**This programme has refused that forecast since Phase 01, for three reasons each sufficient alone.** **It is
not knowable** — FDA is explicit that a 483 is not an all-inclusive list. **It corrupts the programme** —
*"Every design decision acquires a second criterion — how it will look — and the second criterion wins
arguments the first should have won."* **And it is a claim about somebody else's judgement.** A readiness
claim is that forecast in the one grammatical form that does not look like one, **which is why `ADR-0059`
forbids the sentence and this chapter does not write it.**

**The bounded version fails for the reason every bounded version in this repository has failed.** *Ready in
the sense of able to answer, and not in the sense of compliant* becomes *ready* by the second retelling.
**A qualifier does not survive a summary, a slide or a report** — `ADR-0035` established the mechanism for a
landscape fact and `ADR-0050` for a number — **and the retelling that matters happens outside this
repository, in a room where nobody is holding the register.**

## 5. What an investigator can be shown, and what cannot

**A firm can show an investigator what it holds. It cannot show what it does not have, and it must not
present the first as the second.**

**What can be shown, because it exists:**

- **A determination.** Which record types are Part 11 records, made record by record on a published method,
  each with a named determiner, a date and a reviewer.
- **A register.** The systems, the record types, the control positions, the retention derivations, the
  auditable event classes, the backup and restore positions — each with the vantage it speaks at.
- **An evidence index.** Every artefact the programme relied on, indexed against the determination it
  supports, so that a claim can be traced to the thing it rests on rather than believed.
- **A governance record.** Who decided what, on what date, at which sitting, with the minutes of the body
  that took it and the individual it is attributed to.

**What cannot be shown, because Helix does not have it:**

- **A compliant estate.** Where a control is absent the register says so, and no document produced to an
  investigator can make the control present.
- **A validation outcome.** No package has been executed, no protocol run and no test result produced by
  this programme.
- **A performed review.** The periodic review arrangement is established and has not been run — `IS-57` —
  and **an arrangement with a first due date is not a review.**
- **An independent assessment.** The arrangement exists and no assessment has been scoped, commissioned,
  performed or scheduled — `IS-63`, and `09.09-independence-cannot-be-retrofitted.md` owns it.
- **Access that has been performed.** A grant decided is not access administered — `ADR-0061`.

**And there is a third category, which is the one this section exists for: things Helix has, which must not
be shown as though they were something else.**

**A firm that presents its registers as though they were a compliance position has told the investigator
something untrue about itself.** The registers are a description of a position, made by the firm, at a date,
on a method it published. **They are not a certification, they are not an assurance opinion, and they are not
evidence that any control works** — a control position is not validation evidence, and none of this material
was produced by anybody independent of the functions it describes.

**The same applies to the completeness of the repository.** It is possible to hand somebody nine phases of
determinations and registers in a way that invites the inference that a firm this organised must be in good
order. **The inference does not follow, and a firm that arranges its material so that it will be drawn has
made a representation by arrangement rather than by sentence.** `ADR-0064` refuses that in terms: **no
artefact in this phase is written to be read by an agency**, and where a sentence would have been written
differently for such a reader, the version written is the one that serves the internal reader.

**None of which is a reason to withhold anything, and nothing here is a position on what may be withheld.**
A firm asked for a record it is required to keep produces it. **What a firm's own internal quality assurance
audit reports are is a separate question with a published answer that is FDA's and not Helix's**: FDA
Compliance Policy Guide Sec. 130.300 states that *"FDA will not review or copy reports and records that
result from audits and inspections of the written quality assurance program"*, subject to four stated
exceptions — a directed or for-cause inspection of a sponsor or monitor of a clinical investigation,
litigation, an inspection made by inspection warrant where access is authorised by statute, and a judicial
search warrant — and reserves access to records required by regulation relating to quality control
investigations of product failures and manufacturing errors. **That is a statement of FDA's own policy about
a category of record, it is not a right Helix asserts, it is not advice, and nothing in this phase is
arranged around it.** **It says nothing about what any internal audit at Helix did or did not raise, it is
not evidence about `IS-01`, which closed at Phase 08 and stays closed, and it may not be cited in support of
any candidate explanation of it.** **What
this section fixes is the description that travels with whatever is produced**, and the description is that
these are Helix's own account of its own position, at a stated date, with its assumptions on the face of
it.

## 6. A complete register of one's own failures, and what saying so does not mean

**A complete and honest register of a firm's own failures is a better inspection position than an incomplete
register of its successes.**

**The reason is not moral, it is evidential.** An incomplete register cannot be relied on for anything,
including the parts of it that are favourable: a reader who finds one gap has to treat every entry as
possibly subject to the same omission. **A register that records what is absent is a register whose silences
mean something**, because the absences are written down rather than being the residue of what nobody looked
at. **An unexplained exclusion is indistinguishable from an oversight**, and a register that explains its
exclusions is the only kind that can be read at all.

**And it is a better position for a second reason, which is about what a firm can be asked.** A firm holding
a register of its own gaps can be asked what it is doing about them, which is a question with an answer.
**A firm holding a register that omits them can be asked why the omission is there**, which is a question
about the firm's record-keeping rather than about its estate — **and that is the question `OBS-2`, `OBS-4`
and `OBS-6` are all, in different ways, versions of.**

**Saying that is not the same as saying the position is good, and the two must not be run together.**

**The register being honest says nothing about the estate.** Every gap it records is still a gap. Every
control it records as absent is still absent. **The quality of the record and the quality of the thing
recorded are independent**, and a firm that improved only the first has improved only the first.

**Nor is honesty a mitigation, and this repository does not offer it as one.** **An open gap is Helix's own
assessment of its own position** — not a violation, not a breach, not a non-compliance, not a finding and not
an inspectional observation — **and recording one is not a partial discharge of the obligation it concerns.**
Nothing in this phase argues that a documented gap is better than an undocumented one *for the purposes of
the underlying requirement*, because it is not: the requirement is unaffected by whether Helix has noticed.

**And it is not a claim about how anybody will react to it.** How an investigator, an auditor or a customer
reads a register of self-identified gaps is that person's judgement, on a day that has not happened. **This
programme forecasts no such judgement**, and the sentence at the head of this section is a statement about
what a register can support, not a prediction about what anybody will make of it.

**The plainest form of the position is the one to end on, because it is the one that survives compression.**
**Helix can now answer three questions it could not answer in February 2026, and many of the answers say the
position is not what it should be.** **That is a readiness position and it is not a compliance position.**

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **No compliance conclusion.** No statement, implication or forecast that Helix, or any system, or any
  record, is compliant or non-compliant with `21 CFR Part 11`, with Parts 210 and 211, or with anything
  else — **including in a bounded, qualified or paragraph-specific form.**
- **No readiness claim.** **No sentence of the form the organisation would like appears here**, and
  `ADR-0059` forbids it in terms.
- **No score, no register figure and no count.** The answers are `09.02-the-charter-scored.md`'s and
  `logs/objective-score-register.md`'s; the age of the estate-wide position is
  `09.11-the-position-is-ninety-one-days-old.md`'s.
- **No forecast** of what any future inspection will find, what any investigator would make of any artefact,
  or what any agency would do. **The same refusal applies to the internal audit of the remediated programme
  and to the third-party audit by Vashti Okonjo & Partners. Neither has occurred and neither's result is
  anticipated anywhere.**
- **No representation to FDA.** **The only representation Helix has made about the observations is the
  written response of 2026-03-06**, and this chapter neither repeats, extends, amends nor supplements it —
  `ADR-0064`.
- **No closure of any inspectional observation**, and no word meaning closed — `ADR-0051`.
- **No legal conclusion**, and no characterisation of the Ridgemont inspection, or of any condition observed
  at it, as a violation of anything. **A Form FDA 483 is not a final agency determination:** *"The FDA Form
  483 does not constitute a final Agency determination of whether any condition is in violation of the FD&C
  Act or any of its relevant regulations."*
- **No claim that any artefact demonstrates that any control works.** **A control position is not validation
  evidence**, and no validation outcome has been established anywhere in this programme.
- **No maturity rating, no readiness scale and no position on any published index.**
- **No result of any periodic review or of any independent assessment.** **None has been performed** —
  `IS-57`, `IS-63`.
- **No re-determination, re-scoring, re-banding or re-derivation of anything**, and no retention period for
  any record type — `ADR-0044`, `ADR-0049`, `ADR-0050`.
- **No candidate explanation of `IS-01`**, which closed at Phase 08 and stays closed.
- **No statement about any individual.** **No document in this repository assesses any individual's work.**
