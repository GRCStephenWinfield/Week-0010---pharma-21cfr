# ADR-0061 — An access grant is recorded as decided and dated, never as performed, until an artefact evidences the performance

*A decision to grant access is an act of governance. An account somebody can log into is an act of administration. This repository has spent nine phases refusing to record the first as the second.*

## Context

**This decision exists because the programme has just taken the one decision it would most like to report as
an accomplishment.**

The audit function has never held read access to the systems it would need to read in order to test what
this programme determined. That is `IS-55`, raised at the Phase 08 vantage and open; `DP-46` carries the
dependency; `AC-05` is the audit condition that turns on it. **The grant of that access is decided here,
dated here, and taken by the individual who owns the estate and access administration.**

**And the temptation attaches to the same sentence.** The single most quotable thing the last phase of this
programme could say is that the access which nine phases lacked has now been granted. **It is a sentence
about governance that reads as a sentence about the world**, and the distance between the two is a set of
administrative acts on individual systems that nobody has performed and that no artefact evidences.

**The repository has met this shape five times already and refused it every time.** An arrangement to review
a system is not a review of it. A frequency adopted is not a review performed. A completion criterion
written now is not a commitment filed. A configuration is evidence of a setting and not of an operation. A
band is not a validation status. **`PR-33` carries the general form — a requirement read as a description of
the world — and the RAID log records it inverted again at this vantage.** A grant decided and read as access
performed is the same error in another costume, **arriving in the phase with the least remaining time in
which anybody could notice.**

**The decision is taken by the Chief Information Officer**, who is the only individual in the cast able to
cause the access to exist, and who therefore has the strongest claim to say that it effectively does. **The
record is that she decided the grant and declined to record it as performed.**

## Decision

**An access grant is recorded as decided and dated, never as performed, until an artefact evidences the
performance.**

Four limbs.

**A decision record evidences a decision.** It carries the decision, the date, the decider and the systems
it names, and it is indexed as what it is. **It evidences no account, no entitlement, no session and no
capability on any system**, and nothing in this phase reads it as doing so.

**Performance requires an artefact of its own.** What would evidence performance is a record of the access
as administered — system by system, with the entitlement, the individual it attaches to and the date it took
effect — held where access administration records are held. **No such artefact exists at this vantage and
none is asserted.**

**The dependent entries do not move.** **`IS-55` stays open and says so. `DP-46` stays open and says so.
`AC-05` is not described as capable of being met**, and the published position on requested and granted
access is the audit condition register's and is not restated, adjusted or superseded here.

**And the recording convention is general.** It reaches every grant, entitlement, permission, role
assignment and account this repository might ever mention, in any phase, **and not only the read access this
decision concerns.**

## Alternatives considered

**Record the grant as granted — on the ground that in this organisation the CIO's decision to grant read
access is the grant, that provisioning is a clerical consequence of it, and that a record which draws a
distinction the organisation does not draw is recording a fiction of the repository's own.**

**This is the strongest alternative here and it deserves to be put at length, because it is not a plea for
convenience. It is an argument that the distinction is artificial.**

**The first argument is that the decision is the operative act and everything after it is administration.**
Access at Helix is granted by the individual accountable for the estate; once she has decided, no further
judgement is exercised by anybody. **A service desk operative implementing an approved entitlement is not
deciding anything**, and a record that treats the operative's keystroke as the moment access came into
existence has located the accountability in the wrong place. **The repository's own rule points the same
way: ownership means the control is defined, resourced, performed as defined and evidenced — and the owner
here has done the first three.**

**The second is that the alternative record is the one the organisation will act on.** Internal audit will
plan its next pass on the basis that the access is coming. The Board audit committee will read the decision
as the answer to a question it has been asking at its quarterly sittings. **A repository that alone insists the
access does not exist is not preventing anybody from relying on it; it is merely declining to say what
everybody has understood.**

**The third is that the distinction cannot be drawn consistently anyway.** Access is not a single object. It
is an entitlement on a directory, a role on a system, a group membership, a licence, and in some cases a
person with a password. **Asking when access was *performed* is asking a question with several answers**,
and a repository that refuses to record a grant until an artefact evidences performance may be refusing to
record it until an artefact exists that nobody has ever produced for any grant at Helix. **On that reading
the rule is not a discipline; it is a permanent veto** — and a permanent veto on recording anything as done
makes the register useless as a description of the estate.

**The fourth is that the phase is about to end.** There will be no later phase to record the performance
when it happens. **The choice is between a record that says the access was granted and a record that says
nothing was ever granted at all**, and the second is further from the truth than the first.

**Refused, on four grounds.**

**The programme's whole subject matter is the difference between a record of an intention and a record of an
act.** `OBS-4` is about attribution — the ability to say which individual did what. `OBS-6` is about a
change that happened and did not appear in the record the reviewer saw. The retention finding is that a
comparison was never made, not that a period is short. **A programme that spent fourteen months establishing
that Helix's records must evidence acts rather than intentions cannot record its own intention as an act in
its final phase** without conceding every argument it made.

**The third argument is conceded in part and it does not carry.** It is true that access is several objects
and that the artefact which would evidence performance is not defined anywhere at Helix. **That is a finding
about Helix's access administration and it is recorded as one** — `DP-46` asks for exactly that artefact,
system by system, in the working papers. **The absence of a method for evidencing performance is a reason to
name the absence, not a licence to assume the performance.** `02.05 §9` and `GOV-08 §4` fixed the general
form: **a negative that nobody can test is not a determination; it is a silence.** So is a positive.

**The fourth argument inverts.** It is precisely because there is no later phase that the record must be
exact. **A record that says access was granted, published on the last day of a programme with nobody left to
correct it, is the most durable inaccuracy this repository could produce.** A record that says a grant was
decided on a date by a named individual, and that no artefact evidences access on any system, is a record
that a later reader can act on: **the question it leaves open is a question somebody can answer, whereas the
question the alternative closes is one nobody will ever re-open.**

**And the assurance value of the grant depends on the distinction surviving.** The grant exists so that a
future assessment can be independent. **An assessment planned on the assumption that access exists, and
begun to find that it does not, has lost its own start date** — and `PR-68` carries exactly that risk. The
audit function is better served by an accurate account of what it does not yet have than by a comfortable
one of what it does.

---

**Record the grant as *granted in principle*, or *granted, provisioning pending*, or with a status
qualifier of some other kind, so that the decision is visible without asserting the access.**

**A reasonable compromise on its face**, and it is what a great many change records do. It records both
facts, it is honest, and it avoids the awkwardness of a register that appears to say nothing happened when
something plainly did.

**Refused.** **A qualifier does not survive a summary, a slide or a report** — `ADR-0035` in Phase 05,
`ADR-0050` in Phase 07, and the mechanism has never once failed to operate. *Granted, provisioning pending*
becomes *granted* by the second retelling; **the word that survives is the one somebody wanted.** The same
reasoning refused a status field on the disposition record in Phase 08: **a field that permits the word will
be filled with it.** The convention here is not a qualifier on a grant; it is a different object recorded
under its own name — a decision, dated.

---

**Say nothing about the grant at all until it has been performed, on the ground that a decision nobody has
implemented is not a fact about the estate and does not belong in a register that describes one.**

**It has real force**, and it is the mirror image of the first alternative: if performance is what matters,
then the decision is not news.

**Refused, on two grounds.** **The decision is a fact and this repository records facts at their own weight
rather than suppressing the ones that might be over-read.** A dated decision by a named individual is
evidence of something — of governance having addressed a question it had not addressed in nine phases — and
suppressing it would make the record of `IS-58`'s closure unintelligible, because the arrangement it closes
on has this decision as one of its limbs. **And an unrecorded decision is worse than a recorded one for the
purpose the objection cares about**: unrecorded, it still circulates, and it circulates with nothing
attached to it that says access was not performed.

## Consequences

**Accepted — the last phase of the programme reports that the access question is still open**, in the phase
that decided the grant, and reports it in the same paragraph as the decision.

**Accepted — internal audit ends the programme without the access it has never had**, and the arrangement
under which it might obtain it has no date.

**Accepted — the record will be read inside Helix as ungenerous to the individual who took the decision**,
and she took it on that basis and approved the convention that produces the reading.

**Accepted — the convention binds every later grant in this repository**, including ones that would be
easier to record the other way.

**Gained — nothing in this phase asserts a capability that no artefact evidences**, which is the standard
the programme applied to the estate for nine phases and now applies to itself in the one instance where it
would rather not.

**Gained — the entries that depend on the access stay open and stay legible.** A later reader can see
exactly what exists, exactly what does not, and exactly what artefact would change it.

## Refused

**This decision forbids the following in Phase 09 and binds every later phase and reader.**

- **Describing any access, entitlement, permission, role or account as granted, provisioned, in place,
  available, live or effective** on the strength of a decision to grant it.
- **Attaching a status qualifier to a grant** — *granted in principle*, *granted, provisioning pending*,
  *effectively granted*, or any equivalent, **under any label and in any column.**
- **Closing, narrowing, downgrading or annotating `IS-55` or `DP-46` on the strength of the decision**, or
  describing `AC-05` as capable of being met.
- **Restating, adjusting or superseding the published position on requested and granted read access**, which
  is the audit condition register's and does not move here.
- **Citing the decision as evidence that the repository's determinations have been independently tested**,
  or that any assessment has been performed — `ADR-0004`.
- **Reporting the arrangement as an assessment** — `PR-69`, and **an arrangement is not an assessment.**
- **Reporting the result of any independent assessment or any periodic review.** **None has been performed.**
- **Treating this decision as making the determinations in this repository independent retrospectively**,
  which is refused in terms at `../09.09-independence-cannot-be-retrofitted.md`.
