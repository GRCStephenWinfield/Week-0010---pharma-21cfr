# ADR-0063 — The programme closes with its open entries open, and nothing is closed at close-out for the sake of closing it

*A close-out that tidies is a close-out that lies to whoever inherits it.*

|---|---|

## Context

**A close-out is the one moment in a programme's life at which the register is read by everybody and
maintained by nobody.**

The RAID log has been reviewed monthly at `CAL-01` and reported quarterly at `CAL-04` for the whole of the
programme's life. **After this vantage neither of those things happens**, because `CAL-01` is a programme
body and ends with the programme. The register that closes today is the register a later reader will find,
**and there is no further sitting at which anything in it will be corrected.**

**The pressure to close things is at its maximum here and it is entirely respectable in form.** Some entries
have been overtaken: their subject matter has been superseded by a later determination, absorbed into
another entry, or made academic by a change somewhere else. **A register carrying entries whose questions
nobody would now ask looks careless**, and a sweep that closed them would produce a smaller, tidier register
that a later reader could actually work through.

**The register's own rules already answer most of it, and have since Phase 01.** **Closure names the
evidence. "Believed complete" is not a closure condition** — `01.12 §9`. A status change is recorded with a
date and a decision reference and statuses are not edited in place. **An issue closes when the condition that made it an
issue no longer holds, with the evidence named**, and not otherwise. **What Phase 09 adds is the case the
rules did not anticipate**: closure not because the condition ceased, but because the programme did.

**And the decision is taken by the executive sponsor**, who approved the charter, chairs the body that
adopted the registers, and is the individual to whom a tidy close-out would be worth the most. **She takes
it on the last working day of the programme's governance, at the sitting at which the body she chairs
dissolves.**

## Decision

**The programme closes with its open entries open. Nothing is closed at close-out for the sake of closing
it.**

Four limbs.

**An entry closes only on the conditions the register has always required.** The condition that made it an
entry no longer holds, the evidence is named, the closure is dated, and the decision reference is recorded.
**The end of the programme is not one of those conditions and does not become one.**

**No entry closes because its subject matter has been superseded, absorbed or made academic.** Where a later
determination has changed the ground an entry stands on, that is recorded in the entry — **the entry is not
removed and its status does not move.**

**No entry closes because it has been handed over.** A handover moves a question to somebody who may answer
it; it does not answer it — `ADR-0060`. **An entry with a destination and an acceptance is exactly as open
as an entry with neither.**

**And no entry closes because nobody will ever pick it up.** An entry with no destination is not an entry
with no subject matter. **The absence of a holder is information the entry now carries, and closing it would
convert the most uncomfortable fact in the register into the appearance of a resolved one.**

## Alternatives considered

**Run a close-out sweep — review every open entry against what the programme now knows, and close those
whose subject matter has been superseded, absorbed into another entry, or made academic by something that
has happened since, each closure recorded with its reason.**

**This is what a competently run programme does at close-out, it is what the organisation expected, and it
has to be put at full strength because it is not a request to fudge anything.**

**The first argument is that some entries genuinely are stale, and a register that says otherwise is
inaccurate in the opposite direction.** An entry raised in Phase 02 against a register that Phase 04
replaced is not describing a live condition; it is describing a condition in an artefact that no longer
governs. **Leaving it open is not conservatism, it is a false positive** — and a register that carries false
positives is exactly as misleading as one that carries false negatives, with the additional disadvantage
that nobody can tell which are which.

**The second is that an unreadable register is an unused register.** The value of a handover is that
somebody picks it up. **A very long list of open entries, several of which turn out on inspection to be
duplicates or dead, is a list a successor abandons on the second afternoon** — and the entries that mattered
are lost along with the ones that did not. **A sweep is not tidying for appearance; it is the difference
between an instrument and an archive.**

**The third is that the sweep can be made rigorous.** It need not be a judgement call. A closure could be
permitted only where a named later artefact demonstrably supersedes the entry's subject matter, with the
artefact cited and the closure dated — **which is closure naming its evidence, exactly as the register has
always required.** On that construction the sweep is not an exception to the rule; it is an application of
it that the programme has simply never had occasion to make.

**And the fourth is that the alternative to a sweep is not a pure register but a distorted one.** The
entries that stay open are not a neutral sample. **Entries about things the programme could not change stay
open; entries about things it could change were closed as they were resolved** — so a register that closes
nothing at the end over-represents the intractable and reads as a worse account of the programme than the
programme deserves.

**Refused, on four grounds.**

**The third argument is the strongest and it is conceded rather than answered — and conceding it is what
refuses the sweep.** Where a named later artefact demonstrably supersedes an entry's subject matter, **the
register has always permitted that closure and did not need close-out to authorise it.** Such an entry would
already be closed. **What a close-out sweep adds is the set of entries where the supersession is arguable**,
and the sweep is proposed precisely because that set is not empty. **A rule whose only work is done on
arguable cases is a rule for deciding arguable cases in one direction, on the last day, by the people with
the most to gain from the direction.**

**A closure taken at close-out cannot be tested, because there is nobody left to test it.** Every previous
closure in this repository was taken at a vantage with further vantages after it — a monthly review, a
quarterly report, a later phase reading the register as an input. **A closure taken on the last day is
reviewed by nobody, ever**, and it is the one class of closure that the register's own machinery of
correction cannot reach.

**The fourth argument describes a real distortion and prescribes the wrong remedy.** It is true that what
stays open over-represents what the programme could not change. **That is not a defect in the register; it
is the finding.** `../09.03-what-the-score-is-shaped-like.md` takes exactly that shape as its subject, and a
sweep that flattened it would have removed the evidence for the phase's own conclusion. **A register that
reads as a worse account than the programme deserves is a register whose reader can check the claim.**

**And the whole repository stands or falls on the reader trusting the status column.** Nine phases have
insisted that a determination is evaluated against the method in force when it was made, that no entry is
edited in place without a record of what it said before, and that closure names the evidence. **A sweep
performed in the final week, by the closing programme, on entries the closing programme raised, would put an
unverifiable class of closures into the one register a successor has no choice but to rely on.** **A
close-out that tidies is a close-out that lies to whoever inherits it.**

---

**Close every open entry with a status of *transferred*, so that the programme's register is complete and
the entries live on in whatever register the receiving function keeps.**

**The administrative option and a very common one.** It respects the boundary between the programme and the
line, it produces a clean end state, and it does not claim that anything was resolved — *transferred* is not
*closed* in substance, only in the column.

**Refused.** ***Transferred* is a closure with a euphemism attached, and the euphemism will not survive.**
The register would read as fully closed to anybody who does not read the status values, which is everybody
working from a summary. **And the receiving register does not exist.** There is no successor programme, no
successor envelope, and no function has undertaken to open a register of anything. **An entry transferred
into a register nobody keeps has been closed, and the word in the column does not change what happened to
it.**

---

**Keep the entries open but stop maintaining the register, and say so — publish it as a final snapshot
rather than as a live instrument, on the ground that a register nobody maintains is misleading whatever its
statuses say.**

**Its point is sound and this decision concedes it in substance.** After the vantage nobody reviews the
register monthly, nobody reports it quarterly, and no entry's status will change because nothing will cause
one to change. **Presenting it as though it were live would be its own kind of falsehood.**

**Refused as an alternative and accepted as a statement.** The register is published as speaking at its
vantage, as every artefact in this repository is, **and what makes an artefact go out of date and who holds
it is `ADR-0062`'s subject** — the RAID log is one of the artefacts that carries a decay mechanism and a
holder. **What this alternative asks for is already provided by a different decision**, and doing it by
declaring the register dead would remove the one thing a successor can act on: a set of dated, owned,
evidenced entries with their statuses intact.

## Consequences

**Accepted — the programme ends with a register in which the great majority of entries are open**, and no
document in this phase apologises for that or explains it away.

**Accepted — the closures this phase does take are few and each is argued at length**, because a closure
taken on the last day carries a burden the register's ordinary machinery cannot discharge afterwards.

**Accepted — the register will be read as an indictment of the programme**, and the phase's answer is that
it is a description of the position rather than an assessment of anybody's work.

**Accepted — no successor register exists and nothing in this decision creates one.**

**Gained — every status in the register means what it has always meant**, so that a later reader can rely on
the column without knowing which phase an entry was raised in.

**Gained — the entries that record what the programme could not do are still there to be read**, which is
the only reason the phase's own findings can be checked.

## Refused

**This decision forbids the following in Phase 09 and binds any later reader of the RAID log.**

- **Closing any entry because the programme has ended**, because its subject matter has been superseded,
  absorbed or made academic, or because nobody will pick it up.
- **Closing any entry without naming the evidence, the date and the decision reference.** ***Believed
  complete* is not a closure condition.**
- **Closing any entry by handing it over** — `ADR-0060` — or reading a destination or an acceptance as a
  change of status.
- **Recording any status other than the register's own** — no *transferred*, no *superseded*, no *carried
  forward*, no *for information*, no *deprecated*, **under any label.**
- **Editing any entry in place without a record of what it said before**, or altering the status of any
  entry raised in an earlier phase.
- **Producing a reduced, filtered, prioritised or summarised register that omits open entries**, or ordering
  the register so that some entries read as more live than others.
- **Re-opening, re-closing or re-characterising any entry closed in an earlier phase** — **`IS-01` closed at
  Phase 08 and stays closed**, and no candidate explanation of it is named here or anywhere.
- **Describing the register as complete, discharged, cleared or handed over.**
