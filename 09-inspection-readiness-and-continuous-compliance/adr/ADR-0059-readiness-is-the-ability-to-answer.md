# ADR-0059 — Inspection readiness is the ability to answer, and this repository never reports it as a compliance position

*Being able to answer a question and having a good answer to it are two different properties, and only one of them is this programme's to report.*

|---|---|
| Status | Accepted |

## Context

**The phase is called inspection readiness, and the word is the most dangerous one in it.**

In February 2026 Helix could not say which of its records were Part 11 records, which systems held them, or
what its control position was. **The first of those was an issue in its own right** — `IS-02` recorded that
*"No written record exists of which Helix records are Part 11 records. No determination has been made, at
any point, by anybody. Every scoping statement in the estate therefore rests on an unrecorded
assumption."* **The programme built the artefacts that answer all three.**

**And that is the whole of what has changed about the answering, which is a much smaller thing than it
sounds and a much larger one.** The artefacts exist, are under document control, carry named determiners and
dates, and can be put in front of somebody. **Many of the answers they give are that the position is not
what it should be.** The estate is in a state the registers describe accurately and unflatteringly, and
nothing about the estate improved merely because it became describable.

**The hazard is a specific one and it has a name.** *Ready* is a word that already exists in the
organisation's vocabulary with a different meaning attached — the meaning it has when somebody asks whether
the site is ready for an inspection, and expects an answer about how the inspection will go. **A phase that
uses the word without fixing which meaning it carries has handed the reader the other one**, and the other
one is a forecast about somebody else's judgement, which this programme has refused since `01.10 §6`.

**The decision is taken by the Head of Quality Assurance**, who owns the quality system and the record
review procedures, and who is the individual most often asked inside Helix whether the company is ready for
anything. **It is taken before the readiness chapter was drafted**, on the same principle every method
decision in this programme has been taken on.

## Decision

**Inspection readiness is the ability to answer a question about one's own estate. It is not the quality of
the answer, and this repository never reports it as a compliance position.**

Four limbs.

**Readiness is a property of the answering.** A firm is able to answer a question when it holds an artefact
that states the position, names who determined it and when, and can be produced. **Whether the position the
artefact states is a good one is a separate question with a separate answer**, and the two are never reported
in the same sentence without the distinction between them.

**A readiness position is never converted into a compliance position, by any route.** Not by summary, not by
implication, not by adjacency, and not by the arrangement of a document so that a reader supplies the
conversion. **No document in this phase states that Helix is compliant with anything, and none states that
Helix is ready** — the second because the word without the distinction is the first with the distinction
removed.

**What may be shown is named, and what may not be shown is named.** A firm may show a determination, a
register, an evidence index and a governance record. **It cannot show a compliant estate it does not have**,
and a firm that presents its registers as though they were a compliance position has told the investigator
something untrue about itself.

**And the phase forecasts nothing.** No document in it states, implies or anticipates what any inspection
would find, what any investigator would think of any artefact, or what any agency would do about anything.

## Alternatives considered

**Report a readiness position as a position — a stated, evidenced, bounded assessment that Helix is now able
to meet an inspection on the electronic-records subject matter, distinguishing carefully between being able
to answer and being compliant, and saying both.**

**The strongest alternative in this phase, and it is strong because it is what the organisation actually
needs and because the distinction it rests on is one this decision itself makes.**

**The first argument for it is that the distinction is available and the programme has demonstrated it can
hold one.** Nine phases have kept apart applicability and integrity, a rating and a validation status, a
requirement and a control, a disposition and a closure, an arrangement and a review. **Readiness and
compliance is one more pair of the same kind**, and a programme that has held five such pairs across
fourteen months is not obviously incapable of holding a sixth. **The alternative does not ask for the
distinction to be dropped; it asks for it to be stated and then for the readiness half to be reported.**

**The second is that the material genuinely supports it.** A firm that can produce a record-level
determination, a systems register, a control position for every paragraph on every system in scope, a
retention derivation, a periodic review arrangement and an evidence index is in a materially different
position from a firm that can produce none of them. **That difference is real, it is evidenced, and it is
the difference the programme was funded to create.** Declining to report it is declining to report the one
thing the programme did.

**The third is that somebody will report it anyway, and less carefully.** The Board audit committee, the
Quality Council and the site will all form a view about readiness. **A repository that refuses to state one
does not prevent the statement; it merely ensures that the statement is made somewhere with no distinction
attached to it.** A carefully bounded position in the repository is the best available control on the
careless one outside it.

**Refused, on three grounds.**

**The word does not carry its bound.** `ADR-0035` established the mechanism in Phase 05 for a landscape fact
and `ADR-0050` in Phase 07 for a number: **a qualifier does not survive a summary, a slide or a report.**
*Ready in the sense of able to answer, and not in the sense of compliant* becomes *ready* by the second
retelling, **and the retelling that matters happens outside this repository, in a room where nobody is
holding the register.** The bound is the part that gets dropped, always, because it is the part that makes
the sentence long. **This is the same mechanism the whole repository has been built around, and there is no
reason to expect the sixth pair to behave differently from the five before it.**

**A readiness position is a claim about how an encounter will go, and the encounter has another party in
it.** Being able to answer is a property of Helix. **Being ready is a relation between Helix and an
investigator**, and the second term of that relation is somebody else's judgement, exercised on a day that
has not happened, about material nobody has yet asked Helix for. **The programme has refused to forecast an
inspection outcome since Phase 01** — not as an objective, not as a success criterion, not as a milestone,
not in a steering committee slide — and a readiness claim is that forecast in the one grammatical form that
does not look like one.

**And the honest version of the claim defeats itself.** Stated with all its bounds, the position is: *Helix
can now produce artefacts that answer three questions it could not answer in February 2026, and many of the
answers say the position is not what it should be.* **That sentence is in this phase, and it is
`../09.05-readiness-is-the-ability-to-answer.md`'s to make.** What the alternative adds to it is the word,
and **the word adds nothing to the sentence except the risk that the sentence is dropped and the word is
kept.**

---

**Say nothing about readiness at all — publish the artefacts, publish the scores, and let the reader draw
whatever conclusion the material supports.**

**A confident option and not a lazy one.** The material is what it is; a chapter explaining at length what
the phase is not claiming can look like a phase that expects to be misread, and professional readers do not
need to be told that a register is not a clearance.

**Refused, on two grounds.** **Silence is not neutral in a phase with this title.** A phase called
inspection readiness that never says what inspection readiness is has left the definition to the reader, in
a context that supplies one — and the one it supplies is the forecast. **And the reader who over-reads is
not the incompetent reader; they are the downstream one.** The board paper, the customer questionnaire and
the induction slide are produced by people acting reasonably on a summary of a summary, **and the
distinction has to exist in the repository in a form somebody can quote back**, which a silence is not.

---

**Adopt a published inspection-readiness maturity model — one of the several that exist commercially — and
report Helix's position on it, so that the position is expressed on a scale a reader outside Helix can
interpret.**

**It has a real merit: an external scale is not Helix's own invention**, and a position expressed on one is
comparable across time and, in principle, across firms. It would also give the Board audit committee the
kind of instrument it is used to receiving.

**Refused.** **The charter forbids it in terms.** *"There is no objective whose measure is a regulator's
view, an auditor's view, a consultant's assessment of maturity, or a score on a scale somebody sells."* **A
maturity position is an opinion with a number attached**, and the number is what travels. It would also
import a scale whose provenance this repository cannot check, into a repository whose rule is that **a
citation that cannot be looked up is not a citation** — and it would put a rating beside registers that
carry no likelihood, no impact and no score, inviting exactly the reading-across the scoring prohibition
exists to prevent.

## Consequences

**Accepted — the organisation ends the programme with no statement of whether it is ready for anything**,
and the people who want one are no closer to holding one. **The phase does not apologise for that.**

**Accepted — the phase will read as more negative than the underlying position warrants.** A phase that
publishes what it can now answer alongside what the answers say invites a reader to weigh the second against
the first, and the second is heavier.

**Accepted — the boundary will be breached outside this repository before it is breached inside it.** What
this decision can do is make the breach checkable, by ensuring that no document here contains the sentence
somebody will attribute to it.

**Gained — every statement in the phase about what Helix can answer is one Helix can evidence**, at a stated
vantage, from a named artefact, without any proposition about anybody else's judgement in it.

**Gained — the distinction is published in a form that can be quoted back**, at the same version as the
material it bounds, which is what a caveat at the foot of a register is not.

## Refused

**This decision forbids the following in Phase 09 and binds every later phase and reader that cites its
registers.**

- **Writing "Helix is ready" or "Helix is compliant"**, or any sentence that means either, in any document,
  register, diagram, governance record, workbook or worked example of this phase.
- **Stating, implying or forecasting that Helix, any system or any record is compliant or non-compliant with
  `21 CFR Part 11`, with Parts 210 and 211, or with anything else** — including in a bounded, qualified or
  paragraph-specific form.
- **Reporting a readiness position as a compliance position**, or reporting the two so that a reader
  supplies the conversion.
- **Describing any system or product as Part 11 certified, FDA-approved, FDA-validated or FDA-certified.**
  **There is no Part 11 certification and no Part 11 certificate.**
- **Forecasting what any future inspection will find**, what any investigator would make of any artefact, or
  what any agency would do — and **anticipating the result of any internal audit or third-party audit.**
- **Presenting a register, an evidence index or a governance record as though it were a compliance
  position**, or offering the completeness of the repository as evidence about the state of the estate.
- **Adopting, citing or reporting a position on any maturity model, readiness scale or rating instrument.**
- **Reaching a legal conclusion of any kind**, or characterising the Ridgemont inspection or any condition
  observed at it as a violation of anything.
