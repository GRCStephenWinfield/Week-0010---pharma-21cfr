## 1. The principle this form rests on

**The rule the form carries** — `ADR-0058`, `DEC-903`, authorised at `../governance/GOV-41-scoring-method-authorisation.md`:

> **An objective is scored from the artefact its measure names, and from nothing else. Not from effort, not
> from progress, not from intent. The score is binary: met, or not met, with nothing between them.**

**And the charter's own negative claim, which the form exists to honour literally** — `01.06 §3`:

> *"No objective in this set is measured by an opinion. There is no objective whose measure is a regulator's
> view, an auditor's view, a consultant's assessment of maturity, or a score on a scale somebody sells. Each
> of the eight is scored from an artefact that either exists or does not."*

**Three things follow, and they govern every field below.**

**The measure is transcribed, not summarised.** The scorer copies the measure as the charter published it
and scores against the transcription. **A measure restated in the scorer's own words is a measure the scorer
has quietly adjusted**, and `01.06 §9` fixes that nobody may vary a measure during the programme.

**The artefact is named before it is read.** The measure names an artefact; the form records which artefact
that is, where it is held and what state the measure requires it to be in. **An artefact identified after
the reading is an artefact chosen to fit the reading.**

**And the score is a score, not a verdict.** It records whether an artefact is in a described state at a
date. **It is not a finding, not a deviation, not an inspectional observation, not a non-compliance, not a
compliance conclusion and not a statement about the individual who owns the objective.**

**The fourth row is what makes the record an assessment rather than an assertion.** A score nobody could
refute is not a score, **and naming the state of the world that would falsify it is what lets somebody
holding the same artefact disagree.**

## 6. No partial score exists — express statement

**This section is completed on every record and none of its rows may be removed.**

| Statement | Confirm |
|---|---|
| **There is no partial score on this record and none is available anywhere in this phase.** The score at `§5` is met or not met and there is nothing between them — `ADR-0058` | `[yes]` |
| **No proportion, fraction, percentage, count of satisfied elements or degree of satisfaction has been recorded, computed or implied**, on this record or in any register drawn from it | `[yes]` |
| **Where the measure covers a population, the score is taken across the whole population** and no subset has been scored separately as a way of producing a partial result | `[yes]` |
| **Nothing has been subtracted from *not met* for the work that went into it, and nothing added to *met* for the ease of it** | `[yes]` |
| **The information a partial score would have carried is recorded at `§7` and does not enter `§5`** | `[yes]` |

**Why the field is here rather than assumed.** **A binary score loses information, and the loss is the
point.** What it buys is that **nobody can argue a score up**: the only question on the record is whether an
artefact is in a described state, and the artefact is named. **A middle value would move the argument one
step back, to what the population is and how much of it counts — an argument conducted at close-out by the
people who own the objectives.** The lost information is recovered at `§7`, beside the score and never
inside it.

**The third and fourth rows exist because *not met* and *not achievable* are different statements**, and a
record that reported only the first would have said something untrue about why a score stands where it
stands. **The fourth row is a statement about the measure and never about the individual**, and no document
in this repository assesses any individual's work.

**And the first row is stated as work and never as a plan.** **An ordering is a prioritisation, and a
prioritisation is a remediation plan in a thin disguise.** **This form states no CAPA, no deviation, no
remediation plan and no remediation cost.**

## 10. Completion rules

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| **One objective, one record** | Two objectives are two records, whatever their subject matter has in common |
| **`§3`, `§4`, `§5`, `§6`, `§7` and `§8` cannot be blank** | Without `§4` the score has nothing behind it; without `§6` a reader supplies a middle value; **without `§7` the record implies that nothing would change it** |
| **Two values and no others** | **No partial, no proportion, no percentage, no band, no adverb and no hedge**, under any label |
| **The measure is transcribed, never paraphrased** | And checked by somebody other than the scorer |
| **The artefact is named before it is read** | **An artefact identified afterwards is an artefact chosen to fit the reading** |
| **A vantage on every score** | **A score with no date has no way of being wrong** |
| **Two named individuals** | Scoring and check at `§5` — never a team, `ADR-0008` |
| **No aggregate** | **No total, index, proportion, average, trend or scorecard is computed from these records**, here or anywhere |
| **Nothing is graded** | **No objective, artefact, owner or outcome is described as adequate, sufficient, robust, effective or mature** anywhere on this form |
| **A blank is a blank** | **A response column completed for the sake of completion is worse than an empty one, because it stops anybody looking** |
| **No inference across records** | A score on one objective is no evidence about another |
| **Change** | A completed record is amended under change control with a `CR-nnn` record naming the trigger, **and the prior state is retained** |
