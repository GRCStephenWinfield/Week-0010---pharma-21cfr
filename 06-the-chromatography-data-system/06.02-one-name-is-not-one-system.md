## 1. What this chapter settles

Helix runs the same chromatography data system product, at the same version, in two quality control
laboratories. In ordinary speech inside the company that arrangement is called **the CDS**, and the phrase
is convenient, accurate about the product, and the single most dangerous two-syllable abbreviation in this
phase.

**This chapter fixes the rule that every determination in Phase 06 is made twice or it is not made**, says
what goes wrong when it is not, and establishes that comparing the two instances is a separate act from
determining each of them — an act Helix had never performed, on registers where the material to perform it
had been published for months.

**It states no result.** Where the two instances turned out to agree and where they turned out to differ is
`06.08-the-two-instances-compared.md`'s, with `logs/instance-comparison-register.md` behind it. **This
chapter names no divergence and reports no comparison.**

## 2. Two instances, and the register has always treated them as two

Phase 02's systems register did not need this chapter's argument in order to get the arrangement right. It
carries two rows.

| | `SYS-023` | `SYS-024` |
|---|---|---|
| Name, as the register carries it | Chromatography data system, QC laboratory | Chromatography data system, QC laboratory |
| Location | **Ridgemont** | **Cary** |
| Owner | **Dr Sunita Raghunathan** | **Dr Sunita Raghunathan** |
| How it was reached | Register | Register |
| Open or closed | **Closed** | **Closed** |
| Record types held | `RT-12`, `RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15`, `RT-17` | `RT-12`, `RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15`, `RT-17` |

**Five record types on each instance is ten record-type/system pairs**, and Phase 03 assessed all ten
separately and carried all ten as unscored, `ND-04` to `ND-08` on `SYS-023` and `ND-09` to `ND-13` on
`SYS-024`. Phase 05 determined eleven paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` on each instance separately. **The
registers have been right about this since Phase 02**, because `ADR-0009` fixed the inventory as the estate
rather than the register and `ADR-0013` fixed the unit of determination as the record type, which Phase 03
then took to the record type on the system that holds it — `06.01 §3` sets the four units side by side.
Both decisions make an instance the thing that carries a determination.

**So this chapter is not correcting the registers. It is naming a rule the registers already follow, so
that the prose in this phase cannot quietly stop following it**, and so that the comparison at `§5` — which
the registers do *not* perform, because a register records rows and does not read across them — has
somewhere to be argued from.

Two things about the table are worth stating plainly, because both will otherwise be assumed away.

**The two rows are identical in every column the register carries, and that establishes nothing about the
two systems.** The systems register records what a system is, where it is, who owns it, how it was found,
what record types it holds and whether access to it is controlled by the persons responsible for the
records on it. **It does not record a single fact about how either instance is configured**, and
configuration is where this phase's subject matter lives. Two rows that match on every published column may
sit on installations that differ on the questions this phase asks — and whether they do is
`06.08-the-two-instances-compared.md`'s to report.

**And one owner over two systems is not one system.** Dr Sunita Raghunathan owns both, which is correct
under `ADR-0008` — the owner is the most senior individual accountable for the operation the system serves,
and both laboratories are hers. **A single owner is an accountability arrangement, not an identity claim.**
It makes the comparison at `§5` easier to commission and it does not make the comparison unnecessary.

## 3. The failure mode: how an estate acquires a determination nobody made

**This is the sentence the chapter exists for.**

**"The CDS" is how an estate acquires a determination nobody made.**

The mechanism is mundane and it does not require anybody to be careless. It has four steps and each step is
locally reasonable.

**Somebody establishes a fact at one site.** An analyst demonstrates a behaviour at Ridgemont, or a
configuration is read at Cary. The fact is true, established properly, and recorded.

**Somebody writes it up using the product's name.** *The CDS records this.* Not *the Ridgemont instance
records this* — the product's name, because the product is what the person was thinking about and because
the sentence with the product's name in it is shorter and reads better.

**Somebody else reads the write-up and applies it to their site.** They are entitled to: the sentence
names the product, their site runs the product, and nothing in the sentence suggested a boundary.

**And the estate now holds a determination about two systems, of which one was assessed.** No individual
made a false statement. Every sentence in the chain was true when it was written. **The report is not false
about either site individually while being false about the estate**, which is the property that makes this
failure mode so difficult to catch by review: there is no sentence to strike out.

**What has actually happened is that a determination has been created out of a determination and a
grammatical convenience.**

Three features of the failure mode make it worse than an ordinary error.

**It is invisible to the usual checks.** A reviewer verifying the write-up against the evidence finds the
evidence, and the evidence supports the sentence. What the reviewer cannot see is the sentence's scope,
because scope is carried by the subject noun and the subject noun is the product's name.

**It survives being questioned.** Ask *is this true?* and the answer is yes. The question that would break
it is *which installation was this established on, and who established the other one?* — and that question
is only asked by somebody who already holds the rule this chapter is fixing.

**And it propagates in the direction of reassurance.** A fact established at the better-configured
instance, generalised to both, produces an estate-wide position more favourable than the evidence supports.
The reverse propagation — a fact from the weaker instance generalised to both — is equally possible and, in
practice, far rarer, because a finding at one site is normally chased at the other and a clean result at
one site is normally filed.

**Nothing about that is specific to chromatography, to laboratories, or to Helix.** Any estate that runs one
product in two places is exposed to it, and an estate that runs one product in twelve places is exposed to
it twelve-fold. The reason it is fixed here and not earlier is that this is the first phase whose subject
matter is configuration, and configuration is the first thing in the programme that can differ between two
installations of one product **while every register column about them stays identical.**

## 4. The rule: made twice, or not made

**`ADR-0037`, taken at `DEC-603` on 2027-02-05 by Dr Sunita Raghunathan, fixes that `SYS-023` and `SYS-024`
are assessed separately at every point in this phase, and that no determination is ever recorded against
"the chromatography data system", "the CDS", "both instances" or any other subject that is not one named
system.**

The rule applies to everything this phase produces, and the table below is the working form of it.

| Object | How it is determined | What is forbidden |
|---|---|---|
| **The auditable event classes the product offers** | Read once as a property of the product, and recorded as such | Nothing — this is the one object that is genuinely a property of the product rather than of an installation |
| **Whether a class is enabled** | **Determined separately on each instance** and recorded in two columns | A single "enabled" column, a "both" value, or any single figure for the estate |
| **Whether a class can be enabled at all** | A property of the product version, recorded once and applied to both **because both run that version** | Asserting it of an installation without stating that it is a product-version property |
| **Which acts a person can perform** | **Determined separately for each laboratory**, against the permissions that laboratory's people actually hold | Reading one laboratory's permission set and calling it the laboratory permission model |
| **The released results** | **Counted, examined and reported separately per instance**, and reconciled to the total | A pooled population reported without its per-instance split |
| **A reprocessed result** | **Attributed to the instance it was produced on**, always | An unattributed row |
| **The Phase 05 control positions** | Already determined separately, and **inherited unchanged** | Re-determining either row, or merging them |
| **The Phase 03 pairs** | Already determined separately, and **inherited unchanged** | Re-scoring either set, or treating one instance's determination as the other's |

**The rule has a cost and the cost is real.** Every demonstration is performed twice, every configuration
read is performed twice, and the registers carry two columns where one would have been easier to read.
**Roughly half the effort of this phase exists because of `ADR-0037`, and that is the correct price** for
the property it buys: **a reader of any row in any Phase 06 register can tell which installation it is
about, without reading anything else.**

**And the rule cuts both ways, which is the part most easily lost.** It forbids reporting a Ridgemont fact
as an estate fact. **It equally forbids reporting a Cary fact as an estate fact**, including where the Cary
fact is the less favourable of the two. A phase that applied the rule only in the direction that made its
findings larger would have found a new way to be inaccurate.

## 5. The comparison is a separate act, and Helix had never performed it

**Determining a thing twice is not comparing it.**

That sentence looks like a quibble and it is the operative finding of this chapter. Two determinations
produce two rows. **A comparison is a third act, performed on the two rows by somebody looking for
difference, and it produces something neither row contains.**

Phase 03 determined five attributes for each of ten record-type/system pairs and published all ten. Phase
05 determined eleven paragraphs on each instance and published all twenty-two positions. **Both registers
were approved, both were ratified through governance, and both are correct.** In neither case did anybody
read across the row.

**The reason is structural rather than personal, and it is worth stating exactly, because otherwise it
reads as somebody's oversight.**

**A register is organised by row.** Its rows are its unit, its reviewer checks a row against its evidence,
and its approver approves a set of rows. **Nothing in the act of producing or approving a register asks
anybody to hold two of its rows side by side and ask why they differ.** A row that differs from another row
is not an exception, an error or a flag; it is a row. The register does exactly what a register is for and
the comparison falls outside it.

**And a comparison has no natural owner.** The determination has one — whoever made it. The register has
one. **The question "are these two rows the same, and should they be?" belongs to nobody by default**,
which is `01.07`'s ownership rule showing what happens where it has not been applied to an act. **A
comparison nobody owns is a comparison nobody performs**, and its absence produces no artefact, no gap and
no alert.

**So the position at the start of this phase was that Helix had made every determination and had never made
the comparison.** The material was published. The two registers were in the repository, controlled, with
named owners. **What was missing was an act, and the act was one nobody had been asked to perform.**

This phase performs it, as a distinct act, with its own method, its own record and its own register —
`templates/instance-comparison-record-template.md` is the instrument and `logs/instance-comparison-register.md`
is the output. **What it found is `06.08-the-two-instances-compared.md`'s and appears nowhere in this
chapter.**

## 6. What a difference between two instances is a finding about

**`ADR-0042`, taken at `DEC-615` on 2027-02-25 by Amara Sissoko, records that a difference between two
instances of one product is a finding about the estate, not about either instance.**

The decision answers a question that only arises once a comparison has actually been performed, and it
arises immediately: **when two installations of one product differ, which one is wrong?**

**Usually neither, and the question is the wrong question.**

A difference between two instances can arise in at least four ways, and only one of them involves anything
being wrong with an installation.

| How a difference arises | What it is a finding about |
|---|---|
| **Two configuration choices were made, separately, by different people at different times** | **The estate** — specifically, that the choice was available, was unmade centrally, and was therefore made twice with no requirement that the two agree |
| **A procedure at one site requires something no procedure at the other requires** | **The estate's procedural architecture**, and it is Phase 05's *Met but not required* determination seen across two installations rather than within one |
| **One installation was changed and the other was not** | **The change control arrangement**, not the installation that was or was not changed |
| **An assessment reached a different conclusion at each site on the same question** | **The method or its application** — and this is the only case where a difference indicates an error, which is why it is investigated before it is published |

**In three of the four the correct subject of the finding is the estate**, and the reason is the same in
each: the difference exists because nothing required the two to be the same, and **"nothing required them
to be the same" is a fact about how Helix governs its systems, not about how either laboratory runs its
instrument.**

**The practical consequence is a rule about wording**, and `ADR-0042` states it: a divergence is recorded as
*the two instances differ, in this register, on this question, and nothing required them to agree*. **It is
not recorded as a deficiency at the site whose value looks worse**, and no site is named as the correct one
unless a requirement exists that makes it correct.

**And the corollary is the harder half.** A difference that is a finding about the estate is a finding
about the estate *whichever direction it points*, including where the divergent instance carries the more
protective arrangement. **A control that is present at one site and absent at the other, with no procedure
requiring it at either, is not a good news story at the site that has it** — it is Phase 05's `ADR-0031`
argument arriving from a new direction, because a control nothing requires can be withdrawn tomorrow
without a deviation, and a control that exists at one site and not at the other has already demonstrated
that nothing requires it.

## 7. Why this rule is fixed in this phase and not in an earlier one

A fair reader will ask why a rule this general is being fixed in the sixth phase, and the answer is not that
nobody thought of it.

**Phase 02 fixed the inventory as the estate and gave every system its own row.** Phase 03 fixed the unit
of determination as the record type on the system that holds it, expressly because *the same record type is
not at the same risk on every system that holds it* — which is this chapter's rule stated about risk.
Phase 05 fixed the unit of a control position as a paragraph on a system, expressly because *a determination
made at either of the coarser units is a determination that averages a population it has not looked at*.
**Three phases have already refused to collapse a population into its name.**

What is new here is the object. **Phases 02, 03 and 05 all took determinations that a register could carry
in a row, and the rule that protected them was a rule about the unit of a row.** This phase's subject
matter is configuration and act — things that differ between installations of one product **while every
register column stays identical** — and against that, a rule about the unit of a row is necessary and not
sufficient. **What is also needed is a rule about the subject of a sentence**, which is what `ADR-0037`
adds, and a rule about who compares the rows, which is what `§5` and `ADR-0042` add.

**And the phrase this chapter is named for could not have done any harm before now.** "The CDS" in a
scoping document is shorthand for a product; "the CDS" in a sentence about what a system records is a
determination about two installations. **The phrase became dangerous when the subject matter changed, and
that is this phase.**

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no comparison and no divergence.** Where the two instances agree and where they differ is
  `06.08-the-two-instances-compared.md`'s, on `logs/instance-comparison-register.md`. **No register named
  here has a count restated in this chapter.**
- **It states no configuration position.** What either instance's audit trail records is
  `06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s.
- **It re-determines nothing.** The ten pairs `ND-04` to `ND-13` stay unscored and the twenty-two Phase 05
  control positions stand as published. **Phase 06 evidences `CG-15` and `CG-05` and changes neither.**
- **It states no validation outcome**, raises no CAPA and no deviation, and states no remediation plan, cost,
  sequence or priority.
- **It characterises no reintegration** — `ADR-0043`.
- **It disposes of nothing — not `OBS-2` and not `OBS-4`.** `06.11-obs-2-stands.md`, and Phase 08.
- **It states nothing about `SYS-036`, `SYS-060` or `SYS-061`** beyond what Phase 02's register publishes,
  and no retention, backup or archive rule — Phase 07.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, assesses no individual's work, and forecasts no inspection outcome.
- **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted.
