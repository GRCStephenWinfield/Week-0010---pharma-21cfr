*At Cary the reportable result reaches the LIMS because somebody types it, and `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s second sentence is a shall.*

---

## 1. What this chapter reports

**How the reportable result leaves each system, and the provision that reaches the gap between them.**

**On `SYS-023` at Ridgemont the reportable result is transferred to the LIMS electronically.** The value
computed on the chromatography data system is passed to the laboratory information management system
without a person retyping it.

**On `SYS-024` at Cary the reportable result reaches the LIMS by manual transcription.** An analyst reads
the value from one screen and enters it into another.

**That is the fourth of the four disagreements in `logs/instance-comparison-register.md`**, and it is the
second of the two this phase found rather than read. The transfer path per instance is described at
`EV-622`, supplied under `DP-25` from the same source that supplied Phase 05's interface statements.

**The two instances are the same product at the same version** — `ADR-0037`,
`06.02-one-name-is-not-one-system.md` — and the difference is in what Helix built around each of them
rather than in either product.

## 2. The provision

**`21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s second sentence, quoted exactly:**

> *"Input to and output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or data shall be
> checked for accuracy."*

**It is a *shall*.** It is not guidance, it is not a recommendation and it is not a good practice adopted
voluntarily. It sits in the drug CGMP regulations, it applies to Helix, and **it survives enforcement
discretion entirely** — discretion is FDA's stated intention about what it will enforce under Part 11, and
`21 CFR 211.68` is not Part 11.

**Five obligations sit in that paragraph** and the second is the one this chapter is about: appropriate
controls so that changes in master production and control records or other records are instituted only by
authorised personnel · **accuracy checking of input and output** · maintenance of a backup file, except
where certain data — *"such as calculations performed in connection with laboratory analysis"* — are
eliminated by computerization, in which case a written record of the program with appropriate validation
data · and **hard copy or alternative systems designed to assure that backup data are exact and complete
and secure from alteration, inadvertent erasures or loss.** **The backup and integrity limbs are Phase
07's and this chapter states nothing about them.**

**The paragraph's third sentence, quoted exactly:**

> *"The degree and frequency of input/output verification shall be based on the complexity and reliability
> of the computer or related system."*

**Read what it graduates.** It graduates **the degree and the frequency of the verification**. It does not
graduate whether verification is owed, it does not permit verification to be omitted for a simple system,
and it does not make the second sentence conditional on anything. **The obligation is fixed; its intensity
is graduated, and that is the only thing the third sentence authorises.**

**That distinction is worth holding because it is the one this industry most often loses.** A sentence
beginning *risk-based* attaches correctly to the degree and frequency and incorrectly to the existence of
the check. **`PR-09` carries the risk that a graduation is read as permission the second sentence does not
give**, and `PR-49` carries its form on this chapter. Phase 04's complexity-and-reliability determination
graded the estate on the axis this sentence names, and `AS-27` records what that grading rests on; **this is
the first place in the programme where the axis has been asked to do work.**

## 3. Where Phase 03's `IS-18` lands

**`IS-18`, raised by Joachim Reuter on 2026-09-22 and argued at
`../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/03.11-the-flow-the-unit-does-not-see.md §2`:**
*the transfer between systems is assessed nowhere, for 31 of the 34 in-scope record types.* **It has been
carried open through Phase 04 and Phase 05 without a provision attached to it.**

Phase 05 recorded that `21 CFR 11.10(h)` device checks is Not met on five positions and Met but not required
on forty-six, **which is a statement about a check at an interface and not about a transfer having been
assessed.** The entry stayed open with nowhere in particular to go.

**It lands here.** **The provision `IS-18` lands on is `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s second sentence**, and the
transcription break at Cary is the plainest instance of it in the estate: a reportable result crosses from
one computerised system to another through a person, and **no verification of the input or the output of
either system has been identified.** The enquiry is retained at `EV-623` — **the enquiry and not only its
answer**, so that *none identified* can be tested as a search rather than taken as a conclusion.

**Landing is not closing.** `IS-18` stays open, and `IS-39` is raised alongside it rather than in place of
it:

> **On `SYS-024` the reportable result reaches the LIMS by manual transcription, and Phase 03's `IS-18`
> lands on `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s second sentence.**

**`OB-36` and `SOP-DI-04` remain undischarged and unapplied**, and nothing in this chapter discharges
either.

## 4. What the difference between the two instances does and does not mean

**The electronic transfer at Ridgemont is not thereby verified.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s second sentence
reaches input to and output from the computer or related system, and an electronic transfer is input to one
system and output from another. **It is not exempt because no person typed anything**, and no verification
of it has been identified either. **The obligation reaches both instances and neither carries an
identified check.**

**What differs is the degree and frequency the third sentence would graduate**, and that is a question
nobody at Helix has asked in either laboratory.

**The transcription at Cary is not described here as an error, a weakness, a deviation or a practice
requiring correction.** Manual transcription between systems is ordinary in analytical laboratories, it is
performed by trained analysts under Helix's own procedures, and **no document in this repository assesses
any individual's work.** **What is recorded is that a reportable result crosses a boundary and that the
check the regulation requires across that boundary has not been identified.** `PR-49` carries the risk that
this chapter is read as a finding about Cary's practice.

**And nothing here says the transcribed values are wrong.** No value in the 142 examined at
`06.07-reintegration.md §2` was found to have been mistranscribed, because **no transcription check was
performed by this phase and none is stated.** The absence of a check is not evidence of an error, and this
chapter does not convert one into the other.

## 5. What this chapter does not establish

**It states no CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no remediation cost.** **No verification
arrangement is specified, no frequency is set, no degree is graded, and no procedure is drafted or
proposed.** Those follow from the quality system and not from an examination, and Phase 06 states none of
them.

**It states no retention, backup or archive rule.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s backup and integrity limbs are
Phase 07's and are untouched here.

**It re-determines no control position.** `21 CFR 11.10(h)` stands as published — *Met but not required* on
both systems — and **a device check under Part 11 is not the input/output verification `21 CFR 211.68(b)`
requires.** They are different provisions with different objects and neither is read onto the other.

**It states no validation outcome.** **`21 CFR 211.68` is the surviving predicate-rule hook for
computerised systems and it survives enforcement discretion entirely** — `ADR-0003`. `21 CFR 211.68(a)`
requires that equipment used be *"routinely calibrated, inspected, or checked according to a written program
designed to assure proper performance"*, and `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires *"appropriate validation data"*
where laboratory calculations are eliminated by computerization. **It is not a general system-validation
clause and is not cited as one here**; the validation framework and the evidence it requires are Phase 04's,
and that evidence is owed in full on both systems — `IS-34`. Nothing here produces any of it.

**It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises no past practice as a violation of anything, and
**forecasts no inspection outcome.** **It draws no conclusion about `OBS-2` and none about `OBS-4`** —
Phase 08. **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted.
