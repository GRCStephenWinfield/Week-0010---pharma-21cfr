---

## 1. What this chapter settles

**Nothing.** That is its point, and it is worth stating before the first table rather than after the last
one.

Every fact in this chapter has been in a published, controlled register for between one and seven months.
Phase 02 published the two systems, their owner, their record types and their closed determination on
2026-07-31. Phase 03 published ten attribute determinations across the two of them on 2026-09-30. Phase 05
published twenty-two control positions and named both systems in two control gaps on 2027-01-29. **Not one
of those facts is new, not one is restated here differently from the register that owns it, and not one is
re-determined, re-scored or re-banded by anything in this chapter or in this phase.**

**What is new is the arrangement.** Four registers, four vantages, four owners, and in each of them the
two systems appear as two rows with other rows between them. **This is the first document in the
programme that puts what the four say about `SYS-023` and `SYS-024` on one page.** The next five chapters
are about what a depth assessment found; this one is about what was already there to be read.

**The two are assessed separately throughout** — `ADR-0037`, argued at
`06.02-one-name-is-not-one-system.md`. The rule is cited and not rebuilt.

**Both are owned by a named individual and neither is owned by a function** — `ADR-0008`. **Dr Sunita
Raghunathan holds the laboratory instrument estate because she is the most senior individual accountable
for the operation the systems serve**, and not because the systems sit inside the function her title names.

**Both are closed systems because Helix's own people control access to them**, which is the whole of the
test at `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`. Neither determination turns on hosting, on a network boundary or on where a
server sits.

**Note the row that agrees and should have prompted a question.** The name is identical. **Two systems
with the same name, the same owner, the same record types and the same closed determination look, in a
register, like one thing seen twice** — and that is the reading `06.02` exists to refuse.

## 3. The five record types, and the three that had to be argued into scope

**Source: `../02-the-system-inventory-and-part-11-applicability/logs/record-type-register.md`, unaltered.**

| Record type | What it is | Predicate rule | Scope position |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RT-12` | Chromatographic raw data file and acquisition metadata | `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` | Part 11 record |
| **`RT-13`** | **Chromatographic integration and reprocessing record** | **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` and `(a)(5)`** | **Part 11 record — moved into scope** |
| `RT-14` | Chromatographic detector spectral and peak-purity record | `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` | **Part 11 record — moved into scope** |
| `RT-15` | Chromatographic calibration curve and result recalculation record | `21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` | **Part 11 record — moved into scope** |
| `RT-17` | System suitability test record | `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)`, read with `21 CFR 211.194(a)(2)` and `21 CFR 211.165(e)` | Part 11 record |

**Three of the five moved into scope in Phase 02, and all three moved for the same reason: they failed
the printout route's first condition.** The route has three conditions — two FDA states expressly and a
third Helix derives from the narrow interpretation's second category — and the first is that the paper
meets all the applicable predicate-rule requirements. **It does not, because the record is dynamic and a
static printout cannot reproduce it.** `../02-the-system-inventory-and-part-11-applicability/02.06-the-paper-that-was-not-the-record.md` owns that argument and
`ADR-0010` is the decision; neither is restated here.

**`RT-13` is the one to read twice.** Its predicate rule is **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` and `(a)(5)`** — a
complete record of all data secured in the course of each test, including all graphs, charts and spectra
from laboratory instrumentation, **properly identified to show the specific component, drug product
container, closure, in-process material or drug product, and lot tested** — and a record of all
calculations performed in connection with the test, including units of measure, conversion factors and
equivalency factors. **The integration is the
calculation.** A chromatogram is a set of detector responses over time, and the number reported out of the
laboratory is what a set of integration parameters makes of that set. Change the parameters and the same
detector responses produce a different number. **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires a record of that
calculation, and `RT-13` is what Helix calls the record of it.**

**Four pairs carry identical determinations on the two instances and one does not.**
**`ND-04`≡`ND-09`, `ND-05`≡`ND-10`, `ND-06`≡`ND-11`, `ND-07`≡`ND-12`.** **On `RT-17`, *original or a
true copy* is Not determinable at Ridgemont and Not met at Cary.** That divergence was published on
2026-09-30 and read across for the first time in this phase;
`06.08-the-two-instances-compared.md` owns what it means and `logs/instance-comparison-register.md` records
it as a comparison.

**All ten remain unscored and none becomes determinable here.** **Not determinable is a determination and
is not scored** — `ADR-0018` — and an `ND` row that becomes determinable is re-assessed on the trigger at
`ADR-0022` and enters the scored register as a new entry with a new identifier and a date. **Nothing in
Phase 06 fires that trigger.** Ten pairs were examined at depth in this phase and not one of them acquired
a rating, which is the outcome `PR-26` was written to make visible rather than the outcome anybody wanted.

**`RT-13` is the worst of the ten, on both instances.** **All five attributes are Not determinable on
`ND-05` and all five are Not determinable on `ND-10`.** Read that against §3: `RT-13` is the record of the
integration and the reprocessing; `21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires a record of the calculation; and the
calculation is what turns detector responses into the number that leaves the laboratory.

**The record that decides the reportable result is the record about which Helix could establish nothing.**

Phase 03's risk register states what each pair would need. For `ND-05` and `ND-10` the list runs to five
items because there are five undetermined attributes: an account administration record showing which named
individual held the credential in use at the time of each entry; a demonstration that the record can be
rendered from the store that holds it in complete and readable form, with the context needed to understand
it; a record of when the entry was captured as against when the act it records was performed; a statement
of which instance Helix relies on as the original and a demonstrated method by which a copy is shown to be
a true one; and a comparison of the record as held against the event it records or against an independent
instance of the same content. **Not one of the five has been produced, and this phase produced none of
them.**

## 6. The two control gaps that name both systems

**Source: `../05-access-audit-trails-and-electronic-signatures/logs/control-gap-register.md`, unaltered.
A control gap is a paragraph, a set of systems and one cause. It is not a deviation, not a CAPA, not a
finding and not an inspectional observation.**

| Gap | Paragraph | Systems | The cause, as published | Closure condition, as published |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`CG-05`** | `21 CFR 11.10(b)` | 7, including `SYS-023` and `SYS-024` | The only export the product offers is a static rendering that carries neither the processing context nor the audit trail, so what is produced is not a complete copy of the record | An export that carries the record with the context needed to understand it, demonstrated once per record type |
| **`CG-15`** | `21 CFR 11.10(e)` | 6, including `SYS-023` and `SYS-024` | A change to a stored value replaces the previous value in the record the reviewer sees, so record changes obscure previously recorded information | Retention of the previous value in a form the reviewer sees |

**Both gaps sit wholly inside the four paragraphs enforcement discretion reaches in full, and that changes
nothing about how either is stated.** Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention about what it will
enforce; **it is not an exemption, it is not a safe harbour, it confers no rights, and `21 CFR 211.68` and
`21 CFR 211.100(b)` survive it entirely** — `ADR-0035`, `ADR-0003`. The two gaps are described in this
phase in exactly the words the gap register uses.

**The set is wider than those four paragraphs and the phase states it in full once, here.** Discretion
reaches `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)` in full and `21 CFR 11.10(k)` in part — the August 2003
guidance names `(k)(2)` and says nothing about `(k)(1)` — together with the corresponding requirements in
`21 CFR 11.30`, and separately all Part 11 requirements for systems that were operational before
20 August 1997 and meet the guidance's stated conditions. **No Helix system has been assessed against those
legacy conditions**, and neither gap above turns on any of it.

**Phase 06 evidences both and re-determines neither.** The thirty-eight rows in
`logs/reintegration-register.md` are what `CG-15`'s cause looks like in a laboratory, and `CG-05` is why
the export cannot carry both values either. **Neither gap is closed, re-scoped, re-owned or re-caused by
anything in this phase, and neither has moved since 2027-01-21.**

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

**It re-scores nothing, re-determines nothing and re-bands nothing.** The ten pairs stay unscored. The
twenty-two control positions stand as published. `RT-13`'s scope position and predicate rule are Phase
02's and are cited, not re-derived.

**It draws no conclusion about `OBS-2`.** The observation's subject matter is audit trail review in the QC
laboratory and these are the two systems it concerns; **the link is recorded and nothing is inferred from
it here.** `06.11-obs-2-stands.md` owns the boundary and Phase 08 owns the disposition. **The `OBS-4` link
is not engaged by this chapter at all.**

**It states no retention, backup or archive rule**, and it says nothing about `SYS-036`, `SYS-060` or
`SYS-061` beyond what `06.10-what-else-holds-these-records.md` reads off Phase 02's register.

**It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.** **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**,
which stays open and stays unweighted.
