---

## 1. The boundary, before the finding

**This chapter contains the most easily misused sentence in the programme, so the boundary is stated first
and is not deferred to a closing section.**

**`OBS-2` stands exactly as written.** Laboratory records generated on the chromatography data system were
reviewed and approved without the associated audit trail forming part of that review. That was true, it
remains true, and **nothing in this chapter softens it, qualifies it, mitigates it, reduces its scope or
diminishes it in any degree.** **Phase 06 records what `OBS-2`'s subject matter is and disposes of
nothing** — `ADR-0041`, `DEC-613`, taken by Terrence Abbatiello on 2027-02-23. **The disposition is Phase
08's**, and `06.11-obs-2-stands.md` owns the boundary in full.

**A failure to review is not converted into a product limitation by the discovery of a product
limitation.** Those are two facts about the same estate, and they compound. **What Phase 06 adds is that
beginning to review the audit trail would not, by itself, have reached the act that changes the reportable
result — which makes the remediation larger, not smaller.**

**The available misreading is specific and is named so that it can be refused:** *the audit trail would not
have shown it anyway, so the review would not have helped.* **That sentence is refused.** It is refused
on the facts — the audit trail would have shown eight result-changing acts at Ridgemont and six at Cary,
which is not nothing — and it is refused on the logic, because **the review obligation is not conditional
on what a review would have found.** `PR-43` carries the risk that somebody writes it anyway.

## 2. The inversion

**Of the thirty-four auditable event classes the product offers, eleven record an act capable of changing a
reportable result. Three of the eleven — `AE-08`, `AE-15` and `AE-16` — are recorded on neither instance,
and two of those three cannot be enabled at all on this product version.**

**One of the two is the class that records a change to integration parameters applied to a single injection
at review time.**

FDA has named part of that content in terms, for this instrument class. The December 2018 drug CGMP data
integrity Q&A states that *"the audit trail for a high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) run should
include the user name, date/time of the run, the integration parameters used, and details of a
reprocessing, if any."* It is a nonbinding *should* in a guidance rather than a requirement, and it is not a
complete list of acts — `06.04 §7.3`. **The consequence is that what follows is not reported against
Helix's own enumeration alone:** the integration parameters used are named by FDA, and the class that would
record a change to them applied to a single injection at review time is recorded on neither instance and
cannot be enabled on this product version.

**Reviewing the audit trail would not have found the act that changes the reportable result.**

That is the whole of it, and it inverts the shape of the observation. `OBS-2` describes a review that did
not happen. The natural remediation is that the review should happen. **The natural remediation, performed
faithfully and at full cost, would have left the act that sits closest to the reported number unrecorded
and therefore unreviewable.** A reviewer opening the audit trail on either instance, on any day in the
window this phase examined, would have found no entry for it — not because nobody looked, but because there
was nothing to look at.

**The counts are computed at `logs/auditable-event-register.md §4` and are stated at
`06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md §3` and `§4`.** This chapter takes them as given and reads them.

## 3. What follows, and what does not

**Three things follow.**

**First, the remediation is now known to have two parts where everybody had assumed one.** Beginning to
review is one part. **Making the acts recordable is the other, and until it is done the review reaches a
record that is silent on three of the eleven acts that matter most.** Neither part substitutes for the
other and neither is smaller for the existence of the other.

**Second, the two parts have different owners, different routes and different timescales.** Four of the six
classes enabled on neither were choices and close on a configuration change and a sentence requiring it —
`DP-35`, Terrence Abbatiello. **Two cannot be enabled on this product version and close on a vendor
product route and a compensating control** — `DP-33`, Joachim Reuter. `06.05 §5` sets out why the two are
as serious in kind as each other and different in remedy. **`AS-17` — vendors supply technical
documentation sufficient to describe the seven newly in-scope record types — closed in Phase 03 as tested
and failed, on part of this estate.** That is the precedent `DP-33` works against and it is recorded here
rather than discovered later.

**Third, the review procedure question has become sharper without being answered.** `IS-06` records that
Helix's laboratory record review procedure does not state whether the audit trail associated with a result
forms part of the original record reviewed under `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` — *"[t]he initials or signature of
a second person showing that the original records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and
compliance with established standards."* **FDA names that provision expressly**, among the eight it lists
in the answer directing that personnel responsible for record review under CGMP review the audit trails as
they review the rest of the record — `06.04 §2.3`. The construction is therefore FDA's own rather than
Helix's inference, which sharpens `IS-06` rather than answering it: what stays unstated is whether Helix's
own procedure adopts it. **This chapter establishes what the audit trail would and would not have shown a
reviewer. It does not say what a reviewer must do with one**, and `IS-06` stays open, unanswered, and not
answered by implication.

**Four things do not follow.**

**`OBS-2` is not reduced.** It is not partly answered, not substantially addressed, not mitigated and not
extended. **No disposition is drawn in either direction**, because drawing one in the aggravating direction
is the same trespass on Phase 08 with the sign reversed.

**The failure to review is not excused.** The review did not happen, and the reason it did not happen has
nothing to do with `AE-08`; nobody declined to review the audit trail on the ground that it would be silent
about integration parameters at review time. **A finding about what a record contains is not evidence about
why nobody opened it.**

**Nothing here bears on `IS-01`.** Four internal audits between 2023-06 and 2025-09 covered these systems
and raised no finding on `OBS-2`'s subject matter, and four candidate explanations are recorded at
`01.09 §6` with none chosen. **That `AE-08` cannot be enabled today is not a statement about what any audit
could have seen in 2023, 2024 or 2025.** This phase reconstructed no system's configuration at any date
before 2027-02-10. **No document in this phase may be cited in support of any of the four candidates**, and
`EV-630` records the position at the `MS-10` gate. `IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted.

**And this is not an estate finding.** **Two systems of sixty-one were assessed at a depth not available
for the other fifty-nine** — `IS-41`, `ADR-0040`. Whether any other system in the estate offers a class it
cannot enable is unknown, and nothing here may be read as a statement that it does or that it does not.

## 4. `IS-36`, and what would close it

**`IS-36`, raised 2027-02-12, owned by Priyanka Venkataraman, open on both limbs:**

> **Three of the eleven event classes recording an act capable of changing a reportable result are recorded
> on neither instance, and two of the three cannot be enabled on this product version.**

| Limb | Position at the vantage |
|---|---|
| **The four that were choices** | **Open.** `DP-35` is the route |
| **The two that cannot be enabled** | **Open, and nothing is agreed.** `DP-33` is the route |

**What would close each limb, why the two are as serious as each other and different in remedy, and why
enabling the four without writing the sentence would close nothing, are
`06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md §5`'s and are not restated here.** What this chapter adds is that
**both limbs bear on the review obligation and neither substitutes for it.**

## 5. The sentence to carry forward

**A review that had been performed exactly as `OBS-2` says it should have been would have looked at a
record that is silent about three of the eleven acts capable of changing the number it was reviewing — and
on two of the three, no configuration Helix could have set would have made it otherwise.**

That sentence is the phase's contribution to `OBS-2`'s subject matter. **It is not a disposition, it is not
a mitigation, and it is not an argument about anybody's conduct.** It is a fact about a record, established
by looking, and handed to Phase 08 to do something with.

## 6. What this chapter does not establish

**No disposition of `OBS-2` and no disposition of `OBS-4`** — Phase 08. **No CAPA, no deviation, no
remediation plan and no remediation cost.** **No validation outcome** — the evidence Phase 04 requires is
owed in full, `IS-34`. **No audit trail review frequency**; **no primary source names a stand-alone
periodic audit trail review frequency**, this chapter names none, and `IS-04` stays deliberately
unresolved. **No re-scoring of Phase 03, no re-banding of Phase 04, no re-determination of Phase 05** — the
ten pairs stay unscored and `(e)` stays Not met on both rows. **No retention, backup or archive rule** —
Phase 07. **No characterisation of any reintegration** — `ADR-0043`, and `06.07-reintegration.md` is where
that refusal is argued. **No legal conclusion and no forecast of what any inspection will find.**
