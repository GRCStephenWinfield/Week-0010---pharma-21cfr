## 1. What this chapter reports

**The result of the configuration read, and nothing else.** The method is `06.04-the-acts-that-change-a-result.md`'s
and is cited, not rebuilt: **the audit trail is assessed against the acts that change a reportable result,
not against the vendor's event list** — `ADR-0038`. The two-instance rule is
`06.02-one-name-is-not-one-system.md`'s: **every determination is made twice or it is not made** —
`ADR-0037`.

**Every figure below is computed in `logs/auditable-event-register.md §4` from the thirty-four rows in its
`§3` and from nothing else.** This chapter states the figures and reads them. It does not hold a second
copy of the arithmetic, and a reader who wants to check a number should count the register's rows rather
than trust this chapter's tables.

**What the read was.** Both configurations were taken **from the systems and not from a description of
them**, on 2027-02-10, at `EV-608` and `EV-609`. That is worth saying because `AS-22` and `AS-40` — the
assumption that those who supply facts describe the position as it is rather than as procedure says it
should be — is the widest assumption in this repository, and this is the one place in six phases where it
has been narrowed rather than widened; `AS-45` is its Phase 06 form and carries what remains of it. **What
the narrowing costs is `AS-41`:** the configuration read on 2027-02-10 is the configuration at the vantage,
and neither instance records a change to its own audit trail configuration.

## 2. Thirty-four classes, and where they sit

**The product version in use offers thirty-four auditable event classes.** They are `AE-01` to `AE-34`,
allocated in `logs/auditable-event-register.md` and nowhere else.

| Group | Count |
|---|---|
| Enabled on both instances | **17** |
| Enabled on `SYS-023` only | **9** |
| Enabled on `SYS-024` only | **2** |
| Enabled on neither | **6** |
| **Total classes offered** | **34** |

**17 + 9 + 2 + 6 = 34.**

**Ridgemont has 26 classes enabled and Cary has 19.** **17 + 9 = 26** and **17 + 2 = 19**. Those two
figures are stated separately and are never added, averaged, or reported as a single figure for "the CDS"
— `PR-48`, and the register carries two configuration columns and no third.

**Eleven of the thirty-four are enabled on one instance and not on the other — nine on Ridgemont only and
two on Cary only. 9 + 2 = 11.** That is the third of the four disagreements in
`logs/instance-comparison-register.md` and `06.08-the-two-instances-compared.md` owns what it means for the
estate.

## 3. The cut that matters

**Of the thirty-four classes, eleven record an act capable of changing a reportable result.** They are
`AE-06`, `AE-07`, `AE-08`, `AE-09`, `AE-10`, `AE-11`, `AE-12`, `AE-13`, `AE-14`, `AE-15` and `AE-16` —
substituting a processing method version, changing integration parameters across a sequence, changing them
on a single injection at review time, moving a peak start or end point, moving a baseline, taking a peak
into or out of the result, taking a calibration standard into or out of the calibration, dropping a
calibration level from the curve, recalculating a stored result, deleting an injection from a result set,
and overriding a system suitability evaluation.

**The eleven, by configuration:**

| Group | Result-changing classes | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Enabled on both | `AE-06`, `AE-07`, `AE-09`, `AE-10`, `AE-12`, `AE-14` | **6** |
| Enabled on `SYS-023` only | `AE-11`, `AE-13` | **2** |
| Enabled on `SYS-024` only | — | **0** |
| **Enabled on neither** | **`AE-08`, `AE-15`, `AE-16`** | **3** |
| **Total** | | **11** |

**6 + 2 + 0 + 3 = 11.**

**The other twenty-three split 11 · 7 · 2 · 3**, and the two partitions reconcile against each other in
both directions:

| Group | Result-changing | Not result-changing | Group total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enabled on both | 6 | 11 | **17** |
| Enabled on `SYS-023` only | 2 | 7 | **9** |
| Enabled on `SYS-024` only | 0 | 2 | **2** |
| Enabled on neither | 3 | 3 | **6** |
| **Total** | **11** | **23** | **34** |

**6 + 11 = 17 · 2 + 7 = 9 · 0 + 2 = 2 · 3 + 3 = 6 · 11 + 23 = 34.** Every figure in both tables is
computed at `logs/auditable-event-register.md §4` and reconciled at `EV-613`.

**Read the third column of the first table.** Eight of the eleven result-changing classes are enabled at
Ridgemont — six on both plus two on Ridgemont only — and six at Cary. **Two acts capable of changing a
reportable result are recorded at one Helix laboratory and not at the other**: taking a peak into or out of
the reported result, and dropping a calibration level from the calibration curve. Both are ordinary acts
in a chromatography laboratory. **Neither is recorded at Cary and both are recorded at Ridgemont, and the
two laboratories run the same product at the same version.**

## 4. The six enabled on neither, and the line that runs through them

**Four were choices. Two cannot be enabled at all on this product version. 4 + 2 = 6.**

| Class | Configurable | Result-changing | What its absence means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`AE-08`** — integration parameter change applied to a single injection at review time | **No** | **Yes** | The act closest to the reported number leaves no entry, on either instance, and no setting available to Helix would produce one |
| **`AE-16`** — system suitability evaluation overridden and the sequence carried forward | **No** | **Yes** | An evaluation set aside leaves no entry, on either instance, and no setting available to Helix would produce one |
| `AE-15` — injection deletion from a sequence result set | Yes | **Yes** | An injection removed from a result set after acquisition leaves no entry, **and it could have** |
| `AE-19` — report regeneration from unchanged stored results | Yes | No | A report produced again leaves no entry, and it could have |
| `AE-25` — system clock or time zone change on the acquisition server | Yes | No | A change to the clock every other entry is timestamped against leaves no entry, and it could have |
| `AE-33` — custom descriptive field edit on a sample record | Yes | No | An edit to a user-defined field leaves no entry, and it could have |

**Both classes that cannot be enabled record an act capable of changing a reportable result.** That is not
a coincidence anybody has explained and this chapter does not explain it; it records it.

**`AE-08` is the class to read twice.** It records **a change to integration parameters applied to a single
injection at review time** — not to the processing method, not across the sequence, but to one injection
after the sequence has been processed and while somebody is looking at the result. **On this product
version there is no setting that would record it.** `AE-07`, which records the same kind of change applied
to a whole sequence, is enabled on both instances; the difference between the two classes is the scope the
change is applied at, and the narrower scope — the one act somebody performs on the injection whose number
is about to be reported — is the one the product does not offer to record.

**`AE-08` is also the class FDA's own example reaches by name.** The December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity
Q&A, in the answer defining *audit trail*, states that *"the audit trail for a high performance liquid
chromatography (HPLC) run should include the user name, date/time of the run, the integration parameters
used, and details of a reprocessing, if any."* That is a nonbinding *should* in a guidance rather than a
requirement, and it is not a complete list of acts — but **the integration parameters used are named in it,
and on this product version neither instance records a change to them applied to a single injection at
review time.** `06.04 §7.3` states what that sentence is and what it is not, and this chapter does not
restate it.

**The vendor's statement that `AE-08` and `AE-16` cannot be enabled is at `EV-612`, and `AS-43` records
what it rests on.** The statement describes the product version. **It does not describe Helix's licence
tier, its installed options or its support arrangement**, and if it turns out to describe those instead
then two of the six become choices, the four-and-two becomes six-and-nothing, and the most quoted sentence
in this phase changes its meaning.

## 5. The four and the two are as serious as each other, and they close differently

**A class that was not enabled and a class that could not be enabled leave the same absence in the record.
A reviewer opening the audit trail sees nothing for the act in either case.** In that respect the four
choices are exactly as serious as the two limits, and this chapter refuses the reading — available, and
comfortable — that the four are administrative and the two are the real problem.

**They differ entirely in remedy, and the difference is the reason they are counted separately.**

| | The four | The two |
|---|---|---|
| What closes it | **A configuration change**, and a sentence in a procedure requiring the configuration to stay that way | **A conversation with the vendor**, and a compensating control until there is a product route |
| Who can bring it about | Helix, on both instances, this month | The vendor, on a timescale Helix does not control |
| The dependency | `DP-35` — quality unit capacity and account administration | `DP-33` — the chromatography data system vendor |
| The precedent | `DP-27` is larger and no sentence has been written | **`AS-17` closed in Phase 03 as tested and failed**: this vendor estate has already not supplied documentation once |
| The risk | The change is made and nothing requires it to stay — `IS-29`, and the estate already carries 218 of those | The conversation opens and closes with an entry on a product roadmap, which is not a control |

**A configuration change that no procedure requires is a control that can be withdrawn tomorrow without a
deviation** — `ADR-0031`, and `IS-29` carries two hundred and eighteen instances of exactly that condition
across the estate. **Turning the four on without writing the sentence would convert four absences into four
unrequired controls**, which is a smaller problem in the same family rather than the end of the problem.

**And a compensating control is owed on the two from the day this chapter is published, not from the day
the vendor answers.** This phase states none — **no CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no
remediation cost** — because stating one is not Phase 06's to state. What it records is that the two limbs
of `IS-36` close differently and that neither has been agreed.

## 6. What a reviewer would have seen, on each instance

**Take the two instances in turn and ask what an audit trail review would have put in front of a
reviewer.**

**At Ridgemont**, twenty-six classes generate entries and eight of the eleven result-changing classes are
among them. A reviewer would see a processing method substituted, integration parameters changed across a
sequence, a peak boundary moved, a baseline moved, a calibration standard included or excluded, a
calibration level dropped, a peak taken into or out of the result, and a result recalculated. **They would
not see an integration parameter changed on the single injection in front of them, an injection deleted
from the result set, or a suitability evaluation overridden.**

**At Cary**, nineteen classes generate entries and six of the eleven are among them. A reviewer would see
the six that are enabled on both. **They would additionally not see a peak taken into or out of the
result, and would not see a calibration level dropped from the curve** — two acts their colleagues forty
miles away are recorded performing.

**On neither instance would the reviewer see the act that sits closest to the number.**

`06.06-what-review-would-not-have-found.md` owns what follows from that, and this chapter stops here.

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

**It states no conclusion about `OBS-2`.** The observation stands exactly as written and is not softened,
qualified, mitigated or reduced by anything in this chapter. **Its disposition is Phase 08's** —
`ADR-0041`, `06.11-obs-2-stands.md`. **The `OBS-4` link is not engaged here at all.**

**It re-determines no control position.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` is **Not met** on both systems in
`../05-access-audit-trails-and-electronic-signatures/logs/control-position-register.md`, and it is Not met
on both systems after this chapter. **A configuration read is not a control determination**, and no cell
moves.

**It is not a validation outcome.** **Reading a configuration is not validating a system** — `ADR-0034`,
`IS-34`. A class recorded as enabled is a class observed to be generating entries; it is not a class
demonstrated to record every instance of the act it names. No package has been executed against either
system, no protocol has been run and no system validation plan has been written.

**It sets no audit trail review frequency and names none.** **No primary source names a stand-alone
periodic audit trail review frequency.** FDA names the frequencies to be inherited — review after each
significant step under `21 CFR 211.188(b)`, and data review before batch release under `21 CFR 211.22` —
and leaves the firm to determine one only where CGMP specifies none for the underlying data, **which is the
only place FDA's risk-assessment sentence operates.** **FDA's own closing requirement runs to the whole
approach**: *"Your approach to audit trail review and the frequency with which you conduct it should ensure
that CGMP requirements are met, appropriate controls are implemented, and the reliability of the review is
proven."* **This chapter names no frequency, and `IS-04` stays deliberately unresolved.**

**It is not an estate finding.** **Two systems of sixty-one were assessed at a depth not available for the
other fifty-nine** — `IS-41`, `ADR-0040`, `06.01-depth-and-what-it-costs.md`. Nothing in this chapter may
be read as a statement about any other system's audit trail configuration.

**It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.** **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**,
which stays open and stays unweighted; a configuration read on 2027-02-10 is not evidence about a system in
June 2023.
