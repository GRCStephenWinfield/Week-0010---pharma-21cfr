*One hundred and forty-two released results, thirty-eight reprocessed, nine that differ — and none on which the record shows both values.*

---

## 1. What reintegration is, and why it is the phase's hardest chapter

**A chromatogram is a set of detector responses over time. The number reported out of the laboratory is
what a set of integration parameters makes of that set.** Where a peak starts, where it ends, where the
baseline is drawn, which peaks are counted and which are not — each of those is a parameter, and each is a
decision. **Change the parameters and the same detector responses produce a different number, without a
single measurement being repeated.**

Reintegration is that act performed again, after the first pass. It is an ordinary and often necessary part
of chromatographic analysis: a first-pass integration built on a method that suited most of a sequence may
handle one injection badly, and correcting it is the analyst doing the job properly. **It is also the act
that makes the reportable result contestable**, because a record that shows only the final number cannot be
distinguished from a record that shows only the number somebody preferred.

**That is why `RT-13` exists as a record type** and why Phase 02 moved it into scope: the record is
dynamic, a static printout cannot reproduce it, and **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires a record of all
calculations performed in connection with the test.** `RT-13`'s predicate rule is
**`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` and `(a)(5)`**, and `06.03 §3` states it.

## 2. The population, and the funnel

**In the twelve months to 2027-01-31, 142 reportable results were released across the two instances —
`SYS-023` at Ridgemont 86 and `SYS-024` at Cary 56. 86 + 56 = 142.**

**Of the 142, 38 were produced from a reprocessed integration — Ridgemont 21, Cary 17. 21 + 17 = 38.**

**Of the 38, 9 carry a reported value that differs from the value the first integration produced —
Ridgemont 5, Cary 4. 5 + 4 = 9.**

**On none of the 38, and therefore on none of the 9, does the record a reviewer opens show both values.
0 of 38, and 0 of 9.**

| Stage | `SYS-023` | `SYS-024` | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reportable results released in the window | 86 | 56 | **142** |
| Produced from a reprocessed integration | 21 | 17 | **38** |
| Reported value differs from the first integration | 5 | 4 | **9** |
| **Record shows both values** | **0** | **0** | **0** |

**Every row of that table is computed in `logs/reintegration-register.md §3` from the thirty-eight rows in
its `§2`, and reconciled at `EV-619`.** The register is authoritative and this chapter states what it
computes.

**How the comparison was made, and why it had to be made that way.** The question is whether the record
shows the previous value. **A method that consults the record to answer it cannot return a negative, because
the absence being looked for is exactly the absence that would hide it.** The value the first integration
produced was therefore obtained from outside the record the reviewer sees — from processing history
retained on the instance, from the sequence-level processing record, or from the archived first-pass result
set held outside the working store — and the provenance is recorded row by row at `EV-618`. **On no row was
it obtained from the record the reviewer sees.** `ADR-0039` is the decision, `DEC-608`, taken by Dr Sunita
Raghunathan on 2027-02-15, and `AS-45` records what the comparison now rests on.

**All 38 were examined and none was sampled.** A sample would have supported a proportion, and **this phase
publishes no percentages**; nine out of thirty-eight is a fact about thirty-eight results, and a sample
would not have been.

## 3. `21 CFR 11.10(e)`, whole

**The provision the finding is recorded against, quoted in full:**

> *"Use of secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails to independently record the date and time
> of operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic records. Record changes shall
> not obscure previously recorded information. Such audit trail documentation shall be retained for a
> period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records and shall be available for
> agency review and copying."*

**The second sentence is the one this chapter is about.** *"Record changes shall not obscure previously
recorded information."* **It is a no-overwrite requirement**: the prior value must remain discernible.
`../05-access-audit-trails-and-electronic-signatures/05.05-audit-trails.md` owns the clause-by-clause reading and it is not repeated here.

**Nine reportable results carry a value that differs from the value the first integration produced, and on
none of the nine does the record show both.** That is the finding, and it is recorded against that
sentence.

## 4. Three provisions about reasons, none of them `21 CFR 11.10(e)`

**Part 11 imposes no reason-for-change requirement.** There is no textual requirement anywhere in Part 11
for a reason-for-change field. `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires that record changes not obscure previously
recorded information, which is a different obligation about a different object. **Holding a reason-for-change
field is not compliance with it** — `ADR-0036`, taken in Phase 05 by Joachim Reuter, standing framing.

**Three other provisions do speak to reasons, and they are distinct from each other and from Part 11.**

| Source | What it says | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **`21 CFR 211.100(b)`** | Production and control activities **must be documented at the time of performance**, and **any deviation from the written procedures shall be recorded and justified** | **A regulation. A *shall*** |
| **`21 CFR 211.160(a)`** | The requirements of the laboratory controls subpart **shall be followed and shall be documented at the time of performance**, and **any deviation from the written specifications, standards, sampling plans, test procedures or other laboratory control mechanisms shall be recorded and justified** | **A regulation. A *shall*** |
| **FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity Q&A** | *"Documentation should include change justification for the reprocessing."* | **Guidance. A nonbinding *should*, cited as guidance and never as a requirement** — `PR-09` |

**Three distinct provisions, none of them `21 CFR 11.10(e)`.** The two predicate-rule provisions attach to
deviations — `21 CFR 211.100(b)` to **deviations from the written procedures** and `21 CFR 211.160(a)` to
**deviations from the written specifications, standards, sampling plans, test procedures or other
laboratory control mechanisms**, which is the limb that reaches a laboratory; the guidance sentence
attaches to reprocessing and is a *should*.
**None of the three is discharged by the presence of a field**, and none of the three is what the finding
in `§2` is recorded against.

**Where a reason was recorded, on the thirty-eight.** Twelve rows carry one in the reason-for-change field
on the processing method version, against the sequence and not against the injection; four carry one in a
free-text comment on the injection, all four at Ridgemont, because the class that records a comment edit is
enabled there and not at Cary; six carry one in the analyst's bound laboratory notebook, outside the
electronic record; three carry one in the LIMS result comment, on the system that receives the value and
not on the system that produced it; and **thirteen carry none anywhere. 12 + 4 + 6 + 3 + 13 = 38.**
`logs/reintegration-register.md §3.2` holds the counts.

**Those counts are published and are not offered as an answer to anything.** `PR-46` carries the risk that
a reason recorded in one of those four places is reported as compliance with `21 CFR 11.10(e)`. It is not.
**The reason for the change is retained on twenty-five rows and the value the change was made to is
retained on none**, which is the inversion `IS-32` recorded across the estate, seen here at the level of an
individual result.

## 5. What the record cannot show

**Take one of the nine.** A reportable result was released. It was produced from a reprocessed integration.
The value released is not the value the first integration produced. **The record a reviewer opens shows the
released value and does not show the other one.**

**Now ask the question a reviewer is there to answer**: was the reprocessing appropriate? The parameters
may have been corrected because the first pass integrated a shoulder as a single peak, which is the analyst
doing the job properly. The parameters may have been changed until the number moved, which is not. **Both
histories terminate in the same record**, because the record shows one value and neither the value it
replaced nor, on either instance, an audit trail entry for a parameter change applied to a single injection
at review time — `AE-08`, which cannot be enabled on this product version.

**A record that shows one value cannot distinguish a justified reintegration from an unjustified one.**

That is the finding, and it is a finding about the record. It is not a finding about any analyst, any
result or any batch, and **no document in this repository assesses any individual's work.**

## 6. Why none of the thirty-eight is characterised

**No reintegration examined in this phase is characterised as proper, improper, justified or unjustified**
— `ADR-0043`, `DEC-617`, taken by Gideon Halvorsen on 2027-02-25.

**The reason is `§5` and nothing more elaborate.** The distinction is a distinction the record cannot
support, and **a characterisation drawn from a record incapable of supporting it is an assertion dressed as
a finding.** Two sentences were available and both were refused; `EV-628` retains them.

| Sentence available | Why it was refused |
|---|---|
| *Nine reintegrations changed the reported value and the record does not justify any of them* | It reads as a finding of impropriety on nine occasions. **The record's silence is not evidence of anything except the record's silence**, and this programme does not convert an absence into an allegation |
| *The examination found no evidence of improper reintegration* | **The more dangerous of the two.** It is literally true of a record that could not have shown such evidence either way, it reads as an assurance, and it discourages anybody from testing it. **A negative that nobody can test is not a determination; it is a silence** |

**What is recorded instead is whether the record can show what happened**, and on thirty-eight occasions it
cannot. **That is a fact anybody can check against the register**, which is what a finding is supposed to
be.

**`PR-45` carries the risk that one of the two refused sentences is written anyway**, in a briefing, a
slide, a vendor discussion or an audit response.

## 7. What this evidences, and what it changes

**`CG-15` is evidenced and is not re-determined.** Phase 05's gap register records `CG-15` against
`21 CFR 11.10(e)` on six systems including both of these, with the cause that **a change to a stored value
replaces the previous value in the record the reviewer sees**, and the closure condition that the previous
value be retained in a form the reviewer sees. **The thirty-eight rows are what that cause looks like in a
laboratory over twelve months.** The gap is not closed, not re-scoped, not re-owned and not re-caused by
anything here, and it has not moved since 2027-01-21. **`DP-34` is the vendor route on these two systems
and `DP-29` is the estate-wide form; neither has moved.**

**`CG-05` is why the export cannot carry both values either.** Phase 05 records it against
`21 CFR 11.10(b)` on seven systems including both of these, with the cause that **the only export the
product offers is a static rendering that carries neither the processing context nor the audit trail.** So
the previous value is not in the record on screen and it is not in the export of that record. **The two
gaps meet on the same thirty-eight rows**, and `logs/reintegration-register.md`'s last column names both.

**`(e)` is Not met on both systems in the control position register and is Not met on both after this
chapter.** No cell moves. **This is instance evidence for a published position, not a re-determination of
it.**

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

**It characterises no reintegration** — `ADR-0043`, `§6`.

**It says nothing about whether any of the 142 released results was correct.** A reportable result is
released by the quality unit against a specification, and **batch disposition sits with the quality unit
whatever state this programme is in** — `21 CFR 211.22`. Nothing here revisits any release.

**It states no CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no remediation cost.** **A control gap is not a
deviation** and neither is a row in this register.

**It states no retention, backup or archive rule.** The material the comparison was made against at
`EV-618` is held somewhere, and **for how long, in what form, and whether the copy that survives is a true
one is not established anywhere** — **`IS-42`**, Phase 07. `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires audit trail
documentation to be retained *"for a period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic
records"*, **and no retention period exists for any of the 34 in-scope record types** — `IS-14`.

**It re-scores no Phase 03 pair and re-determines no Phase 05 cell.** `ND-05` and `ND-10` — `RT-13`,
undetermined on all five attributes on both instances — remain exactly as published, and **this chapter's
thirty-eight rows determine none of them.**

**It draws no conclusion about `OBS-2` and none about `OBS-4`** — Phase 08. **It reaches no legal
conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.** **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and
stays unweighted.
