## 1. What this chapter settles

The product Helix runs in both quality control laboratories offers **thirty-four auditable event classes**.
They are enumerable, they are documented, and each can be looked at and recorded as enabled or not enabled
on an installation. **A configuration review that works through that list and ticks off what is on is a
perfectly competent piece of work, and it answers a question Helix did not need answered.**

It measures the product.

**What Helix needs to know is different, and this chapter states it as precisely as it can be stated: does
the record show what a person did to the number that leaves the laboratory?**

That question cannot be answered from a vendor's list, because the vendor's list is organised by what the
software can log and the question is organised by what a person can do. **The two populations are not the
same population, they are not in one-to-one correspondence, and neither one is contained in the other.**
This chapter builds the instrument for comparing them: it defines an act capable of changing a reportable
result, works through the categories of such acts on a chromatography data system, records how the eleven
were identified and by whom, and states the honest limits of that identification.

**It states no result.** Which classes each instance has enabled, how the thirty-four partition across the
two installations, and what the comparison of acts against classes produced are all
`06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s, on `logs/auditable-event-register.md`. **This chapter reports
none of it.** `ADR-0038` records the decision the chapter argues.

## 2. What the regulation actually says, and what it does not

Before any method is built, the text. **A method built to satisfy a requirement nobody has read is a method
that will satisfy something else.**

### 2.1 `21 CFR 11.10(e)`, in full

> *"Use of secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails to independently record the date and time
> of operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic records. Record changes shall
> not obscure previously recorded information. Such audit trail documentation shall be retained for a period
> at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records and shall be available for agency
> review and copying."*

Four clauses, and each of them does something specific.

**"…operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic records."** Three verbs, and
**reading is not among them. Part 11 says nothing whatever about recording read or view events**, and a
system that logs them has implemented something Helix chose rather than something the regulation requires.

**"Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information."** A **no-overwrite** requirement: the
prior value must remain discernible. **It is not a reason-for-change requirement. There is no textual
requirement anywhere in Part 11 for a reason-for-change field** — `ADR-0036` fixed that in Phase 05 and this
chapter does not re-argue it.

**"Such audit trail documentation shall be retained for a period at least as long as that required for the
subject electronic records…"** The audit trail **inherits** the retention period of the record it describes
and has none of its own.

**"…and shall be available for agency review and copying."** The obligation runs to FDA.
**`21 CFR 11.10(e)` imposes no internal review obligation at all — no periodic review, no second-person
review, and no review at any frequency.**

### 2.2 Part 11 does not define "audit trail"

**`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and no more**, and *audit trail* is not among them. Neither are
*metadata*, *data integrity*, *static record* or *dynamic record*. **`21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires the use of
audit trails and describes their properties without ever naming the thing.**

The definition the industry actually uses is FDA's, in the December 2018 guidance *"Data Integrity and
Compliance With Drug CGMP: Questions and Answers"*, and it is a guidance definition rather than a regulatory
one:

> *"a secure, computer-generated, time-stamped electronic record that allows for reconstruction of the
> course of events relating to the creation, modification, or deletion of an electronic record"*

**The operative words for this chapter are "reconstruction of the course of events".** That is a
functional standard, and it is the standard against which the question at `§1` is asked. **A record that
enumerates events without permitting the course of events to be reconstructed has met the shape of the
definition and not its purpose** — and whether any particular record does or does not is a matter of fact
about a configuration, which is `06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s to report.

### 2.3 Where the review obligation comes from, and what its frequency is

**Part 11 imposes no audit trail review requirement.** The review obligation comes from the predicate
rules, and FDA sets out how in the 2018 guidance:

> *"Personnel responsible for record review under CGMP should review the audit trails that capture changes
> to data associated with the record as they review the rest of the record (e.g., §§ 211.22(a), 211.101(c)
> and (d), 211.103, 211.182, 211.186(a), 211.192, 211.194(a)(8), and 212.20(d))."*

> *"For example, all production and control records, which includes audit trails, must be reviewed and
> approved by the quality unit (§ 211.192)."*

**FDA's construction is that the audit trail is part of the record being reviewed**, so the obligation
reaches it through the existing review requirement rather than through a new one — **and FDA names
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` in terms**, among the eight provisions it lists. That is the second-person review of
laboratory records: *"[t]he initials or signature of a second person showing that the original records have
been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established standards."* **What no primary
source states is whether Helix's own laboratory record review procedure adopts that construction** —
`IS-06`, open.

**On frequency, the position is stated in full because the short form of it is wrong.** **No primary source
names a stand-alone periodic audit trail review frequency.** What FDA names are frequencies to be
**inherited**: *"If the review frequency for the data is specified in CGMP regulations, adhere to that
frequency for the audit trail review."* The frequencies so specified are **review after each significant
step under `21 CFR 211.188(b)`** and **data review before batch release under `21 CFR 211.22`**, and FDA
leaves the firm to determine one only where CGMP specifies none for the underlying data, **which is the
only place FDA's risk-assessment sentence operates.** **FDA's own closing requirement runs to the whole
approach**: *"Your approach to audit trail review and the frequency with which you conduct it should ensure
that CGMP requirements are met, appropriate controls are implemented, and the reliability of the review is
proven."*

**This chapter names no frequency, sets none as a requirement, and names none by implication.** `IS-04`
stays deliberately unresolved.

### 2.4 What the predicate rules make of the records this chapter is about

The chromatography record types are required records because other regulations require them, and the
citations matter to the argument at `§5`. **`06.03-what-the-registers-already-say.md §3` sets out all five
with their predicate rules, from Phase 02's register, and they are not reproduced here.** Two of them do the
work in this chapter.

**`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` requires *"[a] complete record of all data secured in the course of each test,
including all graphs, charts, and spectra from laboratory instrumentation, properly identified to show the
specific component, drug product container, closure, in-process material, or drug product, and lot
tested."*** **Two limbs, and the second is easy to lose.** The record must be complete, and it must be
*properly identified* to show what was tested — which is the limb the act at `§5.5` reaches, because
re-declaring what an injection is changes the identification without changing a single value.
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires *"[a] record of all calculations performed in connection with the test,
including units of measure, conversion factors, and equivalency factors."*

**`RT-13` sits under both, and that is the whole of why it is this phase's centre of gravity.** An
integration is simultaneously data secured in the course of the test and a calculation performed in
connection with it. **The act this chapter is about — changing how a peak is integrated — changes a
calculation whose record `21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires**, and it does so without touching a single
number a person typed.

## 3. Why the vendor's list is the wrong instrument

**Not a wrong list. The wrong instrument for this question.**

The vendor's enumeration is an honest artefact and it is accurate about the product. Its problem is that it
was built to answer a different question, by a party with different obligations, for a different reader.

| | **The vendor's list** | **The question Helix has to answer** |
|---|---|---|
| **Organised by** | What the software is able to log | **What a person is able to do** |
| **The unit** | An event class in the product's internal taxonomy | **An act, performed by a named individual, with the permissions the estate actually grants** |
| **Its boundary** | The product | **The product, the configuration, the permission set and the laboratory's practice** |
| **Whose obligation it serves** | The vendor's, to describe its product accurately | **Helix's, under `21 CFR 11.10`'s opening words — procedures and controls employed by persons** |
| **What "complete" means** | Every class the product offers is listed | **Every act that can change a reportable result has been located, and its recording position established** |

**The mismatch is not sloppiness at either end.** `21 CFR 11.10` opens by binding *"Persons who use closed
systems to create, modify, maintain, or transmit electronic records"* to employ procedures and controls,
and **that phrasing is why there is no such thing as Part 11 compliant software**: the duty is on the
regulated entity's use of its system, and a vendor cannot discharge it on the firm's behalf. **A vendor may
legitimately say its product provides features that support Part 11 compliance. Compliance is a property of
Helix's validated system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated by its trained people, and it
is not something a supplier can sell or FDA confers.**

**So a list of what the product can log is exactly the artefact a vendor is competent to produce, and
exactly not the artefact that answers Helix's question.**

Three specific failure modes follow from using it as though it were, and this phase's method is built to
avoid all three.

**A class that is enabled and records an act nobody at Helix can perform contributes nothing.** It appears
in the tick-list as a control. It is not one — not because the class is defective, but because the act it
watches for is not available to anybody in either laboratory. **Counting it is counting an empty room.**

**An act that no class records is invisible to the list entirely.** The tick-list has no row for it, and a
review of the tick-list will therefore never produce a question about it. **An absence from an enumeration
does not announce itself**, which is the property that makes this the more dangerous of the two failure
modes.

**And a class whose name resembles an act is assumed to record it.** This is the subtlest of the three. An
event class named for a category of change may record that category in one circumstance and not in another
— at acquisition and not at review, at the sequence level and not at the injection level. **A name is not a
specification**, and `§6` is the part of this method that exists because of it.

## 4. The definition

**An act capable of changing a reportable result is an act that, performed by a person holding the
permissions the estate actually grants, would cause the value reported to the batch record or to the
certificate of analysis to differ from the value that would otherwise have been reported.**

The definition has four elements and each excludes something.

**"An act… performed by a person."** Not an event, not a system occurrence, not a failure. An automatic
recalculation triggered by a scheduled task is not an act under this definition; a person instructing that
recalculation is. **The distinction matters because `21 CFR 11.10(e)` is about *"operator entries and
actions"*, and because the question at `§1` is about what a person did.**

**"Holding the permissions the estate actually grants."** Not permissions the product supports, not
permissions a role definition describes, and not permissions an SOP contemplates. **The permissions in
force on the installation, held by the people who work in that laboratory.** An act that only a permission
set nobody holds would allow is not capable of changing a reportable result at Helix, and it is recorded as
excluded with its reason. This element is the one that makes the determination per-instance rather than
per-product — `ADR-0037` — and the one that makes it perishable, because a permission set can be changed by
one person in one afternoon.

**"Would cause the value reported… to differ."** Capability, not history. **The definition does not ask
whether anybody did it.** Whether any released result carries a value that differs from the value first
produced is `06.07-reintegration.md`'s subject and a different question entirely. **This one asks whether
the act could move the number**, which is what determines whether the audit trail needs to see it.

**"The value reported to the batch record or to the certificate of analysis."** The reportable result, and
not any intermediate quantity. A change that alters a displayed figure on an interim screen, a scratch
calculation or a working file, and that cannot propagate to the reported value, is outside the definition.
**The anchor is the number that leaves the laboratory**, because that is the number a batch disposition
decision under `21 CFR 211.192` rests on and the number a certificate of analysis carries.

### 4.1 What the definition deliberately excludes

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| **Reading, viewing, printing and exporting** | They change no value. **Part 11 requires no read-event logging**, and including them would inflate the population with acts that cannot move the number |
| **Acts on records that are not reportable-result inputs** | An act on an unrelated record is outside the question this phase asks, which is not the same as saying it is unimportant |
| **Acts no permission set in force permits** | Recorded as excluded **with the permission basis stated**, so that a permission change re-opens the exclusion rather than silently invalidating it |
| **System-generated events with no operator** | Not *"operator entries and actions"*. **They are recorded as excluded and not as absent** |
| **Acts that alter presentation only** | A change to how a chromatogram is displayed, where the integration and the calculation are untouched |

**Every exclusion is recorded with its basis, and an exclusion whose basis is a permission set is
re-examined whenever the permission set changes.** An exclusion with no recorded basis would be
indistinguishable from an omission, and `ADR-0014`'s rule holds here as it held in Phase 02: **an
unexplained exclusion is indistinguishable from an oversight.**

## 5. The categories, worked through

Six categories of act, and then the distinction that cuts across all six. **The categories are how the
question was asked; they are not the answer, and they are not the eleven.** How the categories resolved
into identified acts on each instance is `§7`, and what each instance's configuration does about them is
`06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s.

### 5.1 Integration parameter change

A chromatographic peak becomes a number through integration: the software decides where the peak starts,
where it ends, and where the baseline runs beneath it, using parameters an operator can set. **Change the
parameters and the area under the peak changes. Change the area and the quantity changes. Change the
quantity and the reportable result changes.**

**No entry has been edited. No number has been typed. No field has been overwritten.** The raw data file is
untouched and remains exactly what the detector produced. **What has changed is a calculation performed on
it** — which is why `21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)`, requiring a record of all calculations performed in connection
with the test, is the provision that reaches this act, and why `RT-13` sits under both `(a)(4)` and
`(a)(5)`.

**This is the category that makes the whole method necessary**, because it is the clearest case of an act
that changes the reported number while leaving every stored datum intact. An audit trail built on the
intuition that a change is something a person types will not see it.

### 5.2 Manual peak edit

The operator overrides the software's integration for a specific peak by hand: dragging a baseline, forcing
a peak split, adding or removing a peak from the integrated set. **Mechanically distinct from a parameter
change and identical in consequence — the area changes and the reported quantity moves with it.**

The category is separated from `§5.1` for a reason that matters to the assessment rather than to the
chemistry: **a parameter change and a manual edit may be recorded by different classes, at different levels
of the record, and with different content.** Treating them as one act would have made the assessment blind
to a configuration in which one is recorded and the other is not.

### 5.3 Calibration standard exclusion

A quantitative result is calculated against a calibration curve fitted to a set of standards. **Remove one
standard from the fit and the curve moves; move the curve and every sample quantified against it moves with
it.**

**The act's reach is the property that distinguishes it.** A parameter change or a manual edit moves one
injection's result. **A change to the calibration set moves every result quantified against that curve**,
which may be an entire sequence. `RT-15` — the chromatographic calibration curve and result recalculation
record — is the record type this act operates on, and its predicate rule is `21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)`.

### 5.4 Method version substitution at processing

The processing method carries the integration parameters, the calibration model, the peak identification
and the calculation. **Applying a different version of the method at processing time changes all of them at
once, and does so without any individual parameter being touched.**

This category is where the difference between *what was acquired* and *what was reported* is widest.
`RT-12` is the raw data file and acquisition metadata; the processing method applied to it is a separate
object with its own version history. **A record that shows which method was used to acquire the data and is
silent about which version was used to process it has described half of the chain that produced the
number.**

### 5.5 Sample or sequence re-assignment

Changing what an injection is declared to be: its sample identity, its position in a sequence, its type —
standard, sample, blank, check standard — or the batch or specification it is reported against.

**No integration changes and no calculation changes. What changes is which result is reported where**, and
the reportable result on the batch record moves accordingly. The act is at the level of identity rather
than of value, which is exactly why an audit trail organised around changes to values may not see it, and
why it was searched for separately.

**`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)`'s second limb is the predicate rule for this category, which is therefore not
argued from first principles.** The subparagraph requires that the complete record of all data secured in
the course of each test be *"properly identified to show the specific component, drug product container,
closure, in-process material, or drug product, and lot tested"* — `§2.4`. Re-declaring what an injection is
changes exactly that identification, and changes it while every datum and every calculation in the record
stays as it was. An act that alters what the record is identified as showing is reached by the
identification requirement whether or not any value moved.

### 5.6 Result recalculation

Re-running the calculation that turns a peak area into a reported quantity: a change to a dilution factor,
a sample weight, a purity or potency factor for the reference standard, a response factor, or a rounding or
reporting convention.

**`21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires a record of all calculations performed in connection with the test,
including units of measure, conversion factors and equivalency factors**, and this category is that
sentence read as a set of acts. **A conversion factor is a number a person can change**, and changing it
changes every result it is applied to.

## 6. The same act at acquisition and at review

**This is the distinction the method would have missed if it had been built one level coarser, and it is
the reason `§3`'s third failure mode is named.**

An act in any of the six categories can be performed at two points in the record's life, and they are not
the same act for this purpose.

| | **At acquisition** | **At review** |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | While the sequence is being set up and run, before any result exists | After a result has been produced, and potentially after it has been reviewed |
| **What exists before the act** | No reportable result | **A reportable result, produced and recorded** |
| **What the act does** | Determines what the result will be | **Changes what the result already was** |
| **What `21 CFR 11.10(e)`'s no-obscuring clause reaches** | Nothing yet to obscure | **The previously recorded information — squarely** |
| **What a reviewer needs to see** | The parameters used | **The parameters used, the parameters used before, and that a change occurred** |

**The second column is where the whole exposure sits**, and it sits there for a textual reason rather than
an intuitive one. **"Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information"** engages when there
is previously recorded information to obscure. An integration performed at acquisition sets a value. **An
integration performed at review replaces one**, and the clause is about exactly that.

**And the two are routinely recorded by different classes, at different granularities.** A product may
record a change to a processing method as a versioned object while recording a parameter applied to a
single injection at review as something else, or as nothing. **Whether either instance records the act at
both points is `06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s to state, and this chapter states nothing about
it.**

**The methodological rule that follows is stated here and applied there:** an act is assessed at each point
in the record's life at which a person can perform it, and **a class that records the act at one point is
not evidence that the act is recorded at the other.**

## 7. How the eleven were identified

**Applying the definition at `§4` across the categories at `§5` and the two points at `§6` produced the cut
this phase turns on.** This section says how that cut was arrived at, by whom, and on what basis, because a
population produced by an unstated method is a number, and a number cannot be checked. **How many classes
fell on each side of it, and how they fall across the two installations, is
`06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md §3`'s and is computed at `logs/auditable-event-register.md §4`.
No count appears in this chapter.**

**The order of work is the part that matters.** The acts were identified first, from behaviour and
permission, without reference to the vendor's taxonomy. Only then was each of the thirty-four classes read
and asked a single question: **is the act this class records one of the acts identified?** **The cut is the
answer to that question thirty-four times**, and how it falls across the two installations is
`06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s and appears nowhere here.

### 7.1 The two sources

**The product's documented behaviour.** What the product does when each act in each category at `§5` is
performed, at each point at `§6`, read from the vendor's own product documentation for the version in use
and confirmed by demonstration on each installation separately. **Not from marketing material and not from
any vendor statement about compliance**, neither of which is a source about behaviour.

**The permissions the estate actually grants.** Which acts the people working in each laboratory can
perform, established against the permission sets in force on each installation with the Chief Information
Officer's account administration, and read against what the laboratory's own people do. **An act nobody can
perform is excluded with its permission basis recorded** — `§4.1`.

**Neither source is sufficient alone**, and the failure of each alone is instructive. Product documentation
alone yields the vendor's list with extra steps, because it describes capability rather than practice.
Permissions alone yield a list of what people may do with no statement of what the product records when
they do it. **The identification is the intersection, and the intersection is where the assessment's work
actually is.**

### 7.2 Who did it

**The acts were walked with the QC analysts who perform them**, at Ridgemont and at Cary, separately —
`ADR-0037`, and every determination in this phase is made twice or it is not made. Walking the acts with
the people who perform them is the only way to establish what is actually done, as distinct from what a
procedure describes.

**The identification was reviewed by the Computerised System Validation Lead, Colm Ó Braonáin**, who
reports to the Head of Quality Assurance and not to IT — `01.07`, and `ADR-0002`'s reasoning: *"the function
that judges whether a system's validated state holds should not sit in the line that builds and administers
it."*

**The method was authorised before the first act was walked** — `GOV-26`, 2027-02-05 — and the
identification was approved through the quality unit. **A method settled after the results are in is not a
method.**

**And the walk assesses no individual's work.** What an analyst does with the permissions they hold, under
the procedures in force, is a fact about the estate. **No document in this repository characterises any
Helix employee or assesses any individual's work.**

### 7.3 What the identification is, and what it is not

**The identification is Helix's determination.** It is stated as such, in this chapter, in
`logs/auditable-event-register.md` and in `GOV-27`.

**No primary source enumerates exhaustively the acts capable of changing a reportable result on a
chromatography data system, but one FDA guidance names part of the expected content for this instrument
class and it is the anchor for the rest.** No regulation enumerates such acts: `21 CFR 11.10(e)` describes
properties of an audit trail and enumerates nothing; `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` and `(a)(5)` describe records
that must exist and enumerate no acts.

**FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity Q&A goes further than is usually noticed.** In the answer
defining *audit trail* it states: *"For example, the audit trail for a high performance liquid
chromatography (HPLC) run should include the user name, date/time of the run, the integration parameters
used, and details of a reprocessing, if any. Documentation should include change justification for the
reprocessing."* **That is a nonbinding *should*, in a guidance, and it is not a complete list of acts** —
but it is FDA naming, for the instrument class this phase is about, that **the integration parameters
used** and **the details of a reprocessing** belong in the audit trail. **The identified set of acts is
consistent with it and goes beyond it**, and the register at `logs/auditable-event-register.md` records
against each class whether it would carry what that sentence names.

**Beyond that sentence there is no published enumeration against which this population can be checked**,
and the phase says so rather than implying one.

**A different competent reviewer might identify a different set.** That is not a defect to be apologised
for and it is not a reason to withhold the population. It is a property of any determination made against
a standard nobody has published, and the correct response to it is the one this phase takes: **state the
method, state the sources, name the people, publish the population as a register whose rows can be argued
with individually, and record the whole thing as Helix's determination rather than as a finding of fact
about chromatography.**

Three specific limits follow and each is recorded on the register rather than only here.

| Limit | What follows |
|---|---|
| **The set is not proven exhaustive** | An act nobody thought to walk is an act that is not on the register. **The register records the categories searched and the points in the record's life examined, so that a later reviewer can see the shape of the search rather than only its output** |
| **The set is specific to this product at this version, on these two installations** | It is not a general list for chromatography data systems, and **it is not extended to any system not assessed at this depth** — `ADR-0040` |
| **The set is perishable** | A permission change, a configuration change or a version change can add to it or subtract from it. **The determination speaks as at this vantage** |

## 8. The two-sided consequence

**Once acts and classes are treated as two populations rather than one, the assessment has two failure modes
to report and they point in opposite directions.**

### 8.1 A class that is enabled and records an act nobody can perform is worth nothing

It counts in the tick-list. It appears in a configuration screenshot. It contributes a line to any summary
that reports how many classes are on. **And it will never record anything, because the act it watches for
is not available to anybody in that laboratory.**

**The consequence is that a count of enabled classes is not a measure of coverage**, and any figure derived
from one — a proportion, a comparison between two installations, a trend over time — inherits the defect.
**This phase publishes no such figure and derives no such measure.**

**It is not a criticism of the configuration.** Enabling a class that records an unavailable act costs
nothing and does no harm, and a permission set can change tomorrow, at which point the class becomes
valuable. **The defect is in reading the count as coverage, not in the setting.**

### 8.2 A class that is disabled or absent for an act anyone can perform is the whole exposure

The mirror image, and it is not a mirror image in importance.

**If an act can be performed by people who hold the permissions, and no class records it — because the class
is off, or because the product offers no class for it — then the record does not show what was done, and no
amount of reviewing the record will reveal it.**

**Three states, and they are three different problems.**

| State | What it means | What would change it |
|---|---|---|
| **A class exists and is enabled** | The act is recorded. **What the record actually contains is then a separate question**, and content is assessed, not assumed | Nothing — but the content is assessed rather than presumed adequate |
| **A class exists and is not enabled** | **A configuration choice.** It is within Helix's control and can be changed by Helix | A configuration change, under change control |
| **No class exists for the act on this product version** | **Not within Helix's control at all.** No configuration change reaches it | A product change, or a compensating arrangement outside the audit trail |

**The third row is the one with consequences a firm cannot resolve by deciding to be more diligent**, and
the distinction between the second and the third is why `logs/auditable-event-register.md` carries a column
for whether a class is configurable on this product version. **Which acts fall in which state on each
instance is `06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s, and this chapter states none of it.**

### 8.3 What follows for review

**The consequence for the review obligation is the phase's most easily over-read sentence and it is stated
carefully here so that `06.06-what-review-would-not-have-found.md` can report the finding without arguing
the method.**

**Reviewing a record reaches what the record contains.** `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`'s second-person review, and
the quality unit's review under `21 CFR 211.192`, are performed on the record. **Where an act is not
recorded, review is not a weaker control against that act; it is not a control against that act at all**,
because there is nothing in the record for the reviewer to see. A more diligent reviewer, a longer review
and a more frequent review all produce the same result against an act the record does not contain.

**That is a statement about a class of act and a configuration. It is not a statement about any review that
was or was not performed**, and it is emphatically not a defence of anything. **It makes the remediation
larger, because configuration and review are then both owed** — `06.11-obs-2-stands.md` owns that argument
and this chapter does not restate it.

## 9. `ADR-0038` — the audit trail is assessed against acts

**`ADR-0038`, taken at `DEC-605` on 2027-02-10 by Priyanka Venkataraman, records that the audit trail on
each instance is assessed against the acts capable of changing a reportable result, and not against the
vendor's list of event classes.**

Four rules follow, and all four are carried in the method authorised at `GOV-26`.

**The acts are identified first, and independently of the vendor's list.** The order is part of the
decision. A population derived by reading the vendor's list and asking which entries look important is the
vendor's taxonomy with an opinion attached; **the acts are established from behaviour and permission, and
the list is consulted afterwards to ask what records each act.**

**Every act is assessed at every point in the record's life at which a person can perform it** — `§6`. A
class recording the act at one point is not evidence about the other.

**Every act is assessed on each instance separately** — `ADR-0037`. **A class enabled at one laboratory
says nothing about the other.**

**And the register records, for each event class, whether the act it records can change a reportable
result, whether it is enabled on each instance, whether it is configurable on this product version, and
what the class would have shown.** The last column is the one that makes the register answer the question at
`§1` rather than a question about settings. **The partitions the phase publishes are recomputable from
those rows and from nothing else.**

## 10. What this method does not do

- **It sets no audit trail review frequency**, as a requirement or by implication. **No primary source names
  a stand-alone periodic audit trail review frequency**; FDA names the frequencies to be **inherited** —
  review after each significant step under `21 CFR 211.188(b)`, and data review before batch release under
  `21 CFR 211.22` — and leaves the firm to determine one only where CGMP specifies none for the underlying
  data, **which is the only place FDA's risk-assessment sentence operates.** **FDA's own closing requirement
  runs to the whole approach**: *"Your approach to audit trail review and the frequency with which you
  conduct it should ensure that CGMP requirements are met, appropriate controls are implemented, and the
  reliability of the review is proven."* **This chapter names no frequency**, and `IS-04` stays deliberately
  unresolved.
- **It requires no reason-for-change field and reports the presence of one as compliance with nothing** —
  `ADR-0036`. `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires that record changes not obscure previously recorded information,
  and requires no reason to be captured anywhere.
- **It requires no read-event or view-event logging.** *"…operator entries and actions that create, modify,
  or delete…"* does not reach viewing.
- **It configures nothing.** No setting on either instance was changed by this assessment, and this phase
  authorises no configuration change.
- **It states no remediation.** Which of the three states at `§8.2` any act falls into on any instance is
  reported by `06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md` and is not converted into a plan, a sequence, a
  priority, a date or a cost anywhere in this phase.
- **It characterises no reintegration**, as proper, improper, justified or unjustified — `ADR-0043`.
- **It criticises no vendor and describes no product as Part 11 certified, FDA-approved or FDA-validated.**
  **No such thing exists.** A product has features that support compliance; **compliance is a property of
  Helix's validated system in its intended use**, and outsourcing transfers no responsibility.

## 11. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no configuration position and no count of anything.** What each instance records is
  `06.05-the-audit-trail-as-configured.md`'s, on `logs/auditable-event-register.md`.
- **It states no validation outcome.** A demonstration performed to establish what a product does is not a
  test executed against a specification — `06.01 §4.1`, `ADR-0034`. **The evidence Phase 04 requires is owed
  in full.**
- **It disposes of nothing — not `OBS-2` and not `OBS-4`.** `06.11-obs-2-stands.md`, and Phase 08.
- **It re-scores no Phase 03 pair and re-determines no Phase 05 cell.** `CG-15` and `CG-05` are evidenced by
  this phase and changed by nothing in it.
- **It states no retention, backup or archive rule** — Phase 07 — and **nothing about `SYS-036`, `SYS-060`
  or `SYS-061`** beyond what Phase 02's register publishes.
- **It generalises nothing to any system not assessed at this depth** — `ADR-0040`.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, assesses no individual's work, and forecasts no inspection outcome.
- **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted.
