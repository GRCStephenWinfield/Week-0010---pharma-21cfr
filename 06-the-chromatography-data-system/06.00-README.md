## 1. What this phase is

**This is the phase the programme has been walking towards since Phase 02.**

`OBS-2` — audit trails on the chromatography data systems not reviewed as part of laboratory record review
— has been recorded at `02.06`, at `03.09`, at `05.05 §6` and elsewhere, and nothing has ever been
concluded from it, deliberately, for a reason stated each time: **nobody had established what the audit
trails on those two instances actually record.**

**Phase 06 does that technical work.** It assesses two named instances of one product, separately, against
the acts capable of changing a reportable result; it examines a population of released results against the
records those releases rest on; and it compares the two instances against each other as a distinct act.
**`IS-35` closes here.**

**The vantage is 2027-02-26, a Friday, `MS-10`, twenty-eight days after the Phase 05 vantage — the shortest
phase in the programme.** It is short because it has one subject matter, two systems and a method fixed
before the work began, and nothing about its length is apologised for here or anywhere else in it.

## 2. The boundary, stated hard

**Phase 06 disposes of nothing. It disposes of `OBS-2` least of all — that is Phase 08's.**

An inspectional observation is FDA's investigator's, recorded at a point in time, and it is carried under
the 483's own numbering — `ADR-0006`. **Establishing what an observation's subject matter is, in technical
terms, is not disposing of it**, and a disposition requires three things that do not exist at this
vantage: a periodic review method, an internal audit position and a stated Helix response.
`06.11-obs-2-stands.md` argues it and `ADR-0041` records it.

**`OBS-4`'s disposition is Phase 08's too**, and nothing in this phase reaches it.

**And the phase's second boundary is its own depth.** Two systems of sixty-one were assessed at a depth not
available for the other fifty-nine. **A depth finding is not an estate finding, and an examination is not a
disposition.** `06.01-depth-and-what-it-costs.md`, `ADR-0040`.

## 3. Read this first

Six method decisions, and everything else in the phase follows from them. **None of them is a result.**

**Two instances of one product are two systems, and every determination is made twice or it is not made.**
`SYS-023` at Ridgemont and `SYS-024` at Cary are the same product at the same version in two quality
control laboratories, and **one name is not one system.** "The CDS" is how an estate acquires a
determination nobody made. `06.02-one-name-is-not-one-system.md`, `ADR-0037`.

**The audit trail is assessed against the acts that change a reportable result, not against the vendor's
event list.** A configuration review that ticks off the classes a product offers measures the product;
what Helix needs to know is whether the record shows what a person did to the number that leaves the
laboratory. `06.04-the-acts-that-change-a-result.md`, `ADR-0038`.

**A reprocessed result is compared against the first integration**, and where the record cannot show both
values, that is recorded against `21 CFR 11.10(e)`'s requirement that record changes not obscure previously
recorded information. `06.07-reintegration.md`, `ADR-0039`.

**No reintegration is characterised as proper, improper, justified or unjustified.** What is recorded is
whether the record can show what happened. `ADR-0043`, and
`templates/reintegration-examination-record-template.md` carries no field that would characterise one.

**The depth of an assessment is stated wherever its result is reported, and a deep finding is never
generalised to systems not assessed at that depth.** The estate-wide position understates, by an amount
nobody can state, and the phase publishes that limit rather than leaving a later phase to find it.
`06.01-depth-and-what-it-costs.md`, `ADR-0040`, `IS-41`.

**A difference between two instances is a finding about the estate, not about either instance.** It exists
because nothing required the two to agree, and that is a fact about how Helix governs its systems.
`06.08-the-two-instances-compared.md`, `ADR-0042`.

## 5. What Phase 06 deliberately does not establish

None of the following exists at the vantage, and nothing in this phase states, implies or forecasts any of
it. `06.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md` is the full inventory.

- **No disposition of `OBS-2` and no disposition of `OBS-4`** — Phase 08, `MS-12`.
- **No CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan, no remediation cost, no sequence and no priority.**
- **No validation outcome.** No package executed, no protocol run, no test result. **The evidence Phase 04's
  framework requires is owed in full** — `ADR-0034`, `IS-34`.
- **No retention period, no backup rule and no archive rule**, and **nothing about `SYS-036`, `SYS-060` or
  `SYS-061` beyond what Phase 02's register already publishes** — Phase 07, `MS-11`.
- **No re-scoring of Phase 03, no re-banding of Phase 04 and no re-determination of Phase 05.** The ten
  pairs stay unscored, the twenty-two control positions stand as published, and **Phase 06 evidences
  `CG-15` and `CG-05` without changing either.**
- **No characterisation of any reintegration** as proper, improper, justified or unjustified — `ADR-0043`.
- **No periodic review, no internal audit result, no third-party audit result and no inspection outcome of
  any kind.** Phases 08 and 09. **Vashti Okonjo & Partners has audited nothing.**
- **No audit trail review frequency stated as a requirement.** **No primary source names a stand-alone
  periodic audit trail review frequency.** FDA names the frequencies to be **inherited** — review after each
  significant step under `21 CFR 211.188(b)`, and data review before batch release under `21 CFR 211.22` —
  and leaves the firm to determine one only where CGMP specifies none for the underlying data, **which is
  the only place FDA's risk-assessment sentence operates.** **FDA's own closing requirement runs to the
  whole approach**: it *"should ensure that CGMP requirements are met, appropriate controls are
  implemented, and the reliability of the review is proven."* **This phase names none**, and `IS-04` stays
  deliberately unresolved.
- **No generalisation of any depth finding to any system not assessed at that depth** — `ADR-0040`.
- **No answer to `IS-01`**, and **no promotion of any of its four candidate explanations** by statement, by
  ordering or by tone — `ADR-0012`. **`IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted.**
- **No legal conclusion of any kind**, and no forecast of what any future inspection will find.

## 12. Standing framing

These positions hold across all nine phases and are restated here so that a reader arriving at Phase 06 has
them.

- **There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated
  software.** A product has **features that support** compliance; compliance is a property of Helix's
  validated system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated by its trained people. **Outsourcing
  to a service provider transfers no responsibility.**
- **Part 11 applies to records, not to systems**, and this repository never says "a Part 11 system" without
  saying which records make it one — `ADR-0001`, `01.03 §2`.
- **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention. It is not a legal exemption and not a safe harbour**,
  and `21 CFR 211.68` stands whatever FDA does about Part 11 — `ADR-0003`, `ADR-0035`.
- **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by
  the Quality Management System Regulation, and does not apply to Helix.** Adopted voluntarily as good
  practice, **labelled as adopted every time** — `ADR-0005`, `ADR-0026`.
- **GAMP 5 Second Edition, ISPE, July 2022, is good-practice guidance and never a requirement**, and **no
  software-category count and no enumeration of the categories is published in this repository.**
- **FDA uses plain ALCOA, and ALCOA does not map onto `21 CFR 11.10`** — FDA's own footnote 5 maps the
  attributes to the CGMP predicate rules and cites Part 11 nowhere, `ADR-0019`. **The four additional
  attributes are MHRA's**, MHRA itself refers to the shorter acronym and defines all four, and **no
  originator is named for either set.**
- **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, no read-event logging, no cryptographic hashing under
  `21 CFR 11.70`, no multi-factor authentication under `21 CFR 11.200` and no password interval under
  `21 CFR 11.300(b)`.** Where Helix implements any of them, it is Helix's decision and this repository says
  so — `ADR-0036`.
- **A closed system is defined by who controls access** — `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`. Hosting is not the test, and
  **both systems in this phase are closed** — `ADR-0011`.
- **`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and Part 11 contains ten sections.** There is no §11.60, no
  §11.400 and no Subpart D. **Part 11 defines neither "audit trail" nor "metadata" nor "data integrity" nor
  "static record" nor "dynamic record"** — FDA defines them in the December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity
  guidance. Citations in this repository are exact and can be looked up.
- **A Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding.** No document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of
  any kind, and **never forecast what a future inspection will find.** **An open gap is Helix's own
  assessment of its own position** — not a violation, not a breach, not a non-compliance, not a finding and
  not an inspectional observation.
- **Applicability is not integrity, a rating is not a validation status, a requirement is not a control, a
  control position is not validation evidence, and a depth finding is not an estate finding.**
- **All content about Helix is fictional and illustrative.**

## 13. How to read this phase

Read **`06.11`** if you read only one chapter. It is the one that says what the phase did not do with what
it found.

Read **`06.01`** for the boundary between what was assessed and what was not, and **`06.02`** and
**`06.04`** for the two method decisions everything else rests on. Read **`06.03`** before **`06.05`**,
**`06.07`** and **`06.08`**, because it is the chapter that says what was already published and therefore
what is new.

Read **`06.12`** with particular attention. It answers the reading a hostile reviewer will produce first,
and the answer is not that the reading is unreasonable.

Read for the arguments rather than the citations. Where a chapter cites `21 CFR 11.10(e)` or
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`, it is locating a requirement in the text — **not** asserting that Helix meets it.
