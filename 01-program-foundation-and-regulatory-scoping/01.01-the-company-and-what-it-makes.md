---

## 1. What Helix Pharma is

**Helix Pharma, Inc. is a US generic and specialty pharmaceutical manufacturer, incorporated in
Delaware and privately held.** It makes finished dosage forms, it makes them in the United States, and
it sells them into the United States and — for part of its range — into the European Union.

That one paragraph settles more regulatory questions than anything else in this phase, and it settles
them before the programme has assessed a single system. A company that makes **finished
pharmaceuticals** is a company that operates under **21 CFR Part 210** and **21 CFR Part 211**. Those
are its predicate rules. Everything else this phase determines — what Part 11 reaches, what enforcement
discretion is and is not, what applies through the European route and what does not apply at all —
follows from that fact or is measured against it.

The chapters that carry the regulatory determinations are `01.03`, `01.04` and `01.05`. This chapter
supplies the facts about the company that those determinations are made against, and it stops there.

## 2. What it makes

**Oral solid dose.** Immediate-release and modified-release tablets, and hard capsules. **Forty-one
marketed products.**

Helix makes no sterile injectable, no biologic, no cell or gene therapy product, no inhalation product
and no transdermal system. It compounds nothing to patient-specific order. It operates no clinical
trial and holds no investigational programme of its own, so no part of this repository is concerned
with good clinical practice or with the electronic-systems questions that arise in clinical
investigations.

The product mix matters to this programme for two reasons, and they pull in opposite directions.

**Oral solid dose manufacturing is comparatively simple in process terms, and the record set is
not.** Blending, granulation, compression, coating, packaging — a small number of unit operations, each
of them heavily instrumented, each of them generating data that ends up in a batch production and
control record under `21 CFR 211.188`. The record set the programme has to reason about is large and
dense even though the process is not exotic.

**Modified-release products push the analytical burden upward.** Dissolution profiles, in-process
testing and stability testing generate chromatographic and spectroscopic data that fall squarely inside
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` — *"a complete record of all data secured in the course of each test, including
all graphs, charts, and spectra from laboratory instrumentation"*. That citation is why the
chromatography data system is the system this programme will spend the most time on, and Phase 06 is
dedicated to it.

## 3. The three locations

| Site | What happens there | GxP activity |
|---|---|---|
| **Ridgemont, New Jersey** | Manufacturing, packaging, warehousing, and the primary QC laboratory | Yes — this is the registered manufacturing site and the site FDA inspected |
| **Cary, North Carolina** | A second QC laboratory performing stability and release testing | Yes |
| **Princeton, New Jersey** | A leased corporate office — commercial, finance, regulatory affairs administration | **No GxP activity** |

**Ridgemont is the site.** It holds the manufacturing licence position, the batch records, the release
decisions and the warehouse. It is the site FDA inspected from 2026-02-02 to 2026-02-13, and the
Form FDA 483 issued at the close of that inspection is `01.02`'s subject.

**Cary is a laboratory and nothing else.** It performs stability and release testing on product made at
Ridgemont. It generates laboratory records under `21 CFR 211.194(a)` and its results feed release
decisions taken by the quality unit. A programme that scoped itself to "the inspected site" would miss
Cary, and would miss it in exactly the record class the 483 raised.

**Princeton is named here so that it can be excluded once, in writing, rather than argued about
later.** No manufacturing, no testing, no batch record, no release decision, and no record required to
be maintained under a predicate rule is created or held there. Princeton appears in this repository
only as the place some of the programme's people sit.

## 4. The people

**1,140 employees.**

| Function | Headcount |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing and Packaging | 421 |
| Quality Control | 168 |
| Commercial, Finance and Administration | 165 |
| Supply Chain and Warehouse | 112 |
| Quality Assurance | 96 |
| Engineering and Maintenance | 84 |
| IT and Automation | 63 |
| Regulatory Affairs | 31 |
| **Total** | **1,140** |

Two figures in that table are load-bearing for this programme and are worth reading against each other.

**Quality Control and Quality Assurance together are two hundred and sixty-four people.** That is the
population whose day-to-day work produces, reviews and approves the records this programme is about.
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` requires *"the initials or signature of a second person showing that the
original records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established
standards"*, and `21 CFR 211.192` requires the quality unit to review and approve production and
control records. Those are people, doing a task, at a screen. Any control this programme designs that
those people cannot actually perform is a control that will not hold.

**IT and Automation is sixty-three people, and they do not own the records.** They own the platforms.
The distinction is not pedantry — it is the reason the Data Integrity Lead created by this programme
reports to Quality rather than to IT, which is `ADR-0002` and is argued in full at `01.07 §3`.

The twelve named programme roles, the quality unit's position, and who may not do what are `01.07`'s.
This chapter names nobody beyond the owner and approver of the document itself.

## 5. What a finished-pharmaceutical manufacturer is regulated under

**Helix operates under 21 CFR Part 210 and 21 CFR Part 211, and is inspected by FDA's Center for Drug
Evaluation and Research.**

| Instrument | Full title | What it does for Helix |
|---|---|---|
| **21 CFR Part 210** | Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Drugs: General | Sets the general CGMP frame and the status of the regulations |
| **21 CFR Part 211** | Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals | The operative CGMP rule for a finished dosage form manufacturer |

These are the **predicate rules**. The word is FDA's, from the 2003 Scope and Application guidance, and
it means the underlying requirement that makes a record a required record in the first place.
`01.04` owns the predicate rules in full and sets out the five sections this programme leans on —
`21 CFR 211.68`, `21 CFR 211.180(e)`, `21 CFR 211.188`, `21 CFR 211.192` and `21 CFR 211.194(a)`.

Two consequences are worth stating here because they shape everything downstream.

**The obligation to control computerised systems is a CGMP obligation before it is a Part 11
obligation.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires that *"appropriate controls shall be exercised over computer
or related systems to assure that changes in master production and control records or other records
are instituted only by authorized personnel"*, that input and output be checked for accuracy, and that
backup data be maintained and be secure from alteration, inadvertent erasure or loss. That obligation
sits on Helix as a drug manufacturer. It does not depend on Part 11, and `01.04 §3` explains why that
independence is the single most consequential fact in this phase.

**The inspecting centre is CDER.** Not CDRH. Not CBER. That is not an administrative detail, and
`01.05 §4` turns on it.

## 6. What Helix is not

**Helix Pharma makes no medical device. It holds no obligations under 21 CFR Part 820.**

It manufactures no device, no in-vitro diagnostic, no device constituent of a combination product, and
no software that meets the definition of a device. It is not registered as a device establishment. No
part of its quality system is operated under Part 820 or under ISO 13485.

That statement is short and it is doing a great deal of work. **Because Part 820 does not apply, FDA's
Computer Software Assurance guidance does not apply either** — that guidance is issued by CDRH and CBER,
its regulatory anchor is Part 820, and its scope sentence is qualified *"for medical devices"*
throughout. `01.05 §4` owns that consequence in full and this chapter does not restate it.

Helix is also not a federal information system, is not a federal contractor system, and holds no
government authorisation of any kind. Where this programme uses **NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5**, it is using a
control catalogue it chose. There is no FIPS 199 categorization here, no baseline, and no
authorization to operate, because none of those things applies to a privately held drug manufacturer.
`01.05 §8` says so once, properly.

## 7. The six GxP-critical platforms

Helix operates six platforms that the programme treats as GxP-critical at this vantage.

| Platform | What it does | Who supplies it |
|---|---|---|
| **Enterprise resource planning** | Materials, lot genealogy, warehouse transactions, release status flags | In-house implementation |
| **Manufacturing execution system, with electronic batch records** | The electronic batch record, in-process data capture, execution enforcement on the shop floor | In-house implementation, interfaced by Automation |
| **Laboratory information management system** | Sample lifecycle, specifications, result entry and laboratory workflow | **Calderon Vega Informatics** |
| **Chromatography data system** | Acquisition, integration, processing and reporting of chromatographic data | **Meridian Analytical Systems** |
| **Electronic quality management system** | Deviations, CAPA, change control, document control, training records | **Brightpath QMS, Inc.** — vendor-hosted |
| **Learning management system** | Training curricula, completion records, qualification status | In-house implementation |

Alongside these sit building management, environmental monitoring, calibration and warehouse
management systems, and the ordinary infrastructure of a manufacturing site.

Three points about that table, and then this chapter stops.

**A platform is not a Part 11 record.** Naming six platforms is not the same as saying six systems are
in Part 11 scope, and this repository never says "a Part 11 system" without saying which records make
it one. That is `ADR-0001`, argued at `01.03 §2`.

**A vendor supplies features that support compliance. It does not supply compliance.** There is no
Part 11 certification, no FDA-approved system and no FDA-validated software. Meridian, Calderon Vega
and Brightpath supply products with technical features — audit trail generation, signature
manifestation, access control — and what Helix does with those features, in its validated system, in
its intended use, under its own procedures, is where compliance lives or does not.

**The electronic quality management system is vendor-hosted, and that does not make it an open
system.** `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)` defines a closed system as *"an environment in which system access is
controlled by persons who are responsible for the content of electronic records that are on the
system."* The test is **who controls access**, not where the servers are. Whether Brightpath is closed
or open in Helix's configuration is a determination `01.03 §4` explains how to make and Phase 02 will
actually make.

## 8. Where the systems live

Helix runs **two on-premise data centres at Ridgemont** and **one co-located disaster-recovery site**.
Three of its systems are hosted by their vendors rather than by Helix.

That is the whole of what this chapter says about infrastructure, deliberately. **The system inventory
is Phase 02's and this phase does not count the estate.** A foundation chapter that publishes a system
count invites the reader to treat it as a scope statement, and it is not one: a count of computerised
systems is not a count of Part 11 systems, is not a count of GxP systems, and cannot be either until
somebody has determined, record by record, what each system holds. Phase 02 does that work in
`02-the-system-inventory-and-part-11-applicability`.

One infrastructure fact does belong here, because it is a fact about **records** rather than about
systems. `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires that *"a backup file of data entered into the computer or related
system shall be maintained"* and that backup arrangements be *"designed to assure that backup data are
exact and complete and that it is secure from alteration, inadvertent erasures, or loss."* A
disaster-recovery site is an availability arrangement. Whether it also discharges that CGMP
requirement is a separate question with a separate answer, and Phase 07 asks it.

## 9. What this chapter does not do

- It does not count the computerised estate, and it publishes no system inventory. **Phase 02.**
- It does not determine that any system is in Part 11 scope, or out of it. **Phase 02.**
- It does not assess risk, score anything, or apply the ALCOA attributes to anything. **Phase 03.**
- It does not state that any system is validated, or is not. **No validation has been performed under
  this programme at this vantage.**
- It does not describe any control, any audit trail configuration or any electronic signature
  deployment, because none has been implemented.
- It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind about Helix, its systems or its records.

What it establishes is the ground the rest of the phase stands on: a US finished-pharmaceutical
manufacturer, oral solid dose, forty-one products, three locations of which one has no GxP activity,
1,140 people, six GxP-critical platforms, regulated under Parts 210 and 211, inspected by CDER, and
holding no device obligations whatever.
