## 1. What the charter is, and what approved it

The charter is the instrument that gives the Helix Pharma Data Integrity Programme its scope, its
objectives, its money, its people and its end date. It was approved on **2026-03-06** by the Vice
President, Quality as executive sponsor, recorded at **`DEC-102`** and minuted at **`GOV-01`**. It may be
varied only by a further decision of the sponsor, minuted the same way.

The programme exists because FDA conducted a routine CGMP surveillance inspection of the Ridgemont site
and issued a Form FDA 483 at its close. `01.02-the-inspection-and-the-form-483.md` owns the inspection and
owns the statement that **a Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding**. This charter neither repeats nor
qualifies that; it records only that the programme was funded because of it.

## 2. Scope

The charter scopes the programme to **the electronic records Helix relies on to perform activities
regulated under 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211, and the computerised systems that hold them**.

| In scope | Why |
|---|---|
| The six GxP-critical platforms named at `01.01-the-company-and-what-it-makes.md` | They hold or produce the records the predicate rules require |
| The determination of **which records are Part 11 records** | `ADR-0001`. The determination is record by record, and it is **Phase 02's** to make |
| Computerised system validation to the standard **21 CFR 211.68(b)** requires of a drug manufacturer | The obligation is the predicate rule's and survives whatever FDA does about Part 11 — `01.04-the-predicate-rules-and-enforcement-discretion.md §3` |
| Access control, audit trail configuration and electronic signature deployment where the record determination reaches them | The controls at `21 CFR 11.10` attach to records, not to systems |
| Record review procedures under `21 CFR 211.192` and `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` | Both are review obligations the audit trail forms part of |
| The retention and availability of audit trail data under `21 CFR 11.10(e)` | Retention is stated in the regulation in terms of the underlying record's retention |
| Data lifecycle — creation, review, backup, archive, retrieval | `21 CFR 211.68(b)` on backup; `21 CFR 211.180(e)` on the use records are put to |
| Periodic review, internal audit and independent third-party audit of the remediated programme | Phases 08 and 09 |
| The commitments Helix made in its written response of 2026-03-06 | `OBJ-08` |

| Out of scope | Why |
|---|---|
| The four observations on the Form FDA 483 that do not concern electronic records | They are named once at `01.02` and this programme does not report on them. They are owned inside the quality system, not here |
| Any obligation under **21 CFR Part 820** | Helix makes no medical device. `01.05-what-does-not-apply.md` owns the determination |
| Products, formulations, processes and equipment qualification other than where a computerised system holds the record | The programme is about records and the systems that hold them |
| The Princeton corporate office | No GxP activity is performed there |
| Any statement about what a future inspection will find | The programme does not forecast inspection outcomes, at any vantage, in any document |

**Two boundaries are worth stating as boundaries.** First, the programme does not decide which records are
Part 11 records — it builds the method and Phase 02 applies it. Second, the programme owns no product
quality decision. Batch disposition remains where `21 CFR 211.22` puts it, with the quality unit, and
`01.07-roles-governance-and-the-quality-unit.md §2` states that position in full.

## 3. Objectives — `OBJ-01` to `OBJ-08`

Eight objectives, each with **a named individual owner** — `ADR-0008`, no objective is owned by a team —
and **a measure readable from evidence**. A measure is readable from evidence when a person holding the
artefact can score it without asking anybody how they feel about it.

| **`OBJ-02`** | Every GxP-critical platform holds an approved validation package covering its intended use | Colm Ó Braonáin | One approved package per platform, each carrying an intended-use statement and traceable to the obligation at `21 CFR 211.68(b)` | Phase 04, `MS-08` |

| **`OBJ-04`** | Audit trail functionality is configured, enabled and demonstrated on every system holding records determined to be Part 11 records, with the audit trail retained at least as long as the record | Joachim Reuter | A per-system configuration record, plus a dated retrieval demonstration witnessed by a named person not in the operating team | Phase 05, `MS-09` |
| **`OBJ-05`** | Shared and generic accounts are removed from systems holding GxP records — or, where a technical constraint prevents removal, the constraint is documented and a named individual owns the compensating control | Amara Sissoko | The account register shows every shared account either closed with a date, or open with a documented constraint and a named compensating-control owner. No third state | Phase 05, `MS-09` |

**No objective in this set is measured by an opinion.** There is no objective whose measure is a regulator's
view, an auditor's view, a consultant's assessment of maturity, or a score on a scale somebody sells. Each
of the eight is scored from an artefact that either exists or does not.

## 4. Success criteria

| ID | Criterion | Scored from |
|---|---|---|
| `SUC-1` | Every one of the six GxP-critical platforms carries a record-level Part 11 determination with a named determiner | `OBJ-01`, `OBJ-03` |
| `SUC-2` | Every system holding records determined to be Part 11 records has an audit trail that has been demonstrated, not merely asserted | `OBJ-04` |
| `SUC-3` | Both record review procedures state the audit trail position expressly | `OBJ-06` |
| `SUC-4` | The programme closes within the envelope, with the variance stated | `OBJ-07` |
| `SUC-5` | Every commitment in the 2026-03-06 response is closed with evidence, or is open with a named owner and a date | `OBJ-08` |

**There is no sixth criterion, and its absence is deliberate.** The obvious candidate — an inspection that
passes without observations on electronic records — is not a criterion, is not an objective, and is not a
target. `01.10-what-this-programme-will-and-will-not-do.md` carries the standing refusal to forecast an
inspection outcome, and this charter is bound by it.

**$188,000** is unallocated headroom held by the sponsor and is not a contingency line; the contingency
line is inside the committed total.

Three observations on the shape of it.

**Internal labour is the largest line and it is not the largest risk.** It funds the 9.2 FTE-equivalents,
and those are people who already work at Helix and already have day jobs. The line buys their time; it does
not create capacity. `PR-02` carries what happens if the time is not released.

**The advisory line and the audit line are deliberately not the same firm.** Thorne Delacroix Consulting,
LLC designs and drafts. Vashti Okonjo & Partners audits. `ADR-0004` fixes the separation and
`01.07-roles-governance-and-the-quality-unit.md §4` states who may not do what. Combining them would have
saved money on both lines and produced an audit of the auditor's own drafting.

**Contingency of $56,000 is small against a committed $3,412,000, and the headroom outside the committed
total is what actually absorbs a surprise.** The two figures are held by different people on purpose: the
programme manager may draw on contingency, and only the sponsor may release headroom. `PR-01` carries the
case where both are needed.

No figure in this section is a fine, a penalty, a sanction or a forfeiture. **None of those exists anywhere
in this programme**, and professional fees are not one.

## 7. Schedule

**424 days from 2026-03-02 to 2027-04-30.** The milestone set `MS-01` to `MS-13` sits at
`01.11-the-programme-calendar.md §6` with dates, and the recurring commitments the programme creates sit at
`01.11 §3` as `CAL-01` to `CAL-12`.

Three sequencing rules are fixed by the charter and are not the programme's to reorder for convenience.

1. **The record determination comes before the control work.** A control implemented on a system nobody has
   determined holds Part 11 records is a control chosen before the question was asked. `ADR-0001`.
2. **The risk assessment comes before the validation framework.** Phase 03 before Phase 04, so that the
   framework's rigour is set against assessed risk rather than against habit.
3. **The independent third-party audit comes after the internal audit of the remediated programme, and both
   come after remediation.** Auditing an unfinished remediation produces a report about a building site.

## 8. What the charter does not contain

This section exists because absences in a charter are usually invisible, and these three were argued about.

**No target for a future inspection outcome.** Not as an objective, not as a success criterion, not as a
milestone, not as a sentence anywhere. The programme cannot commit to what an investigator will observe,
and a charter that pretends otherwise has set a target it cannot score and has invited every subsequent
document to write toward it.

**No objective whose measure is somebody else's opinion.** Every measure in §3 is scored from an artefact.
Nothing is scored from a maturity rating, an advisory firm's assessment of progress, or a favourable
verbal indication from anybody.

**No claim of certification, and none is available.** There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11
certificate and no FDA approval, validation or pre-evaluation of any software product Helix runs.
`01.03-what-part-11-actually-is.md` states the position. A vendor may legitimately say a product has
**features that support** compliance; compliance is a property of **Helix's validated system in its
intended use**, and the charter buys the validation, not a badge.

## 9. Governance of the charter

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who may vary scope? | The executive sponsor, by minuted decision |
| Who may vary the envelope? | The executive sponsor. Headroom is released by the sponsor; contingency is drawn by the programme manager and reported |
| Who may vary an objective's measure? | Nobody, during the programme. A measure that changes mid-flight is a measure that was chosen to be met |
| Where is variance reported? | `CAL-01`, the monthly Data Integrity Steering Committee, and quarterly at `CAL-04` |
| What happens at close-out? | The standing obligations at `logs/regulatory-register.md` do not close with the programme. `IS-07` records that they have no named owner beyond 2027-04-30 |

`IS-07` is the charter's own defect and is recorded here rather than left for somebody else to find. The
charter creates obligations that outlive it and does not say who holds them afterwards.
