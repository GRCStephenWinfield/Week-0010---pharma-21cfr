*Phase 01 speaks as at **2026-05-29**. Helix Pharma and its programme are fictional and illustrative.
**The regulations, guidances and standards assessed here are real** — 21 CFR Parts 210, 211, 11 and
820, FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance, EU GMP Annex 11, ISPE's GAMP 5 and NIST SP 800-53 —
and every statement about their scope, issuing body and version is drawn from the documents
themselves.*

---

## 1. Four assessed, and two of them do not apply

Most compliance programmes publish what applies. **This one publishes what does not, because that is
where the money is lost.** A programme that quietly assumes a framework applies will build controls
against it, evidence them, audit them and defend them, and will never be asked to justify the premise.
A programme that assumes a framework does not apply, and is wrong, discovers it during an inspection.
Either error is expensive and only one of them is visible from inside.

Four instruments were assessed for applicability to Helix Pharma. **Two apply. Two do not.**

| # | Assessed | Determination | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **21 CFR Parts 210 and 211** — drug CGMP | **APPLIES** in full | Helix manufactures finished pharmaceuticals in the United States |
| 2 | **21 CFR Part 11** — Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures | **APPLIES to a subset of records**, not to systems | Predicate-rule records maintained electronically in place of paper, or relied on to perform regulated activities. **Which records is Phase 02's determination** |
| 3 | **21 CFR Part 820** — Quality System Regulation for medical devices | **DOES NOT APPLY** | Helix manufactures no medical device, no in-vitro diagnostic and no device constituent of a combination product |
| 4 | **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance** | **DOES NOT APPLY** | It is device guidance anchored on Part 820, issued by CDRH and CBER. Its application to Helix falls with row 3 |

A fifth instrument sits outside that frame because it reaches Helix by a different route entirely:
**EU GMP Annex 11 applies to the EU-facing products through the Qualified Person, and not through
FDA.** `§6`.

The determination was approved on **2026-04-24** and the governance record of that approval is
`governance/GOV-04-regulatory-scoping-determination-approval.md`. The decision is `DEC-111`. The
separate decision to adopt the CSA guidance voluntarily is `DEC-112`, and its reasoning is `ADR-0005`.
**The scoping determination itself has no ADR**, and this chapter does not imply one.

## 2. 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 apply

Stated here for completeness and argued at `01.04`. Helix makes finished dosage forms and is therefore
subject to the CGMP regulations for finished pharmaceuticals, inspected by **CDER**. The five sections
this programme leans on — `21 CFR 211.68`, `21 CFR 211.180(e)`, `21 CFR 211.188`, `21 CFR 211.192` and
`21 CFR 211.194(a)` — are `01.04 §2`'s.

**Nothing in this chapter qualifies that.** Where a later section says an instrument does not apply, it
means that instrument and nothing else. Parts 210 and 211 are not reduced by anything below.

## 3. 21 CFR Part 11 applies to a subset of records

Also stated here for completeness and argued at `01.03`. **Part 11 applies to records, not to systems**
— `ADR-0001`. It reaches records required by a predicate rule that are maintained electronically in
place of paper, or maintained electronically in addition to paper and relied on to perform regulated
activities, or submitted to FDA electronically, together with electronic signatures that stand in for
signatures the predicate rules require.

**Which Helix records fall into those categories has not been determined.** No system inventory exists,
no applicability determination has been made for any system, and **this phase publishes neither**.
Phase 02 does that work in `02-the-system-inventory-and-part-11-applicability`. The open question this
phase leaves on the record determination is carried as `IS-02` and is stated at `01.13`.

## 4. 21 CFR Part 820 does not apply — and therefore neither does FDA's CSA guidance

**This section owns that consequence for the whole repository. It is the most important paragraph in
this phase and almost every industry summary gets it wrong.**

### First, Part 820

**Helix Pharma manufactures no medical device.** It makes no device, no in-vitro diagnostic, no device
constituent of a combination product, and no software function that meets the definition of a device
under section 201(h) of the Federal Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act. It is not registered as a device
establishment. **21 CFR Part 820 — the quality system regulation for medical devices, which incorporates
the 2016 edition of ISO 13485 by reference — imposes no obligation on Helix whatever.**

That is not a marginal call and there is nothing to weigh. Oral solid dose tablets and hard capsules
are drugs.

### Second, the consequence

**FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance. It does not apply to Helix.**

| Property | Position |
|---|---|
| **Exact title** | *"Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality Management System Software — Guidance for Industry and Food and Drug Administration Staff"*. Note **"Quality Management System"**, not "Quality System" |
| **Issued** | **2026-02-03** |
| **What it superseded** | Verbatim: *"This document supersedes 'Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality System Software,' issued September 24, 2025."* It also supersedes **Section 6 only** of the 2002 *General Principles of Software Validation*, which otherwise remains in force |
| **Issuing centres** | **CDRH and CBER** — **not CDER**, which is the centre that inspects Helix |
| **Regulatory anchor** | **21 CFR Part 820**, with ISO 13485:2016 incorporated by reference |
| **Scope**, verbatim | *"This guidance provides recommendations regarding computer software assurance for computers or automated data processing systems used as part of production or the quality management system for **medical devices**."* |
| **Parts 210 and 211** | **Not mentioned anywhere in it** |

Every scope sentence in the document is qualified *"for medical devices"* or *"of the medical device"*.
The centres that issued it are not the centre that regulates Helix. The regulation it is anchored on
does not reach Helix. The parts that do reach Helix do not appear in it.

**Helix Pharma is a drug manufacturer under Parts 210 and 211. FDA's CSA guidance does not apply to it,
and this repository states that plainly rather than pretending otherwise.**

The common industry claim that "CSA replaces CSV" is wrong twice over for a company like Helix. It is
device guidance, and even within its own scope it supersedes **Section 6** of the 2002 software
validation guidance — not software validation generally, and not anything at all in Part 211.

**Helix's actual obligation for computerised system validation arises under `21 CFR 211.68`** and,
where electronic records stand in place of paper, under Part 11 as scoped at `01.03`. That is the
citation every validation deliverable in this programme will carry.

## 5. What Helix does with CSA anyway, and how it says so

**Helix adopts the risk-based assurance thinking in FDA's CSA guidance voluntarily, as good practice.
It is labelled as adopted rather than required, every time it is cited.** That is `ADR-0005`,
`DEC-112`, decided 2026-04-24.

The honest position has two halves and the repository publishes both. The guidance does not apply. The
thinking in it is good, it is publicly available, and a drug manufacturer that refuses to read a
well-made document because it is addressed to somebody else is being precious rather than rigorous.

What Helix takes from it:

**The risk anchor, stated correctly.** CSA's risk test is whether **failure to perform as intended may
result in a quality problem that foreseeably compromises safety**. It is not business criticality, it is
not a GxP-versus-non-GxP sort, and it is not patient impact in a loose sense. Helix will apply an
analogous test framed against **drug** product quality and patient safety under Parts 210 and 211,
because a device safety anchor cannot simply be lifted across.

**The correction to conventional wisdom.** The usual summary — unscripted testing for low-risk systems,
scripted testing for high-risk systems — is not what the guidance says. It contemplates that
**unscripted testing may be better suited to assure the software performs as intended even for high
process risk features**. The named unscripted techniques are **scenario testing, exploratory testing and
error guessing**. Helix will not reproduce the oversimplified version.

**Assurance effort proportionate to risk, with the evidence record sized accordingly.** The idea that
the volume of documentation should follow the risk rather than the habit is the part with the most
practical value, and it is the part a validation programme is most likely to lose under time pressure.

What Helix does **not** take from it:

- **No obligation.** No control in this programme is justified by CSA. No deliverable cites it as its
  requirement. Where it appears in a validation rationale, it appears as **adopted good practice** and
  the requirement cited alongside it is `21 CFR 211.68`.
- **No device vocabulary.** Helix does not describe its systems as production or quality management
  system software for medical devices, because they are not.
- **No claim of alignment to a scheme Helix is not in.** Helix does not say it "follows CSA" as though
  that were a compliance status. There is no such status.

**The rule the repository keeps: every citation of the CSA guidance in this repository is accompanied,
in the same sentence or the one after it, by the statement that it is device guidance adopted
voluntarily.** No exceptions, in any phase.

## 6. EU GMP Annex 11 reaches the EU-facing products, through the Qualified Person

Part of Helix's range is supplied into the European Union. For those products, **EU GMP applies through
the European route** — through the marketing authorisation, the EU importer and the **Qualified Person**.

**Helix does not have a Qualified Person of its own, and could not have one.** Helix is a US site with no
EU manufacturing or import authorisation. The QP function belongs to **the holder of the EU manufacturing
and import authorisation for the EU-facing products**, and **Dr Anselm Ferrão acts for that authorisation
holder, not for Helix.** What Helix has is a contractual and audit relationship with the holder, and the
Annex 11 expectations reach Helix through that relationship rather than as a direct obligation discharged
by a Helix role. `01.08-obligations-and-the-regulatory-register.md` records `O12` on that basis, with a
Helix-side owner.

Four further things need saying precisely.

**It does not reach Helix through FDA.** FDA does not enforce EudraLex. An FDA inspection is not an
Annex 11 inspection and an Annex 11 expectation is not an FDA requirement. Conflating the two produces
a control set nobody asked for and a set of citations that will not survive an inspector's question
about where the requirement comes from.

**The Qualified Person is not a US regulatory role.** The QP's certification duties arise under EU law
and are owed within the EU framework. Dr Ferrão is named here because the Annex 11 route runs through
that role, and he is never described as holding any US regulatory position.

**Only the January 2011 version is in force.** Annex 11 — Computerised Systems — was revised in January
2011 with a deadline for coming into operation of 30 June 2011, and that is the text that binds. Its
seventeen sections cover risk management, personnel, suppliers and service providers, validation, data,
accuracy checks, data storage, printouts, audit trails, change and configuration management, periodic
evaluation, security, incident management, electronic signature, batch release, business continuity and
archiving.

**A revision is in consultation and is not binding.** A draft revision of Annex 11 was consulted on
alongside a revision of Chapter 4 and a **new draft Annex 22 on artificial intelligence**; the
consultation closed on 2025-10-07 and no revised text has been adopted or published in final form.
**Nothing in this repository cites a draft as a requirement**, and nothing in it assumes an adoption
date. Where the draft is worth watching — and it is, because it addresses cloud services,
vendor-operated critical systems and data in motion and at rest — it is watched as a horizon item in
the programme calendar at `01.11`, not as an obligation in the register at `01.08`.

## 7. Three things that are not regulations at all

The three below are used by this programme and are not obligations. The distinction is not
housekeeping: an auditor who is told that a control exists "because GAMP 5 requires it" has been given
a false citation, and the programme that gave it has lost the ability to explain what its controls are
actually for.

### GAMP 5 — ISPE good-practice guidance

**"ISPE GAMP 5: A Risk-Based Approach to Compliant GxP Computerized Systems (Second Edition)", ISPE,
July 2022.**

GAMP is an **ISPE Community of Practice**. The Guide is **not a regulation, not a standard, and not an
FDA requirement**. ISPE's own words: it *"does not define a prescriptive method or a standard, but
rather provides pragmatic guidance."* **This repository never writes "as required by GAMP 5"**, in any
phase, for any control.

Helix uses the GAMP 5 framework as industry good practice in support of obligations that arise
elsewhere — under `21 CFR 211.68` and, for Part 11 records, under Part 11.

On the software categories, this repository says only what is verifiable from primary material. The
Second Edition treats **categories three, four and five as a continuum rather than as rigid bins**,
emphasising critical thinking over mechanical categorisation. **This repository publishes no category
count and no enumeration of the categories**, because the Guide is a paid publication and either taken
from secondary commentary would be a claim without a source. Where a category has to be referred to in a
later phase, it is described in words.

### NIST SP 800-53 — a control catalogue Helix chose

**NIST Special Publication 800-53, Revision 5, "Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems
and Organizations", Release 5.2.0, published 2025-08-27.**

Release 5.2.0 added three controls — **SA-15(13) Logging Syntax**, **SA-24 Design for Cyber
Resiliency** and **SI-2(7) Root Cause Analysis** — and **none of the three was added to any SP 800-53B
baseline**.

| Position | Statement |
|---|---|
| What it is | **A catalogue.** The baselines live in a separate publication, **SP 800-53B**, and they are defined by **FIPS 199 impact level, for federal systems** |
| Why Helix uses it | Because a published, maintained, widely understood control catalogue is better than one a programme invents for itself, and because the access, audit and identification control families map usefully onto the technical work this programme has to do |
| **What Helix is not** | **Helix is not a federal system.** There is **no FIPS 199 categorization**, **no baseline selection**, **no authorization to operate** and **no authorizing official** in this programme, and there never will be |
| **What does not exist** | **There is no FDA, GxP or Part 11 baseline or overlay in SP 800-53B.** Any mapping between Part 11 sections and 800-53 controls that appears in a later phase is **this programme's own mapping**, presented as such |
| **What must never be claimed** | Implementing an 800-53 control does not satisfy a Part 11 requirement, and satisfying a Part 11 requirement does not implement an 800-53 control. The two are read side by side and neither substitutes for the other |

There is also a genuine tension worth recording rather than papering over. `21 CFR 11.300(b)` requires
that identification code and password issuances be *"periodically checked, recalled, or revised (e.g.,
to cover such events as password aging)"* and specifies no interval. Current NIST thinking on
authenticators discourages arbitrary periodic password rotation. **Those two positions have to be
reconciled by a Helix procedural decision rather than by pretending they agree**, and the decision is
Phase 05's to take and to justify.

### PIC/S PI 041-1 and MHRA's guidance — reference material

**PIC/S PI 041-1, "Good Practices for Data Management and Integrity in Regulated GMP/GDP
Environments", 1 July 2021**, and **MHRA's "'GXP' Data Integrity Guidance and Definitions, Revision 1:
March 2018"**, are read by this programme as reference material. Neither is an FDA requirement and
neither is cited in this repository as a source of obligation. Note the PIC/S naming — **PI 041-1**, not
"PI-041" and not a later revision number. The four additional data attributes MHRA sets out are
attributed to MHRA wherever they are used, per `01.04 §6`.

## 8. What this determination does not do

- It does not determine that any Helix record is, or is not, a Part 11 record. **Phase 02.**
- It does not assess any system, score any risk or apply any attribute. **Phase 03.**
- It does not describe a validation approach or state that anything is validated. **Phase 04, and
  nothing has been validated under this programme.**
- It does not claim that Helix meets, or fails to meet, any requirement in any instrument named above.
- It reaches **no legal conclusion of any kind**, and it forecasts nothing about any future inspection.

**And it is not permanent.** A scoping determination is true of a company as it is. If Helix ever
acquired a device business, took on a combination product, or began supplying a market with its own
computerised-systems expectations, row 3 and row 4 of `§1` would need to be taken again. The
determination is therefore carried as a standing review item in the programme calendar at `01.11`, and
the assumption that Helix's product portfolio remains device-free is recorded as an assumption in the
register at `01.12` rather than left implicit.
