*Phase 01 speaks as at **2026-05-29**. Helix Pharma and its programme are fictional and illustrative.
**21 CFR Parts 210 and 211, the August 2003 Scope and Application guidance and the December 2018 drug
CGMP data integrity guidance are real**, and everything this chapter says about them is drawn from
those documents. Where they are quoted, they are quoted exactly.*

---

## 1. What a predicate rule is

**A predicate rule is the regulation that made the record a required record in the first place.**

The term is FDA's own, defined in the August 2003 Scope and Application guidance:

> *"The underlying requirements set forth in the Act, PHS Act, and FDA regulations (other than part 11)
> are referred to in this guidance document as predicate rules."*

The relationship between a predicate rule and Part 11 is a relationship of dependency running one way.
**The predicate rule says a record must exist and what it must contain. Part 11 says what conditions an
electronic version of that record must meet before FDA will treat it as trustworthy and reliable.**
Remove the predicate rule and there is no Part 11 record, because there is no required record. Remove
Part 11 and the predicate rule stands untouched.

That asymmetry is the whole of `§3` below, and it is the single most consequential idea in this phase.

**For Helix, the predicate rules are 21 CFR Part 210 and 21 CFR Part 211.**

| Part | Title | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **21 CFR Part 210** | Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Drugs: General | The general CGMP frame and the status of the regulations |
| **21 CFR Part 211** | Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals | The operative CGMP rule for a finished dosage form manufacturer |

Helix makes finished pharmaceuticals — oral solid dose, forty-one marketed products, at the Ridgemont
site, with a second QC laboratory at Cary. `01.01` sets that out. It follows without further argument
that Parts 210 and 211 apply, and that CDER is the inspecting centre.

## 2. The five sections this programme leans on

Part 211 is long. Five of its sections do most of the work in a data integrity programme, and this
repository cites them exactly and repeatedly.

| Citation | Subject | Why this programme leans on it |
|---|---|---|
| **`21 CFR 211.68`** | Automatic, mechanical, and electronic equipment | The obligation to control computerised systems, check input and output, and maintain backup — **and the surviving validation hook**. `§3` and `§5` |
| **`21 CFR 211.180(e)`** | Record retention and the annual product quality evaluation | Records must be maintained so the data in them can be used to evaluate product quality at least annually |
| **`21 CFR 211.188`** | Batch production and control records | What a batch record must contain, including identification of the persons performing and checking each significant step |
| **`21 CFR 211.192`** | Production record review | The quality unit must review and approve production and control records |
| **`21 CFR 211.194(a)`** | Laboratory records | What laboratory records must contain, including all data secured in the course of each test — and the second-person review at `(a)(8)` |

### `21 CFR 211.68` — the section this programme is built on

`21 CFR 211.68(a)` permits the use of automatic, mechanical or electronic equipment, **including
computers**, in the manufacture, processing, packing and holding of a drug product, and requires that
such equipment be *"routinely calibrated, inspected, or checked according to a written program designed
to assure proper performance"*, with written records of those checks maintained.

`21 CFR 211.68(b)` is the paragraph that carries the programme:

> *"Appropriate controls shall be exercised over computer or related systems to assure that changes in
> master production and control records or other records are instituted only by authorized personnel.
> Input to and output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or data shall
> be checked for accuracy. The degree and frequency of input/output verification shall be based on the
> complexity and reliability of the computer or related system. A backup file of data entered into the
> computer or related system shall be maintained except where certain data, such as calculations
> performed in connection with laboratory analysis, are eliminated by computerization or other
> automated processes. In such instances a written record of the program shall be maintained along with
> appropriate validation data. Hard copy or alternative systems, such as duplicates, tapes, or
> microfilm, designed to assure that backup data are exact and complete and that it is secure from
> alteration, inadvertent erasures, or loss shall be maintained."*

Four obligations sit in that paragraph and each of them is a control this programme will have to
evidence: **change control by authorised personnel**, **accuracy checking of input and output**,
**maintenance of a backup file**, and **appropriate validation data**. FDA's 2018 guidance ties its
definition of backup expressly to `21 CFR 211.68(b)` and reads it as **a true copy of the original
record maintained securely throughout the record retention period**, exact, complete and secure from
alteration, inadvertent erasure or loss. That is narrower than the ordinary IT sense of the word: a
rolling disaster-recovery snapshot is an availability arrangement and not, on its own, a CGMP backup.
Phase 07 does that work.

`21 CFR 211.68(c)` is worth knowing because it is usually overlooked: automated equipment used for
operations addressed by `21 CFR 211.101(c)` or `(d)`, `21 CFR 211.103`, `21 CFR 211.182` or
`21 CFR 211.188(b)(11)` **can satisfy the requirement that one person perform and another check**,
where the equipment is used in conformity with the section and **one person checks that the equipment
properly performed the operation**. Any electronic signature or second-review workflow this programme
eventually designs has to be designed against that paragraph rather than around it.

## 3. Enforcement discretion is not exemption

**This section owns that argument for the whole repository, and it is the argument the repository
exists to make. `ADR-0003` records the decision.**

FDA's August 2003 guidance — *"Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and
Application"* — states FDA's intention to exercise enforcement discretion over specified Part 11
requirements. That guidance remains **FDA's current, final, in-force guidance on Part 11 scope**. It
has not been withdrawn, replaced or reissued, and the guidance itself states that **part 11 remains in
effect**.

**What is under enforcement discretion:**

| Part 11 requirement | Subject |
|---|---|
| `21 CFR 11.10(a)` and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Validation of computerised systems** |
| `21 CFR 11.10(e)` and `(k)(2)`, and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails** |
| All Part 11 requirements, for systems **operational before 1997-08-20** | **Legacy systems** |
| `21 CFR 11.10(b)` and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Generating copies of records** |
| `21 CFR 11.10(c)` and corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30` | **Protection of records to enable accurate and ready retrieval throughout the retention period** |

**What FDA says it will enforce**, in its own words:

> *"We intend to enforce all other provisions of part 11 including, but not limited to, certain controls
> for closed systems in § 11.10."*

and, of that set:

> *"We expect continued compliance with these provisions, and we will continue to enforce them."*

The provisions FDA lists as enforced include limiting system access to authorised individuals,
operational system checks, authority checks, device checks, the education, training and experience
determination, written accountability policies, controls over systems documentation, the corresponding
controls for open systems under `21 CFR 11.30`, and **all of the electronic signature requirements —
`21 CFR 11.50`, `21 CFR 11.70`, `21 CFR 11.100`, `21 CFR 11.200` and `21 CFR 11.300`**.

### Now the part that matters

**Enforcement discretion is a stated intention of an agency. It is not a legal exemption, it is not a
safe harbour, and it confers no rights.**

The 2003 guidance carries FDA's standard guidance disclaimer on its face — *"It does not create or
confer any rights for or on any person and does not operate to bind FDA or the public"* — and it is
marked **Contains Nonbinding Recommendations**. An intention is a thing an agency holds today and may
revise. It is not a permission, it does not amend the Code of Federal Regulations, and `21 CFR 11.10(a)`
and `21 CFR 11.10(e)` are still printed in the regulation exactly as they were in 1997.

**And the predicate rule obligation survives it entirely.** The 2003 guidance says so expressly:

> *"Persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements for validation."*

> *"Persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements related to documentation
> of, for example, date, time, or sequencing of events."*

> *"Persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements for record retention and
> availability."*

and, more broadly:

> *"We will enforce all predicate rule requirements, including predicate rule record and recordkeeping
> requirements."*

> *"records must still be maintained or submitted in accordance with the underlying predicate rules,
> and the Agency can take regulatory action for noncompliance with such predicate rules."*

**For a drug manufacturer, the surviving validation hook is `21 CFR 211.68`.** That is a regulation, not
a guidance. FDA has announced no discretion over it and could not exercise discretion over it without
saying so. **`21 CFR 211.68` requires Helix to exercise appropriate controls over its computerised
systems and to hold appropriate validation data whatever FDA does about Part 11.**

**A programme that reads enforcement discretion as permission has read the wrong document.** The
sentence is blunt on purpose. The 2003 guidance is a scope document; it tells a reader which of Part
11's requirements FDA currently intends to press. It says nothing at all about whether a drug
manufacturer must validate its systems, because that question is answered in Part 211, and the guidance
points the reader there in terms.

`ADR-0003` therefore records the programme's position in one line: **enforcement discretion is
recorded as a fact about FDA's stated intentions and is never used as a reason to do less.** Where this
repository notes that a requirement sits inside the discretion set, the note is descriptive. It is
never a justification, never a control rationale, and never an entry in a risk acceptance.

## 4. The 2003 guidance, read correctly

| Property | Position |
|---|---|
| **Title** | *"Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and Application"* |
| **Date on the document** | **August 2003.** FDA's guidance database lists the issue as September 2003 and the Federal Register notice of availability was published 5 September 2003. Calling it the August 2003 guidance is correct and standard |
| **Status** | **Final. In force.** Not withdrawn, not replaced, not reissued |
| **Legal weight** | **Nonbinding.** It represents FDA's current thinking; it does not create or confer rights and does not bind FDA or the public |
| **What it did** | Announced the **narrow interpretation** of Part 11's scope and the enforcement discretion set above, while stating that part 11 remains in effect |

The narrow interpretation itself — the four categories of record Part 11 reaches, and the three
conjunctive conditions of the paper-printout test — is `01.03 §2` and `01.03 §7`. This chapter does not
restate it.

## 5. The obligation that does not move

Set the two instruments side by side and the asymmetry is obvious.

| Question | Under Part 11 | Under the predicate rule |
|---|---|---|
| Must Helix validate its computerised systems? | `21 CFR 11.10(a)` requires it. FDA has stated an intention to exercise enforcement discretion | **`21 CFR 211.68` requires appropriate controls and appropriate validation data. No discretion has been announced** |
| Must Helix keep audit trails? | `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires them. FDA has stated an intention to exercise enforcement discretion | **`21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires that changes be instituted only by authorised personnel; `21 CFR 211.188` and `21 CFR 211.194(a)` require the record content that makes change visible** |
| Must Helix be able to produce complete copies for FDA? | `21 CFR 11.10(b)` requires it. Discretion stated | **`21 CFR 211.180(c)` requires that all records required under Part 211, or copies of them, be readily available for authorised inspection during the retention period at the establishment where the activities occurred** |
| Must Helix protect records through the retention period? | `21 CFR 11.10(c)` requires it. Discretion stated | **`21 CFR 211.180` sets the retention requirements; `21 CFR 211.68(b)` sets the backup requirement** |

**Every row that shows discretion on the left shows an unmoved obligation on the right.** That is not a
coincidence and it is not a coincidence FDA left to chance — the 2003 guidance says the predicate rules
survive in four separate places.

The practical consequence for this programme is that **the discretion set changes almost nothing about
what Helix has to do, and changes something real about where the obligation is cited from.** A
validation deliverable in this programme will be justified under `21 CFR 211.68`, and Part 11 will be
cited where Part 11 is the source. `01.08` builds the obligations register `O1` to `O12` on exactly that
basis: every obligation carries the exact citation it arises from.

## 6. The 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance

**"Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP: Questions and Answers", December 2018.** It applies to
**21 CFR Parts 210, 211 and 212** — drug CGMP. **This is the guidance a drug manufacturer works to**, and
it is the guidance Helix works to.

It supplies the definitions Part 11 does not contain, and `01.03 §3` refers the reader here for them.

| Term | FDA's definition, in the 2018 guidance |
|---|---|
| **Data integrity** | *"the completeness, consistency, and accuracy of data"* |
| **Audit trail** | *"a secure, computer-generated, time-stamped electronic record that allows for reconstruction of the course of events relating to the creation, modification, or deletion of an electronic record"* |
| **Metadata** | *"the contextual information required to understand data"* — often described as data about data |
| **Backup** | Tied to `21 CFR 211.68(b)`: a true copy of the original record maintained securely throughout the record retention period, exact, complete and secure from alteration, inadvertent erasure or loss |
| **Static** | *"a fixed-data record such as a paper record or an electronic image"* |
| **Dynamic** | *"the record format allows interaction between the user and the record content"* |

### ALCOA, attributed correctly

**FDA uses the plain five-letter acronym.** In the 2018 guidance:

> *"Data integrity refers to the completeness, consistency, and accuracy of data. Complete, consistent,
> and accurate data should be attributable, legible, contemporaneously recorded, original or a true
> copy, and accurate (ALCOA)."*

Note **"original or a true copy"**, not simply "original". Rendering the O as "original" alone drops
FDA's own qualifier, and this repository does not drop it.

**The four additional attributes — complete, consistent, enduring, available — are not FDA's.** They are
set out by **MHRA** in its *"'GXP' Data Integrity Guidance and Definitions, Revision 1: March 2018"*,
which expressly says that it *"refers to the acronym ALCOA rather than 'ALCOA +'"* and that the plus
*"has been subsequently added to emphasise the requirements."* The body most often credited with
ALCOA+ deprecates the term.

**This repository names no originator for either acronym**, because no primary source names one. Where
the four additional attributes are used, they are attributed to MHRA's guidance and are not presented
as FDA's. The ALCOA assessment itself is **Phase 03** and nothing in this phase applies the attributes
to anything.

## 7. Audit trail review, and where the obligation actually comes from

**Part 11 imposes no audit trail review requirement.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires audit trails to be
generated, retained for at least as long as the subject records, and available for agency review and
copying. It says nothing about internal review, nothing about a second person, and nothing about
frequency.

**The review obligation comes from the predicate rules**, and FDA sets out how in the 2018 guidance:

> *"Personnel responsible for record review under CGMP should review the audit trails that capture
> changes to data associated with the record as they review the rest of the record."*

> *"For example, all production and control records, which includes audit trails, must be reviewed and
> approved by the quality unit (§ 211.192)."*

FDA's construction is that **the audit trail is part of the record being reviewed**. The obligation
therefore reaches it through the existing review requirement rather than through a new one.

| Record class | The review obligation | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| **Laboratory records** | *"The initials or signature of a second person showing that the original records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established standards"* | **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`** |
| **Production and control records** | Review and approval by the quality unit | **`21 CFR 211.192`** |
| **Operations under `21 CFR 211.101(c)`/`(d)`, `211.103`, `211.182`, `211.188(b)(11)`** | Automated equipment may satisfy the perform-and-check requirement where one person checks that the equipment properly performed the operation | **`21 CFR 211.68(c)`** |

**On frequency, the industry shorthand is wrong.** *"Audit trails must be reviewed quarterly"* is not
what FDA says. FDA's position is:

> *"If the review frequency for the data is specified in CGMP regulations, adhere to that frequency for
> the audit trail review."*

The default is therefore **event-driven and tied to the review frequency of the underlying data** —
review at batch release, review after each significant step, review of the laboratory record when the
laboratory record is reviewed. **Risk-based periodicity applies only where CGMP is silent about the
underlying data**, and in that case FDA directs the firm to determine the frequency using knowledge of
its processes and risk assessment tools.

That distinction is directly relevant to `OBS-2` at `01.02 §4`, and the design work it implies belongs
to Phase 05 and Phase 08. **This chapter states the obligation. It states nothing about how Helix
currently discharges it.**

## 8. What this chapter does not do

- It does not determine which Helix records are Part 11 records. **Phase 02.**
- It applies no ALCOA attribute to anything and performs no risk assessment. **Phase 03.**
- It describes no validation approach, no validation plan and no validation result. **Phase 04, and
  nothing has been validated under this programme.**
- It sets no audit trail review frequency for any Helix system. **Phase 05 and Phase 08.**
- It states nothing about the regulations and guidances that were assessed and do not apply — that is
  `01.05`, and it includes the one that catches most readers.
- It reaches **no legal conclusion of any kind**, and forecasts nothing about any future inspection.
