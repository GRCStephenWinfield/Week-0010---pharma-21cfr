*Phase 01 speaks as at **2026-05-29**. Helix Pharma, Inc., its sites, its people, its systems, its
inspection and every figure in this repository are fictional and illustrative. Where a statement is
made about a real regulation, guidance or standard — 21 CFR Parts 11, 210, 211 and 820, FDA's
guidances, EU GMP Annex 11, ISPE's GAMP 5, NIST SP 800-53 — it is real, it is cited exactly, and the
two kinds of statement are kept visibly apart throughout.*

---

## 1. What this phase is

**This is a new repository, and Phase 01 is the first of nine phases.** Nothing has been published
before it, no prior phase exists to inherit from, and nothing in it may be assumed from anywhere else.

Phase 01 establishes what actually applies to Helix Pharma. It does not build anything, inventory
anything, assess anything or validate anything. It settles the regulatory ground, names who is
accountable, states what the programme has committed to, and publishes the question the programme
opens on.

It exists because FDA conducted a routine CGMP surveillance inspection of the Ridgemont site from
2026-02-02 to 2026-02-13 and issued a **Form FDA 483** at the close, carrying **seven observations, of
which three concern electronic records**. The programme was funded seventeen days later and kicked off
on 2026-03-02.

## 2. Read this first

**Two of the four instruments assessed in this phase do not apply to Helix Pharma, and one of the two
is the one the industry most often assumes does.** `01.05` is the chapter that earns this phase.

**FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance.** It is issued by CDRH and CBER, its
regulatory anchor is 21 CFR Part 820 with ISO 13485:2016 incorporated by reference, its scope sentence
is qualified *"for medical devices"* throughout, and **21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 are not mentioned
anywhere in it**. Helix makes oral solid dose pharmaceuticals and no medical device. **The guidance does
not apply to Helix.** Helix adopts its risk-based assurance thinking **voluntarily, as good practice**,
and labels it as adopted rather than required every time it is cited. `01.05 §4` and `§5` own that, and
`ADR-0005` records the decision.

## 3. What Phase 01 establishes, said first

Five things. Each has exactly one owning chapter and no other chapter restates it.

- **The regulatory scoping determination — which regulations apply to Helix and which do not.** Four
  instruments assessed, two apply, two do not, and EU GMP Annex 11 reaches the EU-facing products by a
  different route entirely. **`01.05`**, resting on the regulation as set out at **`01.03`** and the
  predicate rules at **`01.04`**.
- **The internal audit history.** **Four internal audits between 2023-06 and 2025-09 covered all three
  systems between them — each system falling inside the scope of at least two — and none raised a
  finding on the subject matter of `OBS-2`, `OBS-4` or `OBS-6`.** **`01.09`** publishes that history in
  full, states what may and may not be concluded from it, and carries the open question as `IS-01`.
  **No other document in this phase previews it or explains it away.**
- **The programme charter** — scope, the eight objectives `OBJ-01` to `OBJ-08` with named individual
  owners and measures readable from evidence, the money, the resourcing and the schedule. **`01.06`**.
- **The governance structure, the twelve roles and the quality unit's position** — including the new
  Data Integrity Lead, who reports to Quality and not to IT. **`01.07`**, with `ADR-0002` and
  `ADR-0004`.
- **The obligations register `O1` to `O12`**, each with the exact citation it arises from, a named
  owner, the evidence that would show it discharged, and its status — **including at least one recorded
  as an open gap at this vantage**. **`01.08`**.

Alongside those: the Form FDA 483 and the response filed on 2026-03-06 (`01.02`), the company and what
it makes (`01.01`), the programme's boundaries (`01.10`), the calendar (`01.11`), the four registers
(`01.12`) and the transition into Phase 02 (`01.13`).

## 4. What Phase 01 deliberately does not establish

This is the more important list. A foundation phase that quietly reports downstream outcomes has been
written backwards from a result. **None of the following exists at the vantage, and nothing in this
phase states, implies or forecasts any of it.**

- **No system inventory.** The estate is not counted anywhere in this phase. Phase 02.
- **No Part 11 applicability determination for any system or any record class.** Phase 02.
- **No data integrity risk assessment and no ALCOA assessment.** Phase 03. **Nothing in this phase is
  scored, rated or ranked.**
- **No validation framework, and nothing has been validated under this programme.** Phase 04.
- **No control implementation, no audit trail configuration, no electronic signature deployment.**
  Phase 05.
- **No periodic review performed under this programme.** Phase 08.
- **No internal audit of the remediated programme, and no third-party audit.** Phase 08 and beyond.
- **No further inspection and no inspection outcome of any kind.**

Phase 01 names each of these as planned and says **nothing whatever** about its content. `01.10 §7`
states the same list as absences and `01.13` closes on it.

## 9. Three vocabularies, never swapped

| Term | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Inspectional observation** — `OBS-n` | What a Form FDA 483 carries, in the 483's own numbering | Not a deviation, not a finding, **not a legal finding of any kind** |
| **Deviation** — `DEV-nnn` | Helix's own quality record | Not an observation. **A 483 observation never becomes a deviation**; a deviation may be raised in response to one and the link is recorded |
| **Finding** | What internal audit or the third-party audit raises | Not an observation and not a deviation |
| **CAPA** — `CAPA-nnn` | A corrective and preventive action under Helix's quality system | Not evidence, on its own, that an obligation is discharged |
| **Change request** — `CR-nnn` | A change under change control | |
| **Audit trail** | Not defined in Part 11. FDA defines it in the December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance | Not a Part 11 definition, and not a thing Part 11 requires anybody to review |
| **Validation** | `21 CFR 11.10(a)`: *"Validation of systems to ensure accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and the ability to discern invalid or altered records"* — and, for Helix, an obligation under `21 CFR 211.68` | Not something FDA performs, certifies or approves |
| **Closed system** | `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`: an environment in which system access is controlled by the persons responsible for the content of the records on it | Not a statement about hosting. **A vendor-hosted system is not automatically an open system** |

## 10. Standing framing

These positions hold across all nine phases and are repeated at the foot of the reference chapters so
that a reader arriving at Phase 07 still has them.

- **There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated
  software.** FDA does not certify electronic systems and does not pre-evaluate them for Part 11
  compliance. A product has **features that support** compliance; compliance is a property of **Helix's
  validated system in its intended use**. **"Part 11 certified" is not a thing.**
- **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention. It is not a legal exemption and not a safe
  harbour**, and it confers no rights. **`21 CFR 211.68` requires validation of Helix's computerised
  systems whatever FDA does about Part 11** — `ADR-0003`.
- **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance under 21 CFR Part 820 and does not
  apply to Helix.** Adopted voluntarily as good practice, labelled as adopted every time — `ADR-0005`.
- **GAMP 5 is ISPE good-practice guidance, never a requirement.** Never "as required by GAMP 5", and
  **no software-category count is published in this repository**.
- **A Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding.** *"The FDA Form 483 does not constitute a final Agency
  determination of whether any condition is in violation of the FD&C Act or any of its relevant
  regulations."* A Warning Letter is informal and advisory. **Only a court order binds.** **No document
  in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of any kind**, and none characterises the Ridgemont
  inspection as a violation of anything.
- **No fine, penalty, sanction amount or forfeiture figure appears anywhere.** Programme cost,
  professional fees, licence costs and consultancy fees are stated and are **not** penalties.
- **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, no read-event logging, no cryptographic hashing under
  `21 CFR 11.70`, no multi-factor authentication under `21 CFR 11.200` and no password interval under
  `21 CFR 11.300(b)`.** Where Helix implements any of them, it is Helix's decision and this repository
  says so.
- **A closed system is defined by who controls access** — `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`.
- **FDA uses plain ALCOA.** The four additional attributes are MHRA's, and MHRA itself prefers plain
  ALCOA. **No originator is named for either acronym**, because no primary source names one.
- **NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 is a control catalogue Helix chose**, not an obligation it is under. **No
  FIPS 199 categorization, no baseline, no ATO, no authorizing official** — Helix is not a federal
  system.
- **Never forecast what a future inspection will find.** An inspection outcome is reported once it has
  occurred — `01.10 §6`.

## 12. How to read this phase

Read `01.05` first if you read nothing else. It is the chapter that earns the phase, and the CSA
determination in it is the single fact most likely to be wrong in somebody else's version of this
document.

Read `01.03` and `01.04` as the two reference chapters: what Part 11 actually says, and what the
predicate rules require whatever FDA does about Part 11. Read `01.02` for the inspection. Read `01.09`
for the reason the programme cannot be complacent about its own assurance. Read `01.06` through `01.08`
and `01.11` through `01.12` for the machinery. Read `01.13` for what is still open.

Read for the arguments rather than the citations. Where a chapter cites `21 CFR 11.10(e)` or
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`, it is locating a requirement in the text — **not** asserting that Helix meets
it, and **not** asserting that any Helix record falls under it, because no applicability determination
has been made.
