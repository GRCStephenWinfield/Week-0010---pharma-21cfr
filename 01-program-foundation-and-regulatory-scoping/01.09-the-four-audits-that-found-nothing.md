## 1. The fact

**Four internal audits between 2023-06 and 2025-09 covered all three systems between them — each system
falling inside the scope of at least two — and none raised a finding on the subject matter of `OBS-2`,
`OBS-4` or `OBS-6`.**

The four are `IA-2023-06`, `IA-2024-03`, `IA-2025-01` and `IA-2025-09`, and the three systems are the
chromatography data system, the packaging line systems and the electronic batch record. **No single system
was examined four times**; the four audits between them reached all three, and §4 sets out which audit
covered which.

`OBS-2`, `OBS-4` and `OBS-6` are the inspectional observations on the Form FDA 483 issued at the close of
the inspection of 2026-02-02 to 2026-02-13 that concern electronic records. `01.02-the-inspection-and-the-form-483.md` states
what they are, states them in the 483's own numbering, and owns the statement that **a Form FDA 483 is not
a legal finding**. This chapter does not restate any of that and does not add to it.

What this chapter does is publish the audit history in full and then stop short of the conclusion everybody
wants it to reach.

**A company that missed something has a detection problem; a company whose own audits looked at it and
passed it has a different one.**

That is the whole reason this chapter exists, and it is why the phase would be incomplete without it. The
first kind of company needs to look harder. The second kind needs to know what its looking consisted of —
and Helix does not yet know, because it has never written down which of its records are Part 11 records.
`ADR-0001` makes that determination record by record; Phase 02 makes it; and until it is made, every
explanation of the four audits is a story rather than a finding.

## 2. The audit programme, described as it operated

The internal audit function at Helix is independent of Quality and of IT and reports to the Board's audit
committee. Its work is planned annually, approved by the audit committee, and executed against approved
audit programmes. The four audits below were all planned audits under that plan; none was triggered by a
complaint, a deviation or an external event.

| Attribute | Position across all four audits |
|---|---|
| Function | Internal Audit, Helix Pharma, Inc. |
| Authority | Annual audit plan approved by the Board audit committee |
| Reporting line | Board audit committee. **Not to Quality and not to IT** |
| Basis of testing | Approved audit programmes with checklists derived from the site's procedure set and from prior inspection experience |
| Sampling frame | Documents and records drawn from the quality system and from the areas audited |
| Output | A report with findings, each accepted, assigned an owner and tracked to closure through the quality system |
| Findings closure | **All fourteen findings raised across the four audits were closed before 2026-02-02** |

**This document names no individual auditor and makes no statement about any individual's work.** The
audits are described by what they covered and what they raised. Nothing here is a judgement about anybody
who performed them, and `ADR-0007` — the decision to publish the history — was taken on the express
condition that it would not become one.

### 3.1 `IA-2023-06` — June 2023

| Locations | Ridgemont QC laboratory; Cary QC laboratory |

| Finding | What was raised |
|---|---|
| `IA-2023-06/F1` | The controlled index of laboratory procedures listed two superseded revisions as current |
| `IA-2023-06/F2` | In three sampled instances the sample login record did not carry the date the sample was received for testing |
| `IA-2023-06/F3` | **Two of forty sampled result sheets carried no second-person review signature** |
| `IA-2023-06/F4` | Reference standard log entries were not consistently made at the time of the activity |

**`F3` is the finding to notice.** The audit tested for the presence of the second-person review signature
required by `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`, sampled forty result sheets, and found two without it. The test was
whether the signature was present on the sheet. It was not a test of what the second person had reviewed.

| `IA-2025-01/F3` | **The register of computerised systems held by IT recorded no attribute identifying which systems held records required by a predicate rule** |

Two notes of accuracy on this audit.

**`F3` is the closest any of the four came to the question this programme now asks.** It records that the
IT register carried no predicate-rule attribute. It does not record what should have been in that
attribute, because that determination did not exist to be tested against. `IS-03` carries the same gap
forward at this vantage and `OBJ-01` closes it.

**`F5` is measured against Helix's own standard and against nothing else.** Part 11 specifies no password
interval; `21 CFR 11.300(b)` requires identification code and password issuances to be periodically
checked, recalled or revised and states no interval. Any interval at Helix is Helix's procedural decision.
`01.03-what-part-11-actually-is.md` states the position and this chapter does not extend it.

## 4. The three observations set against the four audits

| Observation | Subject matter | System | Audits whose scope covered that system | Raised by any of the four? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`OBS-2`** | Audit trails on the chromatography data system were not reviewed as part of laboratory record review | Chromatography data system | `IA-2023-06`, `IA-2025-01` | **No** |
| **`OBS-4`** | A shared user account was in use on a packaging line system, so entries could not be attributed to an individual | Packaging line systems | `IA-2024-03`, and `IA-2025-01` through *"the supporting infrastructure"* in its scope statement — **the packaging line systems are not among the six GxP-critical platforms `01.01 §7` lists**, so `IA-2025-01`'s reach over them rests on that phrase and not on the platform list | **No** |

**Every one of the three systems was inside the scope of at least two of the four audits.** This is not a
case of an area that was never visited. The areas were visited, on plan, by an independent function, and
fourteen findings were raised, accepted and closed.

## 5. What the four audits have in common, as a matter of record

The following are statements about the audit records themselves. They are set out because they are facts
about the documents, and they are set out **before** the candidate explanations in §6 so that the two are
not confused. Nothing in this section is a cause.

| Observable feature of the record | What the record shows |
|---|---|
| **Scope statements** | Each audit's scope statement names areas, procedures and record types. **None of the four names a system audit trail as an object of examination** |
| **Checklist provenance** | The checklists were derived from the site's procedure set and, in one case, from prior inspection experience. **A checklist derived from procedures tests conformance to those procedures** |
| **Sampling frames** | The samples were drawn as documents — result sheets, batch records, logs, change records. **No sampling frame in the four is drawn from data held inside a system** |
| **Test design** | Where a review signature was tested, the test was the presence of the signature — `IA-2023-06/F3`. **No test in the four asks what the reviewer examined** |
| **System access** | **The working papers record no request for system read access** by the audit team. Whether such access was available to it at the time is not established from the papers |
| **Record classification** | **No audit programme in the four refers to a determination of which records are Part 11 records**, because no such determination existed at Helix at any point in the period — `IS-02` |

The last is a fact about the company rather than about the audits, and it is recorded here for that
reason. An audit programme cannot scope itself to Part 11 records if nobody has said which records those
are.

## 6. Four candidate explanations, and this chapter chooses none

Four explanations are consistent with everything in §3, §4 and §5. They are set out in full, in no order of
likelihood, and **the programme has not chosen between them.**

**Candidate one — an audit programme scoped to procedures rather than to records.** An audit that asks
whether the procedure was followed will find procedural gaps and will not find gaps in the procedure. If
the site's procedures did not require an audit trail to be reviewed, an audit testing conformance to those
procedures would not raise its absence, and would be right not to on its own terms.

**Candidate two — a checklist derived from a prior inspection.** A checklist built from what an inspector
last asked about is a checklist aimed at the previous inspection. It is efficient, it is common, and it
carries forward the coverage of the last visit rather than the coverage of the current risk.

**Candidate three — auditors without the technical access to read an audit trail.** Examining whether an
audit trail records what `21 CFR 11.10(e)` describes requires the ability to open it, query it and read it
in a system. If that access was not held, the test could not have been performed regardless of how the
audit was scoped.

**Candidate four — a company that had never written down which records were Part 11 records.** If no
determination exists, no auditor can test against it, no scope statement can reference it and no checklist
can be built from it. The absence is upstream of the audit programme entirely.

**These are not mutually exclusive.** They can all be true at once. They can be true in different
proportions for different audits. Two of them — one and four — would also explain each other. **How they
are weighted against one another is not something the programme can say at this vantage, and it does not
say it.**

## 7. Why the programme will not choose

Choosing would be easy and it would be premature. Three reasons, and the third is the one that matters.

**First, the programme does not yet know what there was to find.** Whether an audit trail on any Helix
system records what `21 CFR 11.10(e)` describes is not established. Phase 02 determines which records are
Part 11 records and Phase 05 examines the audit trails. Until both have happened, a statement that the
audits should have found something is a statement about a thing whose properties are unknown.

**Second, the determination that would have anchored the audits did not exist.** `ADR-0001` establishes
that Part 11 applicability is determined record by record. **No such determination has ever been made at
Helix**, and Phase 02 is the first time one will be. **While that is so, none of the four candidates can
be tested**: there is no recorded determination against which any of them could be checked, and a
candidate that cannot be tested cannot be selected or discarded.

**Third, a cause chosen now would be chosen to be comfortable.** Every one of the four candidates assigns
the problem somewhere convenient: to a procedure, to a checklist, to an access permission, or to an absent
document. Picking one at this vantage would close the question before the evidence arrives, and would give
the remediation a shape decided by a guess. The programme has fourteen months and nine phases in which to
find out. It does not need the answer in month three, and it would not be able to defend the one it gave.

**What the programme has committed to instead**: publish the history, name the candidates, and revisit the
question in **Phase 08**, when the periodic review, the internal audit of the remediated programme and the
483 response all come together and the evidence to answer it exists. `IS-01` stays open until then.

Both 2026-05-19 decisions were taken at the Board audit committee sitting minuted at
`governance/GOV-05-board-audit-committee-minute-2026-05-19.md`. The committee's own recorded position is
that a history withheld until its cause is known is a history withheld.

**Publishing this carries a cost and the cost was accepted deliberately.** An organisation that publishes
the audits that raised nothing invites the reading that its assurance function does not work. The
alternative — publishing the observations and not the audit history — invites the reading that nobody
looked. Only one of those two is a decision the audit committee could take about its own function's
records, and it took it.

## 10. What this chapter does not say

- **It does not say the audits were inadequate.** It says what they covered and what they raised.
- **It does not say what any auditor should have done.** No individual is named, assessed or characterised.
- **It does not say the observations were correct or incorrect.** `01.02-the-inspection-and-the-form-483.md`
  records that a Form FDA 483 is not a final agency determination, and this chapter takes the observations
  as recorded, not as adjudicated.
- **It does not convert an observation into a finding or a deviation.** `OBS-n` is the 483's own numbering,
  `IA-YYYY-MM/Fn` is an internal audit finding, and `DEV-nnn` is a Helix quality record. The three are
  never swapped — `ADR-0006`.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind**, and it forecasts nothing about any future inspection.
- **It does not state a cause.** That is the point.
