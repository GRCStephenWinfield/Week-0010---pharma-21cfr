## 1. What happened

**FDA conducted a routine CGMP surveillance inspection of the Ridgemont site from 2026-02-02 to
2026-02-13 — twelve calendar days.** At the close of the inspection, on **2026-02-13**, the
investigator issued a **Form FDA 483** carrying **seven inspectional observations**.

It was a surveillance inspection. It was not for-cause, it did not follow a complaint, a recall or a
field alert, and Helix had not asked for it. Nothing about the site's inspection history caused it and
nothing in this programme's origin should be read as a response to a prior signal. **Helix did not
begin this programme because it foresaw a problem.** It began it because an inspection surfaced one,
and that is the honest account.

The programme was funded seventeen days after the 483 was issued and kicked off on **2026-03-02**. The
charter, the objectives and the money are `01.06`'s.

## 2. A Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding

**This section owns that argument for the whole repository, and every other document cites it rather
than restating it.**

FDA says what a 483 is, and the sentence is worth having in front of the reader exactly as FDA writes
it:

> *"The FDA Form 483 does not constitute a final Agency determination of whether any condition is in
> violation of the FD&C Act or any of its relevant regulations."*

A Form FDA 483 is issued to a firm's management at the conclusion of an inspection when an investigator
has observed conditions that, **in their judgment, may constitute violations**. FDA is explicit that it
is **not an all-inclusive list**. FDA then considers the 483 alongside the Establishment Inspection
Report, the evidence collected and the firm's own response in deciding what further action, if any, is
appropriate.

Three things follow, and this programme holds all three.

**An observation is an observation.** It is a professional judgement recorded at a point in time by the
person who made it. It is not an adjudication, it is not a determination, and it is not a finding of
fact by any tribunal. Nothing in this repository characterises the Ridgemont inspection, or any
observation on the 483, as a violation of anything — and **no document in this repository reaches a
legal conclusion of any kind.** Whether a rule has been breached is a question for lawyers and, if it
ever came to it, a court. It is not a question a compliance document answers.

**The absence of a legal finding is not a reason to do less.** This is the trap on the other side, and
it is the more dangerous one. A programme that reads "not a final agency determination" as "not
established, therefore not urgent" has drawn precisely the wrong inference. The observations describe
conditions at Helix's own site, observed in Helix's own records, and Helix can determine for itself
whether they are accurate without waiting for anybody to rule on them. It did, and it responded on
2026-03-06.

**The 483 is the start of a ladder, and only the top of it binds.**

| Instrument | What it is | Legal force |
|---|---|---|
| **Form FDA 483** | Inspectional observations recorded by the investigator at the close of an inspection, describing conditions that in their judgment may constitute violations | **None.** Not a final agency determination; not all-inclusive |
| **Warning Letter** | An agency-level letter addressing violations of regulatory significance; FDA's principal instrument for achieving voluntary correction | **None.** FDA's Regulatory Procedures Manual: *"A Warning Letter is informal and advisory. It communicates the agency's position on a matter, but it does not commit FDA to taking enforcement action."* |
| **Untitled Letter** | An advisory letter for matters below the Warning Letter threshold; it **requests** rather than requires a written response | **None.** Weaker than a Warning Letter |
| **Injunction or consent decree** | An order of a court — in the case of a consent decree, an injunction to which the defendant has agreed and which is filed in court | **Binding.** This is the only rung with legal force |

**No Warning Letter and no Untitled Letter has been issued to Helix. No judicial action of any kind
exists.** This document records the ladder so that the reader knows where the 483 sits on it, not
because Helix is anywhere else on it. **This repository does not forecast what FDA will do next, and it
does not forecast what any future inspection will find.**

There is no fine, penalty, sanction or forfeiture figure anywhere in this repository. Programme cost
and professional fees appear in `01.06`, and they are not penalties.

## 3. The seven observations, in the 483's own numbering

The 483 carries seven observations. **They are reported here under the numbers the 483 gave them —
`OBS-1` to `OBS-7` — and they are never renumbered, regrouped or given identifiers of the programme's
own.** That is `ADR-0006`, and the reasoning is short enough to state here: **a programme that gives an
inspector's observation its own identifier has started keeping two sets of books.** When the
investigator's number and the company's number diverge, every subsequent conversation with the agency
has a translation step in it, and translation steps are where things get lost.

| Observation | Subject | In this programme's scope |
|---|---|---|
| `OBS-1` | Cleaning validation | No |
| **`OBS-2`** | **Audit trail review in the QC laboratory** | **Yes** |
| `OBS-3` | Environmental monitoring trending | No |
| **`OBS-4`** | **Attribution of entries on a packaging line system** | **Yes** |
| `OBS-5` | Supplier qualification | No |
| **`OBS-6`** | **Change to an approved value in the electronic batch record** | **Yes** |
| `OBS-7` | Training records | No |

**Three of the seven concern electronic records. Four do not.** The four are named once, in the table
above, and this repository does not report on them again — they are being addressed under Helix's
ordinary quality system by their own owners, on their own timelines, and a data integrity programme
that annexed them would be claiming work it is not doing. `01.10 §5` states that boundary and holds it.

## 4. The three that concern electronic records

Stated below **in the programme's own words**. These are summaries written by Helix for its own use.
They are not transcriptions of the 483 and they are not FDA's language.

### `OBS-2` — audit trails on the chromatography data system were not reviewed as part of laboratory record review

Laboratory records generated on the chromatography data system were reviewed and approved without the
associated audit trail forming part of that review. The reviewing analyst examined the result, the
chromatogram as reported and the calculation; the record of who changed what, and when, was not opened.

**The regulatory anchor is the predicate rule, not Part 11.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires audit trails
to be generated, retained and available for agency review and copying. **It imposes no review
obligation at all.** The review obligation for laboratory records comes from
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` — *"the initials or signature of a second person showing that the original
records have been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established standards"* —
and FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance treats the audit trail as part of the record
being reviewed. `01.04 §7` sets that out properly and `01.03 §5` quotes `21 CFR 11.10(e)` in full.

### `OBS-4` — a shared user account was in use on a packaging line system

A user account shared by more than one operator was in use on a packaging line system, with the
consequence that entries made through it could not be attributed to a named individual.

**Attribution is a CGMP requirement before it is a Part 11 requirement.** FDA's position on shared
credentials in the 2018 guidance is grounded in Parts 211 and 212: where login credentials are shared,
a unique individual cannot be identified through the login, and actions such as second-person review
cannot be attributed to a specific person. `21 CFR 211.188(b)(11)` requires identification of the
persons performing and checking each significant step. `21 CFR 11.10(d)` requires limiting system
access to authorised individuals and `21 CFR 11.10(g)` requires authority checks.

One nuance the programme records because it is easy to overstate the rule: **FDA does permit shared,
read-only accounts for viewing data**, where the user cannot modify data or settings. Whether any
account at Ridgemont falls into that category is a Phase 05 question and this document does not answer
it.

### `OBS-6` — a value could be changed after quality unit approval without the change appearing in the record the reviewer saw

The electronic batch record system permitted a value to be changed after the quality unit had approved
the batch record, and the change did not appear in any record the reviewer saw.

**Read that carefully, because it contains two separate propositions and only one of them is about
audit trails.** The first is that a post-approval change was possible. The second is that it was not
visible in the record presented for review. `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires that *"record changes shall not
obscure previously recorded information"* — a no-overwrite requirement. `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires
that changes in master production and control records or other records be instituted only by
authorised personnel. `21 CFR 211.192` requires quality unit review and approval of production and
control records.

**Part 11 does not require a reason-for-change field**, and this programme does not claim that it does.
Where Helix chooses to implement one, it will say that it is Helix's choice. `01.03 §8` lists the
things Part 11 does not require, and the list is one of the more useful pages in this phase.

## 5. What this chapter deliberately does not say

**It does not say what Helix's own internal audit programme had previously examined, or what it
raised.** Internal audit's history with these three systems is the subject of `01.09`, which publishes
it in full — the dates, the scope, the auditor and the findings raised — under `ADR-0007`. That
chapter owns it, states what may be concluded from it and what may not, and carries the open question
as `IS-01`. This chapter does not summarise it, does not anticipate it and does not soften it.

It also does not state:

- Which records on any of the three systems are Part 11 records. **Phase 02 determines that.**
- Whether any of the three systems is validated. **No validation has been performed under this
  programme.**
- What corrective actions have been completed. **None has been completed at this vantage.**
- What a future inspection would make of any of it.

## 6. The response, filed 2026-03-06

Helix responded to the 483 in writing on **2026-03-06, twenty-one days after issue**. The response was
reviewed and released through the quality unit before it went, and the governance record of that review
is `governance/GOV-03-form-fda-483-response-review-and-release.md`.

The response did four things, and the shape of it is worth recording because it is the shape this
programme keeps.

**It addressed all seven observations, not the three this programme took.** A response that answered
only the electronic-records observations would have told FDA that the company reads its own 483
selectively.

**It separated what had already been done from what was planned.** Immediate actions taken during and
after the inspection were stated as completed. Everything else was stated as planned, with an owner and
a date, and nothing was described as complete that was not.

**It committed to a programme rather than to a set of point fixes.** The seventeen days between the 483
and kickoff are the visible part of that decision.

**It made no claim about compliance status.** The response asserts no conclusion about whether any
condition observed was or was not a violation, for the reason given in §2.

### The fifteen-business-day undertaking matches FDA's recommended window

Helix committed, in its response, to filing a written response within **fifteen business days** of any
future inspectional observation.

**The fifteen-business-day figure is FDA's own recommended window, not Helix's invention, and Helix's
commitment matches it rather than beating it.** FDA's *Review of Post-Inspection Responses* policy,
published in the Federal Register on 2009-08-11, provides that a firm is generally allowed fifteen
business days to respond to a Form FDA 483 and that a response received inside that window is reviewed
in detail before the agency decides whether further action is warranted. FDA's **March 2026 draft**
guidance *Responding to FDA Form 483 Observations at the Conclusion of a Drug CGMP Inspection*
recommends the same window. **It is cited here as a draft**, because nothing in this repository cites a
draft as a requirement.

**A recommendation is not a rule FDA imposes**, and what Helix has made is a procedural undertaking to
meet it. The repository states it that way every time it appears, because a company that describes its
own commitments as somebody else's requirements — or somebody else's recommendation as its own
achievement — has confused things that need to stay apart, and the distinction matters most when the
commitment is missed, at which point the question is whether Helix kept its word, not whether it broke
a rule.

**Helix's own response of 2026-03-06 was filed twenty-one calendar days after the 483 — and exactly
fifteen business days after it.** The undertaking is therefore a commitment to keep doing what Helix
did once, stated as a commitment so that it does not depend on anybody remembering.

The undertaking is carried as a standing commitment in the programme calendar at `01.11` and as an
obligation in the register at `01.08`.

## 7. What the 483 changed, and what it did not

**It did not change what applies to Helix.** `21 CFR 211.68` applied on 2026-02-01 exactly as it
applied on 2026-02-14. Part 11 reached the same records before the inspection as after it. An
inspection reveals a state of affairs; it does not create an obligation. The regulatory scoping
determination this phase publishes at `01.03`, `01.04` and `01.05` would have been the same
determination in January.

**It changed what Helix knows, and what it has committed to.** The programme exists, it is funded, it
has a charter, it has a named owner for every element, and it has a governance structure that did not
exist in January. That is the whole of the difference, and it is not nothing.

**It changed the burden of proof inside the company.** Before 2026-02-13, "we believe this is
controlled" was an acceptable answer inside Helix. It is not one now. Every position this programme
takes has to be evidenced, and the evidence index at `01.12` exists so that a reader can find the
evidence rather than take the assertion.
