## 1. What the calendar is for

A programme creates two kinds of commitment. It creates **work that finishes** — a determination, a
validation package, a procedure revision — and it creates **work that recurs**, which does not finish and
which outlives the programme that started it. The milestones at §6 track the first kind. The calendar
entries `CAL-01` to `CAL-12` track the second.

The distinction matters at close-out. On 2027-04-30 the milestones are complete or they are not. The
calendar entries are still running, and `IS-07` records that nobody yet owns them after that date.

The calendar was adopted at `DEC-120` on 2026-05-29 and is owned by the Programme Manager. Each entry
carries **a cadence, a named individual owner and a first occurrence**, and — because a calendar entry
whose omission has no consequence is decoration — **what an omitted occurrence would mean**.

## 2. Two rules the calendar keeps

**A cadence is not invented where a regulation states one, and is not asserted where none exists.** Where
CGMP specifies a review frequency for a record, that frequency governs. Where CGMP is silent, the frequency
is set by risk assessment — and Helix has no data integrity risk assessment, because that is **Phase 03's**.
Any entry whose cadence depends on the second case is marked as undefined rather than given a plausible
number. `CAL-06` is that entry and `IS-04` carries it.

**No entry in this calendar forecasts an outcome.** `CAL-09` and `CAL-10` schedule audits. Neither states,
implies or budgets for what those audits will conclude, and no entry anywhere schedules or anticipates an
inspection.

## 4. `CAL-06` — the entry with an undefined element

**`CAL-06` is not fully defined and the calendar says so rather than filling it in.**

The commitment is that where a record is reviewed, the audit trail associated with that record is reviewed
as part of it. The **cadence** of that review has two halves, and only one of them can be stated now.

| Half | Position at 2026-05-29 |
|---|---|
| **Where CGMP specifies a review frequency for the underlying data** | **Statable.** The same frequency applies to the associated audit trail. `21 CFR 211.192` governs quality unit review of production and control records; `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` governs second-person review of laboratory records |
| **Where CGMP does not specify a frequency** | **Not statable.** The frequency is set from Helix's knowledge of its processes and from risk assessment — and **Helix has no data integrity risk assessment.** That is Phase 03's |

Two further things are undefined for the same reason and are recorded here rather than left implicit.

- **Which records the commitment reaches.** The scope depends on the record determination, which is Phase
  02's. `IS-02` carries it.
- **What the reviewer must do.** Neither review procedure states whether or how the associated audit trail
  forms part of the record reviewed. `IS-06` carries it and `OBJ-06` closes it.

**`IS-04` is the issue.** Owner Terrence Abbatiello, raised 2026-05-29, open and deliberately unresolved in
Phase 01. It is recorded at the point the calendar introduced it, not at the point somebody else noticed
it, and the calendar entry is published with the gap visible rather than with a cadence chosen to make the
table look complete.

**A number was available and was refused.** A quarterly cadence would have filled the cell, would have
looked defensible, and would have been Helix's invention rather than a requirement — FDA's position is that
the review is tied to the review frequency of the underlying data, not to a fixed interval.
`01.04-the-predicate-rules-and-enforcement-discretion.md` states what the predicate rules require. Writing
a number into `CAL-06` now would have created a commitment nobody could trace to a source and would have
stopped anybody asking the question again.

## 5. Where each cadence comes from

A reader is entitled to ask, of any cadence in §3, where the number came from. The answer is one of three
things and never a fourth.

| Source of cadence | Entries | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **A predicate rule position** | `CAL-06` in part | The frequency follows the review frequency of the underlying record. `21 CFR 211.192` and `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` are the anchors |
| **A Helix procedural decision, stated as such** | `CAL-01` to `CAL-05`, `CAL-07` to `CAL-12` | Helix chose the interval. It is defensible, it is written down, and it is **not presented as a regulatory requirement** |
| **Undefined, and marked** | `CAL-06` in part | See §4 |

**No cadence in this calendar is taken from a guidance document and reported as a requirement.** GAMP 5 is
ISPE good-practice guidance and never a requirement. FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device
guidance and does not reach Helix; where its thinking is used, it is adopted voluntarily and labelled that
way — `ADR-0005`. `PR-09` carries the risk that this slips.

**And no cadence is derived from an interval Part 11 does not contain.** `21 CFR 11.300(b)` requires
identification code and password issuances to be periodically checked, recalled or revised and **states no
interval**. Any interval Helix operates is Helix's own procedural decision and is recorded as one.

## 7. Sequencing that is not the programme's to change

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| `MS-06` before `MS-09` | A control cannot be selected for a record nobody has determined to be in scope — `ADR-0001` |
| `MS-07` before `MS-08` | The validation framework's rigour is set against assessed risk. Building the framework first sets the rigour by habit |
| `MS-09` before `MS-10` | The chromatography data system inherits the access, audit trail and signature positions rather than inventing its own |
| `MS-12` before `MS-13` | Internal audit passes over the remediated programme before close-out, and the third-party audit at `CAL-10` precedes `MS-12` |
| **`CAL-09` after `MS-11`, and `CAL-10` after `CAL-09`** | **Both assurance activities come after the remediation they audit.** `01.06-the-programme-charter.md §7` fixes the rule: auditing an unfinished remediation produces a report about a building site. `MS-11` on 2027-03-19 is the last remediation milestone, so `CAL-09` opens on 2027-03-22 and `CAL-10` on 2027-03-29 |
| `CAL-10` performed by a party that designed none of it | `ADR-0004` prohibits the party that designed or drafted the remediation from performing the audit of it |

## 8. Float, and where there is none

| Interval | Days | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| `MS-12` to `MS-13` | 21 | The whole of close-out. `PR-04` carries what happens if `MS-12` moves |
| `MS-11` to `MS-12` | 21 | Phase 08 carries the periodic review, the internal audit and the 483 response commitments together, and both assurance activities sit inside this window |
| `MS-10` to `MS-11` | 21 | |
| `MS-11` to `CAL-09` first occurrence | 3 | Remediation completes at `MS-11` on 2027-03-19; the internal pass over it opens the following Monday |
| `CAL-09` first occurrence to `CAL-10` first occurrence | 7 | One week for the internal audit to be performed and reported before the third party opens |
| `CAL-10` first occurrence to `MS-12` | 11 | The third-party audit's report has to land, be read and be responded to inside this window |

**The tail of the programme is the tight part and it is tight by construction**, because the assurance
activities have to follow the remediation they are assuring and there are 21 days between the end of
remediation and the end of Phase 08. **Sequencing the two assurance activities correctly costs float
rather than creating it**: the window from the third-party audit's opening to `MS-12` is eleven days, and
the only way to lengthen it would be to open the audit before the work it audits is finished, which
`01.06-the-programme-charter.md §7` forbids. `PR-04` records the delivery risk. It carries no likelihood, impact or score, and
`01.12-the-registers.md §4` states why.

## 9. What survives close-out, and the problem with it

Milestones end. Calendar entries do not. On 2027-04-30 the following are still running, and the charter
that created them will have closed.

| Entry | Still running after 2027-04-30 | Held by whom after close-out |
|---|---|---|
| `CAL-05` | Monthly obligations register review | **Not stated** |
| `CAL-06` | Audit trail review as part of record review | **Not stated** |
| `CAL-07` | Annual periodic review, per system | **Not stated** |
| `CAL-08` | Annual training, plus on appointment | **Not stated** |
| `CAL-09` | Annual internal audit | **Not stated** |
| `CAL-11` | Quarterly management review | **Not stated** |
| `CAL-12` | Quarterly regulatory horizon scan | **Not stated** |

Seven of twelve entries outlive the programme and **none of them has a named owner beyond close-out**. The
four governance bodies at `CAL-01` to `CAL-04` are programme bodies and dissolve with it; `CAL-10` is a
one-off inside the programme.

**`IS-07` is that gap, and it is the calendar's own defect rather than one somebody else found.** It is
raised here at the point of introduction, owned by the Programme Manager, and carried open into Phase 02.
A programme that builds seven standing commitments and closes without saying who holds them has handed the
next inspection a list of things that used to happen.

The obvious answer — that the quality unit holds them under `21 CFR 211.22` — is probably right and is
**not written down**, and this document is not going to write it down on the programme's behalf. Assigning
a standing obligation to a statutory function is a decision for the quality unit to take and minute, not
for a calendar to assume.

## 10. What the calendar does not contain

- **No inspection.** The programme does not schedule, anticipate or forecast one. It has no view on when
  the next inspection will occur or what it will observe.
- **No certification event.** **There is no Part 11 certification** and no date on which one could be
  obtained. `01.03-what-part-11-actually-is.md` states the position.
- **No authorization or accreditation milestone.** Helix's use of NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5 is a choice of
  catalogue. There is no categorization, no baseline and no authorization, because Helix is not a federal
  system — `01.05-what-does-not-apply.md`.
- **No cadence taken from GAMP 5 or from any guidance treated as a requirement.** Every cadence above is
  either a predicate rule position, a Helix procedural decision stated as such, or marked undefined.
- **No entry after close-out with a named owner.** That is `IS-07`, and it is the calendar's own defect.
