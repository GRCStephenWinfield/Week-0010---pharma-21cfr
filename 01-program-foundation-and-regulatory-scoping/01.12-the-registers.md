## 1. Four registers, four definitions

The programme keeps four registers and they answer four different questions. Keeping them apart is not
bureaucratic tidiness: an entry filed in the wrong register gets the wrong treatment, and the wrong
treatment for a problem that is already true is to schedule a mitigation for it.

| Register | Series | Count at the vantage | Definition used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Issues** | `IS-01` to `IS-07` | 7 | **A thing that is true now and is a problem** |
| **Assumptions** | `AS-01` to `AS-12` | 12 | **A thing taken as true without evidence, which the plan rests on** |
| **Dependencies** | `DP-01` to `DP-08` | 8 | **A thing the plan needs from somewhere it does not control** |
| **Programme risks** | `PR-01` to `PR-10` | 10 | **A thing that is not true now and would be a problem if it became true** |

**Thirty-seven entries are open across the four registers and nothing has closed.** That is normal 88 days
into a foundation phase and it is stated rather than dressed up. A register with closures in its first
quarter usually contains entries that were never entries.

The full text of all four is at `logs/raid-log.md`. This chapter is what the registers are for and the
rules they keep; the log is the record.

and `01.09-the-four-audits-that-found-nothing.md` sets out why the programme has refused to answer it
early.

## 3. Assumptions and dependencies

**An assumption is something the plan rests on and nobody has tested.** It is reviewed at every phase gate,
and a tested assumption becomes either a fact or an issue. It does not stay an assumption.

`AS-03` is the load-bearing one: that the record-level determination can be completed in Phase 02 from the
existing estate documentation plus targeted technical review, inside the window to `MS-06` on 2026-07-31.
Every date after `MS-06` rests on it. **Recording an assumption is not forecasting an outcome** — it names
what the plan breaks on if the assumption is wrong, and `PR-03` carries that break.

**A dependency is something the plan needs from somewhere it does not control.** Eight are registered.
`DP-06` — the third-party auditor's availability for the window opening 2027-03-29 — is the one watched
hardest, because it can move `MS-12` and Helix cannot move it. `DP-03`, technical cooperation from IT and
Automation, is the one that most directly tests `ADR-0002`: the Data Integrity Lead's determinations
require access the role cannot compel, which is the price of not reporting to the function that holds it.

## 4. Programme risks, and the rule this repository will keep

**No entry in `PR-01` to `PR-10` carries a likelihood, an impact or a score. None ever will.**

The reason is not that scoring is difficult. It is that **a programme risk and a quality risk are different
objects, and putting them on the same scale invites a comparison that is false.**

A programme risk is about **delivery** — that a window compresses, that funded time is not released, that
an engagement boundary drifts. A data integrity risk is about **the reliability of a record a patient's
medicine depends on**. If both appear on a five-by-five grid, a scheduling concern and a threat to the
integrity of a batch record acquire the same vocabulary, and somebody eventually reads across.

**The data integrity risk assessment is Phase 03's.** It does not exist at this vantage; **how many entries
it will hold is not known here, because nothing has yet been assessed into it**, and the attributes it will
be assessed against — plain ALCOA, and the four additional attributes that are not FDA's — are Phase 03's
to set out. **This phase scores nothing.** Not a risk, not an obligation, not a system, not an observation,
not a control. There is no maturity rating anywhere in this phase and no heat map.

Two consequences worth stating.

**A programme risk with no agreed response is recorded with no agreed response.** `PR-10` — key-person
concentration on the Data Integrity Lead and the CSV Lead, each a single holder on the critical path — has
none at the vantage. The cell says so. A response column completed for the sake of completion is worse than
an empty one, because it stops anybody looking.

**A programme risk is never cited as though it were a quality risk.** No document in this repository may
reference `PR-nn` in support of a statement about product quality, patient safety or record reliability.

## 5. What a register entry has to contain

An entry that cannot be acted on is a note. Each register enforces a minimum, and the minimum is different
for each because the four objects behave differently.

| Register | Minimum content | The field that does the work |
|---|---|---|
| Issue | What is true, why it is a problem, named owner, date raised, status, related entries | **What is true.** An issue written as a worry is a risk in the wrong register |
| Assumption | What is taken as true, named owner, **and what breaks if it is false** | **What breaks.** An assumption with no stated consequence cannot be prioritised for testing |
| Dependency | What is needed, by when, **from whom**, named Helix-side owner, status | **From whom.** A dependency on nobody is a task |
| Programme risk | What could become true, named owner, **response — or the fact that there is none** | **Response.** Including its absence, stated as an absence |

**Two entries are written to fail the reader's expectation on purpose.** `PR-10` has no response and says
so. `CAL-06`'s issue, `IS-04`, has no cadence and says so. Both cells could have been filled with something
plausible in about a minute, and both would then have stopped being read.

## 6. These registers and the quality system are not the same thing

Helix runs a quality system. It has deviations, CAPAs and change requests, and they have their own
identifiers — `DEV-nnn`, `CAPA-nnn`, `CR-nnn` — their own procedures and their own owner in the Head of
Quality Assurance. **None of them is a programme register entry and no programme register entry is one of
them.**

| Object | Lives in | Opened by | Never becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issue, assumption, dependency, programme risk | These four registers | The programme | A deviation |
| Deviation `DEV-nnn` | The quality system | Helix, under its own procedure | An inspectional observation |
| CAPA `CAPA-nnn` | The quality system | Helix | A programme risk |
| Change request `CR-nnn` | The quality system | Helix | A finding |
| Inspectional observation `OBS-n` | The Form FDA 483 | **FDA's investigator**, in the 483's own numbering | Anything at all — `ADR-0006` |
| Finding | An internal or third-party audit report | Internal audit or the third-party auditor | A deviation, though one may be raised in response |

**Where a programme entry and a quality record concern the same subject matter, both identifiers are kept
and the link is recorded.** `IS-06` and any procedure change raised to close it are two records, not one
record with two names. The alternative — folding a programme issue into a CAPA so the register looks
shorter — loses the programme's own account of when it knew.

## 7. How an entry moves between registers

| From | To | When |
|---|---|---|
| Assumption | Issue | The assumption is tested and is false |
| Assumption | Closed | The assumption is tested and is true. It becomes a fact and is cited as one |
| Programme risk | Issue | The thing that was not true becomes true |
| Dependency | Issue | The thing needed does not arrive by the date it was needed |
| Issue | Closed | The condition that made it an issue no longer holds, with the evidence named |
| Anything | The Phase 03 risk assessment | **Never.** These four registers do not feed the data integrity risk assessment, and no entry is migrated into it |

The last row is the one that keeps the rule at §4 honest. Prohibiting a score while quietly promoting
delivery entries into a scored quality register would be the same error with an extra step.

## 8. What the registers do not contain

- **No score of any kind.** No likelihood, no impact, no rating, no maturity level, no heat map.
- **No entry about an individual's performance.** Entries describe conditions, artefacts and arrangements.
- **No forecast of an inspection outcome**, and no entry whose closure condition is an agency's opinion.
- **No legal conclusion.** An open gap is Helix's assessment of its own position; it is not a violation, a
  breach or a non-compliance, and no register entry says otherwise.
- **No inspectional observation.** `OBS-1` to `OBS-7` live on the Form FDA 483 in the 483's own numbering
  and are never restated as issues, risks or findings — `ADR-0006`. Where an issue relates to the subject
  matter of an observation, it says so and keeps both identifiers distinct.
- **No deviation.** `DEV-nnn` is a Helix quality record and lives in the quality system, not here.

## 9. Maintenance

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Every entry has a named individual owner — `ADR-0008` |
| Dating | Every entry carries the date it was raised. An entry with no date has no age, and age is what gets an entry looked at |
| Review | All four registers are reviewed monthly at `CAL-01` and reported quarterly to the Board audit committee at `CAL-04` |
| Continuity | The series continue across phases and are never restarted. `IS-01` means the same issue in Phase 09 as it does here |
| Closure | Closure names the evidence. "Believed complete" is not a closure condition |
| Additions | Later phases extend the series. Phase 01 opened `IS`, `AS`, `DP` and `PR` and does not reserve numbers |
