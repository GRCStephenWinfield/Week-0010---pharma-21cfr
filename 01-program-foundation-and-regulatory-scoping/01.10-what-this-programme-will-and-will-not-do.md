---

## 1. What the programme is

**A data integrity and computerised systems compliance programme for Helix Pharma, running from
2026-03-02 to 2027-04-30, across nine phases.**

It exists because an FDA CGMP surveillance inspection of the Ridgemont site produced a Form FDA 483 on
2026-02-13, three of whose seven observations concern electronic records. `01.02` is the account of
that. The charter, the objectives `OBJ-01` to `OBJ-08`, the money and the resourcing are `01.06`'s and
this chapter does not restate them.

What this chapter does is draw the programme's edges. **A programme that has not written down what it
is not doing will be assumed to be doing all of it**, and the assumption survives right up until
somebody asks for the deliverable nobody was ever going to produce.

## 2. The nine phases, and what each will establish

Each phase below is described in the future tense on purpose. **At this vantage, one of the nine has
produced anything, and it is this one.**

| Phase | What it will establish |
|---|---|
| **01 — `01-program-foundation-and-regulatory-scoping`** | **Complete.** What applies to Helix and what does not; the charter; the governance structure and the roles; the obligations register; the 483 response as filed; and the question the programme opens on |
| **02 — `02-the-system-inventory-and-part-11-applicability`** | The inventory of computerised systems, and, **record class by record class**, which records are Part 11 records and which are not. This is where `ADR-0001` stops being a principle and becomes a determination |
| **03 — `03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes`** | The data integrity risk assessment, and the assessment of Helix's record classes against the ALCOA attributes. **This is the phase that scores things. Phase 01 scores nothing** |
| **04 — `04-the-validation-framework-and-policy-architecture`** | The validation framework, the policy and procedure architecture beneath it, and the citation each control is justified by. The obligation cited will be `21 CFR 211.68`, and FDA's CSA guidance will be labelled adopted voluntarily wherever it appears |
| **05 — `05-access-audit-trails-and-electronic-signatures`** | Access control, audit trail configuration and review design, and electronic signature deployment against `21 CFR 11.50`, `21 CFR 11.70`, `21 CFR 11.100`, `21 CFR 11.200` and `21 CFR 11.300` |
| **06 — `06-the-chromatography-data-system`** | The chromatography data system in depth — the dynamic-record problem, integration and reprocessing, and the laboratory record requirements of `21 CFR 211.194(a)` |
| **07 — `07-the-data-lifecycle-backup-archive-and-the-paper-question`** | The data lifecycle: backup as `21 CFR 211.68(b)` means it, archive, retention under `21 CFR 211.180`, and an honest answer on where a paper record legitimately is the record |
| **08 — `08-periodic-review-internal-audit-and-the-483-response`** | Periodic review, the rebuilt internal audit approach, and the closure position on the three observations this programme took |
| **09 — `09-inspection-readiness-and-continuous-compliance`** | Inspection readiness and the operating model that survives the programme's own close-out |

**Two phases are named for a system and a question rather than for a discipline, and that is
deliberate.** Phase 06 exists because the chromatography data system is where the hardest version of
every problem in this programme lives, and a programme that treats it as one line in a generic
remediation plan will do it badly. Phase 07 exists because "we print it, so it does not count" is the
most attractive wrong answer in this field, and it deserves a phase rather than a footnote.

**Phase 03 is where scoring happens.** This phase carries no likelihood, no impact and no score of any
kind, and `01.12` states the rule the repository keeps about that.

## 4. The boundary with the other four observations, and why it holds

**Four of the seven observations on the 483 are not this programme's, and they are not being absorbed
into it.**

`OBS-1` concerns cleaning validation. `OBS-3` concerns environmental monitoring trending. `OBS-5`
concerns supplier qualification. `OBS-7` concerns training records. Each is being addressed under
Helix's ordinary quality system, by the function that owns the subject matter, on its own timeline, and
each was answered in the written response of 2026-03-06 alongside the other three.

There is a real temptation to annex them, and it is worth naming so that it can be refused.

**The temptation.** A data integrity programme with a budget, a steering committee and a programme
manager is the best-resourced thing in the building. Every observation on the 483 has an electronic
record somewhere near it — cleaning validation records are held in a system, training records are held
in a learning management system, supplier qualification records are held in the electronic quality
management system. It is easy to argue that all seven are really data integrity observations.

**Why it is refused.** Because it would be false, and because it would be expensive in a way that
shows up late. `OBS-7` is not an observation about whether training records are attributable; it is an
observation about training. Rewriting somebody else's observation as a data integrity problem so that
this programme can own it would mean the training problem gets a data integrity fix and stays a
training problem. Meanwhile this programme's own scope grows to the point where nothing in it finishes.

**Where the boundary is genuinely soft, it is stated rather than hidden.** If Phase 02's inventory finds
that the learning management system holds records required by a predicate rule and maintained
electronically in place of paper, then **those records** come into this programme's Part 11 scope — and
`OBS-7`, which is about training rather than about records, still does not. The two things share a
system and are not the same subject. **Scope follows records; it does not follow systems, and it
certainly does not follow observation numbers.**

## 5. What remediation means here, and what it does not

**Remediation in this programme means: an obligation is identified with its exact citation, a control
is designed against it, a named individual owns it, it is implemented, it is validated where validation
applies, evidence of it is produced, and the evidence is indexed so somebody else can find it.**

Seven steps. A gap that has been through fewer than seven is not remediated; it is in progress, and the
tracker says so.

**What remediation does not mean:**

| Not remediation | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Buying a system with better features** | A product supplies **features that support** compliance. There is no Part 11 certification, no FDA-approved system and no FDA-validated software, and a purchase order is not a control |
| **Turning a setting on** | A configuration that nobody has validated, nobody owns and nobody has evidenced is a setting, not a control |
| **Writing a procedure** | A procedure that describes work nobody can actually perform at the pace the work runs at will be worked around, and the workaround will be the real process |
| **Closing a CAPA** | `CAPA-nnn` closure is a quality system event. It records that the action was completed. It does not, by itself, establish that the underlying obligation is discharged |
| **Declaring a system compliant** | Compliance is not a status a system holds. It is a property of a validated system in its intended use, under procedures, operated by trained people, evidenced |
| **Telling FDA it is done** | The written response of 2026-03-06 states what was done and what is planned. It claims nothing complete that is not |

**And one vocabulary rule that this programme will not bend.** An **inspectional observation** is the
483's, numbered `OBS-1` to `OBS-7`. A **deviation** — `DEV-nnn` — is Helix's own quality record. A
**finding** is what internal audit or a third-party audit raises. **A 483 observation never becomes a
deviation.** A deviation may be raised in response to one, and where that happens the link between the
two is recorded. Three words, three meanings, never swapped. `ADR-0006` and `01.00 §8`.

## 6. The standing refusal

**This programme will not forecast the outcome of any future FDA inspection, and no document in this
repository will contain such a forecast.**

Not as an objective. Not as a success criterion. Not as a milestone. Not in a steering committee slide,
and not in a sentence beginning "we expect that a future inspection would…".

There are three reasons and they are all sufficient on their own.

**It is not knowable.** An inspection is conducted by a person, at a site, against records, on a date,
with a scope nobody at Helix chooses. FDA is explicit that a 483 is **not an all-inclusive list**.
Nothing about the outcome of a future inspection is derivable from the state of a remediation
programme.

**It corrupts the programme.** A programme with a target expressed as an inspection outcome will
optimise for the appearance of the thing rather than the thing. Every design decision acquires a second
criterion — how it will look — and the second criterion wins arguments the first should have won. The
charter therefore contains **no target for a future inspection outcome and no objective whose measure is
somebody else's opinion**, and `01.06` says so in terms.

**It is a claim about somebody else's judgement.** Forecasting what an investigator will conclude means
asserting a conclusion on their behalf. Helix does not do that, in the same way and for the same
reason that this repository reaches **no legal conclusion of any kind** and does not characterise the
Ridgemont inspection as a violation of anything.

**What the programme will report instead**: what it did, when, with whose name against it, and where the
evidence is. When an inspection happens, its outcome will be recorded once it has occurred, in the past
tense, as fact.

The same refusal applies to the third-party audit by **Vashti Okonjo & Partners** and to the internal
audit of the remediated programme. Neither has occurred. Neither's result is anticipated anywhere in
this repository.

## 7. What has not happened at this vantage

Stated as absences, because a foundation phase that quietly reports downstream outcomes has been
written backwards from a result.

- **No system inventory.** No count of the estate, no register of systems. Phase 02.
- **No Part 11 applicability determination for any system or any record class.** Phase 02.
- **No data integrity risk assessment and no ALCOA assessment.** Phase 03. Nothing in this phase is
  scored.
- **No validation framework, and nothing has been validated under this programme.** Phase 04.
- **No control implementation, no audit trail configuration and no electronic signature deployment.**
  Phase 05.
- **No periodic review has been performed under this programme.** Phase 08.
- **No internal audit of the remediated programme.** Phase 08.
- **No third-party audit.** Vashti Okonjo & Partners has audited nothing.
- **No further inspection, and no inspection outcome of any kind.**
- **No CAPA arising from this programme has been closed.**

Phase 01 names each of these as planned and says **nothing whatever** about its content.

## 8. What would tell a reader this programme worked

Not a status, and not an inspection outcome. Four things, each of which is checkable by somebody
outside the programme.

**Every obligation has an exact citation.** Not "Part 11", not "GAMP", not "FDA expects". A section
number that can be looked up, and a distinction maintained between what a regulation requires, what a
guidance recommends, and what Helix chose. `01.08` builds the register that way.

**Every control has a named individual owner.** Not a team, not a function, not a job title standing in
for a person. That is `ADR-0008`, fixed in this phase before there is a control set to argue about.

**Every claim has evidence somebody else can find.** The evidence index at `01.12` exists so that a
reader can go and look rather than take an assertion.

**The programme can say what it does not know.** `IS-01` is open at this vantage and is stated as open.
A programme that has no open questions eighty-eight days in has not looked.
