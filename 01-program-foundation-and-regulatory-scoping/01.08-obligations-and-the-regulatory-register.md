## 1. What this register is

Twelve obligations, `O1` to `O12`. Each carries **the exact citation it arises from**, **a named individual
owner**, **the evidence that would show it discharged**, and **its status as a programme matter at
2026-05-29**. The register was adopted at `DEC-116` on 2026-05-15 and is reviewed monthly at `CAL-05`.

**This register reaches no legal conclusion of any kind.** It does not state that any requirement has been
met, has not been met, has been breached, or has been violated. Those are conclusions for counsel and for
the agency, and a compliance programme that reaches them in its own documents has confused a work list with
an adjudication. Where the register records an **open gap**, that is Helix's own assessment of its own
position and nothing more.

**What the register is not.** It is not a restatement of the regulations. `01.03-what-part-11-actually-is.md`
states what Part 11 is, `01.04-the-predicate-rules-and-enforcement-discretion.md` states what the predicate
rules require and what enforcement discretion is, and `01.05-what-does-not-apply.md` states what does not
reach Helix at all. This register takes those three as given and does one thing with them: it names, for
each obligation, the person at Helix who holds it and the artefact that would show it discharged.

| Status term | Means |
|---|---|
| **In force** | The obligation exists and is owned. **It does not mean it is discharged** |
| **In force, scope pending** | The obligation exists; **which records or systems it reaches has not been determined**, and the determination is Phase 02's |
| **Open gap** | Helix's own assessment is that its present position needs to change. **Not a violation, not a breach, not a finding, not an observation** |
| **Discharged** | The evidence named in the evidence column exists and has been reviewed |

**At this vantage nothing is discharged.** No system inventory exists, no record determination has been
made, no control has been implemented and no validation has been performed. A register with entries marked
discharged 88 days after kickoff would be describing a different programme.

## 3. The three open gaps, stated plainly

**An open gap is Helix's own assessment of its own position.** It is not an inspectional observation, not a
deviation and not a finding. Those three words belong to three other things and are never swapped here.

### `O2` — audit trail retention cannot be stated for any system

`21 CFR 11.10(e)` expresses retention in relative terms: the audit trail documentation is to be retained
for a period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records. **Helix cannot at this
vantage state, for any system, how long its audit trail data is retained relative to the retention period
of the underlying record.** Nobody has asked the question of the estate in that form, and the answer is not
recorded anywhere.

The gap is not that a retention period is short. The gap is that **the comparison has never been made**,
which means Helix does not know whether it is short. `IS-05` carries it, `OBJ-04` is set to close it, and
Phase 05 does the work.

Two related things this document does **not** say. It does not say what any system's audit trail does or
does not contain — that is Phase 02's determination and Phase 05's configuration work. And it does not
characterise the position under the enforcement discretion FDA has stated over `21 CFR 11.10(e)`;
`01.04-the-predicate-rules-and-enforcement-discretion.md §3` owns that, and its conclusion — that
enforcement discretion is a statement of FDA's intentions and not a legal exemption — is why `O2` is
carried as an obligation rather than set aside.

### `O4` — the laboratory record review procedure is silent on the audit trail

`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` requires the initials or signature of a second person showing that the original
records have been reviewed. **Helix's laboratory record review procedure does not state whether the audit
trail associated with a result forms part of the original record being reviewed.** It does not say it does
and it does not say it does not.

A silent procedure is not a neutral procedure. A reviewer following it reviews what the procedure lists,
and the procedure lists result sheets. `IS-06` carries the gap, `OBJ-06` closes it by requiring both review
procedures — the laboratory one under `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` and the production and control record one
under `21 CFR 211.192` — to state the position expressly.

**The cadence question is separate and is not resolved here.** Where CGMP specifies a review frequency for
the underlying data, the same frequency applies to the review of the associated audit trail. Where CGMP is
silent, the frequency is a matter for risk assessment — and **Helix has no data integrity risk assessment**,
because that is Phase 03's. `CAL-06` therefore has an undefined element, carried as `IS-04` at
`01.11-the-programme-calendar.md §4`.

### `O8` — a shared account is in use on a packaging line system

`21 CFR 11.10(d)` requires system access to be limited to authorised individuals. **`OBS-4` on the Form FDA
483 of 2026-02-13 records that a shared user account was in use on a packaging line system, so that entries
could not be attributed to an individual.** Helix records `O8` as an open gap on its own assessment of its
own position.

Three cautions on how that sentence is to be read.

- **`OBS-4` is an inspectional observation** recorded by an investigator, in the 483's own numbering, and
  it is **not a legal finding**. `01.02-the-inspection-and-the-form-483.md §2` owns that statement and
  quotes FDA's own words for it.
- **`OBS-4` is not a deviation and does not become one.** A deviation, `DEV-nnn`, is Helix's own quality
  record. Where Helix raises a deviation in response to an observation, the link between the two is
  recorded and the identifiers stay distinct. `ADR-0006` fixes the convention.
- **The gap is recorded as Helix's assessment**, not as a conclusion about whether any regulation was
  contravened. This register does not reach that conclusion and no document in this programme does.

`OBJ-05` closes the gap, and closes it in a way that admits reality: shared accounts are removed, or the
technical constraint preventing removal is documented and a named individual owns the compensating control.
There is no third state in which a shared account simply persists unexamined.

## 4. Where the obligations come from, and where they do not

| Source | Reaches Helix? | What this register does with it |
|---|---|---|
| **21 CFR Parts 210 and 211** | **Yes.** Helix manufactures finished pharmaceuticals | `O1`, `O3`, `O4`, `O5`, `O6`, `O7` arise directly |
| **21 CFR Part 11** | **Yes, for a subset of records** — which subset is Phase 02's to determine, `ADR-0001` | `O2`, `O8`, `O9`, `O10`, `O11` arise, several marked *scope pending* for that reason |
| **21 CFR Part 820** | **No.** Helix makes no medical device | Nothing. `01.05-what-does-not-apply.md` owns the determination |
| **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance** | **No.** It is device guidance | Nothing is an obligation. Where its thinking is used it is **adopted voluntarily as good practice** and labelled that way — `ADR-0005` |
| **EU GMP Annex 11, January 2011** | **Through the Qualified Person, for EU-facing products** — not through FDA | `O12` |
| **GAMP 5, Second Edition (ISPE, July 2022)** | Good-practice guidance | **Never an obligation.** No entry in this register cites it as a requirement |
| **NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5** | A **catalogue Helix chose** | **No entry.** There is no FIPS 199 categorization, no baseline and no authorization, because Helix is not a federal system |

**Five obligations — `O2`, `O8`, `O9`, `O10` and `O11` — arise from Part 11 and are therefore all scope
pending until the record determination exists**: nobody has determined which Helix records are Part 11
records. `IS-02` carries that, and `01.09-the-four-audits-that-found-nothing.md` explains why it matters
more than a scoping gap normally would. **Two of them — `O2` and `O8` — are additionally recorded as open
gaps for the separate reasons at §3**, and `O4`'s open gap at §3 arises from a predicate rule rather than
from Part 11 and is not part of this count.

## 5. Why twelve, and how an obligation gets into the register

Twelve is not a target and it is not a ceiling. An obligation enters the register when three conditions are
all met, and the conditions are stated so that the register cannot quietly grow to include everything the
programme happens to be doing.

| Condition | Test applied |
|---|---|
| **It arises from an identifiable source** | A section of a regulation, cited exactly, or a named agreement. **A good idea is not an obligation.** GAMP 5 and FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance produce no entries for this reason |
| **It reaches Helix** | Part 820 produces no entry because Helix makes no medical device. Annex 11 produces one entry, and it reaches the EU-facing products through the Qualified Person and not through FDA |
| **It can be discharged by an artefact somebody can hold** | If nobody can say what evidence would show it discharged, the entry is not an obligation — it is an aspiration, and it belongs in the charter or nowhere |

The third test is the one that removed candidates. "Maintain a culture of data integrity" fails it. "Ensure
data is ALCOA" fails it as written, because ALCOA is a set of attributes an assessment applies to records
and not a discharge condition — and the assessment is Phase 03's.

**Two things that look like obligations and are not.** Enforcement discretion creates no obligation and
removes none; `01.04-the-predicate-rules-and-enforcement-discretion.md §3` states why, and the practical
consequence here is that `O1` and `O2` are both carried in full. And a vendor's statement that its product
has features supporting compliance creates no obligation on the vendor and discharges none of Helix's:
**compliance is a property of Helix's validated system in its intended use**, and `PR-07` carries the risk
of forgetting it.

## 6. How the register is maintained

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Every obligation has a named individual owner. **No obligation is owned by a function**, `ADR-0008` |
| **Citation** | Every entry carries the exact section it arises from. **Never "Part 11.10", never a section that does not exist** |
| **Status changes** | A status change is recorded with a date and a decision reference. Statuses are not edited in place |
| **Evidence** | The evidence column names what *would* show the obligation discharged. It is written before the evidence exists, so that the evidence cannot be defined afterwards to fit what was produced |
| **Review** | Monthly at `CAL-05`, owned by Rebekah Sandquist; reported quarterly to the Board audit committee at `CAL-04` |
| **Additions** | New obligations continue the `O` series. The series is not restarted by a later phase |
| **No forecast** | No entry states or implies what any future inspection will observe |

The full register with its owner and approver block is at `logs/regulatory-register.md`. This chapter is
the reasoning; the log is the record.

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It does not determine which records are Part 11 records.** `ADR-0001` makes that a record-level
  determination and Phase 02 makes it.
- **It does not state that any obligation is met.** Nothing is discharged at this vantage.
- **It does not score anything.** No obligation carries a risk rating; scoring is Phase 03's and
  `01.12-the-registers.md §4` states why this phase scores nothing.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises no inspection outcome as a violation of anything, and
  contains no fine, penalty, sanction or forfeiture figure. None exists in this programme.
