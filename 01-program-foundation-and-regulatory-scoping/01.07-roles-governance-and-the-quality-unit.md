## 1. Twelve roles, and why the list is short

because everybody owns everything. The list below is the whole role set: twelve named individuals, each
with a boundary, and no committee standing in for a person.

an artefact, the artefact-level owner is named in the register that carries the artefact.

## 2. The quality unit's statutory position

**The quality unit is not a programme structure and this programme did not create it.** It exists because
`21 CFR 211.22` requires a drug manufacturer to have one, gives it the responsibility and authority to
approve or reject, and — at `21 CFR 211.22(d)` — requires that its responsibilities and procedures be in
writing and followed.

Three consequences follow, and all three constrain what this programme may do.

**First, the quality unit's approval and rejection authority is not delegable to this programme.** Batch
disposition sits with the quality unit whatever state the data integrity programme is in. Nothing in the
charter, the registers or the calendar moves it.

**Second, the review obligations the programme works to are the quality unit's own.** `21 CFR 211.192`
requires the quality unit to review and approve production and control records. `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`
requires the initials or signature of a second person showing that original laboratory records have been
reviewed. These are predicate rule obligations, they existed before this programme and they will outlast
it. `01.04-the-predicate-rules-and-enforcement-discretion.md` sets out the predicate rules; this document
sets out who holds them at Helix.

**Third, the quality unit's procedures must be written and followed, and a procedure that is silent on a
question is not neutral about it.** `OBJ-06` requires the two review procedures to state expressly whether
and how the audit trail associated with a record forms part of the record being reviewed. At the vantage
neither procedure states it — `IS-06` — and silence has been carried as an issue rather than read as
permission.

| Predicate rule obligation | Section | Holder at Helix |
|---|---|---|
| Quality unit responsibility and authority; written procedures | `21 CFR 211.22` | Dr Marguerite Oyelaran |
| Review and approval of production and control records | `21 CFR 211.192` | Terrence Abbatiello |
| Second-person review of laboratory records | `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` | Dr Sunita Raghunathan |
| Batch production and control records prepared for each batch | `21 CFR 211.188` | Bernard Kwiatkowski |
| Controls over computer or related systems; backup | `21 CFR 211.68(b)` | Colm Ó Braonáin, with Amara Sissoko for the estate |
| Records maintained so data can be used for annual evaluation | `21 CFR 211.180(e)` | Terrence Abbatiello |

## 3. Why the Data Integrity Lead does not report to IT

**`ADR-0002`, taken at `DEC-102` on 2026-03-06 by the Vice President, Quality. The Data Integrity Lead
reports to Quality and does not report to IT.**

The argument is short and it is not about anybody's competence.

**A data integrity function placed inside the organisation that operates the systems is assessing its own
work.** IT and Automation at Helix build, configure, patch and administer the systems that hold the
records. If the person who determines whether a record is adequately controlled sits in the line that
configured the control, then the determination and the thing determined have the same management chain,
the same objectives and the same budget. When those pull in one direction the arrangement looks efficient.
When they pull in opposite directions — an audit trail that is expensive to enable, an account that is
convenient to share — the arrangement has no way of resolving the conflict except by seniority.

Three specific things the reporting line protects.

| What | Why the line matters |
|---|---|
| **The record determination** | `ADR-0001` makes Part 11 applicability a record-level determination. Whether a record is in scope has cost consequences for the operator of the system that holds it. The determiner must not report to that operator |
| **The audit trail position** | `21 CFR 11.10(e)` describes audit trails that are secure, computer-generated and time-stamped, and that do not obscure previously recorded information. Whether a given configuration meets that description is a judgement, and it is one the configuring function should not make about itself |
| **Escalation** | A concern raised inside IT resolves inside IT. A concern raised inside Quality reaches the quality unit's statutory authority under `21 CFR 211.22` |

**What the decision does not say.** It does not say IT cannot be trusted, does not say the Data Integrity
Lead may direct IT, and does not give the role any authority over the estate. Amara Sissoko owns the
estate; Joachim Reuter owns the automation layer; Priyanka Venkataraman owns the method and the
determination and has no operational authority at all. The role's power is that its assessment does not
have to be approved by the people it assesses before it is heard.

**The cost of the decision is recorded too.** The Data Integrity Lead sits outside the function that holds
the technical knowledge of the systems, and every determination therefore requires cooperation the role
cannot compel. `DP-03` carries that dependency and `PR-05` carries what happens if it is not given.

## 4. The three-way separation

**`ADR-0004`, taken at `DEC-109` on 2026-04-10 by the Head of Internal Audit.** Three activities, three
parties, and **no party audits its own work**.

| Activity | Party | May do | May not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Advise and design** | Thorne Delacroix Consulting, LLC | Gap assessment, remediation design, procedure drafting, method development | **Audit, assess or attest to anything it designed, drafted or advised on** |
| **Execute** | Helix — the named owners in §1, with contracted validation resource | Implement, configure, validate, operate, write the records | Audit its own implementation. Self-verification of a step is a check, not an audit |
| **Audit independently** | Vashti Okonjo & Partners, commissioned through Gideon Halvorsen | Independent third-party audit of the remediated programme | **Design, draft or implement anything it audits.** Advisory work would disqualify it from the audit |

Internal audit sits alongside all three and is separate from each: Gideon Halvorsen reports to the Board's
audit committee, is independent of Quality and of IT, and commissions the third-party audit rather than
performing it.

**The rule is stated as a prohibition and not as a preference**, because the version stated as a preference
is the version that gets waived under schedule pressure. `PR-06` carries the risk that advisory scope
drifts into work the third party must later audit, and `AS-09` records the assumption that the advisory
engagement is complete before the third-party audit opens.

**What separation does not buy.** It does not make the third-party audit an approval, a certification or a
determination of compliance. **There is no Part 11 certification.** An audit produces findings; findings
are what internal audit or a third-party auditor raises, and they are not inspectional observations and
not deviations. The three vocabularies are set out at `01.02-the-inspection-and-the-form-483.md` and are
never swapped here.

## 5. The four governance bodies

| Body | Cadence | Chair or reporter | First occurrence | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data Integrity Steering Committee** | **Monthly, with additional sittings called by the chair** | Dr Marguerite Oyelaran | 2026-03-13 (`GOV-02`) | Scope, priorities, budget variance, escalations from the working group; adopts the registers |
| **CSV Working Group** | Weekly | Colm Ó Braonáin | 2026-03-09 — convened informally, ratified at `DEC-105` on 2026-03-13 | Validation approach per system, technical questions, evidence adequacy; escalates upward |
| **Quality Council** | Quarterly | Dr Marguerite Oyelaran | 2026-03-27 | The programme's position within the quality system; interaction with deviations, CAPA and change control |
| **Board audit committee** | Quarterly | Gideon Halvorsen reports | 2026-05-19 (`GOV-05`) | Independent oversight; receives the internal audit position and the 483 response commitment status |

**The Board audit committee is the only body in the set that is not chaired from inside Quality**: the
Steering Committee and the Quality Council are chaired by the Vice President, Quality, and the CSV
Working Group by the Computerised System Validation Lead, whose reporting line into Quality is stated at
§1. It is also the body the internal audit history was taken to. `GOV-05` records that sitting;
`01.09-the-four-audits-that-found-nothing.md` records what was put to it.

**The Steering Committee's cadence is monthly with additional sittings called by the chair, and the
record shows why.** Eight sittings fell between 2026-03-13 and 2026-05-29 — eleven weeks — five of them
in May. A programme standing up is not yet in a rhythm, and the cadence is written as it was actually
kept rather than as a tidy monthly line. `01.11-the-programme-calendar.md §3` lists the dates and
`logs/evidence-index.md` carries the minutes.

**The CSV Working Group met before the decision that constituted it, and that is recorded rather than
smoothed.** It convened informally from 2026-03-09; `DEC-105` constituted the four bodies on 2026-03-13
and ratified it. `governance/GOV-02-steering-committee-inaugural-minute.md §3` carries the ratification.

Each body's calendar entry is `CAL-01` to `CAL-04` at `01.11-the-programme-calendar.md §3`, with the
consequence of an omitted occurrence stated there rather than assumed.

## 6. Who may not do what

A short list, because these are the confusions that produce an unpleasant conversation later.

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| **The advisory firm may not audit its own design** | `ADR-0004` |
| **The third-party auditor may not have drafted what it audits** | `ADR-0004` |
| **Internal audit does not implement remediation** | Independence is the whole of its value |
| **The Data Integrity Lead does not administer accounts, configure systems or approve changes** | The determiner does not operate |
| **The CIO does not determine whether a record is a Part 11 record** | `ADR-0001` puts the determination with the Data Integrity Lead, approved through Quality |
| **The programme manager does not approve validation evidence** | Delivery pressure and evidence adequacy must not sit with one person |
| **The Qualified Person's role is not a US regulatory role** | Dr Anselm Ferrão's obligations reach the EU-facing products through the January 2011 Annex 11. `01.05-what-does-not-apply.md` states the boundary |
| **No individual signs on behalf of another** | An electronic signature identifies the signer; `21 CFR 11.50` requires the printed name, the date and time, and the meaning of the signature to appear in any human readable form of the record |

## 7. Escalation

| Level | Route | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| Technical disagreement on validation approach | CSV Working Group, `CAL-02` | Weekly |
| Resource, scope or budget | Data Integrity Steering Committee, `CAL-01` | Monthly |
| Interaction with a deviation, CAPA or change record | Head of Quality Assurance, then Quality Council, `CAL-03` | As raised |
| A data integrity concern the operating function does not accept | **Data Integrity Lead directly to the Vice President, Quality**, without passing through IT | Immediate |
| Independence concern, or a concern about the programme itself | **Gideon Halvorsen to the Board audit committee**, `CAL-04` | Immediate, reported at the next sitting |

The fourth route is the one `ADR-0002` exists to keep open. The fifth is the one `ADR-0004` exists to keep
open. Both are written down because an escalation route that is understood but not documented is a route
that works until the day it is needed.

## 8. What this document does not establish

- **No system owner assignments**, because there is no system inventory. That is Phase 02.
- **No record owner assignments**, because no record has yet been determined to be a Part 11 record.
  `OBJ-03` will produce them.
- **No control ownership**, because no control set exists. Helix has chosen **NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5 as
  a catalogue** — `01.05-what-does-not-apply.md` states what that choice is and is not — and no baseline,
  categorization or authorization applies, because Helix is not a federal system.
- **No training completion position**, because the training content is Phase 04's.
- **No statement of any individual's performance.** This document assigns roles. It assesses nobody.
