## 1. The hole Phase 03 published against itself

Phase 03 anchored its method on the one sentence in the drug CGMPs that graduates a control by the
properties of the system it runs on, and then recorded, in its own dependency register, that its scale does
not grade on the thing that sentence names.

**`DP-15`, in the register's own words:** *"Neither axis of this scale is the axis `21 CFR 211.68(b)`
names"* — the regulation grades on *"the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system"*,
and Phase 03's assessment grades on **what the record decides** and on **whether an alteration would be
noticed**. The dependency then states what has to happen next: **Phase 04 must state how the two axes relate
to complexity and reliability, or assess those separately, before relying on the assessment to size
input/output verification.**

**This chapter is that statement, and Helix does not reconcile the two. It keeps two graduations and never
merges them.**

**`DP-15` closes here**, and `../04.13-phase-summary-and-transition.md` records the closure with its
evidence. `ADR-0025`, taken at `DEC-408` on 2026-10-27 by Joachim Reuter, is the decision.

## 2. `21 CFR 211.68(b)`, and the sentence that graduates

**Verbatim, in full**, because a provision cited for one sentence has to be read as a whole before the
sentence can be trusted:

> *"Appropriate controls shall be exercised over computer or related systems to assure that changes in
> master production and control records or other records are instituted only by authorized personnel. Input
> to and output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or data shall be checked
> for accuracy. The degree and frequency of input/output verification shall be based on the complexity and
> reliability of the computer or related system. A backup file of data entered into the computer or related
> system shall be maintained except where certain data, such as calculations performed in connection with
> laboratory analysis, are eliminated by computerization or other automated processes. In such instances a
> written record of the program shall be maintained along with appropriate validation data. Hard copy or
> alternative systems, such as duplicates, tapes, or microfilm, designed to assure that backup data are
> exact and complete and that it is secure from alteration, inadvertent erasures, or loss shall be
> maintained."*

**The third sentence is the graduation clause: *"The degree and frequency of input/output verification
shall be based on the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system."***

Three things about it govern everything below.

**It is a *shall*.** Not a recommendation, not a guidance expectation. It is the only place in Part 211
where the intensity of a control is expressly made a function of the properties of the system it is applied
to.

**Its object is exactly one control.** What it authorises is a graduation of **the degree and frequency of
input/output verification**, and nothing else. It does not, by its terms, graduate audit trails, access
control, record review, retention, or the extent of validation. **A regulation cited beyond its own subject
matter stops being a citation** — `03.01 §5` fixed that rule and this phase keeps it.

**And the second sentence names what is being verified.** *"Input to and output from the computer or
related system of formulas or other records or data shall be checked for accuracy."* The object of the
graduated control is **the movement of records into and out of a system**. `IS-18` rests on that sentence,
and `03.11` owns the argument that Phase 03's unit is blind to it.

**A fourth thing is true and it is about Helix rather than about the regulation: the framework uses the
same band a second time, and the regulation does not authorise that use.** Under `21 CFR 211.68(b)` the
complexity-and-reliability band sets **the degree and frequency of input/output verification**. Helix
**additionally** uses the same band, **as its own method choice and not under `21 CFR 211.68(b)`**, as one
of two inputs to evidence depth; on the 25 systems with no established data integrity position it is the
only input. **The regulation authorises the first use. The second is Helix's, and it is labelled as Helix's
wherever it operates.**

**The risk-basing of validation *extent* is a different thing and comes from a different place.** December
2018 Q&A, **Q3**, both sentences of the answer and not only the second:

> *"Yes, a CGMP workflow… is an intended use of a computer system to be checked through validation (see §§
> 211.63, 211.68(b), and 211.110(a)). The extent of validation studies should be commensurate with the risk
> posed by the automated system."*

That is a nonbinding *should*, in a guidance, and this phase never presents it as the authority for
`21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s *shall*, or the other way round. FDA's nonbinding recommendation that validation extent
be risk-based appears in the August 2003 guidance `§III.C.1` — *"We suggest that your decision to validate
computerized systems, and the extent of the validation, take into account the impact the systems have on
your ability to meet predicate rule requirements."* — and again at Q3, **whose answer treats
`21 CFR 211.68(b)` as among the provisions under which a CGMP workflow is checked through validation**.
**Helix's reading — that `211.68(b)`'s third sentence graduates input/output verification alone — is a
reading of the text, not FDA's stated position, and is recorded as Helix's.**

## 3. The two graduations

| | **Data integrity risk** | **System complexity and reliability** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it grades** | **The record.** What it decides, and whether an alteration to it would be noticed | **The system.** Its configuration, its interfaces, and its failure history |
| **The unit it grades** | The **record-type/system pair** — `ADR-0016` | The **system** — one determination per system |
| **What it sets** | **The depth at which the framework requires each of `21 CFR 11.10`'s eleven paragraphs to be evidenced** in respect of that record type. **It removes no paragraph** — `../04.05-the-framework.md §6` rule 7 | **The degree and frequency of input/output verification**, and nothing else **under `21 CFR 211.68(b)`**. Helix additionally uses the same band, **as its own method choice and not under that provision**, as one of two inputs to evidence depth — `§2` |
| **Where it comes from** | Phase 03's `DR` and `ND` registers, taken as an input and never re-scored | **`21 CFR 211.68(b)`, directly and for its own subject matter** |
| **Its own scale** | Phase 03's, published at `03.05 §7`, and Helix is held to it — `IS-19` | **High, Medium or Low**, one band per system, on the criteria at `§4`. Helix's own, and Helix is held to that too |
| **Where the determinations live** | `../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/logs/risk-register.md` | `logs/system-rigour-register.md`, **in a separate column that is never combined with the first** |

**Neither is derived from the other. Neither is averaged with the other. Neither is a proxy for the other.**

## 4. What the complexity-and-reliability determination actually asks

The determination is made once per system, by the system's named owner with the Computerised System
Validation Lead, and it is recorded on the complexity-and-reliability determination record at `templates/`.
It asks two sets of questions and records a band against each system with the basis written out.

**Complexity** is a question about how many ways the system can behave. It asks what the system does beyond
holding and returning what was entered: whether it calculates, transforms, integrates, re-ranges or renders
on demand; how much of its behaviour is configuration rather than product; how many interfaces carry
records into it and out of it, and whether those interfaces are automatic or manual; and how many distinct
record types it holds. **A system whose output is a function of parameters somebody set is more complex than
one whose output is what somebody typed, and the difference is not a matter of taste.**

**Reliability** is a question about how it has actually behaved. It asks what the system's failure history
shows — interruptions, restores, corrupted or lost data, interface failures, unplanned downtime; whether
those events were detected by the system or by a person noticing an oddity; and whether the system is under
change control and vendor support at all. **Reliability here is an observed property of a system in
service, not a vendor claim about a product**, and where Helix holds no history the record says so and the
basis is the absence.

**Three things the determination is not.** It is not a criticality tier, and nothing about the importance
of what the system does enters it — that question belongs to the other graduation. **It is not a software
category**, and this repository publishes no category count and no enumeration of categories; GAMP 5 Second
Edition, ISPE, July 2022, is good-practice guidance and never a requirement, adopted voluntarily and
labelled as voluntary at every citation. And it is not a maturity model: nothing in regulation or guidance
requires a maturity model, a tier or a scale, and this one is Helix's.

## 5. Why they are not merged

**Because they grade different objects and answer different questions, and a number computed across them
would describe neither.**

`ADR-0017` refused, in Phase 03, to average FDA's five attributes into one figure per pair, on the ground
that **a mean of five attributes is a number that describes nothing** — they are not five measurements of
one underlying quantity, and averaging them lets four comfortable determinations conceal one uncomfortable
one. **`ADR-0025` is that discipline applied to a different object.** The objects here are not five
attributes of a record; they are a record's exposure and a system's construction. **They are less
commensurable than the five were, not more.**

Four consequences of merging, each of which the phase would have had to live with.

**A merged index would let a simple system holding a critical record look moderate.** The record decides
whether a batch is released; the system is a spreadsheet-shaped thing with one interface and no failure
history. Averaged, the pair is unremarkable. **The information that would have made anybody look — that the
record decides something — is the information the average destroys.**

**And it would let a complicated system holding an inconsequential record look moderate too**, which is the
same error costing money instead of costing assurance: verification effort spent on movements that do not
matter, on the strength of an index nobody can decompose.

**A merged index cannot be cited to a regulation.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires input/output verification to
be based on complexity and reliability. A firm that bases it on a composite of complexity, reliability and
record risk has based it on something the regulation does not name, and cannot show which part of its number
carried the requirement. **Keeping the graduation separate is what makes the citation true.**

**And a merged index cannot be re-run when one input changes.** Phase 03's registers are re-run on the
trigger at `ADR-0022`; a system's complexity and reliability change when it is reconfigured, re-interfaced
or fails. **Two determinations that move independently must be recorded independently, or every change to
one silently rewrites a number that was justified by the other.**

## 6. Where they disagree, the higher governs

**Where both graduations exist for a system and they point in different directions, the framework requires
the higher of the two. It does not take a mean, and it does not take the lower.**

The rule is narrow and it is worth stating exactly what it does and does not do.

**It does not merge them.** The two determinations stay in two columns and both are readable after the rule
has been applied. What the rule settles is a question about **the resulting rigour** — the evidence set the
system's validation plan has to produce — and not a question about either determination. **Neither column
is edited to match the other.**

**It applies to rigour and not to the input/output verification the regulation graduates.** The degree and
frequency of input/output verification is set by complexity and reliability, alone, because that is what
`21 CFR 211.68(b)` says. **The higher-governs rule cannot lower it and does not reach it.**

**And it is a decision to accept a cost, not to avoid one.** The cheaper rule was available and would have
looked more sophisticated: average the two, or let the more recent determination govern, or let the data
integrity determination govern because it is closer to the patient. **All three would have produced a lower
requirement on some systems than either input on its own supported, and a rule that can produce a
requirement neither of its inputs justifies is not a rule, it is an arithmetic accident.**

**The rule is conservative by construction and cannot be falsified by an outcome; what can be inspected is
its effect.** The data integrity determination changes the requirement on **five of the sixty-one systems
and on no others**, and the register publishes which five. **A reader who thinks two determinations are not
worth five changes has the figure needed to say so.**

**Where the two can be compared at all is a smaller population than the estate**, and the counts — how many
systems the two agree on and how many they disagree on — are in `logs/system-rigour-register.md`, with
**`IS-26`** carrying the disagreement out of the phase. This chapter states the rule; the register states
the result.

## 7. The twenty-five, and why this is a consequence rather than a default

**Phase 02 determined that sixty-one systems hold at least one Part 11 record. Phase 03 scored seventy-four
of the one hundred and twenty-one pairs on those systems and refused to score forty-seven.** Split by
system, that means **thirty-six systems carry only scored pairs and twenty-five carry at least one pair
Phase 03 could not score. Thirty-six and twenty-five make sixty-one.**

**The twenty-five have no established data integrity position.** Not a low one, not a provisional one, and
not one that may be inferred from the pairs on the same system that were scored — `ADR-0018` refused that
inference in Phase 03 and this phase does not reintroduce it by the back door. **A pair Helix cannot assess
is not a pair Helix has assessed favourably**, and a system carrying one is a system about which the first
graduation currently says nothing.

**So on those twenty-five systems the framework's rigour is set by complexity and reliability alone, until
the data integrity position is determined.** `IS-27` carries it.

**That is a stated consequence and it is not a default.** The distinction matters and the phase insists on
it.

**A default is a value the method supplies when it has no input.** It is invisible in the output, it looks
like a determination, and nobody returns to it. **A stated consequence is a recorded fact that one of two
inputs is missing, published with the name of the missing thing and the route by which it arrives.** Every
one of the twenty-five carries, on its row, that the data integrity position is **not established** rather
than a band; the closure conditions are already written, per pair, on Phase 03's unscorable register, and
`DP-16`, `DP-17`, `DP-18` and `DP-20` are the routes.

**And the consequence is not stable.** When a pair on one of the twenty-five is determined, it is
re-assessed under `ADR-0022`, enters the scored register as a new determination with its own date, and the
system's rigour is re-derived under this chapter's rules — which may raise it. **A system whose rigour rests
on one graduation today is a system whose rigour may move when the second arrives**, and the framework says
so rather than presenting today's figure as settled.

## 8. What each graduation may and may not be used for

| Use | Data integrity risk | Complexity and reliability |
|---|---|---|
| Set the depth at which each of the eleven paragraphs is evidenced. **Neither removes a paragraph** | **Yes** — `../04.05-the-framework.md §6` | **Yes — as Helix's own method choice and not under `21 CFR 211.68(b)`**: through the higher-governs rule at `§6`, and as the only input on the 25 systems with no established data integrity position |
| Set the degree and frequency of input/output verification | **No** | **Yes — `21 CFR 211.68(b)`, directly** |
| Set an audit trail configuration or a review frequency | **No** | **No** — Phase 05, and the frequency is inherited from the review of the underlying data |
| Set a retention period | **No** | **No** — Phase 07 |
| Order the estate, or say which system is worked on first | **No** — a band confers no ordering, and nothing in Helix's quality system yet says what any band obliges anybody to do | **No** |
| Substitute for the other where the other is missing | **No** | **No.** Where the first is missing the second sets rigour alone and the absence is recorded — `§7`, `IS-27` |
| Be averaged, indexed or combined with the other | **Never** | **Never** — `ADR-0025` |

## 9. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no result.** How the sixty-one distribute across the complexity-and-reliability bands, how
  many systems the two graduations agree and disagree on, and what rigour any named system carries are in
  `logs/system-rigour-register.md` and are cited, not restated.
- **It re-scores nothing.** Phase 03's registers are an input to this phase and are never re-scored;
  no pair's determination changes here, and no `ND` row becomes a `DR` row.
- **It states no validation outcome and no control position.** Whether any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is
  met, for any system, is Phase 05's — `ADR-0029`.
- **It sets no input/output verification for any system.** It says what sets it. What any system's degree
  and frequency actually is, is a validation plan's, and **no system-level validation plan is written at
  this vantage**.
- **It does not resolve `IS-18`.** The transfer of a record between systems is still assessed nowhere, and
  a graduation of input/output verification is not an assessment of the movement it verifies.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion** and forecasts no inspection outcome.
