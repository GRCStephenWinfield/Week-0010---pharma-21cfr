## 1. What this chapter settles

This is the instrument. `04.01-nothing-requires-a-framework.md` said nothing required it,
`04.02-what-the-framework-has-to-answer.md` said what it was handed,
`04.03-the-fourth-clause.md` said what `21 CFR 11.10(a)` actually asks and
`04.04-two-graduations.md` said what sets what. **This chapter says what the framework is.**

Four things: **the framework document's own parts**, **what a system validation plan must contain**, **what
evidence the framework requires for each of `21 CFR 11.10`'s eleven paragraphs**, and **the rigour rules
that decide how much of it a given system has to produce.**

**It states no result.** No system's rigour, no obligation, no procedure and no package appears here.
`04.06-the-obligations.md` to `04.09-the-policy-architecture.md` own those, and
`logs/system-rigour-register.md` carries the sixty-one systems with their two graduations in separate
columns.

**And it states no control position.** The framework says what evidence is **required**. Whether any
paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is met, for any system, is Phase 05's — `ADR-0029`, and `§10` below.

## 2. The framework document, and what its first section says

The framework is one controlled document with a named owner and a named approver, sitting under the
validation policy and above the procedure set. **`04.09-the-policy-architecture.md` owns the architecture —
the policy, this document's place in it, and the procedures beneath it. This chapter owns the content.**

| Section of the framework document | What it contains |
|---|---|
| **1 — Status** | **That nothing in regulation or FDA guidance requires a validation framework, a validation master plan or a validation policy**, that this instrument is Helix's own, and the three provisions that do require something in writing — `21 CFR 211.100(a)`, `21 CFR 211.22(d)` and `21 CFR 211.68(a)`. **`ADR-0023` requires this section and requires it first** |
| **2 — Scope** | The systems the framework reaches, by reference to Phase 02's determination, with the express statement that **Part 11 applies to records and not to systems** and that a system is in scope because of the records it holds — `ADR-0001` |
| **3 — The four clauses** | `21 CFR 11.10(a)` quoted whole, the four clauses specified separately, and the requirement that the fourth be specified explicitly — `ADR-0024` |
| **4 — The two graduations** | What each grades, what each sets, that neither is derived from or averaged with the other, and the rule where they disagree — `ADR-0025` |
| **5 — The validation plan** | What a system-level validation plan must contain — `§4` below |
| **6 — The evidence requirement** | What evidence is required for each of `21 CFR 11.10`'s eleven paragraphs — `§5` below |
| **7 — The rigour rules** | How the two graduations set depth — `§6` below |
| **8 — Adopted material** | CSA and GAMP 5, **each labelled as adopted voluntarily at every citation** — `ADR-0026`, `§8` below |
| **9 — What the framework does not state** | No control position, no validation outcome, no audit trail configuration, no retention rule — `ADR-0029`, `§10` below |

**Section 1 is first for a reason and the reason is not modesty.** An instrument that presents itself as a
requirement invites a reader to stop asking what actually is one, and a reader who believes the framework is
the obligation will discharge the framework and think the regulation is satisfied.
`04.01 §6` argues it and `ADR-0023` records it.

## 3. The unit, and why it is the system

**The framework's unit is the system.** A validation plan is written for a system; a validation package is
held against one; a system has an owner who can be named.

Phase 03's unit was the record-type/system pair, because the same record type is not at the same risk on
every system that holds it — `ADR-0016`. **Those two units do not line up, and the framework says what
happens where they do not** rather than quietly choosing one.

**The rule is that the system is the unit of the plan and the pair is the unit of the requirement.** A
system's validation plan is one document. Inside it, the evidence required about a record is required per
record type held on that system, because that is the level at which Phase 03 determined anything. **A plan
that states one evidence requirement for a system holding four record types of different exposure has
averaged something the register kept apart**, and `ADR-0017`'s discipline forbids it as firmly here as it
did in Phase 03.

## 4. What a system validation plan must contain

Eleven elements. A plan missing any of them is not approvable, and the plan template at `templates/` carries
all eleven as headed sections so that an omission is visible as an empty heading rather than as an absence.

| # | Element | What it has to say |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The system, identified** | Its `SYS-nnn` identifier, its named owner, and its closed-or-open classification with the basis — **the test is who controls access**, `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`, and hosting is not the test |
| **2** | **The records** | Every in-scope record type the system holds, by `RT-nn`, **with the predicate rule that made each a required record.** Carried from Phase 02's determination and not re-derived |
| **3** | **The data integrity position** | The Phase 03 determination for each pair, **or the statement that it is not established**, with the identifier of the register row it is read from |
| **4** | **The complexity-and-reliability band** | The determination, its band, and its basis — **recorded in its own field and never combined with element 3** |
| **5** | **The rigour** | What follows from elements 3 and 4 under `§6`, written out, including which of the two governed where they differed |
| **6** | **The four clauses of `21 CFR 11.10(a)`** | **Four separate statements of what evidence the plan will produce**, with the fourth stated explicitly — `ADR-0024` |
| **7** | **The eleven paragraphs** | What evidence will be produced for each of `21 CFR 11.10(a)` to `(k)`, at the depth `§6` sets — `§5` below |
| **8** | **Input and output verification** | **The degree and frequency**, set from element 4 alone, under `21 CFR 211.68(b)`, and what is checked for accuracy on the way in and on the way out |
| **9** | **The acceptance basis** | What will be accepted as evidence for each item, decided **before** the evidence is produced, and by whom |
| **10** | **Roles** | The named individuals who write, execute, review and approve. **No control, procedure, record or obligation in this programme is owned by a team** — `ADR-0008` |
| **11** | **What the plan does not cover** | Stated as absences, with the phase or the route that owns each |

**Element 9 is the one that is skipped and it is the one that decides whether the package means anything.**
Acceptance criteria written after the result is known are not criteria; they are a description of the
result. **The framework requires the acceptance basis to be approved before execution, and a plan that is
amended after execution carries the amendment, its date and its approver on its face.**

**No system-level validation plan is written at this vantage.** The framework requires them; none exists.
That is stated here as a fact about 2026-11-30 and `04.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md` carries it.

## 5. What evidence is required, paragraph by paragraph

**The framework requires evidence for all eleven paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10`, `(a)` to `(k)`.** Not for a
selection of them, not for the ones outside the enforcement discretion set, and not for the ones a vendor
has a feature for. **The graduations at `§6` set the depth at which each paragraph is evidenced. They remove
none of them, on any system, at any band** — `§6` rule 7.

**Two things to hold while reading the table.** First, **it states what evidence is required and not
whether any of it exists.** A row is a requirement on a future validation plan; **no row is a statement
that any paragraph is or is not met for any system** — `ADR-0029`. Second, **`21 CFR 11.10` has exactly
eleven paragraphs and Part 11 has exactly ten sections.** There is no `21 CFR 11.60`, no `21 CFR 11.400`
and no Subpart D, and the gaps in the numbering are original to the rule.

| ¶ | Subject | What the framework requires a plan to produce evidence about |
|---|---|---|
| **`(a)`** | **Validation** | **Four separate evidence statements**, one per clause: accuracy; reliability; consistent intended performance; **and the ability to discern invalid or altered records, specified explicitly and never inherited from the first three** — `ADR-0024`, `04.03` |
| **`(b)`** | **Accurate and complete copies** | That copies of the records the system holds can be produced **in both human readable and electronic form**, suitable for inspection, review and copying by the agency — and, for a dynamic record, **what a static rendering does and does not carry**. `02.06` owns the printout test and `21 CFR 211.180(c)` is the predicate-rule position |
| **`(c)`** | **Protection of records throughout the retention period** | What protects the records for as long as they must be kept, and what would demonstrate accurate and ready retrieval. **The retention period itself is not set here** — `IS-14`, `DP-18`, Phase 07 |
| **`(d)`** | **Limiting system access to authorised individuals** | How access is limited and what evidence would show it. **The design is Phase 05's** — this phase specifies the evidence requirement and no access arrangement |
| **`(e)`** | **Audit trails** | What the system records of operator entries and actions that create, modify or delete; that record changes do not obscure previously recorded information; and the retention of the audit trail relative to the subject records. **No configuration and no review frequency is set here** — Phase 05, and `IS-05` |
| **`(f)`** | **Operational system checks** | What enforces permitted sequencing of steps and events, **as appropriate** — the regulation's own qualifier, and it is not dropped |
| **`(g)`** | **Authority checks** | What ensures that only authorised individuals can use the system, **electronically sign a record**, access **the operation or computer system** input or output device, alter a record or perform the operation at hand |
| **`(h)`** | **Device checks** | What determines, **as appropriate**, the validity of the source of a data input or an operational instruction |
| **`(i)`** | **Education, training and experience** | The determination that the persons who develop, maintain or use the system have the education, training and experience to perform their assigned tasks. **`21 CFR 211.25(a)` and `(b)` are the predicate-rule position, and Part 211 does not expressly require the training record itself to be maintained** — the obligation is inferred rather than express, `RT-42` |
| **`(j)`** | **Written accountability policies** | The existence of, and adherence to, written policies holding individuals accountable and responsible for actions initiated under their electronic signatures |
| **`(k)`** | **Controls over systems documentation** | Controls over the distribution of, access to and use of documentation for system operation and maintenance, **and** revision and change control procedures **to maintain an audit trail that documents time-sequenced development and modification of systems documentation** — `(k)(2)`'s own words, and they are the content of the requirement rather than a detail of it |

**Three notes the table would otherwise hide.**

**Enforcement discretion changes nothing in this table.** FDA has stated an intention to exercise
enforcement discretion over `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)`, `(e)` and `(k)(2)`, and over the corresponding
requirements in `21 CFR 11.30`. **An intention is a thing an agency holds today and may revise. It is not a
permission, it does not amend the Code of Federal Regulations, and those paragraphs are still printed in the
regulation exactly as they were in 1997** — `ADR-0003`. The framework requires evidence for all eleven and
records the discretion as a fact rather than as a reason to require less.

**All sixty-one systems are closed systems**, on Phase 02's determination and on the test the regulation
sets, which is **who controls access** — `ADR-0011`. **`21 CFR 11.30` is therefore not engaged for any of
them at this vantage.** The framework nonetheless states the rule for the case where a future classification
changes: an open system's controls **include those identified in `21 CFR 11.10`, as appropriate, *and*
additional measures**, and **the additional measures are additive and never a substitute**. `IS-09` remains
open, and nothing here states a control position for the three formerly misclassified systems.

**And a requirement is not a control.** A row in this table obliges a plan to produce evidence. It does not
implement anything, does not configure anything, and does not assert that anything works.

## 6. The rigour rules

**Rigour, in this framework, means one thing: the depth at which each required evidence item is produced.**
It does not mean which paragraphs are evidenced — all eleven are, for every system, rule 7 — and it does not
mean how many pages, how many test scripts or how many signatures.

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| **1** | **The data integrity position of the record sets the depth at which** each of the eleven paragraphs is evidenced in respect of that record type. **It removes no paragraph** — rule 7. Read per pair from Phase 03's registers, never re-scored |
| **2** | **The complexity-and-reliability band sets the degree and frequency of input/output verification, alone**, under `21 CFR 211.68(b)`, which authorises that use and no other. **Helix additionally uses the same band as one of two inputs to evidence depth — its own method choice, not made under `21 CFR 211.68(b)`, and labelled as Helix's wherever it operates** — `04.04 §2` |
| **3** | **Neither is derived from the other and neither is averaged with the other** — `ADR-0025` |
| **4** | **Where the two point in different directions, the plan produces the higher requirement.** Not a mean, and not the lower. The plan records which of the two governed |
| **5** | **Where the data integrity position is not established, the complexity-and-reliability band is the only input to depth** — rule 2's second use, and Helix's — **and the absence is recorded on the row as not established**, never as a band, never as a default — `IS-27`, `04.04 §7` |
| **6** | **Depth has three levels and they are named, not numbered** — *stated*, *demonstrated*, *demonstrated and independently reviewed*. **A resulting rigour of Low requires each evidence item to be *stated*; Medium requires it to be *demonstrated*; High requires it to be *demonstrated and independently reviewed*.** Phase 03's *Moderate* and this phase's *Medium* are different words for the same depth, and neither vocabulary is converted into the other — `logs/system-rigour-register.md §1`. A number would invite arithmetic |
| **7** | **No evidence item is removed by either graduation.** All eleven paragraphs are evidenced for every system; the graduations move depth, not coverage |
| **8** | **The rigour of a system is re-derived when either input moves** — on `ADR-0022`'s re-assessment trigger, or on a change to the system that alters its complexity or its failure history |

**Rule 7 is the one that keeps this from becoming a tiering scheme.** A framework in which a low band
removes a requirement is a framework in which the band is the compliance decision. **Here the band decides
how hard Helix looks, not whether it looks**, and a reader who finds a system with no evidence requirement
against some paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` has found a defect rather than a low rating.

**Rule 6 refuses a number deliberately.** Three named depths cannot be averaged, cannot be summed across
paragraphs, and cannot be presented as a score. `03.05 §8` made the same argument about the scale: **an
elaborate instrument is not more rigorous, it is more surface area**, and under `21 CFR 211.22(d)` every
additional rule in a written method is an additional way to depart from it.

## 7. What the framework requires of the evidence itself

**Evidence is an artefact that exists.** The framework carries the repository's rule without softening it:
an artefact that has not been produced has no `EV` number, and the evidence index is not a plan.

**Every evidence item is attributed to the paragraph and clause it serves.** An item may serve more than
one; **what is forbidden is a package in which no item is attributed to `21 CFR 11.10(a)`'s fourth clause
and the clause is nonetheless reported as addressed** — `ADR-0024`.

**Every evidence item carries a named individual owner**, because the regulations Helix works to describe
people doing things — `ADR-0008`, and `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`'s *"initials or signature of a second person"*
is the shape of the point.

**And the programme manager does not approve validation evidence.** Delivery pressure and evidence adequacy
must not sit with one person — `01.07 §6`. The Computerised System Validation Lead reports to the Head of
Quality Assurance and not to IT, for the reason `ADR-0002` gives about the Data Integrity Lead: **the
function that judges whether a system's validated state holds should not sit in the line that builds and
administers it.**

## 8. What is adopted voluntarily, and what it is used for

**Two bodies of material are adopted. Neither is a requirement, and each is labelled as adopted at every
citation** — `ADR-0026`, and `ADR-0005` fixed the same discipline in Phase 01.

**FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance.** It is issued by **CDRH and CBER**, its
regulatory anchor is **`21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by the Quality Management System Regulation**, with
ISO 13485:2016 incorporated by reference, and every scope sentence in it is qualified *"for medical
devices"*. **21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 are not mentioned anywhere in it. It does not apply to Helix.** What
the framework takes from it, voluntarily, is the correction to the conventional wisdom: it contemplates that
*"unscripted testing may be better suited to assure the software performs as intended even for high process
risk features, functions, and operations"*, and it names scenario testing, exploratory testing and error
guessing. **The unit it grades is the feature, function or operation and not the system.**
**Helix will not reproduce the oversimplified version**, and where an unscripted technique appears in a
plan the requirement cited alongside it is `21 CFR 211.68`.

**GAMP 5 Second Edition — ISPE, July 2022 — is good-practice guidance and never a requirement.** ISPE's own
words, with their own subject, are that *"GAMP® guidance does not define a prescriptive method or a
standard, but rather provides pragmatic guidance, approaches, and tools for the practitioner."* What the framework takes from it, voluntarily, is the emphasis on critical thinking over
mechanical categorisation. **This repository publishes no software-category count and no enumeration of the
categories**, in this phase or any other, and no plan under this framework justifies a decision by naming
one.

**Neither is ever cited as the reason for anything.** The framework's requirements are cited to
`21 CFR 11.10`, to `21 CFR 211.68`, to `21 CFR 211.22(d)` and to `21 CFR 211.100(a)`. **Where CSA or GAMP 5
appears, it appears as adopted good practice and the requirement cited alongside it is the regulation.**
The labelling is repetitive, and the repetition is the control.

## 10. The framework states no control position

**`ADR-0029`, taken at `DEC-420` on 2026-11-25 by Colm Ó Braonáin: the framework states what evidence is
required and states no control position.**

**Not once, for any system, does any document in this phase say that a paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is met
or is not met.** That is Phase 05's determination at `MS-09`, `IS-09` and `IS-20` are open, and the reason
for the refusal is not caution — it is that **no evidence has been produced.** No validation plan is written,
no protocol has been run, and no test result exists. **A statement that a paragraph is met, made from a
framework rather than from evidence, is a statement about a document.**

The rule has a second limb that matters more than it looks. **A requirement is not a control, and an
obligation is not evidence.** A framework that requires evidence for eleven paragraphs has not implemented
eleven controls, and an obligation register full of rows has not discharged one of them.

## 11. What this chapter does not do

- **It states no result** — no rigour outcome for any named system, no obligation, no procedure, no package.
  `04.06` to `04.09`, and `logs/system-rigour-register.md`.
- **It states no validation outcome.** Nothing is validated by this chapter; **no package is executed, no
  protocol is run and no test result exists.**
- **It states no control position for any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10`, for any system** — `ADR-0029`.
- **It configures no audit trail, sets no review frequency as a requirement, designs no access arrangement
  and deploys no electronic signature.** Phase 05.
- **It sets no retention, backup or archive rule.** Phase 07.
- **It does no chromatography-specific work.** Phase 06.
- **It publishes no software-category count and no enumeration of categories**, and it never writes *as
  required by GAMP*.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome.
