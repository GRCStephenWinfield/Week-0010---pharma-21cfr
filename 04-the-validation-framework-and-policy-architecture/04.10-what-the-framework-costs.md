## 1. What this chapter settles

A framework that states what it requires and not what it takes is a framework nobody has costed. This chapter
states the effort, the sequence, who performs it and — the part that is usually left out — **what the work
displaces**, because the programme has 9.2 FTE-equivalents and every one of them belongs to somebody with a
substantive job.

**No re-costing of the programme has been performed at this vantage and none is stated here.** The committed
figure is $3,412,000 against a $3,600,000 envelope, with $188,000 of unallocated headroom, and it has not
moved since `GOV-01`. What follows describes work, its sequence and its owners. It does not revise a budget
line, spend contingency in advance, or predict that either will be spent.

## 2. What has already been paid, and by whom

The phase itself is sixty-one days, from the Phase 03 vantage of 2026-09-30 to `MS-08` on 2026-11-30, and it
produced fourteen numbered chapters, seven architecture decision records, five governance records, five logs,
four diagrams and four templates. **Three pieces of work inside it are larger than they look on the
inventory.**

| The work | What it took | Who did it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The complexity-and-reliability determination, 61 systems** | A determination per system on three observable features — configuration, interfaces and failure history — each recorded with its basis on its own form. **It could not be derived from anything that already existed**, because `DP-15` recorded that neither axis of Phase 03's scale is complexity and neither is reliability | Colm Ó Braonáin, with Amara Sissoko for the estate and Joachim Reuter for the interfaces | `EV-411`, `EV-412` |
| **The validation package read, 44 packages against four clauses** | Four columns per package, a document-and-page reference for each coverage claim, and a reading rule that refused a validation summary report's conclusion as evidence of anything | Colm Ó Braonáin | `EV-420`, `EV-421` |
| **The procedure estate read, 63 procedures against 38 obligations** | A cell per obligation per candidate procedure, and the three-way classification on every one of the thirty-eight | Terrence Abbatiello, reviewed by Priyanka Venkataraman | `EV-417`, `EV-418`, `EV-419` |

**The procedure estate read is the one to notice**, because it is the piece of work whose output is a
negative. It produced twelve findings of the form *this procedure exists, covers the subject matter, and does
not contain the sentence*, and each of those twelve took a reading of a document that ended in nothing being
found. **Work whose product is a well-founded negative is the work most likely to be cut**, and `PR-31`
carries the risk that a later re-read is scoped to look only where something is expected.

## 3. What the framework requires next, and what it does not

**The framework requires a system validation plan for every one of the 61 systems and not one exists at this
vantage.** That is the largest single item of work the framework creates, it is `IS-28`'s subject, and it is
recorded here rather than at the end so that no reader takes the phase's artefact count for the phase's
output.

| Work the framework creates | Scale | Owner | Sequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **System validation plans** | **61**, one per system, each specifying the evidence for all eleven paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` and specifying the fourth clause of `(a)` explicitly — `ADR-0024` | Colm Ó Braonáin | Opens after `MS-08`; **`DP-05` is the resource** |
| **The 38 obligations** | 38 acts, 7 named individual owners between them | Per `logs/obligation-register.md` | Sequenced by the evidence each depends on, not by band |
| **The seventeen systems with no package** | The plan has nothing to build on. **`PR-17` recorded this in Phase 02 against the seventeen systems the walk found. The population Phase 04 measures is a different seventeen — the systems inside the 61 holding no validation package — and the two overlap by eleven** | Colm Ó Braonáin | With the other 44 and not after them |
| **The nine SOPs into practice** | Approved at `GOV-19`; **none applied to anything at this vantage** | Per `04.09-the-policy-architecture.md §3` | Training and first application both sit after this vantage |

**And what the framework does not create.** It creates no requirement to re-execute any of the 44 packages,
no requirement to re-open any Phase 03 determination, and no requirement to produce a document that restates
another document. **A framework whose first act is to invalidate the estate's existing evidence has spent the
programme's money on paperwork.**

## 5. What it displaces

**Three dependencies draw on the same quality unit capacity and all three are needed now.** `DP-07` is the
review and approval capacity for the two record review procedure revisions; `DP-11` is the capacity to draft
the procedure requiring the applicability determination to be maintained; `DP-19` is the capacity to write
the procedure that says what a determination obliges Helix to do. **Phase 03 recorded the collision rather
than discovering it here**, and `DP-19` was the newest and the largest of the three.

**This phase consumed `DP-19` and did not consume the other two.** `SOP-DI-01` and `SOP-DI-05` are what
`DP-19` bought. `DP-11` is narrowed and stays open, because `SOP-DI-05` fires `ADR-0015`'s trigger but
nothing has been maintained under it. `DP-07` has not moved. **The quality unit's capacity was spent on the
newest of the three, which is the ordinary outcome when three claims arrive at one desk and one of them is
this phase's deliverable.**

Two further displacements are recorded because they are real and neither is a complaint.

**`CAL-02`, the CSV Working Group, met weekly through the phase and its agenda was the framework.** The
technical questions that ordinarily reach it — validation approach per system, evidence adequacy — queued
behind the instrument being built. That is the right order and it is the order `MS-07` before `MS-08` exists
to enforce: **the validation framework's rigour is set against assessed risk, and building the framework
first sets the rigour by habit.**

**The three vendor-hosted systems have not moved.** `IS-09` records that `21 CFR 11.10` has never been
assessed for `SYS-040`, `SYS-041` and `SYS-056`, and this phase did not assess it, because assessing it would
be a control position and `ADR-0029` forbids one. All three carry a rigour band and a place in the
obligations register, and none carries an assessment. **`DP-12` is open and Phase 05 owns it.**

**The $72,000 line is the one this phase makes more visible without changing.** Phase 02 recorded the
mismatch and declined to re-cost it. This phase has now produced a rigour band for all three vendor-hosted
systems and an obligation register that names two of them — `OB-27` on `SYS-040` and `OB-30` on `SYS-056` —
and it declines to re-cost it too, for the same reason: **a figure revised inside the document that discovered
the problem is a figure nobody outside that document has agreed.** `PR-01` carries it.

**No figure in this chapter is a fine, a penalty, a sanction or a forfeiture.** None exists anywhere in this
programme, and professional fees are not one.

## 7. The sequence, and the one ordering rule that is not the programme's

**The framework does not queue the estate and this chapter does not queue it either.** A band is not an
ordering, no system goes anywhere before another because of its rigour position, and no obligation is first
because the pair behind it rates High — the position Phase 03 fixed and this phase keeps without variation.

**What does order the work is evidence dependency**, and it runs in one direction only.

| Order | What has to happen | Why it cannot happen later |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The complexity-and-reliability determination — `OB-34` | The band is the input to the input/output verification requirement. **Done in this phase, for all 61** |
| **2** | The system validation plan for a system | The plan specifies the evidence set. Producing evidence before the plan says what evidence means producing what was to hand |
| **3** | The obligations whose act produces the evidence a plan calls for | An obligation discharged before the plan exists has been discharged against nothing |
| **4** | The evidence itself | It is what Phase 05 assesses a control position against, and it does not exist at this vantage — `IS-28` |

**Two of the four are complete and two are not, and the two that are not are the two that produce anything a
reader would call a result.** Recording the sequence in that form is the only honest way to describe a phase
whose output is a set of requirements.

**The ordering rule the programme does not own is `MS-07` before `MS-08`**, and it has now been paid for: the
framework's rigour is set against assessed risk, so the assessment had to exist first, and it did. **The
corresponding rule for the next interval is that `MS-08` precedes `MS-09` for the same class of reason** —
the evidence set has to exist before anybody can say which of the eleven paragraphs it satisfies.

## 8. What the framework buys, stated honestly

A cost chapter that lists only costs has not made the case, so the benefits are stated — and stated as what
they are, which is smaller than a reader might expect.

- **A question that can be answered.** *Which requirement does this evidence discharge?* now has a route:
  `04.09-the-policy-architecture.md §5` runs it in four steps in either direction.
- **A gap class that is now counted.** The twelve at `IS-24` were invisible to the question an auditor asks,
  and are now a numbered population with an owner — `ADR-0028`.
- **A graduation that exists.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)` has always graduated input/output verification on
  complexity and reliability. **Until `OB-34` was performed, Helix had no complexity-and-reliability
  determination to graduate from**, so the `shall` had nothing to attach to.
- **A refusal that survives.** Phase 03's 47 unscored pairs arrive here as 19 obligations rather than as 47
  low ratings, which is the whole return on `ADR-0018` — `04.06-the-obligations.md §5`.

**And what it does not buy.** It does not buy a validated system, a control, or a single piece of evidence.
**A framework requires evidence; it does not produce any**, and `04.11-what-a-framework-cannot-fix.md §2`
owns that argument in full.

## 9. What this chapter does not state

- **No re-costing, no variance and no revised figure.** The charter's lines stand as approved at `GOV-01`.
- **No forecast of what the next phase will cost**, and no assumption that contingency or headroom will be
  spent.
- **No validation outcome and no control position.** **No package executed, no protocol run, no test result,
  and no statement that any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is met for any system** — `ADR-0029`.
- **No assessment of any individual's work, capacity or performance.** §4 describes what the framework asks
  of a role. **No document in this repository assesses any individual's work.**
- **No forecast of an inspection outcome, an internal audit result or a third-party audit result.** None has
  occurred and none is anticipated anywhere.
- **No legal conclusion of any kind.**
