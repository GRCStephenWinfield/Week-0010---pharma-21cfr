## 1. What this chapter settles

Every preceding chapter of this phase has argued for the framework. This one states what it cannot do, and
it is written from the position that the most dangerous moment in a remediation programme is the one just
after the instrument is approved, when the instrument starts to be mistaken for the remedy.

The chapter makes four arguments. **A framework requires evidence and does not produce any.** **It does not
close a single one of the gaps it names.** **Under `21 CFR 211.22(d)` a written procedure that nobody
follows is exposure rather than compliance, so writing procedures increases Helix's surface area before it
reduces anything.** And **the material Helix adopted voluntarily cannot be leaned on**, because neither
body of it is a requirement and neither confers a status.

**It states no result.** What the framework found, what the procedure estate contains and what Helix's
existing packages address are `04.06-the-obligations.md` to `04.09-the-policy-architecture.md`'s, and this
chapter cites them.

## 2. A framework requires evidence. It does not produce any

**Nothing at Helix is validated by the publication of this phase.**

At the vantage: **no system-level validation plan is written.** No protocol has been drafted, none has been
executed, and **no test result of any kind exists.** No evidence item has been produced against any
paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` for any system. `04.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md` carries the full
inventory of absences and `04.10-what-the-framework-costs.md` carries what producing the evidence will
take.

The distinction the chapter turns on is stated in the phase's own terms and it is not a play on words.
**A requirement is not a control, and an obligation is not evidence.** The framework at `04.05` requires
evidence for eleven paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10`. Requiring it implements nothing, configures nothing,
demonstrates nothing about any system, and changes the condition of not one record in Helix's estate.

**And there is a specific trap on the other side of that sentence.** A programme that has just published a
framework has produced a large, well-argued, approved artefact, and the natural reading of a large approved
artefact is that something has been fixed. **What has been fixed is that Helix now knows what it must
produce.** Phase 03 ended on the same shape of statement about knowing — *Helix now knows, pair by pair and
attribute by attribute, and knowing is not a control.* **Phase 04 ends on its successor: Helix now knows
what evidence it owes, and owing is not producing.**

## 3. It closes none of the gaps it names

Every gap this phase describes is still open at the vantage, and the framework's relationship to each of
them is to **require** something rather than to supply it.

| Gap | What the framework does | What actually closes it |
|---|---|---|
| **The fourth clause of `21 CFR 11.10(a)` is not evidenced** | Requires it to be specified explicitly and evidenced separately in every validation plan — `ADR-0024` | Evidence, produced per system, against plans none of which is written at this vantage. `04.07-the-packages-helix-already-holds.md` reports the position on the packages Helix already holds |
| **Forty-seven pairs cannot be scored** | Records, per system, that the data integrity position is **not established**, and sets rigour from the other graduation alone — `IS-27`, `04.04 §7` | The determinations themselves: `DP-16`, `DP-17`, `DP-18` and `DP-20`, owned outside this phase. **Every one of the forty-seven already carries its closure condition** — `IS-16` |
| **No retention period exists for any in-scope record type** | Requires evidence about protection of records throughout the retention period — `21 CFR 11.10(c)` | A retention schedule. **This phase sets no retention period** — `IS-14`, `DP-14`, `DP-18`, Phase 07 |
| **`IS-18` — the transfer between systems is assessed nowhere** | Graduates the degree and frequency of input/output verification under `21 CFR 211.68(b)` | An assessment of the movement itself. **Graduating a verification is not assessing what it verifies**, and this phase claims no substitute assessment — `04.02 §5.2` |
| **`IS-09` — `21 CFR 11.10` has never been assessed for the three vendor-hosted systems** | Requires evidence for all eleven paragraphs, for every system | Phase 05, `MS-09`. **The framework states no control position** — `ADR-0029` |
| **`IS-01` — why four internal audits raised nothing** | Nothing. It bears on none of the four candidate explanations | Phase 08. **`IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted** — `ADR-0012` |

**The pattern is the point.** In every row the framework's contribution is a requirement and the closure
belongs to somebody else, in another phase, against evidence that does not exist yet. **A framework that
appeared in any of those right-hand columns would be a framework claiming to be its own output.**

## 4. Nine more procedures Helix can be found not to have followed — and the provision that bites depends on which procedure it is

**This is the sharp one, and it is the reason this chapter is owned by the Head of Quality Assurance rather
than by the Computerised System Validation Lead.**

**`21 CFR 211.22(d)`, verbatim:**

> *"The responsibilities and procedures applicable to the quality control unit shall be in writing; such
> written procedures shall be followed."*

**Two clauses, and the second is the one this phase has to live with.** The provision does not require a
validation framework, a validation policy or any particular procedure. **What it requires is that whatever
the quality control unit's procedures are, they are written and then followed.**

**Its object is the quality control unit, and not every procedure in the SOP set is one of that unit's.**
Four of the nine are owned outside the quality unit — `04.09-the-policy-architecture.md` names each owner —
and a regulation cited beyond its own subject matter stops being a citation, which is a rule this phase
applies to itself here. The position across the set is this. Under `21 CFR 211.22(d)` the quality control
unit's own procedures must be in writing and followed; under **`21 CFR 211.100(b)`** written production and
process control procedures *"shall be followed in the execution of the various production and process
control functions and shall be documented at the time of performance"*; under **`21 CFR 211.160(a)`** the
same is true of laboratory control mechanisms. **Between them, every one of the nine is a written commitment
enforceable against Helix, and which provision bites depends on which procedure it is.**

> **A written procedure that nobody follows is exposure, not compliance. A firm that never wrote the
> procedure cannot have departed from it; a firm that wrote it and did not follow it has failed a `shall`.**

**The arithmetic of that runs against this phase.** The policy architecture at
`04.09-the-policy-architecture.md` specifies a validation policy, the framework document and a set of nine
standard operating procedures — `04.09` owns their composition and the split between new and amended
procedures. **The moment those nine are effective, Helix has nine more procedures it can be found not to
have followed**, on top of the existing procedure estate that `04.08-where-the-obligations-land.md` read the obligations
against.
Every requirement inside them is a commitment enforceable against Helix under a provision that says *shall*.

**Three consequences the phase accepts rather than hedges.**

**Writing more is not obviously safer.** The instinct in a remediated quality system is to write the
procedure that would have caught the thing, and to write it comprehensively. **A comprehensive procedure is
a large number of commitments, most of which nobody will resource**, and the failure mode is not that the
procedure is wrong but that it is unperformed. `03.05 §8` made the same argument about the scale in Phase 03
and it applies with more force here, because a framework has more moving parts than a five-by-five grid.

**So the framework is deliberately small.** Eleven elements in a validation plan, three named depths rather
than a numeric scale, one determination per system on the second graduation, and no maturity model, tier
or index anywhere. **An elaborate instrument is not more rigorous. It is more surface area.**

**And the exposure is recorded rather than noted and forgotten.** Phase 03 carried the same exposure for
the scale at `IS-19`, and **`IS-19` closed on 2026-11-20 on its own stated condition** — `SOP-DI-01`
requires the assessment's output as the input to rigour, so something now requires the scale to be applied;
`DEC-418`, `EV-428`, `GOV-19`. `DP-19` was the quality unit capacity to write that procedure, and it was
**consumed in this phase rather than left pending**, as `DP-07` and `DP-11` were; all three drew on the same
capacity and all three were needed here.

**Closing it makes the exposure sharper rather than smaller, and that is the whole point of this section.**
Whether the scale has ever been **applied** to anything is `IS-28`'s question and not `IS-19`'s, and at this
vantage the answer is that **the nine procedures exist and nothing has yet been performed under them** —
no system-level validation plan is written, so nothing has been sized under `SOP-DI-01`. Writing nine
procedures does not create the capacity to follow them — that is `DP-22`, the limb `DP-19` did not
discharge — and `04.10-what-the-framework-costs.md` owns what that costs and what it displaces.

**One thing this section does not say.** It does not say Helix should write less than it needs, or that the
right response to `21 CFR 211.22(d)` is to keep the quality system thin. **The provision is a reason to
write procedures that Helix will actually perform, not a reason to write fewer than the obligations
require** — and `ADR-0027` forbids the other route out, which is to leave an obligation unhomed so that no
procedure has to carry it. **An obligation with no home is published, not absorbed.**

## 5. The adopted material cannot be leaned on

**Neither body of material Helix adopted voluntarily can carry a requirement, and neither confers a
status.** `ADR-0026` fixes the labelling and `04.05 §8` says what each is used for; this section says what
neither can do.

**FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance and does not apply to Helix.** It is issued
by CDRH and CBER; Helix is inspected by CDER. Its regulatory anchor is `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by the
Quality Management System Regulation. Its scope sentences are qualified *"for medical devices"* throughout,
and 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 are not mentioned anywhere in it. **Helix manufactures oral solid dose
pharmaceuticals and no medical device.** The common claim that CSA replaces CSV is wrong twice over for a
company like Helix: it is device guidance, and even within its own scope it supersedes **Section 6** of the
2002 software validation guidance — not software validation generally, and not anything at all in Part 211.
**Helix does not say it "follows CSA" as though that were a compliance status. There is no such status.**

**GAMP 5 Second Edition — ISPE, July 2022 — is good-practice guidance and never a requirement.** No control
in this programme is justified by it, no deliverable cites it as its requirement, and this repository never
writes *as required by GAMP* and publishes no software-category count.

**And no third party can discharge any of this.** There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate
and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated software; FDA does not certify electronic systems and does not
pre-evaluate them for Part 11 compliance. **A product has features that support compliance; compliance is a
property of Helix's validated system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated by its trained
people.** Outsourcing to a service provider transfers no responsibility, and `21 CFR 11.10`'s opening words
put the duty on **persons who use** closed systems.

## 6. Three things that will look like the framework working

Recorded now, because each will be available as a claim before any evidence exists to support it.

**An approved framework.** The framework, the policy and the SOP set are approved at **`GOV-19`** on
2026-11-20, `DEC-418`; **`GOV-16` authorised the method before the framework was drafted.** **Approval is a
governance fact about a document.** It is not a statement that any system is
validated, that any control works, or that any record is reliable.

**A complete obligation register with every row homed.** `ADR-0027` requires every obligation to land in a
named procedure or to be recorded as landing nowhere, and `04.08-where-the-obligations-land.md` publishes
the result. **A row that names a procedure is a row that has found a place for a requirement to live. It is
not evidence that the act is performed**, and where the procedure exists and does not require the act it is
a distinct class of gap that `ADR-0028` insists on keeping visible.

**And a rigour determination for every system.** `logs/system-rigour-register.md` carries all sixty-one.
**A rigour determination says how hard Helix will look. It says nothing about what Helix will find**, and a
system carrying a rigour band has not been validated, assessed for control position, or cleared of
anything.

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no result** — no obligation count, no procedure count, no package count, no rigour outcome for
  any system. `04.06` to `04.09`.
- **It states no validation outcome.** Nothing is validated here; no package is executed, no protocol is run
  and no test result exists.
- **It states no control position.** Whether any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is met, for any system, is
  Phase 05's — `ADR-0029`, `IS-20`, `IS-09`.
- **It sets no retention rule, configures no audit trail, sets no review frequency as a requirement,
  designs no access arrangement and deploys no signature.**
- **It does not criticise any individual.** No document in this repository assesses any individual's work,
  and the capacity constraints named here are facts about a quality unit's workload recorded in the RAID
  log — `DP-07`, `DP-11`, `DP-19`, `PR-05`, `PR-10`.
- **An open gap is Helix's own assessment of its own position.** It is not a violation, not a breach, not a
  non-compliance, not a finding and not an inspectional observation.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome — including any forecast about what an inspection would make of nine new procedures.
