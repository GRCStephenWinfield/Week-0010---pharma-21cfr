## 1. What this chapter settles

`04.06-the-obligations.md` produced thirty-eight acts. `04.08-where-the-obligations-land.md` found that
seventeen of them do not currently arrive at a sentence requiring them — twelve landing in a procedure that
exists and does not require the act, five landing nowhere at all. **This chapter is the response: what Helix
writes, at what level, and how a reader gets from an obligation to the sentence that requires it.**

It is owned by the Head of Quality Assurance rather than by the Computerised System Validation Lead, and that
is deliberate. **The documents below enter Helix's quality system, and the quality system is not the
programme's to alter.** `21 CFR 211.22` gives the quality unit its responsibility and authority, and
`21 CFR 211.22(d)` requires its responsibilities and procedures to be in writing and provides that *"such
written procedures shall be followed."* A validation framework that wrote itself into the quality system
without the quality unit approving it would be a programme structure wearing a procedure's clothes.

## 2. Three levels, and what each is for

| Level | Document | What it does | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Policy** | The computerised system validation policy | States that Helix validates its computerised systems, why, on whose authority, and **that nothing requires this instrument to take the form it takes** | Dr Marguerite Oyelaran |
| **Framework** | The validation framework | The instrument: its parts, what a system validation plan must contain, the evidence required for each of `21 CFR 11.10`'s eleven paragraphs, the two graduations and the rigour rules — `04.05-the-framework.md` | Colm Ó Braonáin |
| **Procedure** | **Nine SOPs — 4 new, 5 amendments. 4 + 5 = 9** | The acts. Each is a document somebody follows, and each closes named obligations | Named per document at §4 |

**The policy is one page and its first substantive statement is a negative.** `ADR-0023`, `DEC-403`, taken by
Colm Ó Braonáin on 2026-10-09, requires the framework document to record in its own first section that
nothing requires a validation framework, a validation master plan or a validation policy — and the policy
carries the same statement, because a policy that opens by implying it is compelled has misdescribed its own
authority before it says anything else. What is required is written procedures: `21 CFR 211.100(a)`,
`21 CFR 211.22(d)`, and `21 CFR 211.68(a)`'s written program designed to assure proper performance.
`04.01-nothing-requires-a-framework.md §2` owns the argument, and `§6` owns `ADR-0023`.

**Three levels and no more.** A fourth level of work instructions was considered and refused at `DEC-419`.
The reason is that every level Helix writes is a level Helix is held to, and a level that exists to restate
the level above it doubles the surface without adding an act.

**Which provision holds Helix to a given one of the nine depends on which procedure it is, and the nine are
not all the quality control unit's.** Under `21 CFR 211.22(d)` the quality control unit's own procedures
must be in writing and followed; under `21 CFR 211.100(b)` written production and process control procedures
*"shall be followed in the execution of the various production and process control functions and shall be
documented at the time of performance"*; under `21 CFR 211.160(a)` the same is true of laboratory control
mechanisms. **Between them, every one of the nine is a written commitment enforceable against Helix, and
which provision bites depends on which procedure it is.** `SOP-DI-03` is the Chief Information Officer's,
`SOP-DI-04` the Head of Automation and Manufacturing IT's, and `SOP-DI-07` and `SOP-DI-08` sit with the
operations and warehouse owners; **none of those four is a quality control unit procedure**, and a
regulation cited beyond its own subject matter stops being a citation.

## 4. What each of the nine actually says

**`SOP-DI-01` — Computerised system validation.** The framework's operating procedure. It requires a system
validation plan per system; it requires the plan to specify the evidence for each of `21 CFR 11.10`'s eleven
paragraphs, `(a)` to `(k)`, and to specify the fourth clause of `(a)` explicitly rather than to let it follow
from the first three — `ADR-0024`; it requires the complexity-and-reliability determination to be made and
recorded with its basis; and it requires the degree and frequency of input/output verification to be set from
that band. **It is the only one of the nine that carries both graduations, and it carries them in two
sections that do not refer to each other's outputs** — `ADR-0025`.

**`SOP-DI-02` — Original record and true copy determination.** One statement per record type of which
instance Helix relies on as the original, and the method by which a copy of it is shown to be a true copy.
`21 CFR 211.180(d)` is the text it works from: *"Records required under this part may be retained either as
original records or as true copies such as photocopies, microfilm, microfiche, or other accurate
reproductions of the original records."* **It closes the single largest closure route Phase 03 identified**
— `DP-17`, 41 determinations of Not determinable and 34 of Not met on one attribute.

**`SOP-DI-03` — Record rendering and readability demonstration.** A record is produced from the store that
holds it, in complete and readable form, with the context needed to understand it, and the production is
recorded. **The act is a demonstration and not an assertion**, which is why it needed a procedure of its own
rather than a clause in the archive procedure: nothing in the estate treated retrieval as a thing to be
tested.

**`SOP-DI-04` — Record transfer verification between computerised systems.** The second sentence of
`21 CFR 211.68(b)`: *"Input to and output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or
data shall be checked for accuracy."* It is the procedural answer to `IS-18`, which Phase 03 raised against
its own method — **31 of the 34 in-scope record types are held on more than one system and the movement
between them is assessed nowhere.**

**`SOP-DI-05` — Change control procedure, amended.** A change to a system, to a record type, to which system
holds a record type, or to an interface fires three triggers: the framework's re-plan trigger, `ADR-0015`'s
determination review trigger and `ADR-0022`'s re-assessment trigger. **It closes no obligation and it is the
amendment the architecture would break without**, because three registers now exist that go stale silently —
the applicability determination, the assessment and the rigour register — and a trigger that depends on
somebody remembering is not a trigger.

**`SOP-DI-06` — Periodic review of computerised systems, amended.** At each periodic review under `CAL-07`
the framework's evidence set for the system is re-confirmed and the confirmation is recorded, including where
it confirms nothing has changed. **A review that is recorded only when it finds something is a control that
reports success by being silent** — `02.09 §5`.

**`SOP-DI-07`, `SOP-DI-08`, `SOP-DI-09`** are the three amendments that add a missing sentence to a procedure
that already covers the subject matter: the capture time and the relied-on instance for the trend records
(`OB-24`), the individual and the relied-on instance for the warehouse monitoring and excursion records
(`OB-08`, `OB-29`), and the determination of the five attributes for the chromatographic integration and
reprocessing record (`OB-18`). **All three are single-sentence-class amendments and all three were invisible
to the question *is there a procedure?***

## 5. The route from an obligation to the sentence that requires it

**A reader must be able to start at any one of the thirty-eight and arrive at a sentence, in four steps, with
no step requiring them to know something the documents do not say.**

| Step | Where the reader is | What they read | What they get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | `logs/obligation-register.md` | The `OB` row | The act, the `DR` or `ND` identifier it came from, **the predicate rule it serves and, separately, the paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` for which it produces evidence in the system validation plan**, the landing, and the owner |
| **2** | The landing column | Either a named procedure, or *lands nowhere* | Which of the three classes the obligation is in |
| **3** | §3 of this chapter | The obligation's row | The document that requires it — one of the nine, or an existing procedure already requiring it |
| **4** | The named document | Its own text | **The sentence** |

**The route runs backwards as well, and that is the direction that gets used.** Somebody holding a sentence
in `SOP-DI-02` can ask what it is for, and the answer is `OB-01`; asking what `OB-01` is for gives `DR-04`,
`DR-08`, `DR-09` and the further scored rows carrying *original or a true copy* Not met; asking what those
are for gives the attribute determination register and the 121 pairs; asking what the pairs are for gives
Phase 02's determination of which records are Part 11 records. **Five documents, four phases, and no step in
the chain is an inference.**

**That chain is the thing `01.10` said the programme would build and it is the thing `IS-02` recorded did not
exist.** No written record existed of which Helix records were Part 11 records, so every scoping statement in
the estate rested on an unrecorded assumption. **A sentence in a procedure that cannot be traced to the
requirement it discharges is the same defect in a later form**, and the register column that would have made
it visible is the landing column.

## 6. What the architecture does not do, at this vantage

**None of the nine has been applied to anything.** They are approved documents in the quality system. The
framework requires a system validation plan per system and **not one system validation plan has been written
at this vantage** — `IS-28`.

**Of the three issues this chapter reaches, two close and the third is untouched — and neither closure is a
record that anything has been done.** Each closes against the condition the entry itself set. **An entry is
not held open against another entry's condition**: that nothing has been performed under the nine is
`IS-28`'s, and borrowing it here would have kept `IS-10` and `IS-19` open on a ground neither of them
states, asymmetrically with `IS-21`, which closed against the same SOP set on the same date.

| Issue | Position after this chapter |
|---|---|
| `IS-10` — the applicability determination exists and nothing requires it to be maintained | **CLOSED 2026-11-20** — `DEC-418`, `EV-428`, `GOV-19`. **`SOP-DI-05` is the approved procedure naming the owner, the trigger and the change-control step that `IS-10` set as its closure condition**, and it fires `ADR-0015`'s review trigger on a change. **Whether it has ever fired is `IS-28`'s, not this entry's** — and `DP-11` still carries the capacity to operate it |
| `IS-19` — the scale is a written procedure Helix will be held to | **CLOSED 2026-11-20** — `DEC-418`, `EV-428`, `GOV-19`. **`SOP-DI-01` is the approved procedure requiring the scale to be applied — the assessment's output as the input to rigour — which `IS-19` set as its closure condition.** **Whether it has ever been applied is `IS-28`'s, not this entry's** — and `DP-19` is consumed |
| `IS-06` — the laboratory record review procedure is silent on whether the audit trail forms part of the original record reviewed | **Unchanged.** **This chapter does not answer it and does not answer it by implication.** The question concerns what an audit trail is and what a reviewer must do with it, which is Phase 05's, and `IS-04`'s undefined cadence sits behind it |

**`IS-21` does close**, and it closes on the whole architecture rather than on any one document. Its text is
that the assessment says where the data is weak and nothing in the quality system says what Helix must do
about it. **After `GOV-19` the quality system says what Helix must do** — thirty-eight acts, each with a
named individual owner, each landing in a named document. **What it does not say is that any of it has been
done.**

## 7. Two things the architecture deliberately does not contain

**No audit trail review frequency.** `CAL-06`'s cadence is undefined in part and `IS-04` records it as
deliberately unresolved. The position is unchanged and is stated here so that no reader infers it was settled
in passing: the review frequency of an audit trail is **inherited from the review frequency CGMP specifies
for the underlying data; where CGMP specifies none, the firm determines it, and only there does risk enter.**
**No primary source names a frequency and none of the nine documents names one.**

**No retention period.** None is recorded against any of the 34 in-scope record types, and `SOP-DI-02`
requires the original to be identified without requiring a period to be set — `IS-14`, `DP-14`, `DP-18`, and
Phase 07 owns it. **A procedure that identified the original and set a retention period in the same breath
would be a Phase 07 answer written in Phase 04**, and the two are separated on purpose.

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **No control position.** **Nothing here says whether any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is met for any
  system** — `ADR-0029`, `IS-20`, Phase 05.
- **No validation outcome.** **No package has been executed, no protocol run, no test result exists**, and no
  system validation plan has been written.
- **No claim that any of the nine is being followed.** **A procedure approved is not a procedure followed**,
  and `21 CFR 211.22(d)` makes the difference the expensive one — `04.11-what-a-framework-cannot-fix.md`.
- **No training completion position.** The role-based content and its completion records are `O9` and
  `CAL-08`, and **no completion position is stated here.**
- **Nothing is cited as required that is not.** CSA is device guidance under 21 CFR Part 820, as amended by
  the Quality Management System Regulation, and does not apply to Helix; GAMP 5 Second Edition is ISPE
  good-practice guidance. **Both are adopted voluntarily and labelled at every citation, and neither is ever
  cited as a requirement** — `ADR-0026`, `ADR-0005`. **No software-category count appears anywhere.**
- **No legal conclusion and no forecast of any inspection outcome.**
