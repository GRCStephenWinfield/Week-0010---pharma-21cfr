## 1. The paragraph, whole

**`21 CFR 11.10(a)`, verbatim and entire:**

> *"Validation of systems to ensure accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and the ability
> to discern invalid or altered records."*

**That is the whole paragraph. It is one sentence, it is nineteen words long, and it contains four
clauses.** Accuracy. Reliability. Consistent intended performance. **And the ability to discern invalid or
altered records.**

A note on the word. Grammatically the four are coordinated noun phrases governed by one verb rather than
four clauses. **This phase uses *clause* as a term of art for the four coordinated objects that one verb
governs**, uses it consistently, and means nothing by it beyond that.

**The fourth clause is routinely dropped from industry summaries of Part 11, and it is the clause this
phase is built on.** It is dropped because it is last, because the first three read as a familiar list, and
because a reader who has already recognised the first three has stopped reading by the time the fourth
arrives. It is also dropped because it is the only one of the four that a conventional validation package
does not know how to evidence.

This chapter does four things. It says what each of the four clauses asks. It says why the fourth is a
different kind of property from the first three. It says why almost nobody evidences it. **And it says that
the fourth clause and Phase 03's largest single finding are the same finding seen from two ends.**

**It states no result.** How many validation packages Helix holds, what they address and what they do not,
is `../04.07-the-packages-helix-already-holds.md`'s, and this chapter cites it rather than reporting it.

## 2. Four clauses, and what each asks

The four are set out separately because `ADR-0024` requires them to be specified and evidenced separately.
The questions below are the framework's phrasing of what each clause asks of a system; the clause text is
the regulation's.

| # | Clause | The question it asks | The shape of the evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **accuracy** | Does the system produce, hold and return the correct value — does the number it reports correspond to the thing it purports to measure or record? | Functional. A specified result, an executed test, an expected outcome and an actual one |
| **2** | **reliability** | Does it do that dependably, under the conditions it is actually used in, including at the boundaries and under load? | Functional, extended over conditions. Repetition, boundary and negative testing, behaviour under failure |
| **3** | **consistent intended performance** | Does it keep doing it — across releases, patches, configuration changes and time — and is the performance the one that was intended rather than merely the one observed? | Functional, extended over time. A specification to compare against, change control, and re-evidence after change |
| **4** | **the ability to discern invalid or altered records** | **If a record held on this system were invalid or had been altered, could that be discerned?** | **Not a demonstration that the system does what it should.** Evidence about what the system retains, exposes and preserves that would allow the question to be answered afterwards, by somebody who was not there. Functional testing — of an audit trail, for instance — can be directed at this clause, but only where it is specified and attributed to it |

**Read the fourth question again and notice what it is not asking.** It is not asking whether the system is
accurate, whether it is dependable, or whether alteration is likely. It is asking whether **the system
affords a means of telling**. A system can satisfy the first three clauses completely and still be one on
which nobody could ever establish whether a particular record is the one that was written.

## 3. Why the fourth is a different property

The first three clauses are all statements of the form *the system does what it should*. They are
demonstrated by making the system do it, in front of a witness, against a written expectation. That is what
a validation protocol is for and it is a mature craft: specify, test, record the result, review the result,
approve.

**The fourth clause is a statement of the form *the system can be interrogated about its own past*.** It is
not demonstrated by making the system work. It is demonstrated by asking what the system would leave behind
if something went wrong, and whether what it leaves behind is enough for a person examining it afterwards
to reach an answer.

**Three differences follow, and each of them is why a validation package built for the first three does not
happen to catch the fourth.**

**The first three are properties of behaviour; the fourth is a property of evidence.** A test that passes
tells you the system behaved. It tells you nothing about whether a record written yesterday can be
distinguished from a record written yesterday and edited today. Those are different questions about
different objects, and passing the first has never implied the second.

**The first three can be evidenced without anybody deciding what the record is; the fourth cannot.** To
discern an altered record you must first be able to say which thing is the record — which instance, held
where, in which form. **That is a documentary decision and not a technical one**, and where it has not been
made the fourth clause has nothing to attach to. This is the hinge of `§5`.

**And the first three are satisfied by the vendor's product working; the fourth is satisfied only by
Helix's arrangements around it.** A supplier can build a system that computes correctly and behaves
consistently. **Whether Helix can discern an altered record depends on what Helix retains, for how long,
and whether anybody can get it back and read it** — and none of that is a property a supplier can sell.
There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated software;
compliance is a property of Helix's validated system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated by
its trained people.

## 4. Why it is almost never evidenced

**Because the first three clauses read as a complete thought.** *Accuracy, reliability, consistent intended
performance* is a serviceable description of a working system, and a reader who reaches those three words
has already formed the impression that the sentence has told them what validation is for. The fourth clause
arrives after the comma that follows *performance* and is carried by an *and* — grammatically an addition,
psychologically an afterthought.

**Because the industry vocabulary for the fourth clause belongs somewhere else.** Discerning an altered
record is discussed under the heading of audit trails, and audit trails are discussed under
`21 CFR 11.10(e)`. A firm that has an audit trail position therefore feels it has dealt with the subject
matter, at the wrong paragraph, and never returns to `(a)`. **The fourth clause is not a cross-reference to
`21 CFR 11.10(e)`.** `(e)` requires the use of secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails and
sets their properties and their retention; `(a)` requires that **validation** establish the ability to
discern. Those are two obligations, in two paragraphs, and satisfying one has never discharged the other.
**This phase states no audit trail configuration and no review frequency** — that is Phase 05's, and
`01.04 §7` owns where the review obligation actually comes from.

**Because the validation deliverable set was designed around the first three.** Requirement specification,
design specification, installation qualification, operational qualification, performance qualification,
traceability matrix, summary report: every one of those artefacts answers a question of the form *does it
do what we said*. **None of them has a section whose subject is what the system would allow somebody to
establish afterwards**, and a deliverable set with no section for a question does not produce an absence —
it produces a package that looks complete.

**And because nothing forced the question.** `21 CFR 11.10(a)` is within the set FDA has stated an
intention to exercise enforcement discretion over. **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention; it
is not a legal exemption, it is not a safe harbour and it confers no rights**, and `21 CFR 211.68` is
untouched by it — `ADR-0003`. But the practical effect on a validation deliverable set that nobody was
challenging is easy to see, and it is stated here as an explanation rather than as an excuse.

## 5. The same finding, seen from the other end

**This is the sentence the chapter exists for.**

Phase 03 assessed one hundred and twenty-one record-type/system pairs against FDA's five ALCOA attributes.
Its largest single result was on **original or a true copy**: the attribute could not be determined at all
on **forty-one** of the pairs. `../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md`
owns the result and `../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/03.08-what-cannot-be-determined.md`
owns the population it sits inside.

Now put the two questions next to each other.

> **`21 CFR 11.10(a)`, fourth clause:** can it be discerned whether a record on this system is invalid or
> has been altered?
>
> **FDA's *original or a true copy*:** is this instance the original record, or a copy that can be
> demonstrated to be a true one?

**A system on which the second question has no answer cannot answer the first, and a system that cannot
answer the first is the reason the second has no answer.** If nobody can say which instance is the original,
then there is no reference against which any instance can be tested, and *altered from what* has no content.
If nothing about the system would let a later reader distinguish the record as written from the record as it
now stands, then no instance can be shown to be an original and no copy can be shown to be true.

**The two findings are the same finding seen from two ends.** Phase 03 approached it from the record and
asked what Helix could determine about the record; this phase approaches it from the system and asks what
the system would let anybody determine. **Neither is a restatement of the other and neither is evidence for
the other** — they are one deficiency in Helix's arrangements, reached by two different instruments, and the
fact that two instruments built for different purposes arrived at the same place is the strongest thing
either of them says.

**Three things follow and the phase holds all three.**

**The fourth clause is not an ALCOA attribute and *original or a true copy* is not a Part 11 control.** FDA's
own footnote 5 maps the five attributes to the predicate rules and **cites 21 CFR Part 11 nowhere**;
`ADR-0019` and `../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/03.04-footnote-five.md` own
that, and **ALCOA is not mapped onto `21 CFR 11.10` anywhere in this repository**. What is claimed here is
narrower and stronger than a mapping: **two separate obligations, from two separate sources, are
undischargeable for one and the same underlying reason.**

**The closure routes are already open and they are not the framework's.** Phase 03 recorded that the
largest single closure route in that phase requires no system change and no purchase — a statement, per
record type, of which instance Helix relies on as the original and of the method by which a copy of it is
shown to be a true copy, at `DP-17`, with a retention rule for it to be tested against at `DP-18` and
`IS-14`. **The framework requires the evidence. It does not produce it** —
`../04.11-what-a-framework-cannot-fix.md §2`.

**And a determination that an attribute could not be determined is not a finding about the record.**
Unassessed is not adverse, and undeterminable is not adverse. Nothing in this chapter says that any record
at Helix has been altered, is invalid, or is not what it purports to be.

## 6. `ADR-0024` — the four are specified and evidenced separately

**`ADR-0024`, taken at `DEC-405` on 2026-10-16 by Colm Ó Braonáin, requires that a validation plan specify
and evidence the four clauses of `21 CFR 11.10(a)` separately, and that the fourth be specified explicitly
rather than assumed to follow from the first three.**

The rule has three limbs.

**Separately specified.** A plan states, for each of the four clauses, what evidence it will produce. Four
statements, not one. A plan that says *the system will be validated to ensure accuracy, reliability,
consistent intended performance and the ability to discern invalid or altered records* has quoted the
regulation and specified nothing.

**Separately evidenced.** The evidence for each clause is identified as the evidence for that clause. **A
single artefact may serve more than one clause and frequently will; what is forbidden is an evidence set in
which no artefact is attributed to the fourth clause and the fourth clause is nonetheless reported as
addressed.** An artefact produced under `21 CFR 11.10(e)` — audit trail functional testing, for instance —
may be attributed to the fourth clause and evidenced there. What may not be done is to discharge `(a)` by
asserting that `(e)` is met, which is an assertion about a different paragraph and attributes nothing.

**And explicitly, never by inheritance.** The fourth clause is never satisfied by an argument of the form
*the system performs consistently, therefore its records can be relied upon, therefore an alteration would
be apparent*. That argument has three steps and each of them is a different claim; **the first three
clauses are premises for none of them.**

`../04.05-the-framework.md §5` sets out what the framework requires a validation plan to contain for each
of the four, and `../04.07-the-packages-helix-already-holds.md` reports what the packages Helix already
holds address.

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no control position.** Nothing here says whether `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, or any other paragraph of
  `21 CFR 11.10`, is or is not met for any system. **The framework says what evidence is required.**
  Whether it is met is Phase 05's — `ADR-0029`, `IS-20`, `IS-09`.
- **It states no validation outcome.** No system is validated in this phase, no protocol is run and no test
  result exists.
- **It states no result about Helix's existing packages** — what they address, and what none of them
  addresses, is `../04.07-the-packages-helix-already-holds.md`'s.
- **It configures no audit trail and sets no review frequency.** The review frequency of an audit trail is
  inherited from the review frequency CGMP specifies for the underlying data; **where CGMP specifies none,
  the firm determines it, and only there does risk enter.** No primary source names a frequency and this
  phase names none.
- **It sets no retention period** — `IS-14`, `DP-18`, and Phase 07.
- **It does not re-score, re-open or reinterpret Phase 03's registers.** They are an input and the
  forty-one is Phase 03's published figure, cited and not re-derived.
- **It maps no ALCOA attribute onto any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10`** — `ADR-0019`.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome.
