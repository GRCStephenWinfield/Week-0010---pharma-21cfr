## 1. What this chapter settles, and what it is careful not to settle

Helix did not arrive at this phase with nothing. It holds forty-four validation packages, produced over
years under a computerised system validation procedure that already existed, by people doing the work
competently against the standard they were given. **This chapter reads those packages against the four
clauses of `21 CFR 11.10(a)` and reports what they cover.**

**It is a statement about coverage and it is not a control position.** Nothing in this chapter says that any
system is or is not compliant with `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, or with any other paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10`, and
nothing in it may be read that way — `ADR-0029`, `DEC-420`, taken by Colm Ó Braonáin on 2026-11-25. Whether a
paragraph is met, for any system, is Phase 05's and `IS-20` carries the refusal forward from Phase 03.

Three things follow from that boundary and they are stated at the front rather than at the back.

**A package that does not address a clause is not a package that fails it.** It is a package whose scope did
not extend there. The reading performed here was a reading of documents against a list of four properties; it
tested nothing in any system and it re-executed nothing.

**No package has been re-executed, re-run or re-tested in this phase.** No protocol has been run and no test
result exists. **Nothing in this chapter is a validation outcome.**

**No package is withdrawn, invalidated or superseded by anything said here.** The forty-four exist, they say
what they say, and they continue to say it.

## 2. The read, and how it was done

The forty-four packages were read between 2026-10-19 and 2026-11-06 against a four-column sheet, one column
per clause of `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, with a fifth column recording the document and page the coverage was found
at. The instrument, the sheet and the reading rule were fixed before the first package was opened — the
approved reading rule is `EV-406`, dated 2026-10-16 — **`EV-420` records that the read was performed under
it**, and the completed sheets are `EV-421`.

**The reading rule, in full, because a coverage claim is only as good as the test that produced it.** A
package was recorded as addressing a clause where it contained a specified requirement, a test or check
directed at that requirement, and a recorded result. **Two of the three was not enough.** A package
containing a requirement with no test was recorded as not addressing the clause, and so was a package
containing a test whose result had not been recorded. **A statement in a validation summary report that a
system is accurate, reliable and fit for intended use was not treated as coverage of anything**, because it
is a conclusion and the sheet was reading for the evidence beneath it.

`21 CFR 11.10(a)` reads, in full and verbatim:

> *"Validation of systems to ensure accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and the ability
> to discern invalid or altered records."*

**Four clauses.** `04.03-the-fourth-clause.md §2` owns what each of them asks and why the fourth is different
in kind; this chapter applies the reading and does not re-argue it.

## 3. What the forty-four cover

| Clause of `21 CFR 11.10(a)` | Packages addressing it, of the 44 |
|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | **44** |
| **Reliability** | **44** |
| **Consistent intended performance** | **41** |
| **The ability to discern invalid or altered records** | **None** |

**Not one of the forty-four packages contains a requirement stated in the terms of the fourth clause, with a
test directed at it and a recorded result.** Some contain audit trail functional testing that could be
attributed to the fourth clause; **none attributes it**, and an evidence set in which no item is attributed
to the clause cannot be reported as addressing it — `ADR-0024`. **Whether such an item, once attributed,
satisfies the clause is Phase 05's.**

That is the finding the read established and it is narrower than the sentence a reader reaches for. **The
read was ruled by clause label**, so a package containing audit trail functional testing scored zero in the
fourth column unless the package itself said what the testing was for; the reading rule at §2 is what makes
the column mean that and nothing wider.

`IS-22` carries it, owned by Colm Ó Braonáin, raised 2026-11-06.

Three of the forty-four do not address consistent intended performance either — `SYS-009` and `SYS-010`, the
two vision inspection systems on the blister lines, and `SYS-049`, the annual product review reporting and
analytics system. In each case the package was raised at installation, tested the system against its
specification on the day, and was closed without any demonstration of performance over a period of intended
use. **41 + 3 = 44**, and the three are named rather than counted so that the reconciliation can be checked.

## 4. Why the fourth column is empty, and why that is not a surprise

It would be easy to read the empty column as a failure of diligence by the people who wrote the forty-four
packages. It is not, and the chapter says so plainly because the alternative reading is available and would
be wrong.

**The first three clauses ask what the system does. The fourth asks what the system would let you see.**
Accuracy, reliability and consistent intended performance are properties of the system's behaviour, and a
validation package is the natural instrument for demonstrating them: specify the behaviour, test it, record
the result. The ability to discern invalid or altered records is a property of the system's **records** and
of what the system retains about them. It is not demonstrated by showing that a calculation is correct. It is
demonstrated by showing that if the calculation's result were subsequently changed, somebody could tell.

**A validation package built to prove that a system does what it is specified to do will address three of the
four clauses completely and the fourth not at all, and will look complete while it does it.**

That is the shape of all forty-four. Every one of them contains a specification, tests against it and
recorded results. Every one of them is silent on the property the fourth clause names, and no reviewer of any
of them was ever asked for it, because the procedure that produced them did not require it — which is the
finding `04.08-where-the-obligations-land.md` generalises and `ADR-0028` makes a class.

## 5. The same finding, seen from the other end

`04.03-the-fourth-clause.md §5` establishes that the fourth clause and Phase 03's largest gap are the
same finding seen from two ends, and the argument is that chapter's. What this chapter contributes is the
arithmetic on the other end of it.

Phase 03 recorded *original or a true copy* as **Not determinable on 41 of the 121 pairs** and **Not met on
34**, which is 75 of 121 either known not to meet it or not shown to meet it — `IS-15`, and
`../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md`
owns it. The four routes by which it fails are recorded there: a record that can be reprocessed and therefore
has no single original in the way a weighing ticket does; an archive holding copies whose trueness nobody has
demonstrated; a record rendered from a query rather than retrieved; and the same record held in several
places with no rule saying which one Helix relies on.

**Each of those four routes is a way of not being able to discern whether a record is the valid one.** And
the reason Helix cannot discern it is, on the coverage evidence, that no validation package it holds was ever
asked to demonstrate that it could.

**Two findings, produced by two phases, on two different populations, using two different instruments, and
they are the same fact.** Phase 03 found it by assessing records and could not say why. This phase finds it
by reading packages and can say what was never asked for. **Neither of the two says whether any control at
Helix works, and neither may be read as saying so.**

## 7. What the seventeen do and do not mean for rigour

**A system with no validation package still has a rigour band**, and the band is set the same way as every
other system's — `logs/system-rigour-register.md`, applying `04.04-two-graduations.md §3`. The absence of a
package changes what the framework has to produce; it does not change what the framework requires.

**Sixteen of the seventeen also have no established data integrity position**, because they carry at least
one pair in Phase 03's unscored register: `SYS-011`, `SYS-021`, `SYS-022`, `SYS-028`, `SYS-033`, `SYS-036`,
`SYS-037`, `SYS-039`, `SYS-046`, `SYS-051`, `SYS-052`, `SYS-055`, `SYS-058`, `SYS-059`, `SYS-060` and
`SYS-061`. **`SYS-038` is the single exception**: its one pair, `DR-48`, was scored on all five attributes.
**On the other sixteen systems
Helix holds neither a validation package nor a determined position about the records**, and their rigour is
therefore set by complexity and reliability alone — `IS-27`.

That is not a statement that those sixteen are the worst systems in the estate. **It is a statement that they
are the sixteen about which Helix currently holds the least**, and the two claims are different.

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **No control position.** **Nothing here says whether `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, or any other paragraph of
  `21 CFR 11.10`, is met for any system.** A coverage statement about a document is not a statement about a
  system — `ADR-0029`, `IS-20`, Phase 05.
- **No validation outcome.** **No package was executed, re-executed or tested; no protocol was run; no test
  result exists.** Nothing here validates or invalidates anything.
- **No judgement of the packages or of the people who wrote them.** The forty-four address what the procedure
  that produced them required. **No document in this repository assesses any individual's work.**
- **Nothing is withdrawn.** No package is superseded, invalidated or set aside by this reading.
- **No statement that any of the seventeen is unfit, uncontrolled or unsafe.** **Unassessed is not adverse**,
  and the absence of a lifecycle is not a statement about a system's condition — `IS-13`.
- **No re-scoring.** Phase 03's registers are an input to this phase and no determination moves here.
- **No `OBS-2` or `OBS-4` conclusion.** The `21 CFR 11.10(a)` coverage position touches the subject matter of
  observations the 483 raised; **the link is recorded and no conclusion is drawn from it**, before Phase 06
  and Phase 08 — the direction `GOV-10` gave and `GOV-15` renewed.
- **No legal conclusion and no forecast of any inspection outcome.**
