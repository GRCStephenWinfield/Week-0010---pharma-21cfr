**The nine procedures enter Helix's quality system, and the quality system is not the programme's to alter.**
`21 CFR 211.22` gives the quality unit its responsibility and authority and `21 CFR 211.22(d)` requires its

## 3. `DEC-414` — every obligation lands somewhere, including nowhere

**Decision.** Every obligation is recorded against a named procedure, or is recorded as landing nowhere. **An
obligation with no home is published, not absorbed.**

| Considered | Position |
|---|---|
| Record an obligation against *the quality system* or *the framework* | **Refused.** An obligation whose home is a category cannot be checked, and the seventeen with no home would have disappeared into it |
| Require a landing on every row, with *lands nowhere* as a completed value | **Adopted** — `ADR-0027` |

**Cost accepted.** Seventeen uncomfortable answers that had to be written down, two new issues, and a
register that cannot be completed quickly. **A register row whose landing column is empty is a defective
row**, and the obligation record at `EV-415` will not accept one.

## 4. `DEC-415` — the register, and the ratio

**Decision.** The obligation register is approved: **`OB-01` to `OB-38`.**

| Source | Count |
|---|---|
| The 74 scored pairs | **14** |
| **The 47 unscored pairs** | **19** |
| The method's own recorded gaps — `DP-15`, `IS-18`, and the fourth clause of `21 CFR 11.10(a)` | **5** |
| **Total** | **38** — **14 + 19 + 5 = 38** |

**The pairs Helix could not assess generate more obligations than the pairs it could.** The committee minuted
the reason rather than the fact: **a determination of *Not met* is a closed question with a bad answer and the
work is bounded; a determination of *Not determinable* is an open question with no answer, and the act the
framework must require is the prior work that would make a determination possible.** **Determining an
attribute is work, and the framework has to require that work before anything can be scored.**

**The committee also minuted what would have happened without `ADR-0018`.** Had the forty-seven been scored
low in Phase 03, **they would have generated no obligations at all** — an obligation is written from a
determination that something is not as it should be, and a pair scored at the bottom of a scale produces
nothing to write one from. **The refusal to score is what makes the nineteen visible.**

## 5. `DEC-417` — two classes of gap, and only one is visible

**Decision.** **A procedure that exists and does not require the act is recorded as a distinct class of gap
from a procedure that does not exist.**

Sixty-three procedures were read against the thirty-eight obligations between 2026-11-09 and 2026-11-17 —
`EV-417`, under a reading rule fixed before the reading opened.

| Where the obligation lands | Count |
|---|---|
| An existing procedure requires the act | **21** |
| **A procedure exists and does not require the act** | **12** — `IS-24` |
| **No procedure at all** | **5** — `IS-25` |
| **Total** | **38** — **21 + 12 + 5 = 38** |

**A procedure that exists and does not require the act is worse than a procedure that does not exist, because
an auditor who asks "is there a procedure?" gets a yes.**

The question is the natural one and it is not a bad one; it is how a quality system is interrogated, by
internal audit, by a third party and by management review. **Against the five it works and returns a no,
which is a finding somebody writes down. Against the twelve it returns an accurate yes and stops**, because
the next question — *and does it require this particular act?* — has to be known to be asked, and knowing to
ask it requires already knowing the act is missing.

**The committee had the precedent read out and it is Helix's own.** `IA-2025-01/F3` recorded that the IT
register carried no predicate-rule attribute, tested it against the procedure that governed it, raised what
that allowed to be raised and closed it there; `IS-03` re-raised the same gap against the determination that
did not exist. **The procedure existed. The determination did not.** The committee recorded expressly that
**this is a description of a mechanism and not a statement that any past audit should have found anything**,
and that **nothing in it bears on `IS-01`**.

| Considered | Position |
|---|---|
| A single category of *procedural gap* covering both classes | **Refused.** Simpler to report, and it hides twelve gaps inside a count that looks like seventeen |
| Two classes, two identifiers, two closure routes | **Adopted** — `ADR-0028` |

**Cost accepted.** A distinction that has to be explained every time it is reported, and a gap class whose
members each require a document to be read rather than listed.

## 6. `DEC-418` — the policy, the framework and the nine

**Decision.** Approved: **the computerised system validation policy · the validation framework · an SOP set
of nine, four new and five amendments. 4 + 5 = 9.** **`../04.09-the-policy-architecture.md §3` carries the
nine in one table, with the obligations each closes and its named individual owner**, and the table is not
reproduced here. **All seventeen unhoused obligations are accounted for across the nine; the other twenty-one
get no new document**, because a procedure already requires the act and a second sentence requiring what the
first requires produces a longer quality system and no change in what anybody does.

**The policy's first substantive statement is a negative**, carrying `ADR-0023` through: nothing requires a
validation framework, a validation master plan or a validation policy, and what is required is written
procedures. **A fourth documentary level of work instructions was refused at `DEC-419`**, on the ground that
every level Helix writes is a level Helix is held to.

**The condition the Council attached to the approval, and it is the one that matters.** **Nine more written
procedures now exist that Helix can be found not to have followed**, and **which provision holds Helix to a
given one of them depends on which procedure it is**. Under `21 CFR 211.22(d)` the quality control unit's own
procedures must be in writing and followed; under `21 CFR 211.100(b)` written production and process control
procedures *"shall be followed in the execution of the various production and process control functions and
shall be documented at the time of performance"*; under `21 CFR 211.160(a)` the same is true of laboratory
control mechanisms. **`SOP-DI-03`, `SOP-DI-04`, `SOP-DI-07` and `SOP-DI-08` are not quality control unit
procedures**, and between the three provisions every one of the nine is a written commitment enforceable
against Helix. `IS-19` recorded that of the scale, and
the same is now true nine times over — which is why `AS-31` was opened and `PR-32` with it, and why `DP-22`
names the capacity to take the nine from approved to applied as a dependency rather than as a step.
