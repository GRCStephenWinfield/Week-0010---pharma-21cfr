*Phase 04 speaks as at **2026-11-30**. Helix Pharma, Inc., its sites, its people, its systems, its inspection and every figure in this repository are fictional and illustrative. Where a statement is made about a real regulation or guidance — 21 CFR Parts 11, 210, 211 and 820, FDA's August 2003 Scope and Application guidance, FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance, FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance, ISPE's GAMP 5 — it is real and is cited exactly, and the two kinds of statement are kept visibly apart throughout.*

---

## 1. What this phase is

Phase 03 ended on a sentence it had raised as an issue against itself: **the assessment says where the data
is weak, and nothing in the quality system yet says what Helix must do about it.** `IS-21` carried it.

**This is the first phase in the programme that says what Helix must do. `IS-21` closes here.**

It is also the phase that has to answer a hole Phase 03 published in its own instrument. `21 CFR 211.68(b)`
graduates a control on *"the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system"*, and neither
axis of Phase 03's scale is complexity or reliability. **`DP-15` required Phase 04 to state how the two
relate, or to assess complexity and reliability separately, before relying on the assessment. Helix
assessed them separately, keeps two graduations, and never merges them. `DP-15` closes here.**

The phase publishes a validation framework and the policy architecture around it, and the first thing it
says is that **no regulation and no FDA guidance requires any of the three artefacts a company would
normally reach for** — a framework, a validation master plan or a validation policy.

## 2. Read this first

Four things, and the rest of the phase follows from them.

**Nothing requires a validation framework.** What is required is **written procedures**:
`21 CFR 211.100(a)` for production and process control; `21 CFR 211.22(d)` for the quality control unit's
own responsibilities and procedures — *"such written procedures shall be followed"*; and
`21 CFR 211.68(a)`'s **written program designed to assure proper performance**. The framework is Helix's
instrument, it was authorised at `GOV-16`, and **`ADR-0023` requires the framework document to say so in
its own first section**, because an instrument that presents itself as a requirement invites a reader to
stop asking what actually is one. `04.01-nothing-requires-a-framework.md` owns the argument.

**`21 CFR 11.10(a)` has four clauses and the fourth is the phase.** Verbatim: *"Validation of systems to
ensure accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and the ability to discern invalid or
altered records."* The first three are what a conventional validation package evidences. **The ability to
discern invalid or altered records is a different kind of property — not that the system works, but that it
can tell you when its records have been tampered with — and it is almost never evidenced.** And it is
**exactly the property whose absence made *original or a true copy* undeterminable on forty-one of Phase
03's pairs. The two findings are the same finding seen from two ends.** `04.03-the-fourth-clause.md` owns
it and `ADR-0024` requires the four to be specified and evidenced separately.

**Two graduations, and they are never merged.** Data integrity risk grades the record and sets **the depth
at which each of `21 CFR 11.10`'s eleven paragraphs is evidenced about it**, removing none of them. System
complexity and reliability grades the system and sets **the degree and frequency of input/output
verification, and nothing else**, directly under `21 CFR 211.68(b)` — and, **as Helix's own method choice
and not under that provision**, is the second input to evidence depth and the only one on the twenty-five
systems with no established data integrity position. **Neither is derived from the other, neither is averaged with the other, and where they
disagree the framework requires the higher rather than a mean.** That is `ADR-0017`'s never-average
discipline applied to a different object. `04.04-two-graduations.md` owns it and `ADR-0025` records it.

**And the framework says what evidence is required and states no control position.** Whether any paragraph
of `21 CFR 11.10` is met, for any system, is Phase 05's — `ADR-0029`. **A requirement is not a control, and
an obligation is not evidence.**

anyway.

## 4. What Phase 04 deliberately does not establish

None of the following exists at the vantage, and nothing in this phase states, implies or forecasts any of
it.

- **No validation outcome for any system.** **No validation package is executed, no protocol is run, and no
  test result exists.** **No system-level validation plan is written at this vantage**, and the framework
  requires one per system.
- **No control position.** **Which paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` are met, for any system, is not stated
  anywhere in this phase** — `ADR-0029`, `IS-20`, `IS-09`. Phase 05, `MS-09`.
- **No access design, no audit trail configuration, no review frequency stated as a requirement, and no
  electronic signature deployment.** Phase 05. The review frequency of an audit trail is inherited from the
  review frequency CGMP specifies for the underlying data; **where CGMP specifies none, the firm determines
  it, and only there does risk enter.** **No primary source names a frequency and this phase names none.**
- **No chromatography-specific work** — Phase 06, `MS-10`. **No retention, backup or archive rule**:
  `IS-14` remains open and `21 CFR 211.180` governs the question, Phase 07, `MS-11`.
- **No periodic review, no internal audit result, no third-party audit result and no inspection outcome of
  any kind.** Phases 08 and 09.
- **No rating changes.** **Phase 03's two registers are an input to this phase and are never re-scored.**
  No `ND` row becomes a `DR` row here, and no pair's determination moves.
- **No answer to `IS-01`**, and **no promotion of its fourth candidate explanation** by statement, by
  ordering or by tone — `ADR-0012`, `02.11`. **`IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted.**
- **No re-opening of the Part 11 applicability determination**; **no legal conclusion of any kind**; and no
  forecast of what any future inspection will find.

**A requirement is not a control, and an obligation is not evidence.**

## 10. Standing framing

These positions hold across all nine phases and are restated here so that a reader arriving at Phase 04 has
them.

- **There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated
  software.** A product has **features that support** compliance; compliance is a property of Helix's
  validated system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated by its trained people. **Outsourcing
  to a service provider transfers no responsibility.**
- **Part 11 applies to records, not to systems**, and this repository never says "a Part 11 system" without
  saying which records make it one — `ADR-0001`.
- **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention. It is not a legal exemption and not a safe harbour**,
  and **`21 CFR 211.68` requires appropriate controls over Helix's computerised systems — and, at `(a)`, a
  written program designed to assure their proper performance — whatever FDA does about Part 11** —
  `ADR-0003`.
- **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by
  the Quality Management System Regulation, and does not apply to Helix.** Adopted voluntarily as good
  practice, **labelled as adopted every time** — `ADR-0005`, `ADR-0026`.
- **GAMP 5 Second Edition, ISPE, July 2022, is good-practice guidance and never a requirement**, and **no
  software-category count and no enumeration of the categories is published in this repository.**
- **FDA uses plain ALCOA. The four additional attributes are other bodies' formulations**, and MHRA itself
  refers to the shorter acronym, states there is no difference in expectations regardless of which is used,
  and defines all four. **No originator is named for either set. ALCOA does not map onto `21 CFR 11.10`** —
  FDA's own footnote 5 maps the attributes to the CGMP predicate rules and cites Part 11 nowhere,
  `ADR-0019`.
- **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, no read-event logging, no cryptographic hashing under
  `21 CFR 11.70` and no password interval under `21 CFR 11.300(b)`. It requires no multi-factor
  authentication in the modern sense** — `21 CFR 11.200(a)(1)` requires two distinct identification
  components, which is not the same thing.
- **A closed system is defined by who controls access** — `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`. Hosting is not the test, and
  **all sixty-one are closed** — `ADR-0011`.
- **`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and Part 11 contains ten sections.** Citations in this
  repository are exact and can be looked up.
- **A Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding.** No document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of
  any kind, and **never forecast what a future inspection will find.** **An open gap is Helix's own
  assessment of its own position** — not a violation, not a breach, not a non-compliance, not a finding and
  not an inspectional observation.
- **Applicability is not integrity, a rating is not a validation status, and a requirement is not a
  control.**

## 11. How to read this phase

Read **`04.03`** if you read only one chapter. Nineteen words of regulation contain four clauses, three
of which the industry evidences and one of which it does not, and the one it does not is the same
deficiency Phase 03 found from the other direction.

Read **`04.01`** for why the phase exists when nothing required it, and **`04.04`** for the answer to
`DP-15` — the one place where a regulation's own words settle a method question a guidance could not.

Read **`04.05`** for the instrument, and **`04.06`** to **`04.09`** for what it produced when it was
pointed at Helix. Read **`04.08`** with particular attention: the class of gap it names is the one an
auditor's most obvious question cannot see. Read **`04.11`** last of the argued chapters — it is the
phase's statement of what it has not achieved, and where a reader persuaded by everything before it should
stop and reconsider.

Read for the arguments rather than the citations. Where a chapter cites `21 CFR 11.10(a)` or
`21 CFR 211.68(b)`, it is locating a requirement in the text — **not** asserting that Helix meets it.
