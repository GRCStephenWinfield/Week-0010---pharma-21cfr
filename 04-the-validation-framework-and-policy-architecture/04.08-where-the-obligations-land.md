## 1. What this chapter settles

Thirty-eight obligations exist and each of them is an act somebody must perform —
`04.06-the-obligations.md` and `logs/obligation-register.md`. **This chapter asks the next question,
which is whether anything in Helix's quality system already requires the act.**

Sixty-three procedures were read against the thirty-eight, between 2026-11-09 and 2026-11-17, and the reading
is `EV-417`. The result divides three ways and the middle class is the subject of the chapter.

| Where the obligation lands | Count |
|---|---|
| **An existing procedure requires the act** | **21** |
| **A procedure exists and does not require the act** | **12** |
| **No procedure at all** | **5** |
| **Total** | **38** — **21 + 12 + 5 = 38** |

`ADR-0028`, `DEC-417`, taken by Terrence Abbatiello on 2026-11-19: **a procedure that exists and does not
require the act is recorded as a distinct class of gap from a procedure that does not exist.**

## 2. The rule the reading applied

**A procedure was recorded as requiring the act only where a sentence in it obliges somebody to perform that
act.** Three things were not treated as requiring it, and each was refused deliberately.

**Subject-matter coverage is not requirement.** A procedure that governs the chromatography data system does
not thereby require anybody to state which instance of a raw data file is the original. It is the procedure
the question would live in; it is not a procedure that asks it.

**A general obligation is not a specific one.** A procedure requiring records to be *complete and accurate*
was not read as requiring the comparison at `OB-12`, or the rendering demonstration at `OB-11`. A sentence
broad enough to cover anything requires nothing in particular, and **a requirement nobody can fail is not a
requirement.**

**Silence is not permission and it is not prohibition either.** Where a procedure neither requires the act
nor excludes it, the reading recorded *does not require the act* and stopped. **A procedure that is silent on
a question is not neutral about it** — the position Phase 01 fixed at `01.07 §2` and this reading applies
without variation.

The reading was performed by the Head of Quality Assurance, who owns the quality system, and reviewed by the
Data Integrity Lead, who owns the determination method — `01.07`, `ADR-0002`. **Neither of the two owns the
validation framework**, which is deliberate: the party that would benefit from a procedure being found
adequate did not perform the finding.

## 3. The twenty-one that land somewhere that requires the act

These are the least interesting rows and they are the largest group, which is worth saying because a chapter
that dwelt only on gaps would misdescribe the estate.

| Obligations | The procedures they land in |
|---|---|
| `OB-02`, `OB-16` | Equipment cleaning and use procedure — entries are required to be made at the time of the activity |
| `OB-03` | Line clearance and packaging set-up procedure |
| `OB-04` | Document control procedure |
| `OB-05`, `OB-15`, `OB-23` | Access administration procedure |
| `OB-06` | Stability programme procedure |
| `OB-07` | Purified water monitoring and release procedure |
| `OB-09`, `OB-27` | Calibration procedure |
| `OB-10`, `OB-26` | Environmental monitoring procedure |
| `OB-12` | Production and control record review procedure — `21 CFR 211.192` |
| `OB-14`, `OB-31` | Reserve sample procedure |
| `OB-19`, `OB-20`, `OB-21`, `OB-22` | Chromatographic data acquisition and processing procedure; laboratory testing and analytical method procedure |
| `OB-30` | Serialisation and distribution procedure |

**Twenty-one obligations arrive at a sentence that already requires them, and what is missing on those rows
is the evidence, the practice, or both.** That is a real position and it is not a comfortable one — a
procedure that requires an act and an attribute recorded *Not met* on the record that act should have
produced are two facts that sit awkwardly together, and `21 CFR 211.22(d)` is where the awkwardness lives:
the quality control unit's responsibilities and procedures shall be in writing and *"such written procedures
shall be followed."*

**But the framework's response to those twenty-one is to require the evidence, not to write a new
procedure.** Writing a second sentence requiring what the first sentence already requires produces a longer
quality system and no change in what anybody does. `04.11-what-a-framework-cannot-fix.md §4` owns the
argument about the procedure nobody follows and it is not restated here.

## 4. The five that land nowhere

**Five obligations reach no procedure in the estate at all.** `IS-25` carries them, owned by Priyanka
Venkataraman, raised 2026-11-17.

| Obligation | The act | Why nothing covers it |
|---|---|---|
| `OB-11` | Demonstrate that a record can be rendered from the store that holds it in complete and readable form, with the context needed to understand it | Nothing in the estate requires a record to be produced from a store as a test. Retrieval is assumed to be a property of the system rather than a thing to be demonstrated |
| `OB-18` | Determine all five attributes for the chromatographic integration and reprocessing record on `SYS-023` and `SYS-024` | The record type was treated as outside Part 11 for the whole period in which it was a Part 11 record — `IS-08`. **No procedure was ever written for a record nobody had determined to be one** |
| `OB-25` | Demonstrate that a record held in the laboratory data archive can be rendered in complete and readable form | As `OB-11`, and compounded: the archives entered the estate as storage infrastructure and no procedure treats them as holding records |
| `OB-29` | Record the individual, the capture time and the relied-on instance for each warehouse temperature excursion search result | The excursion search result is a record produced by running a query. **Nothing in the estate governs a record that is generated rather than entered** |
| `OB-35` | Set the degree and frequency of input/output verification for each system from its complexity-and-reliability band, and from nothing else | The graduation `21 CFR 211.68(b)` names did not exist at Helix until this phase made it, and nothing can be sized from a determination nobody has made |

**Four of the five share a feature.** `OB-11`, `OB-18`, `OB-25` and `OB-29` are all about a record whose
existence is not the same thing as its retrievability: a record in an archive, a record produced by
reprocessing, a record rendered from a query. **Helix's procedure estate is built around records that are
entered by a person and filed**, and it has no vocabulary for a record that a system produces on demand. That
is not a criticism of the estate. It is a description of when the estate was written.

**`OB-35` is the fifth and it is a different animal.** It is the only obligation in the register that arises
from a `shall` and lands nowhere: `21 CFR 211.68(b)` provides that *"The degree and frequency of input/output
verification shall be based on the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system."* `OB-34`
now produces the band. Nothing requires anybody to size anything from it.

## 5. The twelve, and the argument this chapter exists for

**Twelve obligations land in a procedure that exists and does not require the act.** `IS-24` carries them,
owned by Terrence Abbatiello, raised 2026-11-17.

| Obligation | The procedure it lands in | What the procedure says instead |
|---|---|---|
| `OB-01` | Records retention schedule and archive procedure | It says how long classes of record are kept. It does not say which instance of a record Helix relies on as the original, and it carries no entry for any of the 34 in-scope record types — `IS-14`, `DP-18` |
| `OB-08` | Warehouse storage-condition monitoring procedure | It requires the condition to be monitored and excursions to be actioned. It does not require an individual to be recorded against an entry |
| `OB-13` | Computerised system validation procedure | It requires a validation package. It does not require the evidence for each paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` to be specified in it |
| `OB-17` | Chromatographic data acquisition and processing procedure | It governs acquisition, integration and reporting. It does not require a statement of which instance is the original |
| `OB-24` | Environmental and utility monitoring procedure | It requires trends to be reviewed. It does not require the capture time or the relied-on instance to be recorded |
| `OB-28` | Warehouse storage-condition monitoring procedure | As `OB-08`, on a different attribute |
| `OB-32` | Records retention schedule and archive procedure | It governs the archive as a place records go. It does not require anything to be demonstrated about a record once it is there |
| `OB-33` | Records retention schedule and archive procedure | As `OB-32` |
| `OB-34` | Computerised system validation procedure | It requires validation to be risk-based. It does not require complexity and reliability to be determined, and does not say what would be determined about them |
| `OB-36` | Computerised system validation procedure | It requires interfaces to be tested at qualification. It does not require input to and output from a system to be checked for accuracy in operation — the second sentence of `21 CFR 211.68(b)` |
| `OB-37` | Computerised system validation procedure | It requires the system to be shown accurate, reliable and fit for its intended use. **It does not mention the ability to discern invalid or altered records** |
| `OB-38` | Computerised system validation procedure | As `OB-37`, across all eleven paragraphs |

**A procedure that exists and does not require the act is worse than a procedure that does not exist, because
an auditor who asks "is there a procedure?" gets a yes.**

That is the whole of the argument and the rest of this section is its consequences.

**The question is the natural question and it is not a bad one.** *Is there a procedure covering this?* is
how a quality system is normally interrogated, by internal audit, by a third party, by an inspector and by
Helix's own management review. It is a question about the existence of an instrument, and it is asked because
the absence of an instrument is a defect anybody can act on. **Against the five, the question works.** Ask it
of `OB-11` or `OB-35` and the answer is no, and a no is a finding somebody writes down.

**Against the twelve, the same question returns a yes and stops.** There is a records retention schedule.
There is a computerised system validation procedure. There is a warehouse storage-condition monitoring
procedure. Each of the twelve arrives at a document that exists, is controlled, is current, has an owner and
covers the subject matter — and does not contain the sentence. **The yes is accurate and it is the end of the
enquiry**, because the next question — *and does it require this particular act?* — is a question somebody
has to know to ask, and knowing to ask it requires already knowing the act is missing.

**Only the five are visible to the question an auditor actually asks.** The twelve are invisible to it, and
they are invisible in a way that survives being looked at.

**Helix has watched this exact mechanism operate once already, and the record of it is published.** `IS-03`
records that the register of computerised systems carried no attribute identifying which systems held
records required by a predicate rule, and that **the same gap was raised inside the audit history at
`IA-2025-01/F3` and closed there against the procedure that existed**. `01.09` records what the audit finding
could and could not do: *"It records that the IT register carried no predicate-rule attribute. It does not
record what should have been in that attribute, because that determination did not exist to be tested
against."* An audit found the register, tested it against the procedure that governed it, raised what the
procedure allowed to be raised, and closed it. **The procedure existed. The determination did not. And
nothing in the process that produced fourteen findings across four audits had any way of noticing the
difference.**

That is why the twelve are recorded as a distinct class with their own identifier rather than being folded
into a general count of gaps. **A class that has to be counted separately is a class somebody has to look at
separately.**

## 6. Which of the two classes is harder to close

It is worth being honest that the distinct-class argument does not make the twelve harder to fix. It makes
them harder to find, which is a different property.

| | The five | The twelve |
|---|---|---|
| **Visible to *"is there a procedure?"*** | **Yes** | **No** |
| Visible to *"does the procedure require this act?"* | Yes | Yes |
| What closing it requires | A new procedure, or a new section of one | **An amendment to a document that already exists** |
| Effort to close, once found | Larger — the instrument has to be built | Smaller — a sentence has to be added |
| **Likelihood of being found by an ordinary enquiry** | High | **Low** |

**The twelve are cheap to fix and expensive to find; the five are expensive to fix and cheap to find.** A
programme that ranked its remediation by effort would do the twelve first and would never have noticed them.
**`ADR-0027` is what forces every obligation to declare a landing**, including a landing of *nowhere*, and it
is the mechanism by which both classes were produced at all: the register cannot be completed without
answering, for each of the thirty-eight, which of the three the answer is.

## 7. What the nine-document SOP set does about the seventeen

**Seventeen obligations — the twelve and the five — do not currently arrive at a sentence that requires
them.** `04.09-the-policy-architecture.md` owns the response and maps each of the seventeen to the
document that would close it: four new procedures and five amendments, plus a validation policy and the
framework document.

**Nothing here says the seventeen are closed.** The SOP set is approved and none of the nine has been applied
to anything at this vantage. **A procedure approved is not a procedure followed**, and
`04.11-what-a-framework-cannot-fix.md §4` owns that distinction, which runs directly onto
`21 CFR 211.22(d)`'s *"such written procedures shall be followed."*

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no control position.** **Nothing here says whether any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is met for
  any system** — `ADR-0029`, `IS-20`, and Phase 05 owns it.
- **It does not characterise any procedure, or the people who wrote one, as inadequate.** A procedure that
  does not require an act was written to require what it was written to require. **No document in this
  repository assesses any individual's work.**
- **It is not a finding, a deficiency, a deviation or an inspectional observation.** **An open gap is Helix's
  own assessment of its own position. It is not a violation, not a breach, not a non-compliance, not a
  finding and not an inspectional observation.**
- **It does not say that any past audit, internal or external, should have found any of the twelve.** §5 uses
  `IA-2025-01/F3` to describe a mechanism, and **describing a mechanism is not assigning a cause.** Nothing
  here bears on `IS-01`, and `IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted.
- **It states no validation outcome.** **No package has been executed, no protocol run and no test result
  exists.**
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.** It does not predict what any auditor
  or inspector would ask, would find or would conclude; it describes what a question of a given shape can and
  cannot return.
