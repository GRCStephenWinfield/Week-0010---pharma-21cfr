# 07.10 — The Paper Question

*Phase 02's printout route holds for four record types and Phase 07 does not disturb it. What the route did was move them out of 21 CFR Part 11 and into 21 CFR 211.180, and nobody followed them there.*

---

## 1. The route holds, and it was correctly applied

**Four record types are not Part 11 records because the paper-printout route survives all three of its
conditions.** `RT-09` the tablet hardness and friability test printout, `RT-22` the analytical balance
weighing printout, `RT-23` the manual pH and conductivity measurement printout, and `RT-29` the portable
temperature calibrator certificate printout. **That determination is Phase 02's, it is right, and nothing
in this chapter or this phase disturbs it for a moment.**

**It deserves to be said generously, because the work behind it was better than what most programmes do.**
Helix had treated eleven record types as outside Part 11 on the strength of a printout. Phase 02 refused to
carry any of the eleven forward on the existing position and refused equally to declare all eleven in scope
without testing — **which would have put four record types and their instruments into a control programme
they do not need, the same error as the original made in the opposite direction.** It tested each of the
eleven against **the printout route's three conditions — two FDA states expressly and a third Helix derives
from the narrow interpretation's second category** — and it published the test rather than the result.
Seven failed the first condition because the record is dynamic and a static printout cannot reproduce it.
**Four survived, on facts, evidenced instrument by instrument during the walk of the estate at `EV-223`.**
`ADR-0010`, `DEC-210`, Dr Sunita Raghunathan, 2026-07-07; `02.06 §7`.

**The facts the four rest on are recorded rather than assumed.** The tablet hardness and friability testers
retain no retrievable result and have no export capability. The analytical balances hold no record after
the ticket prints. The bench pH and conductivity meters have no storage and no export port. The portable
temperature calibrators hold no retained certificate. **In all four cases the instrument produces a fixed
output and keeps nothing: there is no interaction to lose, because there is nothing left to interact
with.** `AS-14` records the assumption; `PR-16` records what happens if an instrument is replaced.

**This chapter re-opens none of it.** Not a condition, not an instrument, not a record type. **The four
are not Part 11 records and this chapter does not say otherwise** — and `PR-55` carries the risk that
somebody reads what follows as though it did.

## 2. And now the inversion

**A determination that a record is not a Part 11 record is not a determination that it is unregulated.**
`ADR-0046`, `DEC-708`, Priyanka Venkataraman, 2027-03-09.

**Every one of the four is a required record.** That is not a consequence of the printout route; **it is
the reason the printout route was available.** The route's first condition is that the paper record *"meet
all the requirements of the applicable predicate rules"* — **so a record type that reaches the route at all
is a record type a predicate rule requires.** `RT-09` is required by `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)`, read with
`21 CFR 211.110(a)` and `(c)`. `RT-22` by `21 CFR 211.194(a)(3)`. `RT-23` by `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)`.
`RT-29` by `21 CFR 211.68(a)`, read with `21 CFR 211.160(b)(4)`. **The predicate rules were never in
question. They were the premise.**

**So what did the route actually do?** It determined that Helix is not using electronic records in lieu of
paper records for these four, and that the use of computers in generating the paper does not trigger Part
11. **It moved four record types out of `21 CFR Part 11`. It moved them into nothing else, because they
were already there.** **The paper is the record required under Part 211, and everything Part 211 says
about a required record now attaches to the paper.**

**FDA has addressed this fact pattern directly and it is worth quoting, because it states the condition
rather than the conclusion.** At Question 10 of the December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity questions and
answers — *"Is it acceptable to retain paper printouts or static records instead of original electronic
records from stand-alone computerized laboratory instruments, such as an FT-IR instrument?"* — FDA
answers: *"A paper printout or static record may satisfy retention requirements if it is the original
record or a true copy of the original record (see §§ 211.68(b), 211.188, 211.194, and 212.60). During
data acquisition, for example, pH meters and balances may create a paper printout or static record as the
original record. In this case, the paper printout or static record, or a true copy, must be retained
(§ 211.180)."* And FDA states the limitation in the same answer: *"However, electronic records from certain
types of laboratory instruments—whether stand-alone or networked—are dynamic, and a printout or a static
record does not preserve the dynamic record format that is part of the complete original record."*

**That is guidance and it is cited as guidance; the requirement is the predicate rule's. What the answer
supplies is the condition.** The four record types here are the case FDA's second sentence describes: the
instrument keeps nothing, so the printout is the original record, and `EV-223` evidences that instrument by
instrument. **The condition is met on the facts Phase 02 established, and Phase 02's determination is not
disturbed by saying so** — the record types where FDA's limitation bites are the dynamic ones, and Phase 02
kept those inside Part 11 for exactly that reason. **What follows from the condition being met is the whole
of this chapter: the paper is the record, and the obligations that attach to a record required under
Part 211 attach to it.** Nobody discharged any of them.

**`21 CFR 211.180` says three things about a required record that matter here, and they are the
provision's, not this phase's.** `07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md` reads the section whole;
the short form is: **`(a)` and `(b)` set periods for particular classes of record. `(c)` requires all
records required under Part 211, or copies of them, to be readily available for authorised inspection
during the retention period at the establishment where the activities occurred, and requires those records
or copies to be subject to photocopying or other means of reproduction as part of such inspection. `(d)`
permits records to be retained either as original records or as true copies.** None of the three cares
whether the record is paper or electronic. **`(d)` in particular is written for paper: photocopies,
microfilm, microfiche** — and `(c)`'s second sentence lands on a thermal ticket with particular force,
because reproducing one is an act performed on the physical object during an inspection.

**Nobody at Helix followed the four there.** The determination was made, the record types left the Part 11
population, and no document in Phases 02, 03, 04, 05 or 06 states a Part 211 position on the paper — not
where it is kept, not by whom, not for how long, and not whether what is kept will still be readable.
**`EV-726` records the position at the `MS-11` gate: the route holds, and the obligation it left behind
was never discharged.**

## 3. What following them found

**The register is `logs/paper-record-register.md`, `PP-01` to `PP-04`**, approved at `DEC-714` on
2027-03-17 and ratified at `GOV-34`. The custody enquiries are at `EV-723` and the medium enquiry at
`EV-724`; both retain the enquiry and not only its answer.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is a retention location stated for the paper? | **No, on 4 of 4** |
| Is an individual named as accountable for it? | **No, on 4 of 4** |
| Has a retention period been derived for it? | **No, on 4 of 4** |
| Has a reproduction been made under `21 CFR 211.180(d)`? | **No, on 4 of 4** |

**Four questions, four record types, and the same answer sixteen times.**

**On the location.** In each case the paper is filed into something — the in-process log, the analytical
worksheet, the calibration record — and in each case the filing is what the operator or analyst does, not
what a document says must happen. **No procedure, register or record states where the paper is retained
once the thing it was filed into is itself filed.** `21 CFR 211.180(c)` attaches availability to the
establishment where the activities occurred, and the enquiry found nothing stating where that is for any
of the four.

**On the individual.** **`ADR-0008` has required a named individual owner for every control, procedure,
record and obligation in this programme since Phase 01.** For these four there is none. There is an
instrument owner — Dr Sunita Raghunathan owns `SYS-062` to `SYS-068` — but **an instrument owner is not an
owner of paper the instrument has already printed and forgotten.**

**On the period.** `07.03-the-derivation.md`'s derivation covered the 34 in-scope record types. **These
four are not among the 34 and were not derived**, and no earlier phase derived them either, because no
earlier phase was asking. **`RT-09` is the case where that matters most and it has its own section
below.**

**On `21 CFR 211.180(d)`.** The paragraph is a **permission**: records required under Part 211 may be
retained **either as original records or as true copies** such as photocopies, microfilm, microfiche or
other accurate reproductions. `07.11-original-or-a-true-copy-on-the-cgmp-side.md` owns what the permission
requires, what *true* is doing in it, and what FDA has said in guidance about both the permission and the
archiving of records to paper or to a standard electronic format. **What is recorded here is that Helix
has not used the permission on any of the four.** No reproduction has been made of any of the tickets or
certificates. **The original is what there is.**

## 4. The medium

**The medium is thermal on three of the four** — `RT-09`, `RT-22` and `RT-23`. **`RT-29`'s certificate is
printed on plain paper.**

**Helix holds a statement of the archival life of that thermal stock from no source.** The enquiry at
`EV-724` records the sources searched — the stock supplier, the four instrument vendors, Helix's own
qualification records, and the standards set the programme works to — **and records that none of them held
one.** The list of what was searched is retained so that *none found* can be tested as a search rather
than taken as a conclusion.

**What this chapter says about that, and what it refuses to say, are both worth stating precisely.**

**It says: Helix cannot answer a question about a record it is required to keep.** How long the print on a
thermal ticket remains legible under the conditions in which Helix stores it is a question with an answer,
and Helix has not obtained it from anybody.

**It refuses to say what the answer is.** **No claim is made that thermal print fades, that any ticket has
faded, that any record has been lost, or that any of the four is at risk.** Every one of those would be a
claim about a property nobody has established — **which is precisely the criticism this chapter is making,
and making it in the other direction would be the same error with the sign reversed.** **The absence of a
statement is the finding.**

**`RT-29`'s position is different in kind rather than absent.** Plain paper raises a different set of
questions from thermal stock and Helix has not asked those either: no retention location, no named
individual, no derived period and no reproduction. **The medium is the one column of the register on which
the four do not answer alike, and it is not the column that matters most.**

## 5. `RT-09`, and the period the regulation already set

**`RT-09` is the tablet hardness and friability test printout, and it is an in-process control result
specifically associated with a batch.**

**Take it in the order the regulation takes it.** The ticket carries the individual measurements, the mean,
the instrument identity, the date and time, **and the sample identity** — which is a sample from a named
batch, taken during the manufacture of that batch. `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)` requires in-process and
laboratory control results to be documented in the batch production and control record. **The result is a
control record of that batch and of no other**, and the ticket is the record of it.

**`21 CFR 211.180(a)` reaches** *"[a]ny production, control, or distribution record that is required to be
maintained in compliance with this part **and is specifically associated with a batch of a drug
product**…"* — and the period it sets is **at least 1 year after the expiration date of the batch; for an
exempt OTC drug product lacking expiration dating because it meets the criteria for exemption under
`21 CFR 211.137`, 3 years after distribution of the batch.**

**Nothing in that paragraph is about electronic records.** It reaches production, control and distribution
records required under Part 211, and `RT-09` is one. **`21 CFR 211.180(a)`'s period therefore attaches to
the paper**, and it has attached to it throughout — before this programme, before the inspection, and
before Phase 02 determined that the record type is outside Part 11.

**So the position on `RT-09` is this. A period the regulation sets, on a record nobody owns, on a medium
nobody has characterised.**

**Note the shape carefully, because the other three are not in the same position and the difference is not
that theirs is smaller.** `RT-22`, `RT-23` and `RT-29` are records whose predicate rules require the
content and set no period of their own; where a period would come from is the record they are filed into,
and nothing states what that is. **`RT-09` lacks an arrangement for a period the regulation has already
set. The other three lack the arrangement and the determination both.**

## 6. `IS-49`, and what would close it

**`IS-49`** — the four record types the printout route removed from Part 11 have no stated retention
location, no named individual accountable for the paper, no derived period and no reproduction under
`21 CFR 211.180(d)`, and on `RT-09` a period the regulation sets attaches to a thermal ticket nobody owns.
Raised 2027-03-17, owned by Dr Sunita Raghunathan, open.

**`DP-42` is the route:** a stated retention location, a named individual accountable under
`21 CFR 211.180(c)`, and a statement of the archival life of the medium — or a reproduction under
`21 CFR 211.180(d)` — for each of the four. **From the two QC laboratories and the owner of the calibration
programme.** It carries no date, no priority and no order.

**It does not close by the printout route being re-opened.** Moving the four back into Part 11 scope would
be the wrong remedy for the right problem: **the paper would still be the record required under Part 211,
it would still have no location, no owner and no period, and Part 11 would have been applied to a record
type that does not meet the narrow interpretation's categories.** `ADR-0046` is the standing framing and
`PR-55` carries the misreading.

**And it does not close by anything in this chapter, because this chapter states no corrective action.**
**No CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no remediation cost is stated here, implied here, or
attributable to this phase.**

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It re-opens no Phase 02 determination.** **The printout route holds for all four record types, was
  correctly applied, was tested rather than asserted, and is not disturbed** — `ADR-0010`, `ADR-0046`,
  `EV-726`.
- **It states no retention location, names no individual and derives no period for any of the four.**
  Those are Helix's to decide and are recorded as owed — `DP-42`.
- **It makes no claim about the durability of any medium**, in either direction.
- **It asserts nothing about whether any record has been lost, damaged or rendered illegible**, and nothing
  in this phase examined a single ticket, worksheet or certificate for condition. **No paper record was
  inspected.**
- **It brings none of the four inside the 34**, inside the 121 pairs or inside the 61 systems. `21 CFR
  11.10(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)` do not attach to them and no cell of any Part 11 register is created,
  changed or implied.
- **It states no CAPA, no deviation and no remediation plan.**
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**

**A route correctly applied is not an obligation discharged.**
