## 1. One idea

**Phase 06 produced a story. Phase 07 produces something more dangerous than a story: a table with an empty
column.**

That is the specific hazard here and it is unlike anything the programme has published before. A retention
derivation is, on its face, a schedule with the periods missing. **Every professional instinct a competent
reader has will tell them that the document is incomplete and that completing it would be a service.** The
gap looks like an omission. It is a finding.

**And the pressure is not only on the reader.** A phase that has read every applicable retention provision,
enumerated every system that holds a regulated record, and established for each record type whether a
period exists **is one short paragraph away from the document the whole organisation actually wants.** That
paragraph is not written, in this chapter or anywhere in the phase, and **this chapter is where the refusal
to write it is recorded** — in the same phase as the findings, at the same version, as a numbered chapter
rather than as a caveat at the foot of a register.

It is owned by internal audit, which derived no period, walked no archive, read no backup catalogue,
followed no printout into a filing cabinet and took no determination in this phase — `ADR-0004`. **That is
the point of who owns it.**

## 2. What a reader might take from this phase, against what it establishes

**The left column is not a straw man. Every row is a reading a competent, well-disposed reader produces
from the material this phase publishes**, and several of them are readings the material almost invites.

| What a reader might take from this phase | What Phase 07 actually establishes |
|---|---|
| **That Helix now has a retention policy** | **It does not.** **No retention policy is stated anywhere in this phase** — `ADR-0050`. A policy states what the organisation will do, is approved by the authority competent to state it, and becomes enforceable against the organisation once it exists. **This phase states what the regulations require and what Helix holds, and stops** |
| **That Helix now has a retention schedule** | **It does not.** A schedule is an operational instrument that tells somebody what to do with a particular record on a particular date. **This phase publishes a derivation, which is a statement about classes of record and the provisions that reach them** — `07.01-a-period-is-derived-not-chosen.md §8`. **The disposal process, the legal-hold mechanism, the approval route, the exception route and the instance-level inventory a schedule needs do not exist** |
| **That an archive has been designed, specified or procured** | **None of the three.** **No archive design, no migration plan, no storage specification, no format, no fixity mechanism, no location and no vendor** appears anywhere in this phase. **A derivation says how long. It says nothing whatever about how** |
| **That the record types no predicate rule reaches have been given a period, or a provisional one, or a range** | **None of them has.** **No period is set, proposed, ranged, bounded, hinted at or illustrated for any Group C record type**, in a chapter, a register cell, a diagram, a governance record or a worked example. **What is recorded is that a decision is owed and which named individual owes it** — `07.04-what-nothing-sets.md`, `ADR-0044`, `ADR-0050`. **An unchosen period is not a short period; it is no period** |
| **That anything has been authorised to be destroyed** | **Nothing has.** **No document in this phase authorises, requires or schedules the destruction of any record.** A derived period is a **floor** — the regulations that set periods set them as minima — and **a firm that read a derivation as permission to destroy a record on the day the minimum expired would have converted a floor into a deadline** |

Phase 06 established what `OBS-2`'s subject matter is at depth and disposed of nothing — `ADR-0041` — and **this phase disposes of nothing either** |

## 3. The distinctions this phase must not blur

`ADR-0006` established that this repository keeps one set of books, and six phases have added vocabulary to
it. **Phase 07 adds three more objects and none of them is any of the others.**

| Object | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **`RD-nn`** — a retention derivation | **A statement about one record type: its predicate rule as Phase 02 published it, the provision that sets its period or the word *none*, the period as it falls out, its group and the systems that hold it** | **Not a policy, not a schedule entry, not a disposal instruction and not an authority to destroy anything.** A derivation with *none* in its provision cell is **complete**, not unfinished |
| **`BR-nn`** — a backup and restore row | **A statement about one system: whether a backup file is maintained, whether a restore has ever been performed, whether what came back was compared with what went in, and whether the carve-out determination has been made** | **Not a validation record, not a test result, not a control position and not a determination that any backup is adequate.** **A backup that has never been restored is recorded as a backup and never as a control** — `ADR-0047` |
| **`PP-nn`** — a paper record row | **A statement about one record type whose record is now paper under `21 CFR Part 211`**: where it is retained, who is accountable for it, what period is derived for it, whether a reproduction has been made under `21 CFR 211.180(d)`, and on what medium | **Not a re-opening of Phase 02's applicability determination, not a finding against the printout route, and not a characterisation of anybody's filing.** **The route holds** — `ADR-0046` |
| **A derived period** | **A statement about a regulation** — the provision, its triggering event and the minimum it sets | **Not a decision Helix has taken, not a period Helix has chosen, and not a retention rule anybody is yet required to apply** |
| **A group label** | **A label for one of three positions a record type can occupy** against the provisions that set retention periods | **Not a score, not a band, not a rating, not a severity and not an ordering.** Group A, Group B and Group C confer no priority of any kind, and **nothing in the quality system says what any of them obliges anybody to do** |

**And a period set by no predicate rule is not a defect in `21 CFR 211.180`.** The regulation sets periods
for the classes it sets periods for. **Recording that it does not reach a record type is a statement about
the scope of a provision, not a complaint about the drafting**, and no document in this phase reads it as
one.

## 5. `IS-01` stays open, and stays unweighted

**Nothing in Phase 07 bears on `IS-01`.**

`IS-01` records that four internal audits between 2023-06 and 2025-09 covered all three systems between
them, each falling inside the scope of at least two, and that none raised a finding on the subject matter
of `OBS-2`, `OBS-4` or `OBS-6`. **Why is not established.** `01.09 §6` recorded the candidate explanations
and chose none; `ADR-0007` published the history in full; `ADR-0012` and `02.11` established the premise of
one and refused to promote it; `03.12 §5`, `04.12 §5`, `05.12 §5` and `06.12 §5` each confirmed that
nothing in their phase discriminated between them.

**The pressure in this phase takes a different form from Phase 06's, and it is subtler.** Phase 06 supplied
a technical fact that felt like an explanation. **Phase 07 supplies something that feels like a
pattern** — an area of the estate in which, on the programme's own account, a great deal was never
established, never written down and never examined by anybody. **A reader holding that beside four audits
that raised nothing will feel a sentence forming, and the sentence is not written here, in any register, in
any diagram, in any governance record, or in any later phase that cites this one.**

**The candidate explanations are not reproduced in this chapter and none is named, by number or by
content**, because naming one and not the others is a weighting, and reproducing all of them in a chapter
about a different subject is an invitation to read one of them across.

**Three reasons, and each is sufficient alone.**

**This phase reconstructed no state of affairs at any date before its own vantage.** Every derivation,
every register row and every determination in it speaks as at 2027-03-19. **A fact established in March
2027 is not evidence about an audit performed in June 2023**, and treating it as such would be
`ADR-0012`'s error committed with better material.

**This phase examined no audit artefact.** No scope statement, no checklist, no test design, no working
paper, no sampling basis and no auditor's technical access at any historical date. **It has traced no
checklist to its source**, and it could not have: examining the four audit programmes is Phase 08's work,
and internal audit's own function is the subject of it.

**And an absence established in one subject matter is not an explanation of a silence in another.**
`IS-01` is about all three of `OBS-2`, `OBS-4` and `OBS-6`, across four audits, over more than two years.
**A fact about what Helix had and had not established about retention, backup and paper custody in 2027
does not reach what any audit did or did not look at at any earlier date**, and the temptation to read the
one as accounting for the other is precisely the inference `ADR-0012` forbids.

**The candidate explanations stand exactly where Phase 01 left them, and there is no promotion of any of
its four candidate explanations by statement, by ordering or by tone** — `ADR-0012`. **No ranking, no
probability, no confidence level, no leading candidate and no ordering**, in this chapter, in the
registers, in any diagram, in any governance record or in any later phase. **The set is at `01.09 §6`, in the order that
chapter set, and this chapter reproduces none of it. No document in this phase may be cited in support of
any candidate explanation, and if one is, it is wrong and this sentence is why.**

**What is still needed, restated.** The examination of the four audit programmes against what has now been
assessed, in **Phase 08**. **That still does not exist.**

## 6. Two things the phase must never be read as saying

**First: that a route correctly applied is an obligation discharged, or that recording the undischarged
part is a criticism of the route.**

**Phase 02's printout route holds for all four record types, and this phase does not disturb it for a
moment.** The test was applied as it actually reads, with its three conjunctive conditions — two FDA states
expressly and a third Helix derives from the narrow interpretation's second category — each tested
individually, evidenced individually and recorded individually. **It was good work, it produced the right
answer, and the right answer is that those four record types are not Part 11 records.** `ADR-0010` fixed
the method and `02.06 §7` published the four with the facts each rests on.

**What Phase 07 adds is what happened next, which is nothing.** The route moved those records out of
`21 CFR Part 11` and into `21 CFR 211.180`, where the paper is the record required under Part 211 — **and
following them there is a separate act that nobody performed.** `ADR-0046` states the principle: **a
determination that a record is not a Part 11 record is not a determination that it is unregulated.**

**That is a finding about a gap between two determinations, not about either of them.** Anybody quoting
this phase as evidence that the printout route was misapplied, or should be narrowed, or should be revisited
for any of the four, **has read the opposite of what the phase says.**

**Second: that a gap recorded is a gap resolved, or that a decision named is a decision taken.**

The most quotable output of this phase is the sentence that everything now has a period, an owner or an
explicit gap where one of those is missing. **Out of its context that reads as completion, and it is not
completion.** An explicit gap with a named individual against it is **a better position than an implicit
gap with nobody against it**, and it is not the same thing as a period. **A decision that is owed has not
been taken, and naming who owes it changes nothing about the record's actual retention until they take it.**

**And the standing refusals that go with it.** **There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate
and no FDA-approved, FDA-validated or FDA-certified software.** FDA does not certify electronic systems and
does not pre-evaluate them for Part 11 compliance; a product has **features that support** compliance, and
**compliance is a property of Helix's validated system in its intended use, under its procedures, operated
by its trained people. Outsourcing to a service provider transfers no responsibility.** **FDA's Computer
Software Assurance guidance is device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by the Quality
Management System Regulation, and does not apply to Helix**; it is adopted voluntarily and labelled at
every citation — `ADR-0005`, `ADR-0026`. **GAMP 5 Second Edition is ISPE good-practice guidance and never a
requirement, and no software-category count and no enumeration of the categories appears anywhere in this
repository.** **NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5 is a control catalogue Helix chose, not an obligation it is
under: no FIPS 199 categorization, no baseline, no ATO and no authorizing official.**

**And enforcement discretion, stated with all its limbs, because this phase touches three of the paragraphs
it reaches.** **FDA has stated an intention to exercise enforcement discretion over `21 CFR 11.10(a)`,
`(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)` in full and over `(k)` in part — the guidance names `(k)(2)` — together with the
corresponding requirements in `21 CFR 11.30`, and over all Part 11 requirements for legacy systems
operational before 20 August 1997 that meet the guidance's conditions, against which no Helix system has
been assessed.** **It is FDA's stated intention. It is not a legal exemption, it is not a safe harbour, and
it confers no rights** — the guidance carries FDA's own disclaimer that *"It does not create or confer any
rights for or on any person and does not operate to bind FDA or the public"*. **`21 CFR 211.68`,
`21 CFR 211.100(b)` and `21 CFR 211.180` survive it entirely**, and FDA says so itself, whole: *"Persons
must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements for record retention and availability
(e.g., §§ 211.180(c),(d), 108.25(g), and 108.35(h))."* **The parenthetical is FDA's and it names
`21 CFR 211.180(c)` and `(d)` — the availability paragraph and the true-copy permission this phase is built
on.** `ADR-0003`, `ADR-0035`. **A programme that reads enforcement discretion as permission has read the wrong
document.**

## 7. What a reader may legitimately take from the phase

Stated positively, once, so that the chapter is not only a list of refusals.

- **That every in-scope record type has been read against the provisions that set retention periods**, on a
  method fixed and authorised before the first derivation was made — `GOV-31`, `ADR-0044`.
- **That where a provision sets a period, the period is recorded with the provision it comes from and the
  event its clock runs from**, so that somebody else can check it and disagree with it.
- **That where no provision sets a period, that is recorded as the position, with a named individual who
  owes the decision** — and **no number is supplied on their behalf** — `07.04-what-nothing-sets.md`,
  `ADR-0050`.
- **That the systems which hold the records have been identified from Phase 02's register, with their
  published owners**, and that the ownership position is recorded as it is rather than as it might
  conveniently be — `07.05-the-retention-estate.md`, `IS-47`.
- **That backup and restore are recorded as two different things**, and that a restore performed to recover
  from an incident is recorded as what it was — `07.07-backup-is-not-restore.md`, `ADR-0047`.
- **That the four record types the printout route removed from `21 CFR Part 11` were followed into
  `21 CFR 211.180` and their position recorded there**, without the route being disturbed —
  `07.10-the-paper-question.md`, `ADR-0046`.
- **And that a phase which had assembled everything needed to write a retention schedule did not write
  one** — `ADR-0050`.

**None of those is a statement about whether any Helix record can be relied on, or about how long any of
them will actually be kept.** They are statements about what Helix now knows about its own obligations, on
one day.

## 8. The standing framing this phase keeps

- **Part 11 applies to records, not to systems**, and no document here says "a Part 11 system" without
  saying which records make it one — `ADR-0001`, `01.03 §2`.
- **A closed system is defined by who controls access** — `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`. **Hosting is not the test**,
  and all sixty-one systems in scope are closed — `ADR-0011`.
- **`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and Part 11 contains ten sections.** There is no §11.60, no
  §11.400 and no Subpart D. **Part 11 defines neither "audit trail" nor "metadata" nor "data integrity" nor
  "static record" nor "dynamic record" nor "archive"** — FDA defines the first five in the December 2018
  drug CGMP data integrity guidance. Citations in this repository are exact and can be looked up.
- **`21 CFR Part 11` sets no retention period of its own.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` sets a relation whose second
  term comes from the predicate rule.
- **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, no read-event logging, no cryptographic hashing under
  `21 CFR 11.70`, no multi-factor authentication under `21 CFR 11.200` and no password interval under
  `21 CFR 11.300(b)`.** Where Helix implements any of them, it is Helix's decision and this repository says
  so — `ADR-0036`.
- **`21 CFR 11.30`'s additional measures are additive to the `21 CFR 11.10` controls it requires be
  included as appropriate, and never a substitute for them.**
- **`21 CFR 211.68` requires appropriate controls over Helix's computerised systems and, at `(a)`, a
  written program designed to assure their proper performance.** **It imposes no general computerised-system
  validation requirement**, and the word *validation* appears in the section once, inside `(b)`'s backup
  carve-out.
- **FDA uses plain ALCOA**, and its own footnote 5 maps the attributes to the CGMP predicate rules and
  cites Part 11 nowhere — `ADR-0019`. **The four additional attributes are MHRA's; MHRA itself refers to
  the shorter acronym, states there is no difference in expectations regardless of which is used, and
  defines all four. It does not reject them.** **No originator is named for either set.**
- **A Form FDA 483 is not a final agency determination**, a Warning Letter is informal and advisory, and
  only a court order binds. **No Warning Letter, Untitled Letter or judicial action of any kind exists**,
  and **no document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of any kind.**
- **This programme does not forecast the outcome of any future FDA inspection**, and no document in this
  phase contains such a forecast. **The same refusal applies to the third-party audit and to the internal
  audit of the remediated programme. Neither has occurred and neither's result is anticipated anywhere.**
- **An open gap is Helix's own assessment of its own position.** It is not a violation, not a breach, not a
  non-compliance, not a finding and not an inspectional observation.
- **Applicability is not integrity, a rating is not a validation status, a requirement is not a control, a
  control position is not validation evidence, a depth finding is not an estate finding, and a derived
  period is not a decision Helix has taken.**
- **No entry in the four programme registers carries a likelihood, an impact or a score. None ever will.**
- **No document characterises any Helix employee, any auditor or any FDA investigator, and no document
  assesses any individual's work.**
- **All content about Helix is fictional and illustrative.**
