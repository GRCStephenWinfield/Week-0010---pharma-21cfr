*Fifty-four backup files, seven systems with none, four restores that all happened because something broke, and a requirement that is conditional on a question nobody has asked.*

---

## 1. The four numbers

**A backup file is maintained on 54 of the 61 systems. Seven have none. 54 + 7 = 61.**

**A restore has been performed on 4 of the 61.** `SYS-001` the manufacturing execution system, `SYS-025`
the LIMS at Ridgemont, `SYS-043` the enterprise resource planning system and `SYS-060` the analytical data
backup and restore platform. **Every one of the four was incident recovery — a storage array that failed, a
database that corrupted, an upgrade that was rolled back, a volume that went.** **None was a test.**

**On none of the four was the restored content compared with what went in. 0 of 4.**

**And on none of the 61 has Helix determined whether `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s backup carve-out reaches any of
its data. 0 of 61.**

The register is `logs/backup-and-restore-register.md`, `BR-01` to `BR-61`, approved at `DEC-712` on
2027-03-12 and ratified at `GOV-33`. **Every figure above is a count of its rows** and the reconciliation
is at `EV-718`. What a derivation is and why a description of practice is not a position is
`07.01-a-period-is-derived-not-chosen.md`'s; **this chapter applies the same discipline to an arrangement
rather than to a period and does not re-argue it.**

## 2. The provision, quoted whole, including the part that is usually dropped

**`21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s backup limb, with its condition and the sentence that follows it:**

> *"A backup file of data entered into the computer or related system shall be maintained except where
> certain data, such as calculations performed in connection with laboratory analysis, are eliminated by
> computerization or other automated processes. In such instances a written record of the program shall be
> maintained along with appropriate validation data."*

**And the sentence that closes the paragraph, which is a separate obligation and is quoted here because it
is the one that decides what a backup has to be:**

> *"Hard copy or alternative systems, such as duplicates, tapes, or microfilm, designed to assure that
> backup data are exact and complete and that it is secure from alteration, inadvertent erasures, or loss
> shall be maintained."*

**Three things follow, and the industry summary of this paragraph drops all three.**

**First, the requirement is conditional.** The paragraph does not say a backup file shall be maintained. It
says a backup file shall be maintained **except** where certain data are eliminated by computerization or
other automated processes. **A firm that maintains a backup file everywhere has not thereby met the
paragraph; it has satisfied one limb without establishing that the limb applies.** A firm that maintains
none on a particular system has not thereby failed it either. **Until the determination is made, neither
statement can be made about any system.**

**Second, the carve-out names laboratory calculation.** *"[C]alculations performed in connection with
laboratory analysis"* is the regulation's own example, **and it is a chromatography data system's own
subject matter.** `RT-13`'s predicate rule is `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` and `(a)(5)` — the data secured in the
course of the test **and** the record of all calculations performed in connection with it. **Helix operates
two chromatography data systems and has not asked which limb of `21 CFR 211.68(b)` reaches them.**

**Third, where the carve-out applies the obligation does not disappear; it changes shape.** *"In such
instances a written record of the program shall be maintained along with appropriate validation data."*
**A firm that concluded the carve-out reached its laboratory calculations would owe a written record of the
program and appropriate validation data instead**, and Helix holds neither, because Helix has not reached
the conclusion. **`21 CFR 211.68` imposes no general computerised-system validation requirement, and the
word *validation* appears in the section once — inside this carve-out.**

**`ADR-0048`**, `DEC-713`, Colm Ó Braonáin, 2027-03-16: **the requirement is conditional, and the condition
is determined per record type rather than assumed.** It is per record type and not per system because the
carve-out is about data, and a system holding several record types may fall on different limbs for
different ones.

## 3. What a Yes in the backup column is, and what it is not

**Fifty-four systems have a backup file. That is a real fact and it is better than the alternative.**

**It is a claim about a file.** Nobody has established that any of the fifty-four can be read; that it is
complete; that it corresponds to the record it was taken from; or that what would come back would be
usable by anybody.

**FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance ties the word *backup* to `21 CFR 211.68(b)`, and
it says four things, all of which bear on the fifty-four.** FDA *"uses the term backup in § 211.68(b) to
refer to a true copy of the original record that is maintained securely throughout the record retention
period (e.g., § 211.180)"* — **the duration is the predicate rule's, and FDA cites the same section this
phase derives its periods from.** *"Backup data must be exact, complete, and secure from alteration,
inadvertent erasures, or loss (§ 211.68(b))."* *"The backup file should contain the data (which includes
associated metadata) and should be in the original format or in a format compatible with the original
format."* And — the sentence that decides what a Yes in the column can mean — *"Temporary backup copies
(e.g., in case of a computer crash or other interruption) would not satisfy the requirement in § 211.68(b)
to maintain a backup file of data."*

That is guidance and it is cited as guidance; **the requirement is the paragraph's.** But read either
against the register and the point sharpens rather than softens. **Nobody at Helix has established, for any
of the fifty-four, that the file contains the data with its associated metadata, that it is in the original
or a compatible format, or that it is anything other than the kind of temporary copy FDA says does not
satisfy the paragraph at all.** **Exact, complete and secure are properties, and a property nobody has
tested is a property nobody has.**

**`ADR-0047`**, `DEC-711`, Amara Sissoko, 2027-03-12: **a backup that has never been restored is recorded
as a backup and never as a control.** The register carries four determination columns rather than one so
that *maintained*, *restored*, *compared* and *carve-out determined* cannot be collapsed into a verdict —
`PR-53`.

**On three systems the backup is somebody else's arrangement.** `SYS-040`, `SYS-041` and `SYS-056` are
vendor-hosted, and on each of them the backup file is the vendor's and **Helix holds no statement of what
it covers** — `BR-40`, `BR-41`, `BR-56`. `SYS-041` is the electronic quality management system and holds
seven in-scope record types. **A vendor may supply features that support compliance; compliance is a
property of Helix's use of its systems, and outsourcing to a service provider transfers no
responsibility** — `PR-07`, `IS-09`.

## 4. The four restores

**Four restores in the period Helix's records reach, and all four happened because something had already
gone wrong.**

| System | When | What happened | What was not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SYS-043` | 2025-11-14 | A failed upgrade rolled back and the database restored from the pre-upgrade backup file | **No comparison of restored content with what had been backed up.** The system was accepted back into service on the absence of user-reported error |
| `SYS-001` | 2026-04-22 | A storage-array failure recovered from the previous night's backup file | **No comparison** |
| `SYS-025` | 2026-09-08 | A database corruption recovered from the previous backup file | **No comparison.** The laboratory re-entered work from paper worksheets for the intervening period |
| `SYS-060` | 2027-01-19 | A volume failure recovered from the platform's own backup file | **No comparison** |

**An incident recovery answers a different question from a test, and the difference is not a matter of
degree.** An incident recovery asks *can we get the service back*. It is answered when people can work
again. **A restore test asks *is what came back the same as what went in*, and it is answered only by
comparing the two** — which requires knowing what went in, having somewhere to put what comes out, and
being willing to find out that they differ. **Nobody at Helix has asked the second question on any
occasion**, and the four occasions on which the machinery was actually exercised are the four occasions on
which nobody was in a position to.

**The last row is worth reading twice.** **`SYS-060` is named, in Phase 02's own register, the analytical
data backup and restore platform.** It is one of the four systems that constitute the retention estate,
it holds `RT-12` and `RT-13`, and it is the platform Phase 06's comparison material would have to come
from. **A restore has been run on it exactly once, because a volume failed, and the result was not
compared with anything.**

**`IS-46`** carries both limbs, owned by Amara Sissoko, raised 2027-03-12, open. `DP-40` is the
restore-verification limb; `DP-41` is the carve-out determination. **No CAPA, no deviation and no
remediation plan is stated by this chapter**, and neither dependency carries a date, a priority or an
order.

## 5. The seven with no backup file

**`SYS-011` the checkweigher network, `SYS-021` the clean-in-place skid, `SYS-022` the parts washer,
`SYS-033` the particle size analyser, `SYS-046` the cold-chain monitoring system, `SYS-055` the compressed
air and nitrogen quality monitoring system, and `SYS-058` the reserve sample store monitoring and inventory
system.**

**Every one of the seven entered the inventory by walk rather than from the IT register.** Phase 02's walk
found eleven such systems inside the 61; **seven of the eleven have no backup file at all.** They write to
local controller or workstation storage with no route to a backed-up store, which is what a system that
nobody's register knew about tends to do.

**That is a statement about what the register covered and not about the condition of any system.**
`IS-13` — seventeen systems entered the inventory that had never entered a validation lifecycle — has been
open since 2026-06-12 and this is the sharpest illustration of it the programme has produced. **Nothing
here says any of the seven ought to have a backup file**, because `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s requirement is
conditional and the condition has not been determined for any of them either. **What is recorded is that
seven systems holding Part 11 records have no backup file and that nobody has established whether they
need one.**

## 6. What Phase 06 handed forward, and where it stands

**`06.13 §7` recorded, in Phase 06's own words, that which limb of `21 CFR 211.68(b)` reaches `RT-12`,
`RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15` and `RT-17` is a determination nobody at Helix has made.** It named the
paragraph, quoted the carve-out, and handed the question to this phase.

**Phase 07 records that it is still owed, and names who owes it.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)` — controls over
computer or related systems, and backup — is held at Helix by **Colm Ó Braonáin, Computerised System
Validation Lead, with Amara Sissoko for the estate.** `DP-41` is registered against Colm Ó Braonáin and is
open.

**And Phase 07 widens it from five record types to thirty-four and from two systems to sixty-one.** Phase
06 could ask the question of the chromatography estate because it had been there. **The register now
records the same answer on every row of the estate: 0 of 61.**

**Phase 07 does not take the determination.** It is per record type, it requires the technical facts of how
each system produces and eliminates data, and taking it here would be the programme deciding an operational
question it has not investigated — **which is `ADR-0048`'s refused alternative and is recorded as refused
at `logs/decision-log.md §2`.**

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no backup specification, no schedule, no medium, no location, no technology and no vendor**
  — `ADR-0050`.
- **It states no CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no remediation cost.**
- **It makes no claim that any record has been lost**, that any backup is unreadable, or that any restore
  would fail. **Every one of those would be a claim about a property nobody has tested**, which is the
  chapter's own criticism turned round.
- **It determines nothing about `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s carve-out**, for any record type or any system.
- **It states no validation outcome.** Reading a backup arrangement is not validating a system, and
  **`21 CFR 211.68` imposes no general computerised-system validation requirement.** The evidence Phase 04
  requires is owed in full — `IS-34`.
- **It re-determines no Phase 05 cell.** `21 CFR 11.10(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)` stand exactly as published —
  `ADR-0049`.
- **It sets no retention period for a backup set or for anything else**, and a backup inherits the
  question the record it copies has not answered.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**
