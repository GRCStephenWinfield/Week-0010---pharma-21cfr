## 1. Two jobs, and the chapter is deliberately short

**This chapter does two things and nothing else.**

**The first is to read `21 CFR 211.180(d)` for what it actually is — a permission, conditioned on the copy
being true — and to set it beside the ALCOA attribute that shares its wording, so that the repository's
long-standing rule against conflating the two is stated once, in the place a reader would look for it.**

**The second is to name a numeral coincidence before anybody trips over it**, and to say in terms that the
two populations it concerns are unrelated. `§5` does that and it is the reason this chapter exists as a
chapter rather than as a paragraph somewhere else.

**It states no result of this phase.** Both figures at `§5` are cited to the documents that own them.

## 2. `21 CFR 211.180(d)` is a permission

**The first sentence, verbatim:**

> *"Records required under this part may be retained either as original records or as true copies such as
> photocopies, microfilm, microfiche, or other accurate reproductions of the original records."*

**The operative word is *may*.** The paragraph does not impose a duty to make copies, does not prefer
copies to originals, and does not prefer originals to copies. **It grants a permission — that a record
required under Part 211 need not be retained in its original form — and it attaches a condition to the
permission: the retained thing must be a true copy, an accurate reproduction of the original record.**

**Three consequences follow from reading it that way, and all three matter to this phase.**

**A permission has to be relied on to do any work.** A firm that retains originals never needs `(d)` at
all. A firm that retains something other than the original **is relying on `(d)`, whether or not it has
ever said so**, and the condition attaches to it from that moment.

**The condition is about the relation between two things.** *True*, *accurate reproduction* and *of the
original records* are all relational. **A copy is not true in itself; it is true with respect to an
original.** The paragraph therefore presupposes that the original is an identified thing.

**And the paragraph does not say how trueness is shown.** It names media — photocopies, microfilm,
microfiche — and it names a standard — accurate reproduction. **It supplies no method, no test, no
acceptance criterion and no evidence requirement.**

**FDA has supplied a content standard, in guidance, and it cites this paragraph while doing so.** In the
December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity questions and answers, at Question 9 — *"Can electronic copies be
used as accurate reproductions of paper or electronic records?"* — FDA answers that electronic copies
*"can be used as true copies of paper or electronic records, provided the copies preserve the content and
meaning of the original record, which includes all metadata required to reconstruct the CGMP activity and
the static or dynamic nature of the original records"*, and that true copies of dynamic electronic records
*"may be made and maintained in the format of the original records or in a format that allows for the
content and meaning of the original records to be preserved if a suitable reader and copying equipment
(e.g., software and hardware, including media readers) are readily available (§§ 211.180(d) and
212.110)."*

**FDA attaches the same condition to archiving, under the Record Retention heading of its August 2003
Scope and Application guidance**, where it does not intend to object if a firm archives required records in
electronic format *"to nonelectronic media such as microfilm, microfiche, and paper, or to a standard
electronic file format (examples of such formats include, but are not limited to, PDF, XML, or SGML)"* —
and states that *"[p]ersons must still comply with all predicate rule requirements, and the records
themselves and any copies of the required records should preserve their content and meaning."* **That is
the same media list `21 CFR 211.180(d)` gives, carried forward, with the condition attached.**

**Both are guidance and both are cited as guidance; the requirement is the paragraph's.** FDA's statements
are nonbinding recommendations and its current thinking, and nothing in either is called a requirement
here. **But they settle three things this chapter would otherwise have had to argue for itself.**
**FDA reads `(d)`'s reduction-techniques sentence forward to modern formats** and treats *suitable reader
and copying equipment* as reaching software and hardware, including media readers. **FDA states what
trueness is about** — content and meaning, the metadata required to reconstruct the CGMP activity, and the
static or dynamic character of the original. **And FDA still supplies no method, no test and no acceptance
criterion**: a standard is not a procedure. **An obligation whose method is unspecified is still an
obligation, and a firm relying on the permission has to be able to say why its copies are true** — and it
now has FDA's own three terms to say it against.

**Which makes the position at `§4` harder rather than softer.** The third of FDA's terms is the static or
dynamic nature of the original, and **Phase 02 established that seven record types fail the printout
route's first condition because the record is dynamic and a static printout cannot reproduce it** —
`02.06 §4`, `ADR-0010`, `IS-08`. A copy that preserves the content and meaning of a dynamic original has
to preserve the interaction that makes it dynamic, and **a static rendering of an interactive record is one
of the outputs the record can produce rather than the record.** Helix has named no original for any record
type — `§4`, `DP-17` — and no document in this repository states, for any record type, what metadata a
retained copy would have to carry or whether it carries it. **Against a stated standard, that is three
unanswered questions rather than a vacuum**, and
`templates/true-copy-demonstration-record-template.md` is the instrument on which each of the three would
be answered.

## 3. The ALCOA attribute, and why it is a different instrument

Phase 03 assessed five attributes against every record-type-and-system pair in scope, and one of them is
*original or a true copy*. **Its wording is FDA's, and it comes from a guidance, not from `21 CFR 211.180`.**

**FDA's sentence, from the December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity questions and answers, verbatim:**

> *"For the purposes of this guidance, data integrity refers to the completeness, consistency, and accuracy
> of data. Complete, consistent, and accurate data should be attributable, legible, contemporaneously
> recorded, original or a true copy, and accurate (ALCOA)."*

**Note the first five words, because they are the whole of the distinction this section is about.** *For
the purposes of this guidance.* **FDA's definition is scoped to a nonbinding document by its own opening
clause**, and a repository that quoted it without them would have made FDA's guidance sound like a
regulation — which is the error the table below refuses in the other direction.

**"Original or a true copy", not "original" — FDA's own qualifier, which this repository never drops.**

**And FDA itself maps the attribute to the predicate rules, in footnote 5 of the same guidance, verbatim in
the relevant part:**

> *"…for original or a true copy, see §§ 211.180 and 211.194(a)…"*

**So the two instruments are related by FDA's own hand, and they are still not the same provision.** The
distinction is worth stating precisely, because collapsing it is one of the commoner errors in this field
and because Phase 03 built a register on the attribute while this phase reads the regulation.

| | **The ALCOA attribute** | **`21 CFR 211.180(d)`** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | A data integrity characteristic, in a **nonbinding** FDA guidance | A **binding** provision of the drug CGMP regulations |
| **What it does** | Describes a property that complete, consistent and accurate data **should** have | **Permits** retention in a form other than the original, **conditioned on the copy being true** |
| **Whose it is** | FDA's, in guidance — and the attribute set is FDA's plain five | The Code of Federal Regulations' |
| **How Helix uses it** | As an assessment dimension — `../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/logs/attribute-determination-register.md` | As the provision a retained copy relies on |
| **What failing it means** | That an attribute could not be established, or was established as not met, for a pair. **A gap, and not a legal conclusion** | Nothing, on its own. **A permission is not failed; it is either available or not relied on** |

**They point at the same underlying question and they are not the same provision, and this repository never
conflates them.** The underlying question is the one both are circling: *which thing is the record, and can
what Helix holds be shown to correspond to it?* **The attribute asks it as a property of data. The
regulation answers it as a permission with a condition.** A document that cites `21 CFR 211.180(d)` as
authority for an ALCOA attribute, or an ALCOA determination as evidence that `21 CFR 211.180(d)` is
satisfied, has crossed a line this programme has held since Phase 03. **And ALCOA is never mapped onto
`21 CFR 11.10` at all** — FDA's footnote 5 maps the attributes to the CGMP predicate rules and cites
Part 11 nowhere, `ADR-0019`.

## 4. The original nobody has named

**Helix has never recorded, for any record type, which instance of the record it relies on as the
original.**

That is not a new finding of this phase. **Phase 03 recorded it as the missing evidence behind the
*original or a true copy* attribute across a substantial part of the estate**, and named among the routes
by which the attribute fails **the same record held in several places with no rule saying which one Helix
relies on.** The register carries it per pair and this chapter restates none of it.

**What Phase 07 adds is the consequence for the regulation: `21 CFR 211.180(d)` cannot operate without
it.**

The paragraph permits retention of a true copy — *an accurate reproduction of the original records*.
**Every term in the condition is relational, and the relation has a term Helix has not fixed.** A copy
cannot be shown true against an original nobody has named. Not because the demonstration would be
difficult, and not because the evidence is missing: **because the question *is this a true copy?* has no
determinate content until somebody says what it is a copy of.**

**Three practical readings of that, stated so that the point is not left abstract.**

**Where a record exists in one place only, the question is trivial and nobody needs `(d)`.** The instance is
the original and is retained as such.

**Where a record exists in several places, one of them is the original and the others are copies — and
which is which is a fact somebody has to state.** It is not discoverable from the systems. Two identical
files on two platforms are two files; **nothing in either of them says which one the firm relies on**, and
the answer is a determination, not an observation.

**And where the retained instance is a rendering, a conversion or an export rather than a byte-for-byte
duplicate, the condition does real work.** *Accurate reproduction of the original records* is a standard
that a rendering can fail — which is the same territory Phase 02 mapped when it found that a static
rendering of a record the user interacts with is not the record.

**So the position this chapter records, once and plainly: Helix relies on `21 CFR 211.180(d)`'s permission
across its estate, has never identified the original that any retained copy is a copy of, and has therefore
never been in a position to demonstrate that any of its copies is true.** **What follows from that is not
this chapter's** — `07.03-the-derivation.md`, `07.05-the-retention-estate.md` and
`07.10-the-paper-question.md` carry the estate facts, and **the decision that would name an original for
each record type is owed and is not taken here.**

## 5. The numeral coincidence, named and disarmed

**This section names two numerals that are equal and unrelated.** It is written the way it is written
because the alternative — printing them near each other and hoping — is how a reviewer finds a defect that
was avoidable.

**The first. Group A of this phase's retention derivation is 75 pairs.** The figure is
`07.03-the-derivation.md`'s and `logs/retention-derivation-register.md`'s, it is stated here only in
order to be named, and **it is not a result of this chapter.**

**What Group A is a population of.** It is a set of record-type-and-system pairs — the same unit Phase 03
used — whose record type's retention period is set by `21 CFR 211.180(a)` because the record is
specifically associated with a batch. **Membership is decided by one question and one only: does that
paragraph's period reach this record type?** `07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md` argues the
question and `07.03-the-derivation.md` reports the answer.

**Group A therefore has nothing to do with the condition of any record.** A pair is in Group A because of
what its record type is *about*. **It is not in Group A because its data is good, or bad, or assessed, or
unassessed.** A pair whose every attribute is met and a pair about which nothing could be established sit
in Group A on exactly the same footing, provided the record is specifically associated with a batch. **The
partition is a fact about regulations and record subjects, and about nothing else.**

**And the reason a group of record types produces a count of pairs at all is Phase 03's unit, inherited.**
A record type held on several systems is several pairs, so a partition of record types partitions the pairs
with it. **That inheritance is the only structural connection between this phase's derivation and Phase 03's
assessment, and it is a connection about the unit and not about any determination made with it.**

**The second, and it belongs to a different phase and a different question. Phase 03 found the attribute
*original or a true copy* failing or undeterminable on 75 of the 121 pairs.** That figure is
`../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/logs/attribute-determination-register.md`'s
and `../03-data-integrity-risk-assessment-and-the-alcoa-attributes/03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md`'s,
it is carried as `IS-15`, and **it is not a result of this phase at all.**

**Membership of that population is decided by a completely different question: whether one of five
attributes could be established for a pair, and if so with what determination.** It is about the state of
the evidence Helix holds. **It has no relationship whatever to which paragraph of `21 CFR 211.180` supplies
the record's retention period.**

**The two numerals are equal by accident.** They are counts over the same 121 pairs, **which is the only
thing that makes them comparable at all**, and they are produced by two unrelated membership tests asked of
that population — one about what a record is *about*, the other about what can be *established* about it.
**Neither test is any part of the other**: a pair enters Group A on the subject of its record type and
nothing else, and it enters Phase 03's population on the state of the evidence Helix holds and nothing
else. **Neither set is a subset of the other, neither is the complement of the other, and no arithmetic in
this repository relates them.** **The coincidence is arithmetical and not structural**, and an equality
between two counts drawn from one population is exactly the kind of thing that happens without meaning
anything.

**And that last paragraph is a check that was made rather than an assertion.** The two sets were compared
once, to establish the shape of the relation and for no other purpose: **they overlap, neither contains the
other, neither excludes the other, and the extent of the overlap is what two independent membership tests
applied to one population of 121 would be expected to produce.** **The comparison is not published and is
not repeated**, because the size of the overlap is a figure that would itself invite the reading this
section exists to prevent — **and that single check is the whole of the comparison this repository makes
between the two.**

**What follows for every document in this repository, stated as a rule.**

- **No document in this phase joins them.** Not in a table, not in a sentence, not in adjacent paragraphs,
  not in a diagram and not in a governance record.
- **No register cross-references them.** `logs/retention-derivation-register.md` carries no attribute
  column and Phase 03's register carries no group column, deliberately.
- **Neither is a subset, superset, restatement or explanation of the other**, and **no reader should treat
  the equality as evidence of anything.**
- **Neither figure is a result of this chapter.** Each is cited to its owner above.

**The programme has been here before, and that is why the section exists.** Phase 05 published two
partitions of nineteen systems whose numerals coincided by accident — twelve and seven, in both orders —
and the risk of their being read as disjoint was caught at that phase's review rather than by a reader
afterwards. **Phase 05's rule is that two partitions whose numerals coincide are never printed as though
they were disjoint**, and `05.07 §5` states it in terms: *"…the equality of the numerals is a coincidence
and not a relation."* **This phase names its own coincidence
in advance rather than waiting to be told about it**, which is the same discipline applied one step
earlier.

## 6. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no result of this phase.** Both figures at `§5` are cited to the chapters and registers that
  own them, and **nothing in this chapter may be cited for a count.**
- **It does not join the two populations at `§5`, and nothing in it supports any relationship between
  them.**
- **It states no retention period, sets none, proposes none and bounds none** — `ADR-0050`.
- **It states no retention policy, no retention schedule, no archive design, no migration plan, no storage
  specification and no vendor.**
- **It identifies no original for any record type**, and it makes no determination about which instance of
  any record Helix relies on. **That decision is owed and is not taken here.**
- **It demonstrates no copy to be true, and it records no reproduction as having been made** under
  `21 CFR 211.180(d)` — `07.10-the-paper-question.md`, `logs/paper-record-register.md`.
- **It re-scores no Phase 03 pair.** The *original or a true copy* determinations stand exactly as
  published, and **an undeterminable attribute is a gap and not a low score** — `ADR-0018`.
- **It re-determines no Phase 05 control position and re-bands no Phase 04 obligation** — `ADR-0049`.
- **It re-opens no Phase 02 applicability determination.** **The printout route holds for the four record
  types Phase 02 published as out of scope** — `ADR-0046`.
- **It maps no ALCOA attribute onto `21 CFR 11.10`**, in prose or in a register column. **FDA's footnote 5
  maps the attributes to the CGMP predicate rules and cites Part 11 nowhere** — `ADR-0019`.
- **It states no validation outcome**, raises no CAPA and no deviation, and states no remediation plan,
  cost, sequence or priority.
- **It disposes of neither `OBS-2` nor `OBS-4`** — Phase 08 — and **characterises no reintegration**,
  `ADR-0043`.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind** and forecasts no inspection outcome.
- **Nothing here bears on `IS-01`**, which stays open and stays unweighted, and **no candidate explanation
  of it is promoted by statement, by ordering or by tone** — `ADR-0012`.
