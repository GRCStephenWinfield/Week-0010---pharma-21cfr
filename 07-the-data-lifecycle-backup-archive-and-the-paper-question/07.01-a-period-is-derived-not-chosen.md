## 1. What this chapter settles

**This chapter fixes the method by which every retention period in this phase was arrived at, and it does
so before any of them is reported.**

The method is one sentence and the rest of the chapter is the working: **where a predicate rule sets the
period, the period is not Helix's to choose and Helix's only job is to read it correctly; where no
predicate rule sets one, the period is Helix's to determine, and a determination needs a named individual,
a stated basis and a record.** `ADR-0044` records it and `§10` sets it out.

**It states no result.** What the derivation produced is `07.03-the-derivation.md`'s and
`logs/retention-derivation-register.md`'s; what follows for the record types no predicate rule reaches is
`07.04-what-nothing-sets.md`'s. **No count appears in this chapter.** The reading of `21 CFR 211.180`
that the derivation applies is `07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md`'s and is cited here
rather than repeated.

**And it settles a boundary that matters more in this phase than in any before it.** A phase about
retention is under continuous pressure to produce a retention schedule, because a schedule is what everyone
in the room actually wants and because the distance between *these are the periods the regulation sets* and
*this is how long we keep things* looks like a short step. **It is not a short step. It is a different
document, taken by a different authority, and this phase does not take it** — `ADR-0050`, argued at `§11`.

## 2. How most retention schedules are actually written

**A retention schedule at a firm of Helix's size is usually a document that was reverse-engineered from
practice, and the practice was set by circumstances nobody has revisited.**

The mechanism is not disreputable and nobody in it acts badly. It runs roughly like this. At some point in
the firm's history, a decision was needed about how long to keep something — because a storeroom filled up,
because a system was being replaced, because a contract was being signed, because somebody asked. **A
number was produced, by somebody sensible, under time pressure, from a mixture of what the storage
arrangement could support, what a colleague at another firm had said, and a general sense of what was
prudent.** The number went into a procedure. The procedure was approved.

**Years later, the number is the position.** It is quoted to auditors. It is quoted to inspectors. It is
built into system configurations, contracts with storage providers and the parameters of an archive. And if
anybody asks where it came from, the honest answer — which is almost never given, because almost nobody
still knows it — is *from a decision taken in 2011 by somebody who has since left, about a storeroom that
no longer exists.*

**Three things follow from that history, and each of them is a defect a firm cannot see from inside.**

**The schedule describes the arrangement rather than the obligation.** The number in the procedure is a
description of what the firm's storage happens to support. If the storage contract had specified a
different term, the number would be different, and the regulation would be exactly the same. **A document
that would have said something different had a commercial arrangement been different is not a statement of
what the law requires.**

**The schedule cannot be wrong, which sounds like a strength and is the whole problem.** If a period was
chosen rather than derived, there is nothing to check it against. An auditor can confirm that the firm
follows its schedule. **Nobody can confirm that the schedule is right, because *right* has no meaning for a
number with no basis.** Phase 04 recorded the general form of this: **a procedure that exists and does not
require the act is worse than a procedure that does not exist, because the gap is invisible.** A schedule
with an unfounded number in it is the same shape — the column is full, so nobody looks.

**And the schedule is fragile in exactly the direction that hurts.** Because it has no basis, it cannot be
defended when it comes under pressure. A migration, a decommissioning, a cost programme or a system change
proposes a shorter period; **there is no derivation to consult about whether the shorter period is lawful
for any particular record**, so the argument is settled by whoever is most senior in the meeting. **A
position with a basis can lose that argument on the merits. A position without one loses it on seniority.**

**This is backwards, and the reversal is the point of the chapter.** **A retention schedule is a
consequence of a retention position, and a retention position is a consequence of reading the predicate
rules.** A firm that writes the schedule first and calls it the position has arranged the three documents
in the wrong order and will not discover it until somebody asks the one question the arrangement cannot
answer: **why this period?**

## 3. Where a predicate rule sets the period, the period is not Helix's to choose

**This is the half of the method that is usually got wrong in the more damaging direction, because getting
it wrong looks like diligence.**

Where a provision of `21 CFR Part 211` sets a period for a class of record, **the period is a fact about
the regulation.** Helix's task is to read the provision, identify the class it reaches, identify the event
its clock runs from, and record the period as it falls out. **There is nothing here to decide.** A firm that
convenes a meeting to determine how long it will retain a record whose period a regulation sets has
convened a meeting about a question that has already been answered by somebody with the authority to answer
it.

**And a schedule that departs from a period a predicate rule sets is not a statement of policy at all.**
That sentence is the reason this half of the method has to be stated as sharply as the other. A retention
schedule specifying a shorter period than the regulation requires does not represent a different corporate
view of the appropriate retention interval; **it is a controlled document that misstates a federal
regulation and instructs somebody to act on the misstatement.** **The document's existence does not soften
it; the document's existence is the aggravating feature**, because it converts what might have been an
error into a stated intention. **Whether any such schedule would be characterised as anything by anybody is
not this repository's to say, and no document in it reaches a legal conclusion of any kind.**

**Three properties of a regulation-set period, each of which changes what a firm has to do with it.**

**It is a floor and not a ceiling.** The periods `21 CFR 211.180(a)` and `(b)` set are expressed as *at
least*. **Retaining longer is not a departure**, and a firm that keeps records beyond the required period
has not departed from the provision. **What it may not do is record the longer period as though the regulation
required it** — that is a misdescription of a federal regulation in a controlled document, and
`07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md §10` argues why that matters.

**It counts from an event, not from a date of creation.** The provisions that set periods in this area
express them by reference to an event — the expiration date of a batch, the expiration date of the drug
product incorporating a component, the receipt of a complaint. **A period expressed as a number of years
from creation is a different rule**, and a schedule that has silently made that substitution will
under-retain for every long-dated product and over-retain for every short-dated one, without anybody being
able to see which.

**And it is a property of the record class, not of the system.** The period belongs to the record because
of what the record is. **It does not belong to the platform the record happens to sit on**, and it does not
change when the record is copied to a second platform. A firm whose retention rules are expressed per
system has expressed them against the wrong object, and Phase 02 fixed the right object three phases ago:
**the record type is the unit of determination** — `ADR-0013`.

## 4. Where no predicate rule sets a period, the determination *is* Helix's to take

**And this is the half that is usually got wrong in the quieter direction, because getting it wrong looks
like nothing at all.**

A great many records a drug manufacturer is required to keep have no period set anywhere. The record is
required — some provision requires it to exist and to be maintained — **and no provision says for how
long.** `07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md §9` records that the section which sets the drug
CGMP retention periods contains, within itself, a required record for which it sets none.

**Where that is the position, the firm must determine a period, and the determination is a real decision.**
It is not a reading. There is no correct answer sitting in a regulation waiting to be found. **Somebody at
Helix has to decide, on a stated basis, and be identified as having decided.**

**The failure mode here is not a wrong number. It is no number**, and it is very hard to see, because
nothing draws attention to it.

**An unchosen period is not a short period. It is no period.** That is the sentence this section exists
for. When nobody has determined how long a record is kept, the record is not on a short retention; it is on
no retention at all, and its actual survival is a function of whatever the storage arrangement happens to
do — a platform default, a licence term, a disk that fills, a system that is retired, a supplier that
changes its terms. **The record's fate is being decided, continuously, by a series of arrangements that
were never intended to decide it and that nobody reviews as though they did.**

**Three consequences follow, and they are not equivalent.**

**The record may survive far longer than anybody needs.** This is the benign outcome, and it is the one
that lets the problem persist for years. Storage is cheap, nothing is deleted, and the absence of a
decision produces no visible harm.

**Or the record may be destroyed on a schedule nobody set.** A platform retires data at a default interval.
A contract lapses. A system is decommissioned and the migration takes what the migration was scoped to
take. **No decision was taken to destroy the record, and the record is gone**, and the absence of a
decision is precisely why nobody noticed in time to object.

**Or — and this is the one this programme is most concerned with — some downstream obligation that depends
on the period cannot be evaluated at all.** `21 CFR 11.10(c)` requires protection enabling accurate and
ready retrieval *"throughout the records retention period"*. `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires audit trail
documentation to be retained *"for a period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic
records"*. **Both are expressed in terms of a period.** Where the period does not exist, **neither
obligation has a stated endpoint, and a determination that either is met has been made against nothing.**
`07.08-the-endpoint-that-does-not-exist.md` and `07.09-a-relation-with-two-terms.md` carry what that
means on Helix's estate; this chapter records only that **the missing period is not an isolated gap — it is
the second term in two other obligations.**

**So the rule for this half of the method: where no predicate rule sets a period, the derivation records
that fact, records that a decision is owed, and names the individual who owes it.** It does not supply a
period. **A decision owed by a named individual is a thing that can be chased. A number entered on that
individual's behalf is a thing nobody will ever look at again.**

## 5. What a derivation requires

**A derivation is a record with five elements and it is not complete with four.** Each element exists
because a derivation missing it cannot be checked by somebody who was not in the room.

| # | Element | Why it is in the record |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The record type**, as Phase 02's register carries it | The unit of determination is the record type — `ADR-0013`. **A derivation performed against a system, a folder, a report or a document class has been performed against the wrong object** |
| **2** | **Its predicate rule, as Phase 02 published it** | The provision that made the record a required record. **Not re-determined here** — Phase 02's anchors are taken as published, and a derivation that quietly re-anchors a record type has re-opened an applicability determination it has no authority to touch |
| **3** | **The provision that sets the period, or the word *none*** | **The load-bearing cell.** The provision actually relied on, cited exactly, or an express statement that no predicate rule supplies one |
| **4** | **The period as it falls out**, or the statement that no predicate rule sets one | The consequence of reading element 3. **Expressed as the regulation expresses it — with its triggering event — and not converted to a bare number of years** |
| **5** | **The systems that hold it** | Because the record type lives somewhere, and a period nobody can attach to a holding is a period nobody can apply. **The systems come from Phase 02's register and are not re-determined** |

**Element 3 is where a derivation is honest or is not.** It is the cell an incomplete method fills with
whatever citation is nearest to hand. **The rule this phase applies is that the cell carries the provision
actually relied on or it carries *none*, and nothing else** — and the reason is one this programme has
stated since Phase 01 in another context: *a response column completed for the sake of completion is worse
than an empty one, because it stops anybody looking.*

**Element 4's insistence on the triggering event is not pedantry.** A period recorded as *at least one year
after the expiration date of the batch* can be applied to a record by somebody who has the batch in front
of them. **A period recorded as *three years* cannot be applied at all**, because nothing says three years
from what. **A period without its trigger is not a period; it is a duration**, and the difference is the
difference between a rule and a number.

**Element 5 is what makes the derivation usable by the rest of the phase.** A record type's period matters
operationally only where the record is held, and a record type held in several places carries the same
period in all of them. **The period does not change because a copy moved to a different platform**, and one
of the ways firms lose records is by allowing a platform's own retention setting to become the effective
rule for whatever lands on it.

**And a sixth thing, which is not a column but is required of every row: the group the row falls in.**
Group A, Group B and Group C are labels for the three positions a record type can occupy — a period set by
`21 CFR 211.180(a)`, a period set by another predicate rule, and no period set by any. **They are labels
and not scores, they carry no ordering and no severity, and nothing in this programme reads them as a
ranking.** Which record types carry which label is `07.03-the-derivation.md`'s and
`logs/retention-derivation-register.md`'s, and **the partitions must be recomputable from the register's
rows and from nothing else.**

## 6. The hard part, stated honestly

**A derivation is a reading of a regulation against a record type, and competent people can read it
differently.**

That sentence has to be in this chapter, prominently, because everything above it has the tone of an
arithmetic exercise and the work is not one. **The provisions are short, general and old. The record types
are specific, modern and in several cases electronic in ways nobody drafting Part 211 contemplated.
Applying the first to the second is an act of judgement, and a phase that presented it as a lookup would be
misrepresenting its own method.**

**Three places where the judgement is real, offered as classes of difficulty rather than as instances.**

**Whether a record's subject is a batch.** `21 CFR 211.180(a)` reaches records specifically associated with
a batch. Some records are obviously within that and some are obviously outside it. **Some are genuinely
arguable** — a record generated during the manufacture of one batch but maintained as a continuous log
across many; a record about a piece of equipment that carries batch identifiers in its rows; a laboratory
record whose subject is a method but whose data came from batch samples. **Two competent quality
professionals can read the same record type and reach different answers, and neither is being careless.**

**Which provision is the record's predicate rule for retention purposes.** A record type may be anchored on
one provision for its content and touched by another for its retention. Phase 02 re-anchored several record
types at its own review, because the provisions originally cited required acts or written procedures rather
than records. **A derivation inherits those anchors, and the inheritance is itself a judgement about which
published anchor governs the retention question.**

**And whether a provision that mentions records sets a period at all.** Some provisions require a record to
be maintained without saying for how long; some express a duration in terms of use rather than time.
**Reading a maintenance obligation as a retention period is a step, and it is a step that can be taken too
readily.**

**What Helix does about it is the only thing that can be done about it: record who read it and how.**

**Every derivation carries the individual who made it and the basis they relied on.** Not a function, not a
committee, not *Quality*. **A named person, because the regulation's own unit of accountability is a
person** and because a reading nobody is attached to is a reading nobody can be asked about in two years.

**The basis is recorded in terms specific enough to be disagreed with.** *The record's subject is the
equipment across a sequence of uses rather than any identified batch* is a basis. *Not batch-specific* is
not — it is the conclusion with the reasoning removed.

**And the derivation is a determination, which means it can be re-taken.** Phase 02's determination review
trigger — `ADR-0015`, six events with an annual floor — exists because *a determination nobody maintains is
a determination that was true once.* **A retention derivation is a determination of exactly that kind**,
and a change to a record type's form, to the provisions it rests on, or to the systems that hold it is a
reason to read it again.

**The alternative to recording the judgement is not avoiding the judgement.** It is making the same
judgement and concealing it. **A schedule that prints a period with no derivation behind it has not escaped
the difficulty; it has hidden it, and hidden it from the only people who could correct it.**

## 7. This phase derives, and it does not decide

**The boundary between the two halves of the method is also the boundary of this phase's authority, and
they are not the same act.**

**Reading a regulation is not an exercise of authority.** When a predicate rule sets a period, recording
that period is a statement of fact about the regulation. Anybody competent can make it, anybody competent
can check it, and being wrong about it is being wrong about something external and verifiable. **A
programme may do that work and publish the result.**

**Determining a period where none is set is an exercise of authority, and it is not this programme's.** It
is a decision about how long Helix will keep its own records — a decision with cost consequences, storage
consequences, contractual consequences and quality consequences. **It belongs to the individuals who are
accountable for the records, taken through the quality system, recorded as a decision with a name and a
date.** A data integrity programme that took it on their behalf would be doing exactly what
`01.07 §6` forbids in a different context: **the determiner does not operate.**

**So the position this phase takes, and it is `ADR-0050`:**

| Where a predicate rule sets the period | Where no predicate rule sets one |
|---|---|
| **Phase 07 reads it and records it.** The provision, the trigger event, the period as it falls out | **Phase 07 records that no provision sets one, that a decision is owed, and who owes it** |
| The record is a statement about the regulation | The record is a statement about an outstanding decision |
| **Nothing is chosen** | **Nothing is chosen here either** |
| It can be checked against the text | It can be chased against a name and a date |

**And the refusals that go with it, stated so that no later reader has to infer them.** For a record type
no predicate rule reaches, **this phase proposes no period, suggests no period, states no range, states no
bound, and gives no indication of what a reasonable answer might look like.** Not in a chapter, not in a
register cell, not in a diagram, not in a governance record and not in a worked example on a template.

**The reason is not squeamishness and it is worth stating.** A number proposed by a programme document —
even hedged, even labelled illustrative, even in a worked example — **becomes the anchor for the decision
it was meant to leave open.** Somebody will read it, and the decision that was owed will be taken by
ratifying a suggestion instead of by being made. **The way to leave a decision genuinely open is to leave
the space genuinely empty**, and the way to make it get taken is to attach a name and a date to the
emptiness. `07.04-what-nothing-sets.md` carries the names.

## 8. What a derivation is not

Four misreadings, each foreseeable, each refused here rather than in a footnote.

**It is not a retention schedule.** A schedule is an operational instrument: it tells somebody, at the
point of disposal, what to do with a particular record on a particular date. **A derivation tells you what
the regulation requires of a class.** The distance between them is filled with things this phase does not
supply — a disposal process, a hold mechanism for records under investigation or litigation, an approval
route, an exception process, a records inventory at instance level and an owner for each of them.

**It is not a policy.** A policy states what the organisation will do, is approved by the authority
competent to state it, and becomes enforceable against the organisation once it exists — through
`21 CFR 211.22(d)` where it is the quality control unit's own procedure, and through `21 CFR 211.100(b)` or
`21 CFR 211.160(a)` where it is owned elsewhere, **the provision that bites depending on which procedure it
is**.
**A derivation states what the regulation requires.** The first is Helix's and the second is not, and
`ADR-0050` keeps them apart.

**It is not an archive design.** Nothing in this phase specifies a storage medium, a format, a migration
route, a fixity mechanism, a supplier, a location or a technology. **A derivation says how long. It says
nothing whatever about how.**

**And it is not a disposal authority.** No document in this phase authorises the destruction of anything.
**A derived period is a statement of a minimum**, and a firm that read a derivation as permission to
destroy a record on the day the minimum expired would have converted a floor into a deadline. **Nothing in
this phase permits, requires or schedules the destruction of any record.**

## 9. The alternatives, argued fairly

Three method alternatives were available and each was refused. **Each is what a substantial part of the
industry does**, and the refusals are recorded with the case for each stated first.

### 9.1 Write the schedule first and reconcile it to the regulations afterwards

**The pragmatic proposal, and it has a genuine advantage: it produces a usable operational document
quickly**, which is what the business needs, and it can be reconciled to the regulations as a second pass
once the immediate need is met. **A firm with no schedule at all is in a worse operational position than a
firm with an unfounded one**, and that is a fair point.

**Refused, and the reason is what happens to the second pass.** A schedule in force acquires dependants
within weeks — system configurations, contracts, procedures, training. **By the time the reconciliation
runs, changing a period costs real money, and the reconciliation quietly becomes an exercise in justifying
the numbers already in force rather than deriving the numbers that are correct.** The programme has watched
the same mechanism in another form: *a company whose own audits looked at it and passed it has a different
problem.* **An analysis performed after the answer is in place is an analysis with an interest in the
answer.**

### 9.2 Adopt a single conservative period for everything

**The strongest of the three, and it is argued at length in
`07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md §10` because that is where the regulation-reading question
belongs.** In summary: it cannot under-retain, it is administratively simple, it removes discretion from
the point of disposal, and storage is cheap.

**Refused there, on the ground that a period applied by analogy has no basis to be tested against and
conceals precisely the records for which a decision is owed.** It is not re-argued here. **What this
chapter adds is the method consequence: a uniform period would have made the derivation unnecessary, and
the derivation is the only artefact in this phase that can be checked against a regulation.**

### 9.3 Have the programme determine the missing periods itself

**The most tempting, because it would have produced a complete document** — every record type with a
period, no gaps, no owed decisions, and a phase that finished its own work rather than handing part of it
on. **And the programme is better placed than anybody to do it**: it has read the predicate rules, it holds
the record-type register, it knows which systems hold what. **A reasonable person would say that leaving a
gap the programme could have filled is a failure of nerve dressed as discipline.**

**Refused on three grounds and the first is sufficient.**

**The programme has no authority to decide it.** How long Helix keeps its own records is the accountable
owners' decision, taken through the quality system. **A programme that took it would have produced a period
that is not binding on anybody**, since nothing in the quality system would require it, and Phase 05
recorded exactly what that is worth: **a control nobody requires is a control that can be turned off
tomorrow without a deviation.** A period nobody is required to apply is the same object.

**A number proposed is a decision pre-empted.** Even labelled as illustrative, even hedged, a figure in a
programme document becomes the anchor. **The decision that was owed gets taken by ratification, and the
basis nobody wrote is the basis nobody ever writes.**

**And the gap is the finding.** That some of Helix's required records have a retention period set by
nothing, and that nobody has ever decided one, **is a fact about the firm that a completed schedule would
have concealed.** Filling it in would have converted the phase's most useful output into a table that looks
like everybody else's.

**The cost of the refusal, accepted openly.** The phase hands forward a set of decisions rather than a set
of answers, the derivation register carries rows whose period cell says the period is set by no predicate
rule, and Helix ends this phase still unable to state how long it keeps some of its records. **All three
are true, all three are uncomfortable, and all three are more useful than the alternative.**

## 10. `ADR-0044` — a retention period is derived from the predicate rule, never chosen

**`ADR-0044`, taken at `DEC-703` on 2027-03-02 by Terrence Abbatiello, records that a retention period is
derived from the predicate rule and never chosen; that where a predicate rule sets the period Helix reads
it; and that where none does, a named individual must decide and the decision is recorded as a decision.**

Four limbs.

**Every period in this phase carries the provision it comes from.** A derivation row cites the provision
actually relied on, exactly, or it records that no predicate rule supplies one. **No row carries a citation
the period does not rest on**, and no row carries a period whose provision is unstated.

**A period is expressed with its triggering event, in the terms the regulation uses.** Not as a bare
duration. **A period without its trigger cannot be applied to a record**, and a schedule of bare durations
is a schedule nobody can execute correctly.

**Where nothing sets a period, the derivation records that, records that a decision is owed, and names the
individual who owes it.** It supplies no period, no default, no range, no bound and no analogy —
`ADR-0045`, `ADR-0050`.

**And every derivation carries the individual who made the reading and the basis they relied on**, in terms
specific enough for somebody else to disagree with. **The determination is subject to `ADR-0015`'s review
trigger**, because *a determination nobody maintains is a determination that was true once.*

## 11. `ADR-0050` — Phase 07 states no retention policy and no archive design

**`ADR-0050`, taken at `DEC-717` on 2027-03-18 by Dr Marguerite Oyelaran, records that this phase
establishes what the regulation requires and what Helix holds, and stops there.**

**It is the companion to `ADR-0044` and it is the limb that will be tested hardest**, because a phase that
has read every applicable retention provision and enumerated every system that holds a record is one short
paragraph away from writing the schedule, and everybody who reads it will want the paragraph.

**What the decision forbids is set out in the record itself.** In summary, and stated here because a reader
of this chapter should not have to open another file to learn what the method excludes: **no retention
policy, no retention schedule, no archive design, no migration plan, no storage specification and no
vendor; no period set, proposed, ranged or bounded for any record type no predicate rule reaches; and no
disposal authority for anything.**

**And the reason the boundary sits exactly here.** Everything on the permitted side of it is a statement
about a regulation or about a fact of Helix's estate — checkable, falsifiable, and correctable by anybody
who can read. **Everything on the forbidden side is an exercise of Helix's own authority over its own
records, which belongs to the accountable owners and to the quality unit, and which no programme document
can confer on itself by publishing it.**
