# 07.09 — A Relation With Two Terms

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Document ID | `HLX-DI-2027-709` |
| Version | 1.0 |
| Date | 2027-03-19 |
| Owner | Joachim Reuter — Head of Automation and Manufacturing IT |
| Approver | Priyanka Venkataraman — Data Integrity Lead |
| Classification | Confidential — Helix Pharma, Inc. Data Integrity Programme // Illustrative Portfolio Sample |

*`21 CFR 11.10(e)` does not say how long an audit trail is kept. It says how long it is kept relative to something else — and on sixteen systems the something else does not exist.*

---

## 1. The sentence, quoted whole

**`21 CFR 11.10(e)`'s third sentence:**

> *"Such audit trail documentation shall be retained for a period at least as long as that required for
> the subject electronic records and shall be available for agency review and copying."*

**It is a *shall*, twice.** The clauses had been presented in earlier phases in wording the regulation does
not use, and the correction is binding: **shall be retained**, and **shall be available for agency review
and copying.**

**And the first *shall* is a relation and not a period.** It fixes no number of months and no number of
years. It says the audit trail documentation is retained **at least as long as that required for the
subject electronic records** — so the requirement has two terms, and **Part 11 supplies neither of them.**
The first term is what Helix has configured. **The second term is the record's retention period, and that
comes from the predicate rule.**

**`21 CFR Part 11` sets no retention period of its own**, and no statement in this chapter, this phase or
this repository says otherwise.

## 2. `IS-05`, and why it could not be answered for five years of programme time

**`IS-05`** was raised on 2026-05-01 by Joachim Reuter: **Helix cannot state, for any system, how long
audit trail data is retained relative to the retention period of the underlying record.**

It survived six phases. Phase 03 could not answer it because it assessed attributes on pairs and not
configurations on systems. Phase 04 could not answer it because it wrote a framework and executed nothing.
**Phase 05 came closest and stopped**: it determined `21 CFR 11.10(e)` on all 61 systems and recorded at
`AS-37` that it was doing so against a period nobody had set. Phase 06 read two audit trail configurations
at depth and recorded, in its evidence index `§0`, that **the evidence did not permit `IS-05` to be
answered because no retention period existed for any of the five record types**, so the relation had no
second term and the question went to Phase 07 inside `IS-42`.

**Every one of those was right about the obstacle and one of them was wrong about its nature.** The second
term for `RT-12`, `RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15` and `RT-17` **existed in the regulation the whole time** —
`21 CFR 211.180(a)`, at least 1 year after the expiration date of the batch — and nobody at Helix had read
it. That is `07.04-what-nothing-sets.md`'s split, and it is why `IS-05` can close here at all.

## 3. What the configuration read found

**The audit trail retention configuration was read across all 61 systems on 2027-03-10** — `EV-715`,
recorded at `DEC-716` — and the working paper that sets it against the derived periods is `EV-730`.

| Cut | Value |
|---|---|
| Systems on which audit trail retention is configured as **a fixed window** | **26** |
| Of those 26, systems where **the window is shorter than the longest derived period of a record type the system holds** | **11** |
| Systems on which **every in-scope record type held falls in the group no predicate rule sets a period for**, so the relation has **no second term at all** | **16** |

**On 26 systems the audit trail is kept for a fixed length of time.** The window is a setting in the
product, expressed in the product's own units, and **no procedure in Helix's quality system requires any
of them to be what it is** — which is `IS-29`'s finding seen in a new place: **a control that is present
and required by nothing can be changed without breaching anything, and nothing would record the change.**

**On 11 of the 26 the window is shorter than the longest period the regulation sets for a record type the
system holds.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires the audit trail documentation to be retained **at least as long
as** the subject electronic records. **Where the second term is a period running from the expiration date
of a batch and the first term is a fixed window, the comparison is now statable and on eleven systems it
comes out one way.** **This chapter states the comparison and states no window length as a requirement**,
because Part 11 sets none and Helix has set none.

**And on 16 systems the relation has no second term at all.** Those are the sixteen systems that hold only
record types no predicate rule sets a period for — the same sixteen `07.08-the-endpoint-that-does-not-exist.md`
identifies, computed at `logs/retention-derivation-register.md §3.3`. **On a system holding only such
record types, *at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records* has nothing on the
right-hand side.** The obligation is not met and it is not unmet; **it is not yet a comparison.**

## 4. The three positions are three different things

**A reader who takes 26, 11 and 16 as three degrees of the same problem will have misread all three.**

**The 11 is a comparison that comes out one way**, and it is the only one of the three that is a statement
about a configuration being what it is. Both terms exist and they are in a stated relation to each other.
**Whether Helix does anything about it is not this phase's to say**, and no remediation, CAPA or
configuration change is stated or implied.

**The 16 is not a comparison at all.** Nothing about those systems' configurations is criticised, because
nothing about them can be assessed against `21 CFR 11.10(e)`'s retention limb until a period exists.
**A window of any length on a system whose records have no period is neither long enough nor too short.**
**The remedy is not a configuration change; it is fourteen decisions** — `IS-44`, `DP-38`.

**And the 26 is a population rather than a finding.** It is the set of systems on which the first term of
the relation exists as a fixed window; the remaining systems either retain no audit trail or retain it
without a stated limit, and **Phase 05's `(e)` determinations for all 61 stand exactly as published** —
`ADR-0049`. **Nothing in this chapter moves a `(e)` cell, and the twenty-nine systems on which `(e)` is
Not met are Not met for the reasons Phase 05 recorded and not for anything here.**

**And one coincidence is named here rather than left to be found.** Phase 05 recorded `21 CFR 11.10(e)` as
*Met but not required* on 26 of the 61 systems. **The 26 systems on which a fixed audit trail retention
window is configured are a different population, established by a different read on a different date, and
the equality of the numerals is arithmetical and carries no meaning** — `PR-52`'s discipline applied to a
second pair. **No document in this phase joins them.**

## 5. `IS-05` closes on the derivable limb

**`IS-05` asked whether Helix could state, for any system, how long audit trail data is retained relative
to the retention period of the underlying record. It can now state it for every system.**

**CLOSED 2027-03-18** — `DEC-716`, ratified at `GOV-35`. **Closure evidence:** `EV-715` the audit trail
retention configuration read across the 61 on 2027-03-10 · `EV-707` the retention derivation register,
which supplies the second term where there is one · `EV-730` the working paper · `EV-731` the closure
record · **`GOV-31` the method authorisation, `GOV-32` the derivation accepted, and `GOV-35` the
ratification.** **The closure names its evidence** — *believed complete* is not a closure condition.

**What `IS-05` never asked is what happens where the relation has one term.** It was written on the
assumption, reasonable in May 2026 and wrong in a way nobody could have known then, that every record in
scope had a retention period somewhere and that Helix simply could not state it. **For sixteen systems the
comparison is not unstatable because Helix has not looked; it is unstatable because the second term does
not exist.** That is not an answer to `IS-05` and it is the reason `IS-05` can go no further.

**The remainder is `IS-44`'s** — fourteen record types with a period set by no predicate rule, and a
decision owed by a named individual. **`IS-43` carries the parallel consequence for `21 CFR 11.10(c)`**,
where an obligation with the same missing term was determined met on thirteen systems.

**This is the second of the phase's two closures by splitting**, and the shape is the same as `IS-14`'s: an
issue written as one question turns out to have been two, and the half that closes is the half somebody
could have answered by reading.

## 6. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no retention period of any kind**, for an audit trail or for a record, and **`21 CFR Part 11`
  sets none** — `ADR-0050`.
- **It publishes no window length as a requirement, a target, a minimum or a recommendation.**
- **It re-determines no Phase 05 cell.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` on all 61 stands as published — `ADR-0049`.
- **It sets no audit trail review frequency and names none.** **No primary source names a stand-alone
  periodic audit trail review frequency**, and `IS-04` stays deliberately unresolved.
- **It states no CAPA, no deviation, no configuration change and no remediation plan.**
- **It asserts nothing about whether any audit trail entry has been lost.** No system was queried for
  historical entries and no absence is reported.
- **It says nothing about what `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires of audit trail content**, which is
  `../05-access-audit-trails-and-electronic-signatures/05.05-audit-trails.md`'s, or about what any audit
  trail on any system records, which is Phase 06's for two systems and nobody's for the other fifty-nine.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**
