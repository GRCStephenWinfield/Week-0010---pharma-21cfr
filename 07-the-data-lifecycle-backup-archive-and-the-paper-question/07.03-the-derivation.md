## 1. What this chapter reports

**This chapter reports the result of applying the derivation method to the 34 record types Phase 02
determined are Part 11 records.** What a derivation is, and why a schedule written from practice is not a
retention position, is `07.01-a-period-is-derived-not-chosen.md`'s — `ADR-0044`, `DEC-703`, Terrence
Abbatiello, 2027-03-02. The reading of `21 CFR 211.180` paragraph by paragraph, and of the operative words
in `(a)`, is `07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md`'s — `ADR-0045`, `DEC-705`, Dr Marguerite
Oyelaran, 2027-03-05. **Neither argument is restated here. This chapter states where they land.**

The register is `logs/retention-derivation-register.md`. **It is authoritative and every figure below is
computed from its rows.** The thirty-four derivation records behind it are at `EV-706`, and each of them
retains the search and not only its result: **a row reading *None* records that no provision was found by
a search that was made, and not that nobody looked.**

**One sentence about what the method was not allowed to do, because it explains the shape of everything
that follows.** **The provisions were read before the practice was described** —
`07.01-a-period-is-derived-not-chosen.md §2` owns why, and it is not restated here. **The practice appears
in this phase only where a chapter asks what would have to be true for a period to be operated**, which is
`07.05`, `07.06` and `07.07`.

## 2. The partition

**The 34 partition into three groups, and the groups are positions rather than grades.**

| Group | Basis | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | The record is a production, control or distribution record **specifically associated with a batch of a drug product**, and `21 CFR 211.180(a)` sets its period | **17** |
| **B** | A predicate-rule provision other than `21 CFR 211.180(a)` sets the period | **3** |
| **C** | **No predicate rule sets a period.** The record is required, and how long it must be kept is set by nothing | **14** |
| | **Total** | **17 + 3 + 14 = 34** |

**Group A — seventeen record types.** `RT-01`, `RT-03`, `RT-04`, `RT-06`, `RT-07`, `RT-08`, `RT-11`,
`RT-12`, `RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15`, `RT-16`, `RT-17`, `RT-20`, `RT-21`, `RT-35`, `RT-43`. The period is
**at least 1 year after the expiration date of the batch; for an exempt OTC drug product lacking
expiration dating because it meets the criteria for exemption under `21 CFR 211.137`, 3 years after
distribution of the batch.** The batch record, the dispensing record, the in-process
control results, the line clearance and labelling records, the packaging inspection record, the analytical
result, the five chromatography and dissolution record types, the stability time-point result, the
out-of-specification investigation, the distribution record and the batch disposition record. **These are
the records a batch is made of, and the regulation says how long they are kept.** **Two of the seventeen
are rows a competent reader can reach a different answer on, and the register records the basis and the
competing reading for both rather than leaving them to be found** —
`logs/retention-derivation-register.md §2.1`.

**Group B — three record types, and they are three because their periods come from elsewhere.** `RT-28`
purified water quality monitoring and release, and `RT-34` component receipt, quarantine and release
status, take their period from **`21 CFR 211.180(b)`**, which reaches records for components, drug product
containers, closures and labelling: at least 1 year after the expiration date, or — for an exempt OTC drug
product lacking expiration dating because it meets the criteria for exemption under `21 CFR 211.137` —
**3 years after distribution of the last lot of drug product incorporating the component or using the
container, closure or labelling.** **Water is a component**, on the definition at `21 CFR 210.3(b)(3)` —
*"any ingredient intended for use in the manufacture of a drug product, including those that may not appear
in such drug product"* — which is why the water monitoring record is here and not in Group A. `RT-41`, the
product complaint record, takes its period from **`21 CFR 211.198(b)`**: **at least 1 year after the
expiration date of the drug product, or 1 year after the date that the complaint was received, whichever is
longer; and for an exempt OTC drug product lacking expiration dating because it meets the criteria for
exemption under `21 CFR 211.137`, 3 years after distribution of the drug product.**
**That is a period set by a provision that is not `21 CFR 211.180` at all**, and a derivation that had
looked only at `211.180` would have put `RT-41` in Group C and been wrong.

**Group C — fourteen record types, and this is the chapter's subject.** `RT-02`, `RT-05`, `RT-18`,
`RT-19`, `RT-25`, `RT-26`, `RT-27`, `RT-32`, `RT-33`, `RT-38`, `RT-39`, `RT-40`, `RT-42`, `RT-44`. **Every
one is a required record under a predicate rule. On none of them does any predicate rule say for how
long.** `07.04-what-nothing-sets.md` is what follows from that; `logs/retention-derivation-register.md §2`
carries the reason for each of the fourteen, and **the fourteen reasons are fourteen different reasons.**

**Read the other way, the partition says something shorter and harder.** **Twenty of the thirty-four have
a retention period set by regulation. Fourteen do not. 20 + 14 = 34.**

## 3. The reconciliation to the pairs

**The unit of the retention derivation is the record type. The unit of Phase 03's assessment was the
pair — one record type on one system — and there were 121 of them.** Because the derivation register
carries Phase 02's systems column for each record type, **the partition can be recomputed at pair level
without touching Phase 03's register at all**, and it must reconcile to the same 121 or one of the two
registers is wrong.

| Group | Record types | Pairs |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | 17 | **75** |
| **B** | 3 | **6** |
| **C** | 14 | **40** |
| | **34** | **75 + 6 + 40 = 121** |

**It reconciles.** The arithmetic is at `logs/retention-derivation-register.md §3.2`, row by row, and the
working paper is `EV-708`. **No pair was added, removed, re-scored or re-assessed**, and Phase 03's

formally reach.

**A caution about the first of those three figures, and it is stated here so that nobody has to work it
out later.** **Group A's pair count is a count of pairs whose record type takes its period from
`21 CFR 211.180(a)`. It is unrelated to any figure Phase 03 published about any of the five attributes**,
and where a reader has both in mind, `07.11-original-or-a-true-copy-on-the-cgmp-side.md` is the one
chapter that holds them together and says what the relation between them is. **No chapter in this phase
other than that one prints an attribute count**, and no table in this phase puts one beside a retention
figure.

## 4. `RT-02`, and it is the row to read twice

**The electronic master production and control record falls in Group C.**

**Take the provision first.** `21 CFR 211.180(a)` reaches *"[a]ny production, control, or distribution
record that is required to be maintained in compliance with this part **and is specifically associated
with a batch of a drug product**…"* — and the period it then sets runs from the expiration date of **the
batch**.
`07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md` reads the paragraph whole and owns what the operative words
exclude; the short form is that **the paragraph's period is expressed by reference to a batch, and a
record that is not specifically associated with one has no batch for the period to run from.**

**Now take the record.** `21 CFR 211.186` requires a master production and control record for each drug
product and each batch size, prepared, dated and signed by one person and independently checked, dated and
signed by a second. **It is the document every batch of that product is made against.** It carries the
name and strength, the complete list of components, the statement of theoretical yield, the description of
the containers and closures, and the complete manufacturing and control instructions with the sampling and
testing procedures and the specifications. **Everything in a batch record that is not an observation is
a transcription from it.**

**And the transcription is not a description of Helix's practice; it is a requirement.**
`21 CFR 211.188(a)` requires the batch production and control record to include *"[a]n accurate
reproduction of the appropriate master production or control record, checked for accuracy, dated, and
signed"*.

**That is the objection to this finding and it has to be met rather than avoided.** If an accurate
reproduction of the master is inside every batch record, and every batch record has `21 CFR 211.180(a)`'s
period, has the master got a period after all?

**No, and the reason is exact.** What `21 CFR 211.188(a)` puts inside the batch record is a reproduction of
the master **as it stood for that batch**. The master production and control record as a controlled
document is something else: it has versions, it has superseded versions, it carries the identity of the
person who prepared it and of the second person who independently checked it, it has a change history, and
it is the artefact against which any question about *what were we supposed to be doing in 2024* is
answered. **None of that is inside any batch record.** A firm holding a complete set of batch records
holds, in each of them, one frozen reproduction and no account of what the master was, how it changed, or
who authorised the change. **`21 CFR 211.180(a)`'s period reaches the reproduction because the reproduction
is part of a record specifically associated with a batch of a drug product. It does not reach the record
the reproduction was taken from**, and `21 CFR 211.186` sets no period for it.

**Which is exactly why `21 CFR 211.180(a)`'s period does not reach it.** The master is associated with
every batch of the product ever made against that version, and it is specifically associated with none of
them. **There is no single batch whose expiration date the period could run from.** The paragraph does
not fail to reach the master by oversight or by drafting slip; it reaches records *of* batches, and a
master is a record of an intention.

**And `21 CFR 211.186` sets no period — and it goes further than that.** It says what the master must
contain and what must be done to it — prepared, dated, signed, independently checked, dated and signed
again, under a written procedure that is followed, with the nine items at `(b)` — and it says nothing at
all about how long it is kept. **The words *maintained* and *retained* appear nowhere in the section**,
which is a stronger statement than *it sets no period*: the section that requires the master to exist
never says it is to be kept. **The only provisions that speak to the master being kept at all are
`21 CFR 211.180(c)` and `(d)`**, and both presuppose a retention period rather than supplying one — `(c)`
requires records required under Part 211, or copies of them, to be **readily available for authorised
inspection during the retention period** and to be subject to reproduction as part of that inspection, and
`(d)` permits what is retained to be an original or a true copy. **Nor does any other provision the derivation
searched, and the search is retained at `EV-709` rather than asserted here.**

**So the position is this.** The batch record — `RT-01` — takes its period from `21 CFR 211.180(a)` and
keeps it for at least a year past the expiry of the batch it describes. **The document the batch record
was made from takes its period from nothing.** A firm that retained its batch records for exactly the
period the regulation sets, and no longer, could reach the end of that period holding a complete set of
batch records and no statement of what any of them was supposed to say.

**`IS-45`** carries it, owned by Priyanka Venkataraman, raised 2027-03-09, open. **It is one of the
fourteen at `IS-44` rather than a separate route** — the remedy is the same remedy, a decision taken by a
named individual and recorded as a decision. **It is carried as its own entry because a reader who takes
Group C as a list of peripheral record types will have misread the finding**, and `RD-02` is the row that
makes that impossible.

**Two sentences on what this is not.** **It is not a claim that Helix has destroyed a master production
and control record**, and nothing in this phase establishes that any record has been disposed of; disposal
is not this phase's subject and no record in the estate is dispositioned by anything here. **And it is not
a proposal.** No period is set for `RT-02`, none is proposed, and no range or bound is offered — `ADR-0044`,
`ADR-0050`. **What is recorded is that the decision is owed and that nobody has taken it.**

## 5. The two that share a predicate rule and do not share a group

**`RT-21` and `RT-38` are both anchored on `21 CFR 211.192`, and they fall in different groups.**

`RT-21` is the out-of-specification investigation record. **An out-of-specification investigation is
opened on a named result, from a named test, on a named batch.** It is a control record of that batch, it
travels with that batch's record through review and disposition, and `21 CFR 211.180(a)`'s period attaches
to it exactly as it attaches to the result that triggered it. **Group A.**

`RT-38` is the deviation, investigation and corrective and preventive action record. **A deviation may
concern one batch, several batches, a procedure, a facility, a supplier or a computerised system, and many
concern no batch at all.** The class is not batch-associated even though some of its members are.
`21 CFR 211.192` requires the investigation and sets no period for the record of it. **Group C.**

**The point is not the two record types. It is that the group is a property of the record's subject and
not of its citation.** A derivation that had partitioned by predicate rule would have put `RT-21` and
`RT-38` in the same group and been wrong about one of them, and the same trap sits under `RT-25` — whose
predicate rule includes `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)`, the paragraph that requires in-process and laboratory
control results to be documented **in the batch record.** **A viable-count result is referred to in a
batch record; it is a record of a room.** The reference carries the batch record's period; the monitoring
result does not acquire one.

## 6. What the derivation found that nobody had written down

**Twenty periods existed and Helix had never read them.** They were not wrong, not disputed and not
missing from anywhere except Helix's own documents. `21 CFR 211.180(a)` has said what it says since long
before this programme opened, and `RT-01`'s period has been a year past batch expiry throughout.
**`IS-14` recorded since 2026-07-24 that no retention period was recorded against any of the 34, and that
wording was exactly right** — what was absent was Helix's record of having read the regulation, and not
the regulation.

**Fourteen periods do not exist anywhere and never did.** No provision supplies them, no guidance supplies
them, and no amount of reading will. **They require a decision, taken by a named individual, recorded as
a decision** — `ADR-0044`. **Nobody has taken one.**

**Those are two different failures, they have two different remedies, and reporting either as progress
against the other would be the most damaging thing this phase could do.**
`07.04-what-nothing-sets.md` is where that is argued, and `PR-50` carries the risk that the twenty are
reported inside Helix as *retention resolved*.

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It sets no retention period for any record type**, reads none off any system, proposes none, bounds
  none and offers no range — `ADR-0044`, `ADR-0050`.
- **It states no retention policy, no retention schedule, no archive design and no migration plan.**
- **It establishes nothing about whether any record is actually retained for the period the regulation
  sets.** A period read is not a period operated, and `IS-46`, `IS-47` and `IS-48` carry what would have to
  be true for it to be.
- **It re-scores no Phase 03 pair.** The 121 are partitioned and not recounted; `IS-15` and `IS-16` stand
  exactly as published.
- **It re-determines no Phase 05 cell and re-bands no Phase 04 obligation** — `ADR-0049`.
- **It re-opens no Phase 02 determination.** The 34 are Phase 02's 34, the printout route holds for the
  four record types it was applied to, and `logs/paper-record-register.md` is where those four are.
- **It disposes of nothing.** `OBS-2` and `OBS-4` are Phase 08's.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**
