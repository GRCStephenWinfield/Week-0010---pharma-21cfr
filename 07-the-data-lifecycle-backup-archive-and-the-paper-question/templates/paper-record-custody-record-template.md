## 1. Why this form exists

**A determination that a record is not a Part 11 record is not a determination that it is unregulated.**

Where Helix determined that a printed record satisfies the printout route — **the route's three conditions,
two FDA states expressly and a third Helix derives from the narrow interpretation's second category** — the
determination establishes that `21 CFR Part 11`'s conditions do not attach to the electronic version. **It
establishes nothing whatever about the paper.** The predicate rule that made the record a required record is
untouched: **remove Part 11 and the predicate rule stands untouched.**

**So the record does not leave regulation; it moves.** Out of `21 CFR Part 11` and into `21 CFR 211.180`,
where the paper is now the record required under Part 211 — subject to `(c)`'s availability obligation at
the establishment where the activities occurred, to `(d)`'s permission and its condition, and, **where the
record's subject is a batch, to `(a)`'s period**.

**And the failure mode this form exists to prevent is structural rather than careless.** An out-of-scope
determination closes a file. The record leaves the register everybody works from, it generates no further
questions, **and the completeness of the determination is what stops anybody asking the next one.** **An
out-of-scope determination is the only kind of determination that removes its own subject from view.**

**The unit is one record type**, as Phase 02's register carries it — `ADR-0013`.

**What this form is not.** It is not an applicability determination, not a re-opening of one, not a
retention schedule entry, not a filing instruction and **not an assessment of anybody's custody of
anything.** It records whether a thing is stated, not whether somebody did their job.

| Field | Enter |
|---|---|
| **Retention location, stated** | `[the location — building, room, cabinet or contracted store, named / recorded as not stated]` |

| Where they are not, on what basis the record is available for inspection there | `[stated, or recorded as not established]` |
| How the record is retrieved | `[described — the index, the reference, who holds the key]` |
| **How the record, or a copy of it, would be reproduced during an inspection** | `[described — the equipment, where it is, who operates it / recorded as not established]` |

**`21 CFR 211.180(c)`, first sentence, verbatim:**

> *"All records required under this part, or copies of such records, shall be readily available for
> authorized inspection during the retention period at the establishment where the activities described in
> such records occurred."*

**The obligation is expressed in terms of the activity, not the record.** The place that matters is where
the work was done. **And *readily available* is a property of an arrangement rather than of a cabinet** — a
record that exists somewhere and that nobody can find on request has not met the sentence.

**The paragraph's second sentence, verbatim, and it is the one this form's reproduction row serves:**

> *"These records or copies thereof shall be subject to photocopying or other means of reproduction as part
> of such inspection."*

**That is a second obligation and it bears directly on a paper record on a thermal medium.** A ticket that
has faded, or that cannot be photocopied legibly, is a record the sentence is not satisfied about,
**however available it is.** The row above is completed from what exists, and where nothing exists it
records that nothing does.

**The paragraph's immediate-retrieval saving does not reach a paper record.** Its terms are *"Records that
can be immediately retrieved from another location by computer or other electronic means…"*, and **a paper
record held at another establishment is not saved by it.**

**The period cell follows `ADR-0044` exactly: the provision actually relied on, or the word *none*.** **The
route did not change what the record is about**, so where the record's subject is one identified batch,
`21 CFR 211.180(a)`'s period attaches to the paper. **A period the regulation sets does not become optional
because the record is a printout.**

**The medium rows are not an antiquarian detail.** Where a medium's behaviour over the retention period
bears on whether the record will still be readable at the end of it, that is a fact about the record's
retention. **The row asks what Helix holds and from what source, and where the answer is that it holds a
statement from no source, the row says so** — because *a negative that nobody can test is not a
determination; it is a silence.*

**And `21 CFR 211.180(d)` is a permission conditioned on the copy being true.** Verbatim:

> *"Records required under this part may be retained either as original records or as true copies such as
> photocopies, microfilm, microfiche, or other accurate reproductions of the original records."*

**The paragraph permits a reproduction. It does not require one, and it does not say how a copy is shown to
be true** — `../07.11-original-or-a-true-copy-on-the-cgmp-side.md` owns that argument, and
`true-copy-demonstration-record-template.md` is the instrument.
