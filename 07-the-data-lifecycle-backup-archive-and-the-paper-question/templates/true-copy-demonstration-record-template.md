## 1. The provision this form is built on

**`21 CFR 211.180(d)`, first sentence, verbatim:**

> *"Records required under this part may be retained either as original records or as true copies such as
> photocopies, microfilm, microfiche, or other accurate reproductions of the original records."*

**And the paragraph does not end there.** Its next sentence addresses reduction techniques: where such
techniques, microfilming among them, are used, **suitable reader and photocopying equipment must be readily
available.** This repository states that sentence in its own words rather than quoting it, and states it at
all because **a permission to retain in a reduced or converted form carries with it an obligation to keep
whatever is needed to render it.** A firm holding records in a proprietary format whose reader it no longer
licenses is in the position of a firm holding microfiche and no microfiche reader.

**Four things about the first sentence govern the whole of this form.**

**The operative word is *may*.** The paragraph imposes no duty to make copies, prefers neither copies nor
originals, and requires nothing of a firm that retains originals. **It grants a permission and attaches a
condition to it.**

**The condition is that the retained thing is a true copy — an accurate reproduction of the original
records.** *True*, *accurate reproduction* and *of the original records* are all relational terms. **A copy
is not true in itself; it is true with respect to an original.**

**The paragraph therefore presupposes that the original is an identified thing**, and `§2` of this form is
where it is identified. **A copy cannot be shown true against an original nobody has named** — not because
the demonstration would be difficult, but because the question *is this a true copy?* has no determinate
content until somebody says what it is a copy of.

**And the paragraph supplies no method.** It names media and it names a standard. **It states no test, no
acceptance criterion and no evidence requirement.** That is recorded as a fact about the source material
and not as a licence: **an obligation whose method is unspecified is still an obligation**, and **the
method on this form is Helix's own and is labelled as Helix's.**

**FDA has stated a content standard, in guidance, and this form is built against it.** At Question 9 of
the December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity questions and answers, FDA states that electronic copies *"can
be used as true copies of paper or electronic records, provided the copies preserve the content and
meaning of the original record, which includes all metadata required to reconstruct the CGMP activity and
the static or dynamic nature of the original records"*, and that true copies of dynamic electronic records
*"may be made and maintained in the format of the original records or in a format that allows for the
content and meaning of the original records to be preserved if a suitable reader and copying equipment
(e.g., software and hardware, including media readers) are readily available (§§ 211.180(d) and
212.110)."* **That is guidance and it is cited as guidance; the requirement is the paragraph's.** **It
states three terms and no procedure** — content and meaning, the metadata required to reconstruct the CGMP
activity, and the static or dynamic character of the original — and `§4` of this form is where each of the
three is answered on the instance in hand.

**What this form is not, and this matters more here than on any other instrument in the phase.** **It is
not an ALCOA attribute determination.** *Original or a true copy* is also the wording of one of FDA's five
ALCOA attributes, from the December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity questions and answers, and Phase 03
assessed that attribute across the estate. **The attribute is a data integrity characteristic in a
nonbinding guidance; `21 CFR 211.180(d)` is a binding retention permission. They point at the same
underlying question and they are not the same provision, and this repository never conflates them** —
`../07.11-original-or-a-true-copy-on-the-cgmp-side.md §3`. **Completing this form does not change any
Phase 03 determination, and no Phase 03 determination completes this form.**

it, because an original identified in order to make a comparison succeed is not an original.

deviation** — `ADR-0031`.

**The second row decides how hard the rest of the form is.** A byte-for-byte duplicate raises a narrow
question. **A rendering, an export or a conversion raises the question the phrase *accurate reproduction*
was written for**, and it is the same territory Phase 02 mapped when it found that a fixed-data output of a
record the user interacts with is not the record.

**The last row is `(d)`'s reduction-techniques sentence applied to a modern medium.** **A copy that cannot
be rendered is not available, whatever its fidelity**, and `21 CFR 211.180(c)` requires records to be
*readily available* for inspection during the retention period.

## 4. What the record consists of

**A copy is true of the whole record or it is not true.** This section enumerates the whole.

| Field | Enter |
|---|---|
| **The data** | `[the values, results or content the predicate rule requires]` |
| **The metadata that forms part of the record** | `[enumerated — or recorded as none, with the basis]` |
| **The audit trail associated with the record** | `[named — or recorded as not part of the record, with the basis]` |
| **The processing context** | `[the method, parameters, calculations or query that produced the content — or recorded as not applicable, with the basis]` |
| **Signatures and their manifestations** | `[the printed name of the signer, the date and time of execution, and the meaning — or recorded as none]` |
| **The predicate rule's own content requirements** | `[the provision, and each element it requires]` |
| **Whether the original record is static or dynamic, and on what basis** | `[static / dynamic, with the basis — and where dynamic, what interaction the format allows]` |

**The static-or-dynamic row is here because FDA's Question 9 answer names it** as one of the three things a
true copy preserves, and because **a fixed rendering of a record whose format allows interaction is one of
the outputs that record can produce.** Whether a copy of a dynamic original preserves the content and
meaning of the original is a determination and is recorded at `§6` rather than assumed here.

**The predicate-rule row is the control on all the others.** What the record consists of is decided by the provision
that requires it, not by what a system happens to export. **A comparison performed against what the export
contains has compared the export.**

**The fifth row is here because `21 CFR 11.50(b)` requires the three signature elements to be included as
part of any human readable form of the electronic record**, and a copy that drops the meaning of a signature
has dropped a required element of the thing it purports to reproduce. **Whether that renders it an
inaccurate reproduction for `21 CFR 211.180(d)` purposes is a determination and is recorded at `§6` rather
than assumed here.**

**Presence and agreement are two questions and the form asks both.** An element that is absent from the copy
cannot disagree with the original, **and a comparison that only checks agreement will record no
discrepancy** — which is how an incomplete copy passes a verification.

**A value that matches at a different precision has not matched**, and a rendering at a lower resolution
than the original is a rendering from which a different reading might be taken.

original is relying on `21 CFR 211.180(d)` whether or not it has ever said so**, and the condition attaches
to it from that moment.
