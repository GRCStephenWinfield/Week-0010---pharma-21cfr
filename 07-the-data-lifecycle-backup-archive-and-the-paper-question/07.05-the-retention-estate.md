## 1. Four systems, named from the published register and nothing more

**Where a Helix record goes when the system that made it has finished with it is a question the programme
had not asked before this phase.** Phase 06's `IS-40` pointed at three of the four; Phase 02's systems
register had carried all four since 2026-07-31. **The following table is read off that register and adds
nothing to it** — `EV-711`, `DEC-709`, Amara Sissoko, 2027-03-10.

| System | Name, as Phase 02 publishes it | Location | Owner | Entry source | Record types held |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`SYS-036`** | Laboratory data archive and long-term retention store | **Ridgemont data centre** | **Dr Sunita Raghunathan** — Head of Quality Control | Register | `RT-11`, `RT-12`, `RT-13` |
| **`SYS-059`** | Batch record archive and retention platform | **Ridgemont data centre** | **Amara Sissoko** — Chief Information Officer | Register | `RT-01`, `RT-02` |
| **`SYS-060`** | Analytical data backup and restore platform, QC laboratories | **Ridgemont data centre** | **Amara Sissoko** — Chief Information Officer | Register | `RT-12`, `RT-13` |
| **`SYS-061`** | Laboratory data archive and retention store | **Cary** | **Amara Sissoko** — Chief Information Officer | **Walk** | `RT-11`, `RT-12`, `RT-13` |

**Three of the four are owned by the Chief Information Officer. None is owned by the quality unit. And
`SYS-061` entered the inventory by walk rather than from the IT register.**

**Two things about that table are worth saying before anything is drawn from it.** The first is that
**ownership of a system is not ownership of the records in it** — `02.02 §4` — and nothing here suggests
otherwise. The Chief Information Officer owning a storage platform is unremarkable and is what the role
is for. **The second is that Helix's role set names no Head of Records and no archivist**, in the same way
it names no Head of Supply Chain and no Head of Engineering and Maintenance; where a system has been
allocated to the most senior individual accountable for the operation it serves, that allocation is
recorded and is not a criticism.

## 2. What the fourth system says about the other three

**`SYS-061` was found by walking the Cary site.** It was not in the IT register, it had never entered a
validation lifecycle, and it is one of the seventeen entries `ADR-0009` produced in Phase 02 — `IS-13`,
still open since 2026-06-12. **It holds `RT-11`, `RT-12` and `RT-13`: analytical result records, and the
chromatographic raw data and reprocessing records that Phase 06 spent four weeks on.**

**A retention store nobody had a register entry for is a retention store nobody had a determination
about.** Every question this programme has asked of a system since Phase 02 — is it GxP-relevant, does it
hold a Part 11 record, is it open or closed, what is its rigour band, what is its control position — was
asked of `SYS-061` only because somebody walked past it. **`DP-13` — confirmation and acceptance of
ownership for the seventeen the walk found — has been open since 2026-06-12 and is not discharged**, and
`SYS-061` is one of the seventeen.

**The programme does not treat that as evidence that anything is wrong with `SYS-061`. Unassessed is not
adverse.** What it records is the width of the question: **if the estate could hold one retention store
the register did not know about, the number of retention stores it holds is a number established by a walk
in June 2026 and by nothing since** — `AS-13`.

## 3. The provision, and what it actually requires of these four

**`21 CFR 211.180(c)`, first sentence, whole:**

> *"All records required under this part, or copies of such records, shall be readily available for
> authorized inspection during the retention period at the establishment where the activities described in
> such records occurred."*

**Read the operative words carefully, because the obligation is narrower and more specific than the
general sense of *keep your records safe*.** It is an availability obligation. It runs for the length of
the retention period. It is satisfied by the records **or copies of them**. And it attaches to a place —
the establishment where the activities occurred — which is `07.06-where-the-records-are-and-where-the-activity-was.md`'s
subject and not this chapter's.

**The paragraph has three sentences and the second one is a further obligation**, requiring that the
records or copies of them *"shall be subject to photocopying or other means of reproduction as part of
such inspection."* **Availability is a state and reproduction is an act**, and the act is performed during
the inspection. `07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md §7` reads the paragraph whole and this
chapter adds no reading of its own; what it records below is that neither limb has a named holder on any
of the four.

**What this chapter records is that no individual is named as accountable for that availability on any of
the four systems.** The enquiry is retained at `EV-712` and its answer is *none identified on any of the
four*. **Every one of the four has a named individual system owner, and a system owner is accountable for
the system.** What none of them has is a named individual accountable for the proposition that the records
the system holds will be readily available for authorised inspection for as long as they have to be.

**That is a narrower finding than it may look, and the narrowness is deliberate.** **`21 CFR 211.22`
gives the quality unit the responsibility and authority to approve or reject, and `21 CFR 211.22(d)`
requires its responsibilities and procedures to be in writing and followed** — but `21 CFR 211.22` does
not, in terms, make the quality unit the custodian of every record Part 211 requires, and this chapter
does not say that it does. **The accurate statement is the one above: `21 CFR 211.180(c)` requires records
required under Part 211, or copies of them, to be readily available for authorised inspection during the
retention period, and on none of the four systems is any individual named as accountable for that
availability.** `ADR-0008` has required a named individual owner for every control, every procedure,
every record and every obligation in this programme since Phase 01, and **this is an obligation with no
holder.**

## 4. What the four hold, and what that makes of the gap

**Between them the four hold five record types**: `RT-01` the electronic batch production and control
record · `RT-02` the electronic master production and control record · `RT-11` the analytical result
record · `RT-12` the chromatographic raw data file and acquisition metadata · `RT-13` the chromatographic
integration and reprocessing record.

**Four of the five take their period from `21 CFR 211.180(a)`** — `RT-01`, `RT-11`, `RT-12` and `RT-13`,
at `RD-01`, `RD-09`, `RD-10` and `RD-11`. **The fifth is `RT-02`, whose period is set by nothing** —
`RD-02`, `IS-45`. **So on `SYS-059`, the batch record archive and retention platform, one of the two
record types held has a period the regulation sets and the other has none at all**, and no arrangement on
that platform distinguishes them because no arrangement on that platform is expressed in terms of either.

**And on the same four systems the backup and restore position is what `logs/backup-and-restore-register.md`
records.** A backup file is maintained on all four — `BR-36`, `BR-59`, `BR-60`, `BR-61`. **A restore has
been performed on one of them, `SYS-060`, and it was incident recovery rather than a test, and what came
back was not compared with what went in.** `07.07-backup-is-not-restore.md` owns that argument; what it
means here is that **the store of last resort for Helix's analytical data has been read back exactly once,
because something broke, and nobody checked the result.**

**Phase 05's control position for the four is a matter of published record and is not re-determined by
anything here.** `21 CFR 11.10(c)` — protection enabling accurate and ready retrieval throughout the
records retention period — is **Not met** on all four of `SYS-036`, `SYS-059`, `SYS-060` and `SYS-061`.
**That determination stands exactly as published** — `ADR-0049` — and it is stated here only so that a
reader does not have to go and look: **the four systems that constitute Helix's retention estate are four
of the seventeen on which `(c)` was determined not met.**

## 5. `IS-47`, and what would close it

**`IS-47`** — three of the four systems that constitute the retention estate are owned by the Chief
Information Officer, none is owned by the quality unit, and one of the four entered the inventory by walk
rather than from the register. Raised 2027-03-10, owned by Amara Sissoko, open.

**It does not close by the systems changing hands.** Moving a storage platform from IT to Quality would
change an owner column and would not produce an individual accountable for the availability of a record
under `21 CFR 211.180(c)`; **the two are different objects and the second is the one that is missing.**
**It closes when a named individual is accountable for that availability, for the records these four
systems hold, for the periods those records have** — which for `RT-02` is a period that does not yet
exist, which is `IS-44`'s.

**This chapter names no candidate for that accountability and proposes no allocation.** Naming one would
be taking a decision that belongs to the quality unit, in a chapter owned by the Chief Information Officer,
about an accountability that reaches records the Chief Information Officer does not own. **`PR-41` carries
the risk that a cut by system owner is read inside a function as a criticism, and it is live here**: the
three-of-four is a fact about where systems sit in an estate and **no document in this repository assesses
any individual's work.**

## 6. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no archive design, no storage specification, no migration plan and no vendor** — `ADR-0050`.
- **It establishes nothing about the condition of anything held on the four systems.** No record was
  opened, read, rendered or tested. **Unassessed is not adverse.**
- **It sets no retention period and proposes none**, for `RT-02` or for anything else.
- **It re-determines no Phase 05 cell.** The `(c)` positions on the four are cited from the published
  register and stand as published — `ADR-0049`.
- **It states no CAPA, no deviation and no remediation plan**, and `IS-47` carries a closure condition
  with no date, no priority and no order.
- **It makes no claim that the four are the whole of Helix's retention estate.** They are the systems
  Phase 02's register describes as archive, retention or backup platforms; **whether a record retained by
  Helix sits anywhere else is not established here**, and `IS-12` — where the records of the nine removed
  register entries now are — is still open and was not looked at.
