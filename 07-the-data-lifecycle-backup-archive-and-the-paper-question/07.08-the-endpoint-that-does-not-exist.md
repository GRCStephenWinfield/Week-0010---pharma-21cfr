# 07.08 — The Endpoint That Does Not Exist

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Document ID | `HLX-DI-2027-708` |
| Version | 1.0 |
| Date | 2027-03-19 |
| Owner | Joachim Reuter — Head of Automation and Manufacturing IT |

*A retrieval obligation with a stated endpoint, determined met on thirteen systems whose records have no endpoint — and not one of the thirteen determinations was wrong.*

---

## 1. The boundary, before the finding

**This chapter re-determines nothing.** No cell of
`../05-access-audit-trails-and-electronic-signatures/logs/control-position-register.md` moves, is marked
provisional, is marked disputed or is proposed for review. **The thirteen cells this chapter is about stand
exactly as Phase 05 published them on 2027-01-29** — `ADR-0049`, `DEC-715`, Terrence Abbatiello,
2027-03-18.

**And this is not a claim that the thirteen determinations were wrong.** **Each was made on the method
authorised at `GOV-21` before the first system was looked at, against the evidence set Phase 04's framework
specified for `21 CFR 11.10(c)`, against the facts then established.** Phase 05 disclosed at the time that
it was doing so in the absence of a stated retention period: **`AS-37` records, in Phase 05's own words,
that a determination against `(c)` and against `(e)`'s retention clause was being made by assessing
protection and retention against the record's own life as the systems presently hold it.** The assumption
was published. It was not tested then and it is not tested now.

**The boundary is stated first because the finding invites the opposite reading and a reader in a hurry
will reach for it.** `PR-54` carries that risk, and `EV-729` retains the record of the three formulations
that were available — re-determine, mark provisional, mark disputed — and the reason each was refused.

## 2. The paragraph, quoted whole

**`21 CFR 11.10(c)` is one sentence and the sentence is the whole of the paragraph:**

> *"Protection of records to enable their accurate and ready retrieval throughout the records retention
> period."*

**Three words do the work at the end.** The obligation is not *protect the records*. It is not *enable
retrieval*. **It is to protect them so that they can be accurately and readily retrieved throughout the
records retention period** — and the paragraph therefore has a term. **It starts when the record is
created and it runs to the end of a period Part 11 does not set.**

**`21 CFR Part 11` sets no retention period of its own.** It sets requirements about records that other
regulations require, and the duration of those requirements comes from the predicate rule.
`07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md` reads `21 CFR 211.180` and owns where the periods come
from; **the short form is that `(c)`'s endpoint is the predicate rule's, and where the predicate rule sets
none, `(c)` has no endpoint.**

## 3. The three counts

**The counts below are computed from two published registers and from nothing else.** The Group C
membership is `logs/retention-derivation-register.md §3.3`'s, computed from Phase 02's systems column. The
`(c)` column is Phase 05's, read and not re-read. The working paper is `EV-728`.

| Cut | Value |
|---|---|
| Systems holding at least one record type for which **no predicate rule sets a period** | **24 of the 61** |
| `21 CFR 11.10(c)` as Phase 05 published it | **Met on 44 · Not met on 17. 44 + 17 = 61** |
| **Systems in both sets** | **13** |

**The thirteen, named:** `SYS-001`, `SYS-025`, `SYS-026`, `SYS-034`, `SYS-035`, `SYS-040`, `SYS-041`,
`SYS-042`, `SYS-044`, `SYS-045`, `SYS-047`, `SYS-049`, `SYS-050`.

**On thirteen systems, an obligation to enable accurate and ready retrieval throughout the records
retention period was determined met, in respect of an estate that includes at least one record type for
which no records retention period exists.**

**The other eleven of the twenty-four are systems on which `(c)` was determined not met**, and nothing
about the missing endpoint changes that determination either. **A protection obligation that is not met is
not met whether or not it has a term**; the finding is only interesting on the Met side, because that is
where a determination has asserted something about a duration.

**And the shape of the thirteen is worth a sentence.** `SYS-001` is the manufacturing execution system and
holds `RT-02`, the master production and control record. `SYS-041` is the electronic quality management
system, holds seven in-scope record types, and **four of the seven are in Group C** — `RT-18`, `RT-38`,
`RT-39` and `RT-40`. `SYS-042` is the learning management system and holds one
record type, `RT-42`, which is in Group C — **so on `SYS-042` the obligation has no endpoint at all rather
than a partial one.**

## 4. The other derivable cut, and what it is for

**The same two registers give a partition of the 61 that no earlier phase could have computed.**

| Cut | Count |
|---|---|
| Systems holding **only** record types for which no predicate rule sets a period | **16** |
| Systems holding **only** record types for which a predicate rule sets one | **37** |
| Systems holding **both** | **8** |
| **Check** | **16 + 37 + 8 = 61** |

**16 + 8 = 24**, which is the first row of `§3`.

**The sixteen are the sharp case.** On a system holding only Group C record types, **there is no record on
it whose retention period is set by anything**, so `21 CFR 11.10(c)`'s term is absent entirely rather than
partially. `07.09-a-relation-with-two-terms.md` uses the same sixteen for `21 CFR 11.10(e)`'s retention
limb, where the consequence is different and the population is the same.

**The eight are the case that will be misread.** A system holding both kinds of record type has a term for
some of what it holds and no term for the rest, **and a single determination cell against `21 CFR 11.10(c)`
cannot distinguish them.** That is not a criticism of the cell. **The unit of Phase 05's grid is a
paragraph on a system** — `ADR-0030` — and a paragraph on a system is the unit the phase argued for and
published. **The consequence is that the grid cannot express a position that varies by record type within
a system**, which is a property of the unit and is recorded here rather than repaired.

## 5. What follows, and what does not

**What follows is `IS-43`**, owned by Terrence Abbatiello, raised 2027-03-18, open: **on thirteen systems
`21 CFR 11.10(c)` was determined Met against a records retention period that does not exist.**

**It does not close by any cell being changed.** It closes when a period exists for the record types those
thirteen systems hold — which is `IS-44`'s, and `DP-38`'s, and belongs to the quality unit. **The
determination and the period are two different objects and only one of them is missing.**

**What does not follow — and each of these is a sentence somebody will write if it is not refused here:**

**It does not follow that the thirteen systems are unprotected.** `(c)` was determined met on the evidence
Phase 04's framework specifies, and the protection those determinations were made against is in place.
**What is absent is the term the obligation runs for, not the protection.**

**It does not follow that Phase 05's method was defective.** The method was authorised before the first
system was looked at, applied identically to 671 positions, and it disclosed its own assumption at
`AS-37`. **A method that publishes what it is assuming is doing the thing this finding depends on.**

**It does not follow that the thirteen should be re-determined once a period exists.** They may be, on
`ADR-0015`'s and `ADR-0022`'s triggers and through `SOP-DI-05`, and **this phase neither requires it nor
schedules it.** `SOP-DI-05` has still not fired on any change — `AS-19`, `DP-32`.

**And it does not follow that enforcement discretion softens any of it — the opposite, once the discretion
is read in its own terms.** `21 CFR 11.10(c)` sits inside the discretion set, and **the heading it sits
under in FDA's August 2003 Scope and Application guidance is *Record Retention*.** FDA's statement
reproduces the paragraph's own words: it intends to exercise enforcement discretion *"with regard to the
part 11 requirements for the protection of records to enable their accurate and ready retrieval throughout
the records retention period (§ 11.10 (c) and any corresponding requirement in §11.30)"*. **It then says
what survives, and it names the sections:** *"Persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule
requirements for record retention and availability (e.g., §§ 211.180(c),(d), 108.25(g), and 108.35(h))."*
**And it says how a firm should decide:** *"We suggest that your decision on how to maintain records be
based on predicate rule requirements and that you base your decision on a justified and documented risk
assessment and a determination of the value of the records over time."*

**That is guidance and it is cited as guidance.** It is nonbinding, it is FDA's current thinking, and its
third sentence is a *suggest* rather than a requirement. **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention
about what it will enforce; it is not an exemption, not a safe harbour, and it confers no rights** —
`ADR-0003`, `IS-33`. **And `21 CFR 211.180` was never inside it at all**: the retention periods this
chapter turns on are predicate-rule periods, and the discretion has nothing to say about them.

**Read together, the three sentences make the finding sharper rather than softer.** FDA said it would not
enforce `21 CFR 11.10(c)`, told firms to comply with `21 CFR 211.180(c)` and `(d)` instead, and recommended
that the retention decision be based on the predicate rule — **which is `07.01-a-period-is-derived-not-chosen.md`'s
method, in FDA's own words.** **And the two paragraphs FDA named are the two that presuppose a retention
period without supplying one.** For the record types the thirteen systems hold, no predicate rule supplies
it — so the obligation FDA said survives is an obligation with no term, and Helix had performed the
derivation that would have shown that for no record type at all until March 2027.

## 6. Why this is the phase that could see it

**Every input to this chapter was published before it opened.** Phase 02's record-type register carried the
predicate rule and the systems for all 58 record types on 2026-07-31. Phase 05's control position register
carried the `(c)` column for all 61 systems on 2027-01-29. **The intersection could have been computed in
January.**

**It could not have been computed with meaning.** Until the derivation existed, *record types for which no
predicate rule sets a period* was not a set anybody could enumerate — **`IS-14` said no period was recorded
against any of the 34, which made the whole estate look alike.** The derivation splits it, and the split is
what makes thirteen systems visible as a class. **`IS-14`'s wording was accurate and its shape hid this**,
which is the second thing the split at `07.04-what-nothing-sets.md` bought.

## 7. What this chapter does not establish

- **It re-determines nothing, re-scores nothing and re-bands nothing.** The thirteen cells stand as
  published; so do the other 658 — `ADR-0049`.
- **It sets no retention period, proposes none and bounds none.**
- **It states no CAPA, no deviation and no remediation plan**, and `IS-43` carries a closure condition with
  no date, no priority and no order.
- **It states no validation outcome**, and a control position is not validation evidence — `IS-34`.
- **It asserts nothing about whether any record on the thirteen systems could in fact be retrieved.** No
  retrieval was attempted and none is reported.
- **It makes no claim about the other 231 paragraph-on-system positions inside the discretion set** —
  `21 CFR 11.10(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)` and `(e)` on 61 systems is 244 positions, of which this chapter is about
  13 — and `IS-33` stands unchanged.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**
