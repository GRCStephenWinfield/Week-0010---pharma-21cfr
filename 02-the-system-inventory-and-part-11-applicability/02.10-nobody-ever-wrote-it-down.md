## 1. The question, and the shape of the answer

Phase 01 left one question open in two halves, carried by two identifiers that are never conflated.

**`IS-02`** — *which records actually are Part 11 records, and did anybody ever write that down?*
**`IS-01`** — *why did four internal audits raise nothing on the subject matter of `OBS-2`, `OBS-4` and
`OBS-6`?*

This chapter answers the second half of `IS-02`. `02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md` answers the
first: thirty-four record types of fifty-eight are Part 11 records.

**The answer to the second half is no.** No written Part 11 applicability determination existed anywhere
in Helix's quality system. Not in a validation plan, not in the system inventory, not in a policy, not in
an SOP. **And no procedure required one to exist.**

**This chapter does not touch `IS-01`.** `02.11-what-this-does-not-establish.md` owns that boundary and
refuses to cross it. What follows is a statement about documents Helix does and does not hold.

## 2. What was searched, and how

A negative finding is only worth anything if the search that produced it can be inspected. This section
exists so that a reader can see the search was real, decide whether it was adequate, and disagree with it
if they wish.

**Search authority.** The search was commissioned under `DEC-201`'s boundary for the phase, with its
question fixed in writing before execution and recorded at `EV-226`. **`DEC-216`, taken by the Head of
Quality Assurance on 2026-07-28, records its outcome** and closes `IS-02`; it was ratified at the Data
Integrity Steering Committee sitting minuted at
`governance/GOV-10-steering-committee-minute-2026-07-30.md`.

**Search question, fixed before the search began.** *Does there exist, anywhere in the Helix quality
system or its supporting documentation, a record that determines whether any specified Helix record is a
record within the scope of 21 CFR Part 11?* A statement that Part 11 exists, that it applies to the
company, or that a system is "Part 11 compliant" was **not** accepted as a determination. A determination
identifies a record and states an answer.

**Search period.** From the earliest controlled document held in the quality system's index to 2026-05-29,
the Phase 01 vantage.

**Who searched.** Quality Assurance, with Records Management and with IT and Automation for the
repositories they hold. The Data Integrity Lead reviewed the method before the search and the results
after it — `ADR-0002` puts that role outside IT for exactly this kind of reason.

**What was searched, by repository.**

| # | Repository or document class | What was looked for | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Controlled procedure index — every SOP, work instruction and policy in force at any point in the search period | A procedure requiring a Part 11 applicability determination, or defining how one is made, or naming who makes it | **Nothing found** |
| 2 | Superseded and withdrawn procedure archive | The same, in case a requirement had existed and been removed | **Nothing found.** No procedure requiring a determination has ever been in force |
| 3 | Validation plans, protocols, summary reports and periodic review records for the GxP-critical platforms | A record-level statement of which records held by the system are Part 11 records | **Nothing found.** Several documents state that a system "meets Part 11 requirements" or "supports Part 11". None identifies a record |
| 4 | The IT register of computerised systems, all revisions held | An attribute identifying which systems hold records required by a predicate rule, or a Part 11 attribute of any kind | **Nothing found** — the same gap `IS-03` carries, and the same gap recorded at `IA-2025-01/F3` |
| 5 | Quality policy set, including the data governance and documentation policies | A statement of scope at record level | **Nothing found.** The documentation policy addresses retention and legibility and does not reach Part 11 scope |
| 6 | Change control records for GxP systems | A determination made as part of a system change or implementation | **Nothing found.** Change records address the change, not the scope of the records the system holds |
| 7 | Supplier and vendor documentation held by Helix | A vendor statement Helix had adopted as its own determination | **Nothing found**, and nothing of the kind would have been one. §5 |
| 8 | Internal audit reports and working papers for the four audits published at `01.09` | A reference to a determination against which the audit was scoped | **Nothing found.** `01.09 §5` had already recorded that no audit programme in the four refers to such a determination |
| 9 | Steering committee, quality council and management review minutes | A decision to make, defer or dispense with a determination | **Nothing found.** The question does not appear |
| 10 | Regulatory correspondence, including the response to the Form FDA 483 filed on 2026-03-06 | A determination stated to FDA | **Nothing found.** The response commits to work; it makes no scope statement about records |

**Where the search was limited, the limit is stated.** The controlled document index reaches only
documents that were indexed. A determination written by an individual and never put under document control
would not be found by this method and would not, on Helix's own rules, be a determination — an
uncontrolled document is not part of the quality system. **The search establishes the absence of a
controlled record. It does not establish that nobody ever privately formed a view**, and it does not need
to: `21 CFR 211.22(d)` requires the responsibilities and procedures applicable to the quality control unit
to be in writing and to be followed, and the company's own quality system runs on written procedures. **A
view nobody wrote down is not one an auditor, an inspector or a successor can use.**

## 3. The finding

**This section owns it for the repository.**

> **No written Part 11 applicability determination existed anywhere in Helix's quality system — not in a
> validation plan, not in the system inventory, not in a policy, not in an SOP. And no procedure required
> one to exist.**

Both halves matter and the second is the heavier of the two.

**The first half is an absence of a record.** It could have been an oversight — a document that should
have been written and was not, in a company that otherwise knew it was owed. That would be a gap with a
shape, and gaps with shapes get closed.

**The second half is an absence of a requirement**, and it is the reason the first half is not an
oversight. There was nothing in the quality system that would have caused anybody to notice the missing
document. No procedure named it, no procedure owned it, no procedure's completion depended on it. No
periodic review looked for it. No change control step asked for it. The determination was not late; **it
was not on the list.** A company can only fail to do what it has said it does, and Helix had not said it
did this.

**What existed instead was a mixture of three things, none of which is a determination.**

**Assertions about systems.** Documents saying a platform "meets Part 11 requirements" or "is Part 11
compliant". Every one of them is a statement about a system, which is the wrong object —
`ADR-0001`, `02.04-the-record-type-as-the-unit-of-determination.md` — and none identifies a record.

**Assertions about paper.** The position at `02.06 §1`: we print it, we sign the paper, the paper is the
record. Applied to eleven record types, and correct for four of them.

**Assertions from suppliers.** Product literature describing features that support compliance. §5.

Three assertions do not add up to a determination, and the arithmetic does not improve with volume.

## 4. What follows from the finding, stated precisely

**It follows that no Helix record had a written Part 11 status before 2026-07-24**, the date the
determination was approved at `governance/GOV-09-part-11-applicability-determination-approval.md`.
Phase 01 said the same thing prospectively at `01.13 §9`: *no record at Helix has been determined to be a
Part 11 record. Not one.* Phase 02 has now searched for the counter-example and has not found one.

**It follows that the eleven printed record types had never been tested against the paper-printout route's
three conditions**, because there was no instrument that would have asked. `02.06` applies the test; seven
of the eleven fail its first condition and are Part 11 records.

**It follows that the three vendor-hosted systems' classification rested on an IT standard rather than on
`21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`**, because no quality-system document reached the question.
`02.07-open-and-closed.md` owns that finding.

**It does not follow that anything was non-compliant, in breach, or in violation of anything.** This
chapter states what documents Helix holds. **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind**, and no reader
may take one from it. An absence recorded by a company about its own quality system is an assessment the
company has made and published; it is not an adjudication, and nobody in this repository is adjudicating.

**It does not follow that the records were unregulated in the meantime.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)` required
appropriate controls over Helix's computerised systems throughout, `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` required the
second-person review of laboratory records throughout, and `21 CFR 211.192` required quality unit review
of production and control records throughout. **Those obligations never depended on a Part 11
determination and were never affected by its absence.** What the absence removed was the ability to say,
in writing, which records also sat inside Part 11 — and therefore the ability to specify a control set
against them, to audit against it, or to explain it to anybody.

**And it does not follow that the absence explains anything about the four internal audits.**
`02.11-what-this-does-not-establish.md` owns that, and refuses it.

## 5. Why a vendor statement was never going to close this

One category in the search deserves its own paragraph, because it is the one most likely to be offered as
an answer.

**There is no Part 11 certification.** FDA does not certify electronic systems and methods used to obtain
electronic signatures, and does not perform preliminary evaluations of electronic systems to determine
whether they comply with Part 11 requirements. There is no FDA registry, list or approval of "Part 11
compliant" software, and no such artefact could have been found by the search because none exists to be
found.

**What a vendor can legitimately supply is features that support compliance** — audit trail generation,
signature manifestation meeting `21 CFR 11.50`, access control supporting `21 CFR 11.10(d)`. `21 CFR
11.10` places its obligation on *"persons who use closed systems"*. **Compliance is a property of Helix's
validated system in its intended use, under Helix's procedures, operated by Helix's trained people.** It
is not a property a supplier can sell, and outsourcing to a service provider transfers no responsibility.

`PR-07` has carried this since Phase 01. The search confirms that no vendor document in Helix's possession
purports to be a determination, and the finding here is simply that none could have been.

## 7. `IS-10` opens in its place

Closing `IS-02` creates a new problem, and it is a smaller version of the one just closed.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| ID | **`IS-10`** |
| Raised | 2026-07-28 |
| Owner | Terrence Abbatiello |
| Text | **The Part 11 applicability determination now exists, and nothing in Helix's quality system yet requires it to be maintained.** `ADR-0015` makes it a controlled document with a named owner and a review trigger, and that decision lives in this repository rather than in a procedure of the quality system. Until a procedure requires the determination to exist and to be reviewed on the trigger, its maintenance depends on the diligence of its owner |
| Status | **Open** |
| Related | `ADR-0015`, `DEC-215`, `IS-02`, `DP-11`, `PR-13`, `02.09` |

**This is the second half of the finding at §3 turned around.** The absence of a requirement is what made
the absence of the determination invisible. Writing the determination fixes the document and does not fix
the requirement. **A determination nobody is obliged to maintain is a determination that was true once**,
which is the reasoning `ADR-0015` records.

**What would close `IS-10`:** an approved procedure in the quality system that requires a Part 11
applicability determination to exist for the estate, names its owner, states the review trigger, and makes
its review a step in the change control of any GxP system. `DP-11` carries the quality unit capacity that
needs, and the work sits in **Phase 04**, where the policy and procedure architecture is built.

**It is recorded as open rather than as planned**, because a thing that is not true now and is a problem
now is an issue, whatever the plan says about it — `01.12 §1`.

## 9. What this chapter does not say

- **It does not say why four internal audits raised nothing.** `02.11-what-this-does-not-establish.md`
  owns that question and declines to answer it. **`IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted.**
- **It does not say the absence caused anything.** It says what was searched and what was found.
- **It names no individual in connection with the absence**, and characterises no Helix employee, no
  auditor and no FDA investigator. There is nobody in this chapter but the roles that searched and the
  roles that signed.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, does not characterise any past practice as a violation of anything,
  and forecasts nothing about any future inspection.
- **It states no risk, score or rating.** Phase 03.
- **It states no validation status** for any system, and the documents at row 3 of §2 that assert Part 11
  compliance are described as documents, not assessed as claims. Assessing them is Phase 04's and Phase
  05's.
