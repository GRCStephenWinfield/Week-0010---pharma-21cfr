## 1. What this chapter is

Phase 02 found three things that were not in the plan. This chapter states what each of them costs,
**expressed as work with a named owner and a phase, and not as regret.**

The distinction is practical. A finding written up as a concern produces a paragraph. A finding written up
as work produces a task somebody has to schedule, and a task somebody has to schedule is the only version
that survives contact with a programme plan.

**Three rules govern this chapter and each of them removes something a reader might expect to find.**

**No milestone moves and none is forecast to move.** `MS-06` to `MS-13` stand exactly as
`01.11 §6` published them. This chapter says what has to fit inside the existing windows; it does not
predict whether it will, and a programme that reports a slip before one has occurred is reporting a
feeling.

**Nothing here is scored.** `PR-11` to `PR-18` carry no likelihood, no impact and no score. The
data integrity risk assessment is **Phase 03**'s, `MS-07`, 2026-09-30, and putting a delivery concern on
the same scale as a threat to the reliability of a batch record is a category error this repository does
not make.

**No figure here is a fine, a penalty, a sanction or a forfeiture.** None exists. Where money is
mentioned it is programme cost, drawn from the envelope Phase 01 approved.

## 2. Consequence one — seven record types in scope that nothing had budgeted for

**Helix had treated eleven record types as out of Part 11 scope because they were printed and the paper
was said to be the record. Applying the printout route's three conditions — two FDA states expressly and a
third Helix derives from the narrow interpretation's second category — seven of the eleven fail the first.
Four survive and are genuinely out of scope. 7 + 4 = 11.**

**`02.06-the-paper-that-was-not-the-record.md` owns that determination and this chapter does not restate
the argument.** What it states is the consequence: **those seven record types were Part 11 records the
whole time**, they are inside the thirty-four, and every downstream phase that scoped itself against the
old position is scoped short.

| Work this creates | Phase | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| The seven record types enter the data integrity risk assessment as records in scope, with the systems that hold them | **Phase 03**, `MS-07` | Priyanka Venkataraman |
| The validation framework's treatment of dynamic records has to reach records nobody had previously placed in scope | **Phase 04**, `MS-08` | Colm Ó Braonáin |
| Access, audit trail and signature work has to cover the systems that hold them | **Phase 05**, `MS-09` | Joachim Reuter |
| The record retention and archive position for dynamic records has to be examined, because a printout that cannot reproduce the record cannot be the retained copy either | **Phase 07** | Amara Sissoko |

**One boundary is drawn here explicitly.** Three of the seven are chromatography data system record types
and bear on the subject matter of `OBS-2`. **`02.06` records that link and draws no conclusion from it**,
and neither does this chapter: what it means for `OBS-2` is Phase 06's and Phase 08's, and nothing in a
cost chapter is entitled to anticipate it.

**What the seven do not create is a compliance conclusion.** A record type that is in scope is a record
type the requirements reach. Whether Helix meets those requirements for it has not been assessed, by
anybody, at this vantage.

## 3. Consequence two — three systems whose position under `21 CFR 11.10` has never been assessed

**The three vendor-hosted systems were classified open on the wrong test and are closed systems —
`02.07-open-and-closed.md`, `ADR-0011`.** The measures Helix deployed for them are not wrong and are not
removed. What was wrong is that they were **recorded as compliance with `21 CFR 11.30`**, which requires
open-system users to employ procedures and controls that **include those identified in `21 CFR 11.10`, as
appropriate**, *and* additional measures — **the additional measures are additive to the `§11.10` controls
and never a substitute for them**, and recording encryption and digital signatures as the whole of a
compliance position leaves the `§11.10` controls unaddressed. So **the eleven paragraphs of
`21 CFR 11.10` have never been assessed for those three systems at all.** That is `IS-09`.

**The cost is an assessment that was never scheduled, of three systems, against eleven paragraphs.**

| Work this creates | Phase | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| The three systems enter Phase 03's assessment as closed systems holding Part 11 records, on the same footing as the rest of the sixty-one | **Phase 03** | Priyanka Venkataraman |
| Their position under each paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10(a)` to `(k)` is examined for the first time — validation, copies, protection of records, access limitation, audit trails, operational and authority checks, device checks, training, accountability policies, systems documentation | **Phases 04 and 05** | Colm Ó Braonáin; Joachim Reuter |
| The hosted arrangement is qualified, including what Helix has read from the vendor and not audited | **Phases 04 and 05** | Amara Sissoko |
| The IT standard that produced the network-position rule is corrected, so the next system classified is classified on the regulation's test | **Phase 04**, policy architecture | Amara Sissoko |

**A note on the envelope, stated precisely.** Phase 01's committed budget carries a line for
**qualification of the vendor-hosted system — $72,000** — in the singular. **The estate contains three
vendor-hosted systems.** No re-costing has been performed at this vantage and none is stated here; the
programme's machinery for a variance is what Phase 01 built — **$56,000 of contingency inside the
committed total, drawn by the Programme Manager and reported, and $188,000 of unallocated headroom
released only by the sponsor** — and any call on it goes to the Data Integrity Steering Committee at
`CAL-01`. **This chapter does not spend it in advance and does not predict that it will be spent.**

## 4. Consequence three — seventeen systems with no validation lifecycle behind them

**Seventeen systems entered the inventory at `02.02 §2.1` that had never been in the register, and none of
them had ever entered a validation lifecycle.** That is a statement about the absence of a lifecycle. It
is **not** a statement about the condition of any of the seventeen, and this repository does not convert
one into the other.

**The obligation it engages is not Part 11's.** It is **`21 CFR 211.68`**, which requires appropriate
controls over computer or related systems used in the manufacture, processing, packing and holding of a
drug product, and requires input and output to be checked for accuracy with a written record of the
program and appropriate validation data. **That obligation exists whatever FDA does about Part 11** —
enforcement discretion is a stated intention, not an exemption, and `ADR-0003` fixed the programme's
position on it in Phase 01.

| Work this creates | Phase | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Each of the seventeen is assessed for GxP relevance and record types — **done**, in this phase | Phase 02 | Priyanka Venkataraman |
| Those of the seventeen that are GxP-relevant enter the validation framework's population, with no prior lifecycle documentation to build on | **Phase 04**, `MS-08` | Colm Ó Braonáin |
| Documentation that would ordinarily exist — an intended use statement, a configuration record, a change history — has to be established rather than retrieved | **Phase 04** | Colm Ó Braonáin |
| The procurement and commissioning routes that let a system arrive without an entry are closed, so the next one does not | **Phase 04**, policy architecture | Amara Sissoko |

**The fourth row is the one that pays for itself.** Assessing seventeen systems is finite work. A route by
which systems enter the estate unobserved is a generator, and closing it is the only intervention in this
chapter that reduces future work rather than performing it.

## 5. What this does to the schedule between `MS-06` and `MS-09`

**Nothing moves.** The three milestones between this vantage and the end of Phase 05 stand as published,
and what follows is a statement of where the new work lands inside them.

| Milestone | Date | Days from the previous milestone | What Phase 02's findings add to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`MS-06`** — Phase 02 complete | **2026-07-31** | — | **This vantage** |
| **`MS-07`** — Phase 03 complete: data integrity risk assessment and the ALCOA attributes | **2026-09-30** | **61** | Seven record types and three systems that no earlier scope statement carried, entering an assessment that has to be complete over the thirty-four record types and the sixty-one systems |
| **`MS-08`** — Phase 04 complete: validation framework and policy architecture | **2026-11-30** | **61** | A framework that has to reach dynamic records, the seventeen with no lifecycle behind them, and the two policy gaps at `§3` and `§4` — and the procedure `IS-10` says does not yet exist |
| **`MS-09`** — Phase 05 complete: access, audit trails and electronic signatures | **2027-01-29** | **60** | Three systems assessed against `21 CFR 11.10` for the first time, plus the systems holding the seven record types |

**Three observations about those windows, none of them a forecast.**

**The work is front-loaded into Phase 03 and it is the phase with the least room to absorb it.** Sixty-one
days, an assessment whose population is now larger than the one anybody had in mind when the phase was
planned, and a dependency on the determination this phase has just produced. `AS-03`, the load-bearing
assumption from Phase 01, was that the record-level determination could be completed **from the existing
estate documentation plus targeted technical review**, inside the window to `MS-06`. **It held on its date
and failed on its method**: the determination was delivered inside the window, and it could not be made
from the existing documentation — the register was wrong by twenty-six entries in both directions and a
physical walk was required. That is why `ADR-0009` exists. **An assumption that held only in part
transfers to the next window not at all**, and nothing here asserts that it does.

**The policy work at Phase 04 has grown in a way that is easy to miss.** Three separate gaps now need a
procedure: the maintenance of the determination (`IS-10`), the classification rule the IT standard got
wrong (`§3`), and the routes by which a system can enter the estate without an entry (`§4`). None of them
is large on its own. All three land in the same phase, and all three are procedural work rather than
technical work, which competes for a different and scarcer kind of attention.

**The tail is unchanged and remains tight by construction.** `01.11 §8` records twenty-one days between
`MS-11` and `MS-12`, twenty-one between `MS-12` and `MS-13`, and eleven days from the opening of the
independent third-party audit to `MS-12`. **Nothing in Phase 02 lengthens or shortens that tail**, and
`PR-04` from Phase 01 continues to carry it.

## 6. The programme risks these three consequences carry

**Phase 02 opens eight programme risks, `PR-11` to `PR-18`, and `logs/raid-log.md` is the controlled
register that holds all eight.** The six below are the ones this chapter's three consequences produce, and
they are cited by the register's numbering rather than by any numbering of this chapter's own. The other
two — **`PR-11`**, that the seven newly in-scope record types are read inside Helix as a criticism of the
laboratory, and **`PR-16`**, that an instrument behind one of the four surviving printout record types is
replaced — arise from `02.06`'s determination and are carried in the RAID log.

**None of the eight carries a likelihood, an impact or a score, and none ever will.** A programme risk is a
thing that is not true now and would be a problem if it became true. Scoring belongs to the data integrity
risk assessment, which is Phase 03's and does not exist at this vantage.

and two of the risks it describes were found to have no entry in the RAID log. They were opened as new
identifiers rather than by overwriting an existing one.

**`PR-12` is the one to watch, because half of it is the risk created by getting something right.** A
correction that removes a label people associated with extra effort is read, by somebody, as permission to
do less. The correction did the opposite: it replaced a control set that had been recorded as sufficient
with a control set that has never been assessed.

## 7. What this chapter refuses to say

Three sentences a chapter like this is expected to contain, and the reason each is absent.

**"The programme is at risk of missing `MS-07`."** Not stated. No milestone has moved, no window has been
re-planned, and a programme that publishes a forecast of its own slippage two months before the date has
substituted a mood for a status. `PR-14`, `PR-17` and `PR-18` name what would have to be true for the work
not to fit, which is the statement that can be checked.

**"Helix was non-compliant with `21 CFR 11.10` for three systems."** Not stated, and it would be a legal
conclusion this repository does not reach. **The position is unassessed, and unassessed is not adverse.**
`IS-09` says exactly that and no more.

**"The seven record types were being kept out of scope deliberately."** Not stated and not implied.
Eleven record types were treated as out of scope on a reading of the printout route that is common in this
industry and that this phase found to be wrong for seven of them. **Nothing in this repository
characterises any Helix employee, any auditor or any FDA investigator**, and nothing here is an
adverse statement about anybody's work.

## 8. What this chapter does not do

- **It moves no milestone and forecasts no slip.**
- **It scores nothing.** Phase 03 owns the assessment and its scale.
- **It restates no determination.** `02.02`, `02.06`, `02.07` and `02.08` own theirs.
- **It states no validation status for any system**, and no assessment of any system against any
  requirement.
- **It re-costs nothing and states no new figure.** The envelope, the committed total, the contingency and
  the headroom are Phase 01's and are unchanged.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind**, characterises nothing as a violation, and forecasts
  nothing about any future inspection.
