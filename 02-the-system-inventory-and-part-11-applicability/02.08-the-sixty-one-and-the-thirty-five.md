## 1. The split, and what it is a split of

`02.03-what-makes-a-system-gxp-relevant.md` determined that **96** of the estate's systems are
GxP-relevant. This chapter divides those 96 on a different question.

**61 hold at least one of the 34 Part 11 record types. 35 are GxP-relevant and hold none. 61 + 35 = 96.**

The 61 are in scope **because of the record types they hold** — `ADR-0001`, `ADR-0013` — and the table at
§3 names the record types for every one of them. The 35 are the chapter's argument and §4 is where it is
made.

**GxP-relevance and Part 11 applicability are two different questions and the second is not a subset test
run on the first.** They happen to be nested here because Part 11's reach starts from a predicate rule and
a record required by a predicate rule is a GxP record. The nesting is a consequence, not a definition, and
a reader who treats the two as the same question will get the 35 wrong.

## 2. How a system got into the 61

One rule, applied mechanically once the record-type determination existed.

> **A system is in the 61 if it holds, creates, modifies, maintains, archives, retrieves or transmits at
> least one instance of at least one of the 34 record types determined to be Part 11 records at
> `02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md`.**

No system was placed in the 61 on any other ground. Not because it is important. Not because it is
expensive. Not because it is a platform, or is validated, or is named in a procedure, or was mentioned in
an inspectional observation. **Inheritance is the only route in**, and that is what `ADR-0001` looks like
when it is applied rather than asserted.

**Seven of the 34 record types entered scope during this phase** — `RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15`, `RT-16`,
`RT-26`, `RT-27` and `RT-33`, at `02.06-the-paper-that-was-not-the-record.md`. Their systems entered with
them. Some of those systems were already in the 61 on other record types; the chromatography data systems
at `SYS-023` and `SYS-024` were already in on `RT-12` and `RT-17`, and gained three record types rather
than a status. Others were not: the warehouse and cold-chain monitoring systems at `SYS-045` and `SYS-046`
would have been in the 61 on `RT-32` alone, and the environmental monitoring systems at `SYS-037` and
`SYS-039` on `RT-25`. **The seven changed the reach of the determination inside systems more than they
changed the count of systems**, and that is a distinction the register carries and a system-level method
could not have expressed.

**The closed and open classification of all 61 is at `02.07-open-and-closed.md`** and is not restated
here. This chapter says which systems hold which record types; it does not classify anything.

because the register records **every** record type a system holds, not only the ones that put it in scope.
`RT-10` sits in the manufacturing execution system alongside five record types that are in scope — the
flagship case at `02.04 §3` — the laboratory instrument booking calendar sits in the LIMS and the quality
department meeting minute sits in the electronic quality management system, and none of them acquires a
status from its neighbours. A register
that showed only the in-scope types would make each of those systems look homogeneous, and the homogeneous
reading is the one `02.04 §3` exists to prevent.

## 4. The thirty-five, which are the chapter's argument

**Thirty-five systems are GxP-relevant and hold no Part 11 record.** They are `SYS-062` to `SYS-096` in
the register at `logs/systems-register.md`.

They are not a residue and they are not an afterthought. **They are the evidence that "GxP system" and
"Part 11 system" name different sets**, and without them `ADR-0001` would be a claim the phase asserted
rather than a rule the phase applied. If every GxP-relevant system had turned out to hold a Part 11 record,
the record-level method would have produced the same answer as the system-level method it refused, at
several times the cost, and a reader would be entitled to ask what it bought.

Six of them, named, with what they do.

**`SYS-062` and `SYS-063` — the tablet hardness and friability tester networks, Ridgemont and Cary.**
They perform in-process physical testing, the results of which are recorded under
`21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)`. They print a ticket carrying the
individual measurements, the mean, the instrument identity and the date and time; the operator signs it
and it is attached to the in-process log. **They retain no retrievable result and have no export
capability.** That is why `RT-09` survives all three conditions of the paper-printout route at
`02.06 §7`, and it is why the testers hold no Part 11 record. They are GxP-relevant, they sit under
`21 CFR 211.68`, and they are not Part 11 systems.

**`SYS-068` — the portable temperature calibrator fleet.** Used by metrology to perform the calibration
checks `21 CFR 211.68(a)` requires written records of. The certificate prints, is filed in the calibration
record, and the calibrator keeps nothing — `RT-29`. The record that *is* a Part 11 record is the
calibration record itself, `RT-19`, and it lives at `SYS-040` and `SYS-050`, not here.

**`SYS-074` and `SYS-075` — the air handling unit local controllers for manufacturing zones A and B.**
They control. They hold setpoints and they act on them. **The data that becomes a record — the
differential pressure, temperature and humidity trend at `RT-27` — is acquired and retained upstream at
`SYS-051`.** A controller that is essential to the environment a product is made in, and that holds no
record required by a predicate rule, is exactly the case the record-level method was built to handle
correctly. Under a system-level method it would have been swept in, and Helix would have spent Phase 05
arguing about audit trails on a setpoint.

**`SYS-087` — the barcode label verifier in the packaging hall.** It checks that a printed code is
readable and correctly formed and rejects what is not. The record of what was inspected and rejected is
`RT-08`, and it is written by the line systems at `SYS-003` to `SYS-006` and the vision systems at
`SYS-009` to `SYS-011`. The verifier itself holds nothing that survives the shift.

**The other twenty-nine are of four kinds**: instruments that print and retain nothing; controllers whose
data becomes a record on another system; local monitoring or alarm units that annunciate without
retaining; and **controllers that hold no record at all, on any system, because the operation they perform
produces none a predicate rule requires**. The fourth kind is the one a three-way split leaves out, and
`SYS-071`, `SYS-076`, `SYS-077`, `SYS-078`, `SYS-080`, `SYS-082`, `SYS-086` and `SYS-088` are in it. The
full list, with the reason each holds no Part 11 record, is at `logs/systems-register.md`.

## 5. What being in the 35 does and does not mean

This is the section most likely to be misread, so it is stated flatly.

| It **does** mean | It **does not** mean |
|---|---|
| No record type held by the system is within `21 CFR Part 11`'s reach under the narrow interpretation | That the system is outside GxP. It is GxP-relevant — `02.03` determined that on its own test |
| No paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` attaches to it **by way of a Part 11 record** | That `21 CFR 211.68` does not reach it. **It does.** `21 CFR 211.68(a)` requires calibration, inspection and checking according to a written programme, with written records maintained; `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires appropriate controls over computer or related systems |
| That Phase 05's Part 11 control work is not scoped to it | That it will not be validated. Validation obligation arises under `21 CFR 211.68`, not under Part 11, and enforcement discretion over `21 CFR 11.10(a)` changes nothing about it — `ADR-0003` |
| That the determination has an answer for it, written down, with a determiner and a date | That the answer is permanent. Replace an instrument with one that retains data and the answer changes — `ADR-0015`, `PR-16` |

**The last row is the live one.** Several of the 35 are out of Part 11 scope because of what an instrument
does not do. That is a fact about a purchase order from some earlier year, not a property of the process,
and it will stop being true at some point without anybody deciding that it should. `AS-14` records the
assumption and `ADR-0015` sets the trigger that is supposed to catch its failure.

## 6. What the split does for the phases that follow

| Phase | Uses the 61 for | Uses the 35 for |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 03** — data integrity risk assessment, `MS-07` | The assessment population, expressed as record types held by systems | Included where a GxP record's integrity depends on them, and the assessment says so on its own terms |
| **Phase 04** — validation framework | Intended use expressed as record types held | Intended use expressed without Part 11 record types. `21 CFR 211.68(b)` is the obligation for both |
| **Phase 05** — controls, audit trails, signatures | The scope boundary of the Part 11 control work | **Out of that boundary**, and the boundary is defensible because the reason is written down per system |
| **Phase 08** — periodic review and internal audit | The population a Part 11 review programme covers | The population it does not, and the reason it does not |

**Phase 05's boundary is the reason this chapter is worth its length.** A control programme that cannot say
why a system is outside it will eventually be asked, and "it did not seem relevant" is not an answer. Thirty-five
rows with a named record-type reason each is an answer.

## 8. What this chapter does not state

- **It classifies no system as closed or open.** `02.07-open-and-closed.md` owns the test and applies it
  to all 61.
- **It states no validation status** for any of the 96, and none is implied by being in the 61 or the 35.
- **It scores nothing.** No criticality, no risk, no priority, no rating. Phase 03, `MS-07`.
- **It assesses no control, no audit trail and no signature.** Phase 05.
- **It says nothing about whether the records in the 61 are any good.** That is `IS-11`, and
  `02.13-phase-summary-and-transition.md` carries it out of the phase.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion** and forecasts no inspection outcome.
