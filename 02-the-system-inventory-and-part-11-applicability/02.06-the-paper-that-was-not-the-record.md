## 1. The sentence that carried eleven record types

Helix had a position on printed records. It was not written down as a determination — no determination
existed, and `02.10-nobody-ever-wrote-it-down.md` establishes that — but it was written down in
procedures, in validation summaries and in the standing understanding of two laboratories, and it took
roughly this form:

> *We print it, the analyst signs the paper, the reviewer signs the paper, and the paper goes in the file.
> The paper is the record.*

**Eleven record types were treated as outside Part 11 on the strength of it.**

The sentence is not absurd. It is a compressed version of something FDA actually says, and the version
that circulates in this industry — *if you print it, Part 11 does not apply* — is a compression of the
compression. What it leaves out is that the route Helix was relying on is a test with **three conditions —
two FDA states expressly and a third Helix derives from the narrow interpretation's second category** —
that the conditions are **conjunctive**, and that the first does almost all of the work. §2 reads the
passage and shows which two are FDA's and where the third comes from.

**This chapter applies that test to all eleven. Seven fail the first condition and are Part 11 records.
Four pass all three and are genuinely out of scope. 7 + 4 = 11**, and the seven sit inside the
thirty-four at `02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md`.

## 2. Where the route comes from, in FDA's own words

FDA's **August 2003 guidance, "Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and
Application"**, is still the agency's current, final, in-force guidance on Part 11 scope. It sets out what
it calls the **narrow interpretation**, and on the printout question it says:

> *"when persons use computers to generate paper printouts of electronic records, and those paper records
> meet all the requirements of the applicable predicate rules and persons rely on the paper records to
> perform their regulated activities, FDA would generally not consider persons to be 'using electronic
> records in lieu of paper records' under §§ 11.2(a) and 11.2(b). In these instances, the use of computer
> systems in the generation of paper records would not trigger part 11."*

Read the sentence for its structure rather than its conclusion. It contains **two express requirements
joined by "and"**, and a third that follows from the guidance's own definition of what a Part 11 record
is: a record maintained electronically **in addition to** paper and **relied on** to perform regulated
activities is a Part 11 record. The route is therefore only available where the electronic record is
**not also** relied on.

**The third condition is Helix's derivation, and it is labelled as Helix's wherever this repository uses
it.** It is not in the passage quoted above, and no document in this phase presents it as FDA's text. The
test Helix operates is therefore **the printout route's three conditions — two FDA states expressly and a
third Helix derives from the narrow interpretation's second category** — and stating it that way makes the
test stronger, not weaker: all three are tested, and the reader can see which is whose. `ADR-0010` fixes
the method and §8 records it.

**The guidance is non-binding by its own terms** — it states FDA's current thinking and creates no rights.
What imposes obligations on Helix are the predicate rules, and they are cited in every row of this chapter.

## 3. The three conditions, two stated and one derived

| # | Condition | The question it asks | Who answers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | The paper record **meets all the requirements of the applicable predicate rules** — **FDA states it expressly** in the passage at §2 | Does the printout contain everything `21 CFR Part 211` requires the record to contain — not most of it, not the part the reviewer looks at, all of it? | The record owner, against the predicate-rule text |
| **2** | The person **relies on the paper record** to perform the regulated activity — **FDA states it expressly** in the passage at §2 | Is the paper what the analyst and the reviewer actually read, check and sign? | Observation of the process, not the procedure's description of it |
| **3** | The electronic record is **not also relied on** — **Helix derives it** from the narrow interpretation's second category, `ADR-0010` | Does anybody go back into the system — to re-check a peak, re-run a query, look at a trend, resolve a query from the quality unit? | Observation, plus what the system is capable of retaining |

**All three must hold, and the derived condition is tested as rigorously as the two FDA states.** Failing
any one leaves the electronic record inside Part 11's reach. The
conditions are not equally hard: two and three are questions about behaviour, answerable by watching what
people do, while **condition one is a question about the record itself** and can only be answered by
reading the predicate rule and then reading the printout.

**The route is not an escape hatch and this programme does not use it as one.** Where it holds it holds,
and §7 says where. Where it does not hold, printing changes nothing at all.

## 4. Condition one, and where the static and dynamic distinction actually comes from

The reason seven record types fail condition one is that **the record is dynamic and a static printout
cannot reproduce it.** Before that sentence is used, its terms have to be attributed correctly, because
this is exactly the point at which portfolios in this field go wrong.

**`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and no more** — *Act*, *Agency*, *Biometrics*, *Closed
system*, *Digital signature*, *Electronic record*, *Electronic signature*, *Handwritten signature*, *Open
system*. **Part 11 defines neither "static record" nor "dynamic record".** It does not define "audit
trail" either. Anybody who tells you the static/dynamic distinction is a Part 11 concept has not opened
Part 11.

**The distinction comes from FDA's December 2018 guidance, "Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP:
Questions and Answers"**, which applies to `21 CFR Parts 210, 211 and 212` — drug CGMP, and so the
guidance a drug manufacturer works to. It puts the distinction in two short sentences:

> *"Static is used to indicate a fixed-data record such as a paper record or an electronic image."*

> *"Dynamic means that the record format allows interaction between the user and the record content."*

**That is the whole of it, and it is enough.** If the format allows the user to interact with the content
— to change what is integrated, to change what is plotted, to change what is queried — then the content a
user sees is one of many the record can produce. A printout captures one of them. The predicate rule
asks for the record.

**Condition one is where the two ideas meet.** `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` requires laboratory records to
include *"a complete record of all data secured in the course of each test, including all graphs, charts,
and spectra from laboratory instrumentation"*. A static rendering of a dynamic data set is **a** graph. It
is not *all data secured in the course of the test*, because the data that would support a different
rendering is not on the page. **The printout does not meet all the requirements of the applicable
predicate rule, and condition one fails.** Printing more pages does not fix it; the missing thing is not
volume, it is the interaction.

## 6. The seven, each argued

### 6.1 A chromatogram that can be reintegrated — `RT-13`

Integration is a decision, not a measurement. Where a baseline is drawn, where a peak starts and stops,
whether a shoulder is split, whether a valley is dropped — each of these changes the area, and the area
becomes the result. A chromatography data system records the integration parameters applied, permits them
to be changed, and retains the reprocessing history.

**The printed chromatogram shows one integration.** It cannot show that a different one was applied
earlier, cannot show what a different one would produce, and cannot answer the question a reviewer under
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` is there to answer — whether the original records have been reviewed for accuracy
and completeness. **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` asks for all data secured in the course of the test.** The
integration record is part of that data.

**And integration is a calculation as much as it is data.** Where the baseline is drawn determines the
area, and the area determines the reported result, so the integration parameters are a step in arriving at
the number rather than a presentational choice about it. **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires a record of all
calculations performed in connection with the test**, and a printed chromatogram showing one integration
records neither the parameters applied nor the arithmetic they drove. `RT-15` is anchored on `(a)(5)` for
exactly this reasoning at §6.3, and `RT-13` takes `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` and `(a)(5)` together for the same
reason. Condition one fails.

### 6.2 Detector spectral and peak-purity data — `RT-14`

A diode-array detector acquires a spectrum across the whole of a peak. Peak purity is assessed by
comparing spectra taken at different points on it. The assessment is an interaction: the analyst selects
points, applies thresholds, and looks at the comparison.

**A printed purity plot is the output of one set of selections.** The underlying spectra are the data, and
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` names *spectra from laboratory instrumentation* expressly. Condition one fails.

### 6.3 Calibration curve and result recalculation — `RT-15`

The result is calculated from a curve, and the curve is fitted. Weighting, the inclusion or exclusion of a
calibration level, the choice of fit — each changes the response factor and therefore the reported value.
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires *a record of all calculations performed in connection with the test,
including units of measure, conversion factors, and equivalency factors.*

**A printed result sheet gives the number that came out.** It does not give the calculation as an object
that can be checked, and it cannot show what an alternative fit would have produced. Condition one fails.

### 6.4 A dissolution profile — `RT-16`

A profile is a set of measurements across vessels and time points. A reviewer plots it, compares units,
looks at an outlier vessel, and applies a similarity comparison over a selected set of points. Every one
of those is an interaction with the content.

**A printed profile is one plot over one selection.** `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` asks for all data secured.
Condition one fails.

### 6.5 A viable-count trend that can be re-ranged — `RT-26`

An environmental monitoring trend is not a picture. It is a query over a data set with a date range, a
location or zone grouping, and alert and action limits drawn on it. Change the range and a signal appears
or disappears. Change the grouping and an out-of-trend location is averaged away or is not. **A printed
trend is one range over one grouping at one moment**, and the data that would support a different range or
a different grouping is not on the page.

The record obligation is `21 CFR 211.100(b)`, which requires production and control activities to be
documented at the time of performance, read with `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)`, under which the batch record
documents in-process and laboratory control results. **`21 CFR 211.113(a)` is the substantive condition
this record evidences; it requires written procedures designed to prevent objectionable microorganisms in
drug products not required to be sterile, it specifies no record content, and the determination does not
treat it as if it did. The record obligation, and therefore condition one, runs through
`21 CFR 211.100(b)`.** One rendering of a re-rangeable data set does not document the control activity as
performed. Condition one fails.

### 6.6 A pressure, temperature and humidity trend that can be re-ranged — `RT-27`

The same shape, on a different data set and a different substantive rule. Differential pressure,
temperature and humidity are trended across manufacturing, warehouse and controlled-storage areas, and the
trend is a query: a range, a zone grouping and a set of limits drawn over stored readings. **Change any of
the three and the picture changes**, which is the definition of a record the user interacts with.

The record obligation is again `21 CFR 211.100(b)` read with `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)`. **`21 CFR 211.46(b)`
is the substantive condition this record evidences; it is an equipment-and-facility requirement for
adequate ventilation and, where appropriate, control of air pressure, humidity and temperature, it
specifies no record content, and the determination does not treat it as if it did. The record obligation,
and therefore condition one, runs through `21 CFR 211.100(b)`.** A single printed range does not document
the conditions as they held across the operation. Condition one fails.

### 6.7 A search result that depends on the query — `RT-33`

A warehouse temperature excursion search is a query. Its result is a function of the threshold, the date
range, the sensor set and the duration filter chosen by the person running it. Two people asking about the
same week can produce two different lists, both correct for their query.

**A printed search result is an answer to one question that is not itself printed on the page.** The record
obligation is `21 CFR 211.100(b)`, which requires production and control activities to be documented at the
time of performance. **`21 CFR 211.142(b)` is the substantive condition this record evidences — written
procedures describing warehousing, including storage of drug products under appropriate conditions of
temperature, humidity and light so that identity, strength, quality and purity are not affected. It
specifies no record content, and the determination does not treat it as if it did. The record obligation,
and therefore condition one, runs through `21 CFR 211.100(b)`.** A result that cannot be re-derived, because
the query that produced it is not part of it, does not document the activity it purports to document.
Condition one fails.

### The pattern

**All seven are the same failure.** In each case the paper is a rendering, and the record is the thing the
rendering was made from. The distinguishing feature is not that the record is important, or complex, or
laboratory-generated. It is that **the format allows interaction between the user and the content** — the
2018 guidance's own words — and a fixed-data output of an interactive record is not the record.

## 7. The four that survive, and the facts they rest on

A chapter that reports only the failures has not applied a test; it has announced a result. **Four record
types were tested on the same three conditions, two stated and one derived, and all three hold. They are
out of scope and the
determination says so.**

| ID | Record type | Predicate rule | Why condition 1 holds | Why conditions 2 and 3 hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RT-09` | Tablet hardness and friability test printout | `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)`, read with `21 CFR 211.110(a)` and `(c)` | `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)` requires the batch record to document in-process and laboratory control results. The printed ticket carries the individual measurements, the mean, the instrument identity, the date and time, and the sample identity — the whole of what is required to be recorded of this in-process control | The operator signs the ticket and it is attached to the in-process log, which is what the reviewer reads. **The instruments retain no retrievable result and have no export capability** |
| `RT-22` | Analytical balance weighing printout | `21 CFR 211.194(a)(3)` | The provision requires a statement of the weight or measure of sample used for each test. The printed ticket carries the mass, the balance identity, the date and time, and the analyst's identification | The ticket is affixed to the worksheet and countersigned. **The balances hold no record after the ticket prints** |
| `RT-23` | Manual pH and conductivity measurement printout | `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)` | The measurement is a single value with its temperature and its calibration status. There is no underlying data set from which a different value could be rendered — **the record is static in the 2018 guidance's own sense** | The printout is attached to the worksheet and signed. **The bench meters in both laboratories have no storage and no export port** |
| `RT-29` | Portable temperature calibrator certificate printout | `21 CFR 211.68(a)`, read with `21 CFR 211.160(b)(4)` | The certificate carries the as-found and as-left values, the reference standard, the tolerance, the date and the technician, which satisfies the written-record obligation at `21 CFR 211.68(a)`, read with the programme content required by `21 CFR 211.160(b)(4)`. **Neither paragraph prescribes certificate content**; **condition one holds because the certificate is a fixed record of a single check with no underlying data set from which a different result could be rendered**, not because the certificate matches a prescribed list | The certificate is filed in the calibration record, which is the record relied on. **The calibrators hold no retained certificate** |

**On `RT-09` and what `21 CFR 211.110(a)` actually requires.** Tablet hardness and friability are not among
the six in-process controls enumerated at `21 CFR 211.110(a)(1)` to `(6)`; they are in-process controls
under the paragraph's non-exhaustive opener, and the register says so rather than implying the paragraph
names them. The paragraph requires **written procedures** describing the in-process controls, tests or
examinations to be conducted, and it specifies no record content. **What the record must carry comes from
`21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)`, and that is what condition one is tested against** — a rule that requires no record
cannot be failed, or passed, on the content of one.

**These four survive on facts, and the facts are recorded rather than assumed.** Each instrument's
capability was checked during the walk of the estate and is evidenced at `EV-223`. `AS-14` records the
assumption in the register: that the instruments behind these four record types retain no retrievable
electronic record and have no export capability.

**And the assumption is the point of failure to watch.** Replace a bench pH meter with a model that logs
to a network share, and `RT-23`'s condition three stops holding the day the new meter is installed —
without anybody making a decision, writing a document or noticing. `PR-16` records that, and
`ADR-0015`'s review trigger, named at `02.09-the-systems-register.md §5`, is supposed to catch it.

**Note what the four have in common and the seven do not.** In all four, the instrument produces a fixed
output and keeps nothing: there is no interaction to lose, because there is nothing left to interact with.
That is the narrow circumstance in which the printout route is honest, and it is much narrower than the
sentence at §1 suggests.

## 8. `ADR-0010` — the route is tested, never asserted

**`DEC-210`, 2026-07-07, Dr Sunita Raghunathan.**

**Decision.** Where a paper printout is said to take a record type out of Part 11 scope, **the printout
route's three conditions — two FDA states expressly and a third Helix derives from the narrow
interpretation's second category** — are tested individually, evidenced individually and recorded
individually. The route is never asserted, never inferred from the existence of a printout, and never
applied to a record type without a statement of whether the record is static or dynamic and why.

**Refused:** carrying forward the eleven on the existing position, which was cheaper and would have been
defensible right up to the moment somebody asked to see the test. **Refused equally:** declaring all
eleven in scope without testing, which would have put four record types and their instruments into a
control programme they do not need — the same error as the original, made in the opposite direction.

**Cost accepted:** eleven separate test sheets, four instrument capability statements, and seven record
types entering scope that no budget line carried. `02.12-what-the-determination-costs.md` states what that
does to the work.

## 9. `IS-08`, and the `OBS-2` link recorded without a conclusion

**`IS-08` is the issue this chapter creates.** Seven record types were Part 11 records throughout the
period in which Helix treated them as outside Part 11, and nobody had said so because no determination
existed to say it in. Its full text is at `logs/raid-log.md §1`.

**Three of the seven — `RT-13`, `RT-14` and `RT-15` — are chromatography data system record types.**

`OBS-2`, an inspectional observation on the Form FDA 483 issued at the close of the inspection of
2026-02-02 to 2026-02-13, concerns audit trails on the chromatography data system not being reviewed as
part of laboratory record review. **The programme records that the three sit on the same system as the
subject matter of `OBS-2`. It draws no conclusion from it.**

That refusal is deliberate and it is worth stating why, because the inference is available and it is
tempting.

**First, the phase has not looked at any audit trail.** Whether the chromatography data system's audit
trail records what `21 CFR 11.10(e)` describes is not established here and is not established anywhere in
Phase 02. **Phase 05** examines audit trails; **Phase 06** is the chapter on the chromatography data
system itself.

**Second, a Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding.** It records conditions that in the investigator's
judgment may constitute violations, is not a final agency determination of whether any condition is in
violation of anything, and is not an all-inclusive list. This chapter takes `OBS-2` as recorded, not as
adjudicated, and reaches no legal conclusion of any kind about it or about anything else.

**Third, the review obligation `OBS-2` concerns does not come from Part 11 at all.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)`
requires audit trails to be generated, retained and available to the agency; it imposes no internal review
duty. The duty comes from the predicate rules — `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` requires *"the initials or
signature of a second person showing that the original records have been reviewed"* for laboratory
records, and `21 CFR 211.192` requires quality unit review of production and control records. That
obligation was Helix's whatever the Part 11 answer was, and it did not become Helix's on 2026-07-07.

**Fourth, connecting the two here would answer a question this phase is not entitled to answer.**
`02.11-what-this-does-not-establish.md` owns that discipline and argues the boundary rather than merely
observing it. **Phase 06 and Phase 08 are where the `OBS-2` link is picked up.**

## 10. What this chapter does not establish

- **It does not establish that any record is inaccurate, incomplete or unreliable.** It establishes which
  records Part 11 reaches. Applicability is not integrity — Phase 03, `MS-07`.
- **It does not assess any audit trail**, control, signature or configuration. Phase 05.
- **It does not state or imply a validation status** for the chromatography data system or anything else.
- **It scores nothing** — no risk, no likelihood, no impact, no criticality.
- **It says nothing about how the prior position arose**, and characterises no person's work.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, does not characterise Helix's past treatment of the eleven as a
  violation of anything, and forecasts nothing about any future inspection.
- **It does not present the printout route as unavailable.** Four record types use it correctly and are published as out of scope with their reasons — `ADR-0014`, `02.05 §9`.
