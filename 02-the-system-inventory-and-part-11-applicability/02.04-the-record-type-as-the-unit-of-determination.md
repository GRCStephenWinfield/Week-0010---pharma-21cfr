## 1. What this chapter settles

Phase 01 decided **how** the determination would be made and did not make it. `ADR-0001`, taken at
`DEC-104` on 2026-03-13, fixed that Part 11 applicability is determined **record by record, not system by
system**. That is a method, and a method is not yet an instrument. Between "record by record" and a
completed register there is a question nobody had answered: **what, exactly, is the thing being
determined?**

A record is a single object — one batch record for one batch, one chromatogram for one injection. There
are millions of them at Helix and there will be millions more. A determination made one record at a time
is not a determination; it is a census that never finishes and is out of date before it does. **The unit
had to be the class, not the instance.**

This chapter fixes that unit as the **record type**, records the decision at `ADR-0013` / `DEC-205`,
explains why the obvious alternative produces a wrong answer in both directions, and sets out how a record
type was defined and bounded so the enumeration could be finite and checkable.

**It does not give the enumeration or the split.** `02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md` owns those, and
`02.06-the-paper-that-was-not-the-record.md` owns the paper-printout test that moved seven of them.

## 2. The argument: a record type is the unit, and a system inherits its scope

**This section owns this argument for the repository.**

**A record type is a class of record that shares one predicate-rule position, one lifecycle and one set of
reliance facts.** The electronic batch production and control record is a record type. The chromatographic
integration and reprocessing record is a record type. Every instance of the type has the same answer to
the four questions the narrow interpretation asks, because those questions are asked of the class and not
of the instance.

From that, a second proposition follows and it is the one that does the work in the rest of the phase:

> **A system is in Part 11 scope because of the record types it holds. It has no scope of its own.**

That sentence is `ADR-0001` restated at the level of the instrument. Scope originates at the record type,
is determined once for the type, and is then **inherited** by every system that holds an instance of it. A
system that holds one Part 11 record type is in scope for that record type. A system that holds none is
not in scope, however important it is, however expensive it was and however carefully it is validated
under `21 CFR 211.68(b)`.

Three consequences the programme now lives with.

**Determination is done once and reused.** Fifty-eight determinations were made. They then propagated
across an estate of two hundred and twenty-two systems without being re-argued, because the argument
belongs to the type. `02.02-the-inventory-corrected.md` establishes the estate; the determination did not
have to be repeated for each entry in it.

**Two systems holding the same record type get the same answer.** The Ridgemont and Cary chromatography
data systems hold the same record types. Nothing about their size, their user population or their site
changes the determination, because the determination was not made about them.

**A system's answer changes when its record types change, and only then.** That is what makes the
determination maintainable, and it is why `ADR-0015` sets the review trigger on a **change in the record
types a system holds** rather than on a calendar date. `02.09-the-systems-register.md` names the trigger.

## 3. Why determining system by system produces a wrong answer in both directions

The alternative was available, is faster, and is how most inventories in this industry are built: walk the
estate, mark each system in or out, publish the list. It was refused at `DEC-104` before anybody knew what
the answer would be, and Phase 02 has now seen both of its failure modes in Helix's own estate.

**It is wrong in the over-inclusive direction.** A system marked "a Part 11 system" acquires the label for
everything it holds. The manufacturing execution system holds the electronic batch production and control
record required by `21 CFR 211.188`. It also holds a shift handover note that no predicate rule requires.
Under the system-level method, the handover note inherits the batch record's control set — and the
organisation then argues about whether a handover note needs an audit trail under `21 CFR 11.10(e)`. That
argument cannot be won, because the premise is wrong. The cost is not only wasted control effort. It is
that a control set applied to everything is a control set nobody can defend the boundaries of, and a
boundary nobody can defend is one that gets moved under pressure.

**It is wrong in the under-inclusive direction, and this is the direction that cost Helix something.** A
system marked out of scope takes everything it holds out with it. Helix had treated eleven record types as
outside Part 11 because they were printed and the paper was said to be the record. Some of those record
types sit inside systems that were, on any reading, laboratory systems. Under a system-level method the
question stops at the system: the system was said to be covered by the printout arrangement, so the record
types inside it were never examined one at a time. **Testing at the record-type level is what surfaced
that seven of the eleven had been Part 11 records all along** —
`02.06-the-paper-that-was-not-the-record.md` sets out the test and the seven.

**The two failure modes are the same failure.** A system-level determination asks a question about the
wrong object and then applies the answer to objects it was never asked about. It over-covers what it
catches and under-covers what it misses, and neither error is visible from the list it produces, because
the list has one row per system and the mistake is inside the row.

| Method | What is asked | What is inherited | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **System by system** | Is this system a Part 11 system? | Every record it holds inherits the system's answer | **Both directions.** Non-predicate records acquire controls; predicate records inside an "out" system are never examined |
| **Record type by record type** — `ADR-0013` | Is this record type a Part 11 record? | Every system holding the type inherits the type's answer | Enumeration must be complete, and completeness has to be argued — §5 |

## 4. Why `ADR-0001` was set in Phase 01, before anybody knew the answer

`DEC-104` was taken on 2026-03-13, eleven days into the programme. At that date Helix had no inventory, no
record enumeration and no determination of any kind. `IS-02` had been raised the same day. **Nobody
involved knew how many record types there were, how many would be in scope, or that the printed eleven
would be re-opened at all.**

That sequencing was deliberate and it is the reason the method carries any weight now.

A method chosen **after** the answer is visible is a method chosen for its answer. Had Helix enumerated
first, seen that eleven record types would come back into scope on a record-level test, and only then
decided whether to test at record level, every reader of this repository would be entitled to ask which
way the decision would have gone if the number had been forty rather than eleven. The question does not
arise, because **the method was fixed on 2026-03-13 and the enumeration was approved on 2026-06-30, three
and a half months later**, and the method is minuted at `GOV-02`.

**The same discipline is why the seven are reported rather than absorbed.** A programme that sets its
method in advance has to publish what the method finds, including the findings that are inconvenient and
the ones that were nobody's plan. `02.06` reports the seven; `02.12-what-the-determination-costs.md`
reports what they cost.

## 5. How a record type was defined and bounded

An enumeration is only useful if two people applying the definition arrive at the same list. Four rules
were fixed at `DEC-206` and applied throughout.

**Rule one — one predicate-rule position per type.** If two candidate records are required by different
predicate-rule provisions, or one is required and the other is not, they are different record types. This
is what separates the analytical result record required by `21 CFR 211.194(a)` from the laboratory
instrument booking calendar that no provision requires, even though the same analysts create both in the
same week.

**Rule two — one set of reliance facts per type.** If two candidate records differ in whether the
electronic form is relied on to perform the regulated activity, they are different types. This rule is
what made the printout question answerable at all: it forces the reliance facts into the definition of the
type rather than leaving them as an attribute of the system.

**Rule three — bounded by the business name, not by the table name.** A record type is named as the
business names it — *batch production and control record*, *system suitability test record*. It is not
named after a database object, a file extension or a screen. Names taken from the implementation change
when the implementation changes, and a determination whose subject changes name has to be made again.

**Rule four — a type is not split by site, by instance or by product.** The Ridgemont and Cary
laboratories both hold analytical result records. That is one record type held in two places, not two
record types. Splitting by site would have doubled the enumeration and would have invited two different
answers to one question.

**What a record type deliberately is not.** It is not a system, not a module, not a screen, not a report,
not a document class in a document management system, and not a folder. Each of those is an
implementation of one or more record types, and each is capable of holding several types at once — which
is exactly the condition §3 describes.

**Where the rules produced a judgement call, the judgement is recorded.** Two examples, both visible in the
register at `logs/record-type-register.md`. The yield and reconciliation statement was **merged** into the
electronic batch production and control record, because `21 CFR 211.188(b)(7)` makes it a required content
element of that record rather than a record in its own right. The chromatographic integration and
reprocessing record was **kept separate** from the chromatographic raw data file, because the reliance
facts differ: the raw data is acquired once and the integration record is created, and can be re-created,
by a reviewer afterwards. Rule two therefore separates them, and `02.06` is where that separation earns
its keep.

## 6. The four questions asked of every record type

The determination instrument is the form at
`templates/record-type-determination-template.md`, and it asks the narrow interpretation's questions in a
fixed order. The order matters: the first question is dispositive if the answer is no.

| # | Question | Source | If the answer is no |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the record **required to be maintained or submitted under a predicate rule**? | `21 CFR 11.1(b)`; FDA's August 2003 Scope and Application guidance | **Stop. It is not a Part 11 record**, whatever format it is held in and however important it is to Helix |
| 2 | Is it maintained **in electronic format in place of paper**, or in addition to paper **and relied on** to perform the regulated activity? | The August 2003 guidance's narrow interpretation | Test the paper-printout route against **the three conditions, two stated and one derived** — `02.06` |
| 3 | Is it **submitted to FDA** in electronic format, where the record type is one the agency accepts electronically? | `21 CFR 11.1(b)` and `21 CFR 11.2(b)(2)` — the document type must be one identified in public docket No. 92S-0251 | Not dispositive on its own |
| 4 | Does it carry an **electronic signature intended to be the equivalent of a handwritten signature required by a predicate rule**? | `21 CFR 11.3(b)(7)`; `21 CFR 11.50` | Not dispositive on its own |

**Question one is asked first because it is the only one that can end the enquiry.** FDA is explicit that
records not required to be retained under predicate rules, but nonetheless maintained electronically, are
not Part 11 records. Twenty of the twenty-four out-of-scope record types stop at question one, and
`02.05` publishes each with its reason — `ADR-0014`.

**A negative answer removes nothing else.** A record type determined not to be a Part 11 record still sits
in a computerised system, and `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires appropriate controls over that system whatever
the Part 11 answer is. The determination is about the reach of one part. It is not a statement that
anything is unregulated, and no row in any register in this phase may be read that way.

## 7. What the unit does for the phases that follow

| Phase | What it receives | Why the unit matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 03** — data integrity risk assessment, `MS-07` | The population to be assessed: the record types determined in scope, and the systems that hold them | An assessment scoped to systems would assess the shift handover note alongside the batch record. Scoped to record types, it assesses what a predicate rule requires |
| **Phase 04** — validation framework | The intended use each system is validated against, expressed as the record types it holds | `21 CFR 211.68(b)` reaches every system. The record types tell the framework what each one is **for** |
| **Phase 05** — controls, audit trails, signatures | The paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` that attach, per record type | A control specified per record type can be evidenced. A control specified per system can only be asserted |
| **Phase 07** — the paper question | The four record types where the printout route survives, and the facts each survival depends on | `02.06 §7` states those facts. Phase 07 is where they are tested again |

## 8. The series

**`RT-01` to `RT-58`.** Fifty-eight record types were enumerated **across the estate**, with the
enumeration driven from the GxP-relevant systems and **extended to the record types held elsewhere so that
every exclusion is published with its reason** — `ADR-0014`. **Forty-one of the fifty-eight are held on the
ninety-six; seventeen are held only on systems determined not to be GxP-relevant.** Each carries a named
determiner, a date, a quality unit reviewer and a completed determination form. The series is
opened here, published at `02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md`, and held as a controlled register at
`logs/record-type-register.md`.

**`RT-14` means the same record type in Phase 09 as it does here.** The series is never renumbered and
never restarted. Where a record type is retired, its identifier is retired with it and is not reissued;
where a new one is recognised, it takes the next free number. That rule exists so that a control specified
in Phase 05 against `RT-14` still means something when somebody reads it two years later.

**The determination is a controlled document with a named owner and a review trigger** — `ADR-0015`,
`DEC-215`. `02.09-the-systems-register.md` names the trigger and states what the register carries.

## 9. What this chapter does not do

- **It does not give the split.** How many of the fifty-eight are Part 11 records is `02.05`'s.
- **It does not argue the paper-printout test or name the seven.** `02.06`.
- **It does not state the inventory arithmetic.** `02.02` owns the estate; `02.03` owns the GxP split;
  `02.08` owns the split of the GxP-relevant estate between systems that hold a Part 11 record and systems
  that do not.
- **It classifies no system as closed or open.** `02.07-open-and-closed.md`.
- **It scores nothing** — no risk, no criticality, no rating. Phase 03.
- **It states no validation status for any system**, and being in or out of Part 11 scope is not one.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome.
