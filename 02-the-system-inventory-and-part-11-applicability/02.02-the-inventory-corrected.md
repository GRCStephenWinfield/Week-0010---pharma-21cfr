## 1. The number

**214 − 9 + 17 = 222.**

| Line | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| The IT register as it stood when Phase 02 opened | **214** | Entries in the asset and support register maintained by IT and Automation |
| Removed | **−9** | Register entries for systems that no longer exist. The system was gone; the entry was not |
| Added | **+17** | Systems found by the walk that were not in the register at all |
| **The corrected inventory** | **222** | **`SYS-001` to `SYS-222`** |

**The 214 is the register's number, and it was never a Phase 01 determination.** `01.01 §8` states
expressly that Phase 01 does not count the estate, and it gives the reason: a count of computerised
systems is not a count of GxP systems and is not a count of Part 11 systems, and publishing one invites a
reader to treat it as a scope statement. What the programme carried into Phase 02 was the register's
entry count. What it now has is an inventory, and the two differ by twenty-six entries moving in opposite
directions.

**Twenty-six corrections on a base of two hundred and fourteen is not a scandal and this chapter does not
present it as one.** An asset register drifts because asset registers are maintained by transactions and
estates change without them. What matters is not the size of the drift. It is that **nothing in Helix's
quality system would have detected it**, that a determination made on the register would have been made
about a population that was not the population, and that the drift ran in both directions at once — so no
single reconciliation habit, in either direction, would have found all of it.

`02.01-what-an-inventory-is-for.md` sets out the counting rules the walk applied and the method fixed at
`GOV-06` on 2026-06-05. Every figure in this chapter is the product of those rules; a different rule set
would produce a different honest number, which is precisely why the rules were written down first.

## 2. The seventeen, and the nine

### 2.1 The seventeen the register never had

**Seventeen systems were in service, in a Helix building, doing work, and had no entry in the register.**
None was concealed. Each arrived by a route the register's design does not observe, and the routes are
worth naming because they are the routes by which the next one will arrive.

| Route in | What it looks like | Why the register never saw it |
|---|---|---|
| **Bought as equipment rather than as IT** | A controller, workstation and application supplied with an instrument, on a capital equipment line, commissioned by the equipment vendor | There was no IT purchase, no IT project and no support contract to create an entry. The asset that was registered was the instrument |
| **Installed during commissioning and never handed over** | A vendor-supplied application configured at qualification of a piece of plant, left running afterwards | Handover to IT was not a step in the commissioning protocol, so no transaction occurred |
| **Registered as hardware, not as a system** | A workstation present in the register as a PC, running an application that creates and holds records | The register's unit was the purchasable thing. Counting rule 1 counts the function |
| **Grown out of a departmental tool** | A local application maintained by the department that uses it, on infrastructure IT does support | The infrastructure had an entry; the system running on it did not |
| **Supplied as a hosted service by a vendor** | A service consumed under a contract held outside IT. **The walk found no instance of this route, and it is listed because a route with no instance today is not a route that cannot produce one; `ADR-0015`'s review trigger covers it** | No hardware, no licence key and no support tier — nothing the register's entry format was built around |

**No sub-count is published for those routes.** The seventeen are individually identified in
`logs/systems-register.md`, each with the route by which it entered the inventory and the evidence for it
in `logs/evidence-index.md`. Publishing a split by route would put a number on a category the walk did not
set out to measure, and the phase does not manufacture figures it did not determine.

Two things about the seventeen are worth stating plainly and once.

**Every one of them now has a named individual owner.** Several had no owner of any kind before the walk,
in the sense that no individual was accountable for the system as a system. That is `ADR-0008` doing
retrospective work, and `§4` sets out what it cost.

**None of them had ever entered a validation lifecycle.** That is a statement about the absence of a
lifecycle, not a statement about the condition of any system, and it is stated here once because it is a
fact about the inventory. **What it costs in work is
`02.12-what-the-determination-costs.md`'s**, and the obligation it engages is `21 CFR 211.68`, which
applies to Helix's computerised systems whatever FDA does about Part 11 — `ADR-0003`.

### 2.2 The nine that were entries and nothing else

**Nine register entries described systems that no longer exist.** In each case the system had been
removed, replaced or switched off, and the entry survived the event.

| How the entry outlived the system | What the walk found |
|---|---|
| **Replaced in a project that added the successor and never closed the predecessor** | Two entries in the register, one system in the room |
| **Removed in a laboratory or line refit** | The room reconfigured, the equipment gone, the entry still carrying a support tier |
| **Consolidated into another system** | The function absorbed, the original entry left behind with its licence line intact |
| **Switched off and left in place, then removed later** | Neither the switch-off nor the removal generated a transaction the register recognised |

**All nine were gone before the programme kicked off on 2026-03-02.** None was decommissioned during the
programme, none was removed as a consequence of the walk, and nothing in this phase caused a system to
leave the estate.

**Removing an entry is a change to a controlled record and was treated as one.** Each removal is recorded
in `logs/systems-register.md` with the evidence that the system is gone — the project record, the refit
record, or the physical absence confirmed by the area supervisor at the walk — and with the date the
removal was approved. **An inventory that quietly loses entries is a document nobody can audit backwards.**

**One question the nine raise is not answered here.** A system that has been removed may have left records
behind it, and where those records are, in what form, and for how long they must be retained, is a
retention and archive question. `21 CFR 211.180` governs the retention of the records themselves.
**Phase 07 asks that question**, an issue in `logs/raid-log.md` carries it, and this chapter records
only that the systems are gone from the estate.

## 3. The inventory is the estate, not the register

**This section owns that argument for the whole repository.**

When a register and an estate disagree, one of them is wrong, and there is an established organisational
habit about which one gets corrected. **The estate gets corrected.** Somebody is asked to explain the
system that is not on the list, an entry is created to make the list right, and where an entry has no
system behind it the entry is quietly left alone because removing it is work with no visible benefit. The
document is treated as the fact and the world as the exception.

**That habit inverts the relationship, and `ADR-0009` reverses it.**

> **The inventory is a statement about the estate. Where the register and the walk disagree, the register
> is reconciled to the walk, and never the walk to the register.**

The reasoning is short. **The register is a description; the estate is the thing described.** A
description that disagrees with its subject is wrong about its subject, whatever process produced it and
however carefully that process was followed. There is no version of this in which a system that exists
stops existing because it is not written down.

Three practical rules follow, and they bind every later phase.

**A system found in the estate enters the inventory, and the question of how it got there is answered
afterwards.** The entry is not held open pending an explanation. A programme that admits systems only once
their provenance is understood has made its inventory a function of its investigation capacity.

**A register entry with no system behind it is removed, with evidence, and the removal is recorded.** It is
not retained on the ground that removing it would leave a gap in the numbering. `SYS-001` to `SYS-222`
number the systems that exist.

**The reconciliation is not a one-off.** The walk was true on the days it was performed.
**`ADR-0015`** makes the determination a controlled document with a named owner and a review trigger, and
`02.09-the-systems-register.md §5` names the triggers — because a reconciliation nobody repeats produces
an inventory that was accurate once.

**`ADR-0009` was decided on 2026-06-12 by Amara Sissoko and is recorded at `DEC-203`.** The corrected
inventory was approved the same day at `GOV-07`. **The decision and the approval are deliberately the same
date**: the rule about which document yields to which was taken in the act of applying it, and not
retrospectively once the result was known to be comfortable.

## 4. The 222 by owner

Two hundred and twenty-two systems, and every entry names an individual owner — `ADR-0008`. **Of the
ninety-six GxP-relevant systems, five named individuals hold all ninety-six, and the largest single
holding is thirty-three.** That is a real span of accountability for one individual and it is published
rather than smoothed. The remaining one hundred and twenty-six sit with the corporate functions that
operate them; their owners are carried on the controlled entries and are not published in the extract at
`logs/systems-register.md §3`.

**No system is owned by a team.** That is `ADR-0008`, taken in Phase 01 on 2026-05-27 before there was an
estate to argue about, and Phase 02 is the first phase that had to pay for it.

| What the ownership rule required | What it cost |
|---|---|
| Every one of the 222 entries names an individual, not a function, a group or a distribution list | A conversation per contested entry, several of them about systems whose owner had left the organisation or the role |
| The owner is accountable for the entry's accuracy — the function, the location, the record types held | An owner who cannot describe what a system holds is an owner in name, and entries were reassigned before the inventory could be approved |
| An unowned system is not an acceptable inventory state | Systems among the seventeen that had never had an owner acquired one at the point of entry, not later |

**Five owners across ninety-six GxP-relevant systems is an uneven distribution and the inventory does not
pretend otherwise.** **The alternative to an uneven distribution is a fictional one** — systems assigned
to owners who do not operate them, in order to make a table look balanced — and that produces an inventory
whose owner column cannot be relied on for anything. Two of the five hold systems outside the function
their title names, because Helix's role set has no Head of Supply Chain and no Head of Engineering and
Maintenance; `logs/systems-register.md §0` records that under *On owners* rather than leaving it to be
noticed.

**Ownership of a system is not ownership of the records in it, and the two are not the same
accountability.** The system owner is accountable for the system as an entry in this inventory. Who is
accountable for the **records** a system holds follows the record type rather than the system, and **that
accountability is not published in this phase**. The record-type determination records a determiner and a
quality unit reviewer per type (`EV-222`); **a named record owner per type is Phase 04's, with the policy
architecture.** A reader who conflates system ownership with record accountability will misread the
register.

## 5. A note about `IA-2025-01`, and nothing more

**`IA-2025-01/F3` recorded that the register of computerised systems held by IT carried no attribute
identifying which systems held records required by a predicate rule.** That finding was raised in January
2025, accepted, and closed against the procedure that existed at the time. `01.09 §3.3` publishes it.

**That is the whole of what this chapter says about it.** It is recorded here because a reader of the
inventory is entitled to know that the gap the walk addressed had been observed inside Helix before this
programme existed, and because omitting it would look like a programme presenting its own work as a
discovery.

**No conclusion is drawn from it.** Not about the audit, not about the finding, not about its closure, and
not about why the attribute was still absent when Phase 02 opened. `IS-03` carries the gap forward from
Phase 01 and the determination in this phase is what closes it. `IS-01` — the separate question of why
four internal audits raised nothing on the subject matter of `OBS-2`, `OBS-4` and `OBS-6` — is not touched
by this chapter, is not advanced by it, and is Phase 08's. **`02.11-what-this-does-not-establish.md` owns
the discipline that keeps those two apart.**

## 6. What the corrected inventory does not carry

The inventory is an inventory. It is not an assessment, and the entries do not carry things that have not
been determined.

| Not carried | Why |
|---|---|
| **Any validation status** | No validation framework exists. **Phase 04**, `MS-08` |
| **Any risk rating, score, criticality tier or ranking** | The data integrity risk assessment is **Phase 03**'s, `MS-07`, and **nothing in this phase is scored** |
| **Any ALCOA assessment** | Phase 03 |
| **Any statement that a system meets, or fails to meet, a requirement of Part 11** | Nothing has been assessed against `21 CFR 11.10` — `02.07-open-and-closed.md §6` |
| **Any audit trail configuration or control status** | Phase 05 |
| **Any vendor compliance claim** | There is no Part 11 certification and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated software. A vendor supplies **features that support** compliance |
| **Any forecast of what an inspection would make of an entry** | This repository forecasts no inspection outcome |

What the inventory does carry — per system, per entry — is
`02.09-the-systems-register.md`'s, and the GxP determination applied to the 222 is
`02.03-what-makes-a-system-gxp-relevant.md`'s.

## 8. What this chapter does not do

- **It does not determine that any of the 222 is GxP-relevant.** `02.03`.
- **It does not determine that any of the 222 holds a Part 11 record.** `02.05` enumerates the record
  types; `02.08` places the systems.
- **It does not classify any system as closed or open.** `02.07`.
- **It states no validation status and scores nothing.** Phases 03 and 04.
- **It draws no conclusion from `IA-2025-01/F3`** and says nothing about `IS-01`.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind**, characterises the Ridgemont inspection as no violation of
  anything, and forecasts nothing about any future inspection.
- **It characterises no individual.** The register was maintained for the purpose it was built for, by
  people who were never asked for a determination.
