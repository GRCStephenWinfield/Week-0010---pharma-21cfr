*Phase 02 speaks as at **2026-07-31**. Helix Pharma, Inc., its sites, its people, its systems, its
inspection and every figure in this repository are fictional and illustrative. Where a statement is made
about a real regulation or guidance — 21 CFR Parts 11, 210 and 211, FDA's August 2003 Scope and
Application guidance, FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance — it is real and is cited
exactly, and the two kinds of statement are kept visibly apart throughout.*

---

## 1. What this phase is

**Phase 02 answers the question Phase 01 left open, and the answer to half of it is no.**

Phase 01 published a regulatory scoping determination: which regulations reach Helix Pharma and which do
not. It settled that **21 CFR Part 11 applies to a subset of records rather than to systems** —
`ADR-0001`, argued at `01.03 §2` — and it published no inventory, no record enumeration and no
applicability determination, because it had none. It carried the gap forward as `IS-02` and said so in
plain terms at `01.13 §7`.

This phase does that work. It walks the estate, counts it, determines which of its systems are
GxP-relevant, enumerates the record types those systems hold, applies the Part 11 applicability test to
each record type, derives each system's position from the record types it holds, and classifies the
systems that hold Part 11 records as closed or open on the test the regulation actually states.

**It determines applicability and nothing beyond it.** Whether the data in any of those records is
attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate is a different question, nobody at Helix
has asked it yet, and Phase 03 is where it is asked. **Applicability is not integrity**, and this phase
determines only the first.

## 2. Read this first

**A system is in scope because of the records it holds.** That sentence is `ADR-0001` and it is the
mechanism the whole phase runs on. It has a cost — the determination had to be made record type by record
type, fifty-eight times, before any system could be placed — and it has a payoff that shows up in one
number: **thirty-five systems at Helix are GxP-relevant and hold no Part 11 record at all.** Those
thirty-five are what `ADR-0001` looks like when it is applied rather than asserted, and
`02.08-the-sixty-one-and-the-thirty-five.md` is the chapter that argues them.

**No document in this phase writes "a Part 11 system" without saying which record types make it one.**

## 3. What Phase 02 found, said first

Five things. Each has exactly one owning chapter, and no other chapter restates it.

- **Which records actually are Part 11 records — and whether anybody ever wrote that down.** **Thirty-four
  record types of fifty-eight are Part 11 records, held across sixty-one systems of two hundred and
  twenty-two.** And **no written Part 11 applicability determination existed anywhere in Helix's quality
  system — not in a validation plan, not in the system inventory, not in a policy, not in an SOP — and no
  procedure required one to exist.** The enumeration is
  **`02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md`**; the systems are
  **`02.08-the-sixty-one-and-the-thirty-five.md`**; the answer to the second half is
  **`02.10-nobody-ever-wrote-it-down.md`**, and **`IS-02` closes there.**
- **Seven record types were in Part 11 scope all along.** Helix had treated eleven record types as out of
  scope because they were printed and the paper was said to be the record. The determination tests **the
  printout route's three conditions — two FDA states expressly and a third Helix derives from the narrow
  interpretation's second category** (`§10`), and **seven of the eleven fail the
  first** — the paper does not meet all the applicable predicate-rule requirements, because the record is
  dynamic and a static printout cannot reproduce it. **Four survive the test and are genuinely out of
  scope.** **`02.06-the-paper-that-was-not-the-record.md`**, with **`ADR-0010`** and **`IS-08`**.
- **The inventory was wrong.** The IT register carried **214** entries. The physical walk of the estate
  found **17 systems that were not in the register** and **9 register entries for systems that no longer
  exist**. **214 − 9 + 17 = 222.** **`02.02-the-inventory-corrected.md`**, with **`ADR-0009`**: the
  register is reconciled to the walk, never the walk to the register.
- **Three systems had been classified open on the wrong test.** Helix's IT standard called the three
  vendor-hosted systems open because they sit outside the site firewall. **`21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)` turns on
  who controls access**, Helix administers every account on all three, and **all three are closed
  systems** — as are all sixty-one. The measures Helix deployed for the three are not wrong and are not
  removed; what was wrong is that they were recorded as compliance with `21 CFR 11.30`, which requires
  open-system users to employ procedures and controls that **include those identified in `21 CFR 11.10`,
  as appropriate**, *and* additional measures — **the additional measures are additive and never a
  substitute** — so **`21 CFR 11.10` had never been assessed for those three systems.**
  **`02.07-open-and-closed.md`**, with
  **`ADR-0011`** and **`IS-09`**.
- **And the thing this phase refuses to do.** `01.09` recorded four candidate explanations for why four
  internal audits raised nothing on the subject matter of `OBS-2`, `OBS-4` and `OBS-6`, and the fourth was
  that nobody had written down which records were Part 11 records. **Phase 02 has established that premise
  as fact. It does not promote the candidate to the answer.** **`02.11-what-this-does-not-establish.md`**,
  with **`ADR-0012`**. **`IS-01` stays open and stays unweighted, and Phase 08 is where it is returned
  to.**

**The fifth is the one to read twice.** A phase that has just proved the premise of a comfortable
explanation is a phase under pressure to publish the explanation, and the discipline is not to.

## 4. What Phase 02 deliberately does not establish

None of the following exists at the vantage, and nothing in this phase states, implies or forecasts any
of it.

- **No data integrity risk assessment and no ALCOA assessment.** **Phase 03**, `MS-07`, 2026-09-30.
  **Nothing in this phase is scored, rated or ranked** — not a system, not a record type, not a
  programme risk.
- **No validation framework and no validation status for any system.** Phase 04, `MS-08`. Where this
  phase says a system has never entered a validation lifecycle, that is a statement about the absence of a
  lifecycle and not a statement about the state of any system.
- **No control implementation, no audit trail configuration and no electronic signature deployment.**
  Phase 05, `MS-09`.
- **No periodic review, no internal audit of the remediated programme and no third-party audit.**
- **No further inspection and no inspection outcome of any kind.** This phase forecasts none.
- **No assessment of whether `21 CFR 11.10`'s controls are met by any system.** The determination says
  which systems the section reaches. It says nothing about whether any of them satisfies it.
- **No answer to `IS-01`.**

## 10. Standing framing

These positions hold across all nine phases and are restated here so that a reader arriving at Phase 02
has them.

- **There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated
  software.** FDA does not certify electronic systems and does not pre-evaluate them for Part 11
  compliance. A product has **features that support** compliance; compliance is a property of Helix's
  validated system in its intended use.
- **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention. It is not a legal exemption and not a safe
  harbour.** **`21 CFR 211.68` requires validation of Helix's computerised systems whatever FDA does
  about Part 11** — `ADR-0003`.
- **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance issued by CDRH and CBER for computers
  and automated data processing systems used in device production and the device quality system under
  `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by the Quality Management System Regulation. Helix makes no medical device
  and it does not apply to Helix.** Adopted voluntarily as good practice, labelled as adopted every time —
  `ADR-0005`.
- **The printout route's three conditions — two FDA states expressly and a third Helix derives from the
  narrow interpretation's second category.** FDA's August 2003 passage states two conditions joined by
  *"and"*: the paper must meet **all** the applicable predicate-rule requirements, and persons must **rely
  on the paper** to perform their regulated activities. The third — that the electronic record is not
  **also** relied on — follows necessarily from the narrow interpretation's own second category and is
  **Helix's derivation, labelled as Helix's everywhere it is used.** The determination tests all three.
  **Nowhere in this repository is the set of three attributed to FDA's text.** Where brevity is needed,
  the permitted short form is *the three conditions, two stated and one derived*.
- **GAMP 5 is ISPE good-practice guidance, never a requirement**, and no software-category count is
  published in this repository.
- **A Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding.** No document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of
  any kind, and none characterises the Ridgemont inspection as a violation of anything.
- **No fine, penalty, sanction amount or forfeiture figure appears anywhere.**
- **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, no read-event logging, no cryptographic hashing under
  `21 CFR 11.70`, no multi-factor authentication under `21 CFR 11.200` and no password interval under
  `21 CFR 11.300(b)`.**
- **A closed system is defined by who controls access** — `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`. Hosting is not the test.
- **`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and Part 11 contains ten sections.** Citations in this
  repository are exact and can be looked up.
- **Never forecast what a future inspection will find.**

## 11. How to read this phase

Read **`02.06`** if you read only one chapter. It is the chapter that earns the phase: a test with three
conditions — **two stated and one derived**, `§10` — applied to eleven record types, producing seven that
fail and four that pass, with the reasoning shown for both groups.

Read **`02.02`** and **`02.03`** for the estate — what was found and how it was sorted. Read **`02.04`**
and **`02.05`** for the unit of determination and the enumeration. Read **`02.07`** for the closed and
open determination, which is the finding most likely to be wrong in somebody else's version of this
document. Read **`02.10`** for the answer to Phase 01's question and **`02.11`** for what that answer
does not license. Read **`02.12`** for what all of it costs in work, and **`02.13`** for what is still
open.

Read for the arguments rather than the citations. Where a chapter cites `21 CFR 11.10(e)` or
`21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)`, it is locating a requirement in the text and identifying which records it
reaches — **not** asserting that Helix meets it, because no assessment of any control has been performed.
