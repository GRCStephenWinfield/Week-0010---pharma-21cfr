---

## 1. The question this chapter answers, and the one it does not

**The question:** of the 222 systems in the corrected inventory, which ones are doing something in a
process regulated under 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211?

**The question this chapter does not answer:** which systems hold Part 11 records. That is a different
question, it produces a different set, and treating the two as one is the commonest scoping error in this
field. `§6` says so at length and `02.04-the-record-type-as-the-unit-of-determination.md` owns the
argument.

The inventory this chapter sorts is `02.02-the-inventory-corrected.md`'s: **214 − 9 + 17 = 222**. This
chapter does not restate how it was arrived at.

## 2. The test

**A system is GxP-relevant if any one of three limbs is satisfied. The limbs are disjunctive — one is
enough — and each is anchored in the predicate rule rather than in a judgement about importance.**

| Limb | The test | The anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Operation** | The system performs, controls, monitors or enforces an operation in the **manufacture, processing, packing or holding** of a drug product | **`21 CFR 211.68(a)`** — *"Automatic, mechanical, or electronic equipment or other types of equipment, including computers, or related systems … may be used in the manufacture, processing, packing, and holding of a drug product"* |
| **2 — Record** | The system **creates, holds, transforms or transmits a record that a predicate rule requires** to be made or maintained | **`21 CFR 211.180`**, **`21 CFR 211.188`**, **`21 CFR 211.194(a)`** and the sections that require the specific record |
| **3 — Decision** | The output of the system is **relied on by the quality unit** in a decision the predicate rules assign to it — release, rejection, disposition, investigation | **`21 CFR 211.22`** — the quality unit's responsibilities — and **`21 CFR 211.192`** for production record review |

**And one limb that is not in the test, deliberately.**

**Business criticality is not a limb.** A system whose failure would stop the site earning money is not
GxP-relevant for that reason. A system whose failure nobody outside one department would notice may be
squarely GxP-relevant on limb 2. **The two properties are unrelated**, and a determination that quietly
substitutes one for the other produces a scope that tracks operational anxiety rather than the
regulations.

**No system was assessed by risk, and no system carries a score, a tier or a criticality rating.** The
data integrity risk assessment is **Phase 03**'s, `MS-07`, 2026-09-30. **Nothing in this phase is
scored**, and a GxP determination expressed as a risk rating would be a category error twice over: it
would answer a scope question with a risk statement, and it would pre-empt an assessment that has not
happened.

**FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance was not used as the test.** It is device guidance issued by
CDRH and CBER for computers and automated data processing systems used in device production and the device
quality management system under **`21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by the Quality Management System
Regulation**, which took effect on 2026-02-03 and incorporates ISO 13485 by reference. **Helix makes no
medical device and it does not apply to Helix** — `01.05 §4`, `ADR-0005`. Its risk-based thinking is adopted voluntarily elsewhere in this programme and is labelled as
adopted every time. **GAMP 5 was not used as the test either**: it is ISPE good-practice guidance and
never a requirement, and no category, count or enumeration from it appears in this determination.

## 3. The evidence for each determination

**Every one of the 222 determinations is recorded with the evidence that produced it**, on the system
inventory entry form in `templates/`, and carried into `logs/systems-register.md`. A determination whose
basis is not recorded cannot be re-examined, and `ADR-0015` makes re-examination the point.

| Evidence type | What it establishes | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| **The function statement** | What the system does, written by its named owner and confirmed at the walk by the area supervisor | The walk — `02.01 §5` |
| **The data flows in and out** | Whether the system receives from, or feeds, a system that holds a required record | Interface documentation and configuration review with Automation |
| **The procedure references** | Whether any controlled procedure instructs a person to use the system to make, check or approve something | A search of the controlled procedure set |
| **The use of the output** | Whether anything the system produces is read, signed, filed or relied on in a CGMP decision | Interview with the owner and the using function, recorded |
| **The record inventory of the system** | What the system actually holds, as distinct from what it was bought to hold | Inspection of the system with its administrator |

**Two evidence rules were fixed before the assessment and applied without exception.**

**The owner's statement is evidence, and it is not the only evidence.** Where a system owner said a system
held nothing required, the record inventory of the system was inspected. Determinations changed on that
inspection, in both directions, and the changes are visible in the register because the initial position
and the final position are both recorded.

**A system that feeds a required record is assessed on what it feeds, not on what it is called.** A
monitoring device whose readings are transcribed into a batch record is participating in the making of a
record `21 CFR 211.188` requires, whatever the department that bought it calls it.

## 4. The result

**96 GxP-relevant · 126 not GxP-relevant. 96 + 126 = 222.**

| Determination | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **GxP-relevant** | **96** | At least one limb of `§2` is satisfied, and the limb and its evidence are recorded on the entry |
| **Not GxP-relevant** | **126** | No limb is satisfied. The system exists, it is owned, it is in the inventory, and no predicate-rule obligation runs to it through this determination |
| **The corrected inventory** | **222** | `SYS-001` to `SYS-222` |

**The 126 are in the inventory and stay in it.** A system determined not to be GxP-relevant is not deleted,
because the determination is a statement that has to be maintainable — and a system that has been assessed
and excluded, with its basis recorded, is a different thing from a system nobody looked at. When the review
trigger fires, the 126 are re-examined alongside the 96.

## 5. The borderline cases, argued

**A determination that produced no arguments was not a determination.** Some of the 222 were genuinely
contested, in the sense that the evidence supported a serious case on both sides and the decision had to
be reasoned rather than read off. Five of those are set out below, each with the argument that lost.

| # | The system | Determination | The limb that decided it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The **building management system** serving the manufacturing and packaging suites at Ridgemont | **GxP-relevant** | Limb 1, with limb 2 in support |
| 2 | The **calibration and maintenance management platform** used by Engineering and Maintenance | **GxP-relevant** | Limb 2 |
| 3 | The **site badge access and physical security system** at Ridgemont | **Not GxP-relevant** | No limb satisfied |
| 4 | The **corporate email and document collaboration platform** | **Not GxP-relevant** | No limb satisfied — and the determination is conditional in a way the entry records |
| 5 | The **stability pull scheduling tool** used by the Cary laboratory | **Not GxP-relevant** | No limb satisfied |

### 5.1 The building management system — in

**The case against.** It is a facilities system. It runs air handling, it is maintained by Engineering, it
was bought on a facilities budget, and nobody at Helix has ever described it as a quality system.

**The case for, which won.** It maintains and records the conditions of the areas in which drug product is
manufactured and packed. **`21 CFR 211.46`** requires adequate ventilation and, where appropriate, air
filtration and control of air pressure, humidity and temperature for the manufacture of the drug product;
**`21 CFR 211.42(c)`** requires operations to be performed within specifically defined areas, with separate
or defined areas or other control systems as necessary to prevent contamination or mixups. A system that
both **enforces** those conditions and **records** whether they held during a
manufacturing operation satisfies limb 1 on the enforcement and limb 2 on the record.

**Why it was contested rather than obvious.** The department that owns it does not think of it as a
GxP system, and the argument "we have never called it that" is the argument that most often keeps a system
out of a scope statement. The determination turned on what it does, not on what it is called.

### 5.2 The calibration and maintenance management platform — in

**The case against.** It is a maintenance planning tool. It schedules work; the calibration is performed by
a technician with an instrument, and the result is what matters.

**The case for, which won.** `21 CFR 211.68(a)` requires that equipment used in the manufacture,
processing, packing and holding of a drug product be *"routinely calibrated, inspected, or checked
according to a written program designed to assure proper performance"* and that **"written records of
those calibration checks and inspections shall be maintained."** The platform holds the programme and the
records of the checks. That is limb 2, on the face of the section, and the counter-argument mistakes the
schedule for the whole of what the system holds.

### 5.3 The badge access and physical security system — out

**The case for including it.** Physical access to laboratories and production areas is a control, access
records could support an investigation, and a reader who expects "security system" to imply "GxP" will be
surprised by the answer.

**The case against, which won.** No predicate rule requires the record this system creates, no CGMP
operation depends on its data, and no controlled procedure instructs anybody to rely on it for a decision
the predicate rules assign. It is a security control over premises. **It is not GxP-relevant on any of the
three limbs**, and importing it because it feels adjacent would be a determination made on atmosphere.

**Where it sits in the 126.** The class is **Facilities, energy, site security and site services**, and
`logs/systems-register.md §3` carries it there. The class is named for what the systems in it do rather
than for the areas they serve, because a label that asserted "non-GxP areas" would contradict the argument
above: this system controls access to laboratories and production areas and is excluded on the limbs, not
on where its doors are.

**The determination is conditional and the entry says so.** If a Helix procedure ever relies on badge
records as evidence of who was present for a regulated operation, limb 3 is engaged and the determination
changes. That condition is written into the entry, and it is one of the cases `ADR-0015`'s review trigger
exists to catch.

### 5.4 The corporate email and document collaboration platform — out

**The case for including it.** People discuss regulated work on it, attachments containing GxP content
move across it, and a determination that excludes it looks convenient.

**The case against, which won.** **No record required by a predicate rule is maintained on it.** Helix's
document control and quality management procedures require the controlled record to be held in the
controlled system, and the determination rests on that requirement being what it says. The platform holds
correspondence about regulated work, which is not the same category as a record a regulation requires to
exist.

**This is the most fragile determination of the five, and it is published as fragile rather than as
settled.** It depends on a behaviour — that required records are not maintained there — rather than on a
technical property of the system. **The assumption is recorded in `logs/raid-log.md`**, with what would
falsify it: a controlled procedure that points at a mailbox or a shared folder for a required record, or a
record type in `02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md` whose only copy turns out to live there.

**Where it sits in the 126.** The class is **End-user computing and collaboration** —
`logs/systems-register.md §3`.

### 5.5 The stability pull scheduling tool — out

**The case for including it.** It sits in a QC laboratory, it concerns stability studies, and stability
testing is a CGMP activity under **`21 CFR 211.166`**.

**The case against, which won.** `21 CFR 211.166(a)` requires a **written testing programme** and the
records of the testing performed under it. At Helix, the written programme and the protocols are
controlled documents held elsewhere, and the results are laboratory records held elsewhere again. The tool
holds a working schedule derived from the programme; it makes no required record and nothing the quality
unit relies on. **A system in a regulated room is not GxP-relevant by location.**

**Where it sits in the 126.** The class is **End-user computing and collaboration**, as a departmental
productivity tool — `logs/systems-register.md §3`. **None of the eleven classes is a laboratory class or a
Cary class**, and that is the point rather than an omission: the classes record what a system is, and a
scheduling spreadsheet used by a laboratory is a scheduling tool used by a laboratory. Classifying it by
the room it is used in would reintroduce the location test `§5.5` has just refused.

**Read 5.3, 5.4 and 5.5 together and the shape of the test is visible.** Adjacency is not the test.
Location is not the test. What is required, and by which section, is the test.

## 6. GxP-relevant is not the same question as Part 11 applicability

**State it as plainly as it can be stated: the 96 are not the Part 11 systems, and this chapter has not
determined that any of them holds a Part 11 record.**

The two questions differ in what they are asking and in what makes them true.

| | **GxP relevance** | **Part 11 applicability** |
|---|---|---|
| Asks | Does this system take part in a regulated process? | Is this **record** a record Part 11 reaches? |
| Unit | The system | **The record type** — `ADR-0001`, `ADR-0013` |
| Turns on | The predicate rules, and the three limbs at `§2` | Whether a predicate rule requires the record, **and** how it is maintained and relied on — the narrow interpretation's four categories at `01.03 §2` |
| Can be true of | A system holding no electronic record at all | A record held on a system nobody would call a platform |
| Determined by | This chapter | **`02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md`**, record type by record type |

**A GxP-relevant system can hold no Part 11 record whatever.** That is not a curiosity; it is a set with
members, it is what `ADR-0001` predicts, and **`02.08-the-sixty-one-and-the-thirty-five.md` publishes the
split and argues it.** This chapter names the fact and stops there.

**The order of operations is not negotiable.** GxP relevance narrows the population over which record
types are enumerated. The record-type enumeration determines applicability. The systems inherit their
position from the record types they hold. Running that sequence backwards — deciding which systems are
"Part 11 systems" and then looking for records to justify it — is exactly the failure `ADR-0001` was
written in Phase 01 to prevent, before anybody knew what the answer would be.

## 7. What the 126 are

The 126 are described by class in `logs/systems-register.md`, which tabulates the 96 in full and
summarises the rest with counts that reconcile. In prose, they are what a manufacturing company's estate
is mostly made of once the regulated part is taken out of it:

- **Finance, payroll, human resources and procurement systems** operated at Ridgemont and Cary. **Systems
  located at the Princeton corporate office are not in this inventory at all** — the walk excluded
  Princeton on the recorded basis that no operation, record or quality decision within `21 CFR Part 211`
  occurs there (`GOV-06`, `02.01 §5`).
- **Commercial and customer-facing systems** — order management, pricing, customer relationship
  management.
- **Network, telephony and end-user infrastructure management** — the systems that manage the estate
  rather than take part in it.
- **Facilities systems serving areas with no GxP operation.**
- **Site services** — catering, car parking, meeting-room booking, visitor registration.

**None of the 126 is unimportant and none of them is unowned.** Each has a named individual owner under
`ADR-0008`, each carries its determination and the basis for it, and each is inside the review trigger.
What they do not carry is any obligation arising from Parts 210 and 211 through this determination.

## 8. What this chapter does not do

- **It does not determine that any system holds a Part 11 record.** `02.05` and `02.08`.
- **It does not classify any system as closed or open.** `02.07`.
- **It scores nothing, rates nothing and ranks nothing.** Phase 03.
- **It states no validation status for any of the 96 or the 126**, and it does not say that any system is
  or is not validated. Phase 04.
- **It does not assess any system against `21 CFR 11.10`, `21 CFR 211.68` or any other requirement.** It
  determines only whether the requirement's subject matter reaches the system.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind** and forecasts nothing about any future inspection.
