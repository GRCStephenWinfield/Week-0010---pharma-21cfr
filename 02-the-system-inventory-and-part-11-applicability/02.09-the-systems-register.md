## 1. What the register is

**`SYS-001` to `SYS-222`.** One identifier per system in the corrected inventory, never reused and never
renumbered. The register itself is `logs/systems-register.md`; this chapter states what it carries, what
it deliberately does not, how each entry's Part 11 position is derived rather than asserted, and what
makes it get looked at again.

**It is a controlled document from the date of its approval**, with a named individual owner, a change
route and a review trigger. That is `ADR-0015`, decided on **2026-07-24 by Terrence Abbatiello** and
recorded at **`DEC-215`**, and the reasoning is short enough to state in one line: **a determination
nobody maintains is a determination that was true once.**

The register is where four separate determinations meet, each made by a different chapter and none of
them made here:

| Determination | Result | Owning chapter |
|---|---|---|
| The corrected inventory | **214 − 9 + 17 = 222** | `02.02-the-inventory-corrected.md` |
| GxP relevance | **96 GxP-relevant · 126 not. 96 + 126 = 222** | `02.03-what-makes-a-system-gxp-relevant.md` |
| Part 11 applicability, record type by record type | **34 of 58 record types are Part 11 records** | `02.05-the-fifty-eight-record-types.md` |
| Systems holding at least one Part 11 record | **61 hold one · 35 hold none. 61 + 35 = 96** | `02.08-the-sixty-one-and-the-thirty-five.md` |
| Closed or open, for the sixty-one | **All sixty-one are closed systems** | `02.07-open-and-closed.md` |

**The register restates none of those arguments.** It carries their outputs, per system, with a pointer to
the determination that produced each one. A register that argues is a register that will eventually
disagree with the chapter it is arguing with.

## 2. What each entry carries

Sixteen fields. Every one of them is either an observed fact, a determination with a stated basis, or a
pointer — and none of them is an opinion.

| Field | What it holds | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Identifier** | `SYS-nnn` | Assigned on entry to the inventory; never reused |
| **Name and function** | What the system is and what it does, in a sentence a person outside the owning department can read | The walk, confirmed by the owner and the area supervisor |
| **Location** | Site, area or hosting arrangement — Ridgemont, Cary, a data centre, the disaster-recovery site, or vendor-hosted | The walk and the hosting records |
| **Owner** | **A named individual.** Never a team, a function or a distribution list — `ADR-0008` | Assigned and accepted before approval of the inventory |
| **Source of the entry** | Whether the entry came from the register, or from the walk as one of the seventeen | `02.02 §2.1` |
| **GxP determination** | GxP-relevant, or not | `02.03 §4` |
| **Basis of the GxP determination** | Which of the three limbs was satisfied, and the evidence that satisfied it | `02.03 §2` and `§3` |
| **Record types held** | The `RT` identifiers of every record type the system holds, in scope or out | `02.05`, and `logs/record-type-register.md` |
| **Part 11 determination** | Whether the system holds at least one Part 11 record — **derived, not entered.** `§4` | `02.08` |
| **The record types that make it so** | The `RT` identifiers that carry the determination, named individually | `02.05` |
| **Closed or open** | For each of the sixty-one. **Blank, not "closed", for the systems that hold no Part 11 record** | `02.07` |
| **Basis of the closed or open determination** | Who administers access, and the evidence for it | `02.07 §4` |
| **Interfaces** | The systems this one receives from and feeds | Interface documentation and configuration review |
| **Determination date and determiner** | When each determination was made, and by whom, as a named individual | The determination records |
| **Approval reference** | The governance record that approved it — `GOV-07` for the inventory, `GOV-09` for the applicability determination | `logs/decision-log.md` |
| **Last reviewed, and against which trigger** | The date of the last review and the trigger that caused it | `§5` |

**Two fields do the work that makes the register defensible.** *The record types that make it so* means
no entry can say a system is in scope without naming what puts it there — which is `ADR-0001` enforced by
the shape of the form rather than by good intentions. *Determination date and determiner* means every line
can be traced to a person and a date, which is what turns a table into a record.

## 3. What the register deliberately does not carry

**This section owns that list.**

| Not carried | Why not, and where it belongs |
|---|---|
| **Any validation status** | No validation framework exists at this vantage. **Phase 04**, `MS-08`, 2026-11-30. A validation column would be filled with a guess or a blank, and a blank in a validation column is read as a failure |
| **Any risk rating, score, tier, band or criticality** | The data integrity risk assessment is **Phase 03**'s, `MS-07`, 2026-09-30. **Nothing in this phase is scored** |
| **Any ALCOA assessment** | Phase 03 |
| **Any statement that a system meets or fails a requirement** | Nothing has been assessed against `21 CFR 11.10` or against `21 CFR 211.68`. The register says which requirements reach a system, not how it fares under them |
| **Any audit trail configuration, access configuration or signature configuration** | Phase 05, `MS-09`. What a system is configured to do is not what a determination determines |
| **Any GAMP category** | GAMP 5 is ISPE good-practice guidance and never a requirement, and this repository publishes no category count or enumeration |
| **Any vendor compliance claim** | **There is no Part 11 certification and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated software.** A vendor supplies features that support compliance |
| **Any priority order for remediation** | Priority follows assessed risk, and there is no assessment. Phase 03 sets it, Phase 04 acts on it |
| **Any expectation about what an inspection would say** | This repository forecasts no inspection outcome, ever |

**The temptation this list resists is a single column.** A register with a *status* column that mixes
scope, validation and risk is easier to read and impossible to defend, because no reader can tell which
question a given cell answers. Five determinations were made in this phase; five columns carry them; the
columns that would carry determinations nobody has made are absent rather than empty.

## 4. How the Part 11 determination is derived, not entered

**No system's Part 11 position was typed into the register.** Each one is derived, by a rule stated in
advance, from the record types the system holds.

> **A system holds at least one Part 11 record if and only if at least one of the record types it holds
> was determined to be a Part 11 record. The obligations that attach to the system are the union of those
> arising from the record types it holds, and the record types that generated them are named on the
> entry.**

That is `ADR-0013` — the record type is the unit of determination — and it is `ADR-0001` from Phase 01
carried into practice. `02.04-the-record-type-as-the-unit-of-determination.md` owns the argument.

Three consequences follow for the register, and all three are visible in it.

**A system's position can change without the system changing.** If a record type's determination is
revisited, every system holding it re-derives. The derivation is mechanical, so the re-derivation is too,
and the register does not need to be re-argued system by system.

**No entry can carry the phrase "a Part 11 system" alone.** The field that says a system is in scope is
adjacent to the field that names the record types that put it there, and an entry with the first and not
the second does not pass review.

**The thirty-five are the proof the rule is a rule.** Thirty-five GxP-relevant systems hold no Part 11
record, and they appear in the register with a GxP determination, a full record-type list, and a Part 11
determination of *no* derived from that list. **They are in scope for `21 CFR 211.68` and out of scope for
Part 11**, both stated, on the same line, from the same evidence.
`02.08-the-sixty-one-and-the-thirty-five.md` argues them.

## 5. The review trigger

**`ADR-0015` makes the determination a controlled document with a named owner and a review trigger. The
trigger is named, it is not a calendar entry dressed up as a control, and it is recorded on the
determination review trigger record in `templates/` each time it fires.**

**Owners.** The **inventory** is owned by the Chief Information Officer, Amara Sissoko. The
**determination** — GxP relevance, record types, Part 11 applicability and the closed and open
classification — is owned by the Data Integrity Lead, **Priyanka Venkataraman**, who reports to Quality
and not to IT under `ADR-0002`. **The two owners are deliberately different people**: the function that
operates the estate does not own the determination made about it.

**The trigger — six events, any one of which requires the affected entries to be re-determined.**

| # | Trigger event | What is re-determined |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **A system enters or leaves the estate**, including a system found outside the register | The new or departing entry in full, and the inventory total |
| **T2** | **A record type changes the form it is maintained in** — paper to electronic, electronic-plus-paper to electronic only, or a change in which copy is relied on | That record type's applicability determination, and every system holding it |
| **T3** | **A system begins to hold, or stops holding, a record type** | That system's Part 11 determination, by re-derivation under `§4` |
| **T4** | **The administration of access to a system changes** — to or from a party that is not responsible for the content of its records | That system's closed or open determination — `02.07 §8` |
| **T5** | **A predicate rule, or FDA guidance the determination relies on, changes** | Every determination resting on the changed text. The quarterly horizon scan at `CAL-12` is what detects it |
| **T6** | **A change control, deviation or investigation reveals a record type or a system the determination does not carry** | The affected entries, and a check of the determination's completeness |

**And a floor, so that the trigger cannot be satisfied by nothing happening.** The determination is
reviewed **annually in full** whether or not any of `T1` to `T6` has fired, and the review is recorded
even when it changes nothing. **A trigger set with no floor is a control that reports success by being
silent.**

**What the trigger is not.** It is not a periodic review of a computerised system — that is `CAL-07`,
annual per system, and Phase 08's subject. It is not an audit. It is a review of **a determination**
against the facts the determination was made on.

## 6. `IS-10` — the register exists and nothing yet requires it to be maintained

**`ADR-0015` says the determination shall be maintained. Nothing in Helix's quality system says so.**

That distinction is the whole of `IS-10`, and it is stated here because this is the chapter that describes
the machinery. A programme decision binds the programme. A procedure binds the company after the
programme has closed on 2027-04-30. **At this vantage there is no SOP that requires the Part 11
applicability determination to exist, to be reviewed, or to be updated when a system or a record type
changes** — which is unsurprising, because until 2026-07-24 there was no determination for a procedure to
be about.

**`02.10-nobody-ever-wrote-it-down.md` owns that finding**, states where the programme looked for a
procedure that would have required one, and records `IS-10` as opening in place of `IS-02`. This chapter
records only its consequence for the register: **the trigger at `§5` is currently held by a programme
decision and by two named individuals, and it needs a procedure behind it before the programme ends.** The
work is in `02.12-what-the-determination-costs.md` and the policy architecture that would carry it is
Phase 04's, `MS-08`.

`IS-07`, carried forward from Phase 01, is the same shape of problem for the calendar: seven standing
commitments outlive the programme with no owner named after close-out. **The register is now the eighth
thing in that category, and it is recorded rather than assumed away.**

## 7. How the register is changed

| Change | Who may make it | What is required |
|---|---|---|
| A new system enters the inventory | The inventory owner | `T1`; a completed system inventory entry form; a named individual owner accepted before the entry is approved |
| A system leaves the inventory | The inventory owner | `T1`; evidence that the system is gone; the entry is closed with its history intact and **its identifier is not reused** |
| A GxP determination changes | The determination owner | The basis re-recorded against the three limbs at `02.03 §2` |
| A record type's applicability changes | The determination owner | The record type register updated first; the systems re-derive under `§4` |
| A closed or open determination changes | The determination owner, on evidence from the system owner | `T4`; the access administration evidence refreshed |
| Any change to an approved entry | Under change control | A `CR-nnn` change record, with the trigger that caused it named |

**No entry is edited in place without a record of what it said before.** The register carries its own
history, because the question a later reader will ask is not what the register says — it is what it said at
the time somebody relied on it.

## 8. What this chapter does not do

- **It does not restate the determinations.** It says what carries them. The arguments belong to `02.02`,
  `02.03`, `02.05`, `02.07` and `02.08`.
- **It states no validation status, no risk rating and no criticality.** Phases 03 and 04.
- **It asserts no system's Part 11 position independently of the record types it holds.**
- **It does not describe any control, configuration or remediation.** Phase 05.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion of any kind**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and
  forecasts nothing about any future inspection.
