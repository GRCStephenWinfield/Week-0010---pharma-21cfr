# 08.08 — Nothing Requires a Periodic Review

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Document ID | `HLX-DI-2027-808` |
| Version | 1.0 |
| Date | 2027-04-09 |
| Owner | Dr Marguerite Oyelaran — Vice President, Quality |

| Classification | Confidential — Helix Pharma, Inc. Data Integrity Programme // Illustrative Portfolio Sample |

*No FDA source requires a periodic review of a computerised system; an EU guideline asks for one as a should; and Helix adopts it voluntarily and labels it EU-derived every time.*

---

## 1. One idea

**Nothing in `21 CFR Part 11`, and nothing in Parts 210 and 211, requires a periodic review of a
computerised system.**

Helix is establishing one anyway. **`ADR-0053`**, taken by the Vice President, Quality on 2027-04-01 at
`DEC-808`: **periodic review is adopted voluntarily where FDA requires none, and its EU-derived limb is
labelled EU-derived at every citation.**

This chapter establishes what requires a periodic review and what does not. **It reports no arrangement and
no frequency** — the arrangement is `08.09-the-review-established.md`'s and
`logs/periodic-review-register.md`'s — **and it reports the result of no review, because none has been
performed.**

**The chapter is written the way it is because the alternative is the commonest error in this subject
area.** A great many firms run periodic system reviews under a procedure that cites a US regulation as its
basis. The citation is almost always `21 CFR 211.180(e)`, and it does not say what the procedure says it
says. **A control performed for thirteen years against a mis-cited obligation is a control nobody has ever
been able to argue about**, because the argument was foreclosed by the citation.

## 2. What `21 CFR Part 11` requires, and what it does not

`21 CFR 11.10` opens with an obligation on **persons**: *"Persons who use closed systems to create, modify,
maintain, or transmit electronic records shall employ procedures and controls designed to ensure the
authenticity, integrity, and, when appropriate, the confidentiality of electronic records, and to ensure
that the signer cannot readily repudiate the signed record as not genuine. Such procedures and controls
shall include the following:"* It then lists eleven paragraphs of controls, `(a)` through `(k)`, and the
chapeau's *"when appropriate"* qualifies confidentiality alone and not the list. **The eleven are not
discretionary on a closed system: the "as appropriate" that permits selection among them appears in
`21 CFR 11.30`, which reaches open systems only, and all sixty-one systems in scope are closed** —
`ADR-0011`. The words *"as appropriate"* do appear twice inside `21 CFR 11.10`, at `(f)` and at `(h)`, and
in each case they govern the content of that one paragraph and not whether any of the eleven applies.
**The controls are therefore owed in full, without qualification, on every one of the sixty-one.**

**Not one of the eleven is a periodic review of the system.** `(a)` requires validation. `(k)` requires
controls over systems documentation, including revision and change control procedures maintaining an audit
trail of modifications. **Neither is a recurring evaluation of the system's continuing state, and neither
names an interval.**

**And `21 CFR 11.10(e)` imposes no review obligation at all.** Its text, whole:

> *"Use of secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails to independently record the date and time
> of operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic records. Record changes shall
> not obscure previously recorded information. Such audit trail documentation shall be retained for a
> period at least as long as that required for the subject electronic records and shall be available for
> agency review and copying."*

**Generate, do not obscure, retain, make available to the agency. That is the whole of it.** The
availability limb runs to FDA and to nobody else. **There is no internal review obligation in the paragraph,
no second-person review, and no frequency of any kind** — and no Part 11 requirement anywhere for a
reason-for-change field. `../05-access-audit-trails-and-electronic-signatures/` owns the estate position on
that paragraph and this chapter does not restate it.

## 3. Parts 210 and 211, and the paragraph that is habitually mis-cited

**Nothing in Parts 210 and 211 requires a periodic review of a computerised system either.**
`21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires appropriate controls over computer or related systems so that changes in master
production and control records or other records are instituted only by authorised personnel; it further
requires that *"[i]nput to and output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or
data shall be checked for accuracy"* and that *"[t]he degree and frequency of input/output verification
shall be based on the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system."* `21 CFR 211.68(a)`
requires equipment so used to be routinely calibrated, inspected or checked according to a written program
designed to assure proper performance. The second of the two sentences quoted from `(b)` is a recurring
obligation and the only one in Parts 210 and 211 that scales to a computerised system's complexity and
reliability — **but its object is the degree and frequency of input/output verification and nothing else
under that provision, not the system's continuing validated state. Neither paragraph is a periodic review,
and `21 CFR 211.68` imposes no general computerised-system validation requirement** — the word *validation*
appears in the section once, inside `(b)`'s backup carve-out.

**That distinction is why the band Phase 04 built on this sentence is used here as an analogy Helix adopts
and never as an authority Helix cites** — `08.09-the-review-established.md §3`, `ADR-0054`, `GOV-18`.

**Then there is `21 CFR 211.180(e)`, which is the one the industry reaches for. Its first sentence,
verbatim:**

> *"Written records required by this part shall be maintained so that data therein can be used for
> evaluating, at least annually, the quality standards of each drug product to determine the need for
> changes in drug product specifications or manufacturing or control procedures."*

**The paragraph then requires that written procedures be established and followed for such evaluations**,
and specifies what they shall include provisions for: a review of a representative number of batches,
whether approved or rejected, and where applicable the records associated with the batch — `(e)(1)` — and
*"[a] review of complaints, recalls, returned or salvaged drug products, and investigations conducted under
§ 211.192 for each drug product."* — `(e)(2)`. **The closing words of `(e)(2)` say the unit outright: the
evaluation is per drug product, not per system.**

**Read what the object of that evaluation is. It is a drug product.** The subject of the sentence is the
records; the thing evaluated is *the quality standards of each drug product*; and the purpose is stated
inside the paragraph — *to determine the need for changes in drug product specifications or manufacturing
or control procedures*. **The unit is a product. The output is a decision about specifications and
procedures.**

> **`21 CFR 211.180(e)` says nothing whatever about computerised systems, and it is never cited in this
> repository as the basis for a periodic review of one.**

**The confusion is easy to reconstruct and worth naming, because naming it is what stops it recurring.**
Both objects are reviewed; both reviews are annual in the usual arrangement; both are performed by quality
people, out of the same procedure set, at the same time of year. **The word *annual* is doing the work, and
it is doing it in a sentence about a medicine.** An annual product quality evaluation could be performed
perfectly for a decade without anybody ever looking at whether a computerised system remains in a validated
state, because that is not what the paragraph asks anybody to look at.

**One further property of the paragraph, recorded because it belongs here and is argued elsewhere.** `(e)`
requires the evaluation and requires written procedures for it, **and it sets no retention period for the
record the evaluation produces** — `../07-the-data-lifecycle-backup-archive-and-the-paper-question/07.02-specifically-associated-with-a-batch.md §9`
owns that argument and this chapter cites it rather than repeating it.

## 4. What does ask for one, and it is not FDA's

**EU GMP Annex 11, *Periodic evaluation* — clause 11 in EudraLex Volume 4, whose clauses run in a flat
sequence 1 to 17, and numbered 4.11 in Health Canada's GUI-0050 adoption, which nests the whole annex
beneath its own section 4. EU-DERIVED. Quoted whole:**

> *"Computerised systems should be periodically evaluated to confirm that they remain in a valid state and
> are compliant with GMP. Such evaluations should include, where appropriate, the current range of
> functionality, deviation records, incidents, problems, upgrade history, performance, reliability,
> security and validation status reports."*

**Four things about that text, and each of them is carried on the face of every citation of it in this
phase.**

**It is a *should*, in a guideline.** Not a *shall*, not a *must*, and not in a regulation. **Only the
January 2011 version of Annex 11 is in force**, with a deadline for coming into operation of 30 June 2011;
a draft revision was consulted on alongside a revision of Chapter 4 and a new draft Annex 22, the
consultation closed on 2025-10-07, **and no revised text has been adopted or published in final form.**
Nothing in this repository cites a draft as a requirement, and the draft is watched as a horizon item at
`CAL-12` and never cited as an obligation.

**It is EU-derived, and it does not reach a US drug manufacturer through FDA.** **FDA does not enforce
EudraLex. An FDA inspection is not an Annex 11 inspection and an Annex 11 expectation is not an FDA
requirement.** No document in this phase describes any Annex 11 obligation as binding Helix's US-facing
route, **because none does.**

**Its second sentence is the content of the review.** The clause names nine things — the current range of
functionality, deviation records, incidents, problems, upgrade history, performance, reliability, security
and validation status reports — **qualified by *where appropriate*.** They are the guideline's own items,
they are quoted rather than paraphrased, and they are labelled EU-derived wherever a review row names them.
**They are the reason the adopted review has a defined content at all**, because no FDA source supplies one.

**And it asks a question, not for a document.** *Confirm that they remain in a valid state and are
compliant with GMP.* **A review that produces a signed form and answers no question has not performed the
clause**, and the nine items exist to make the question answerable rather than to be a checklist.

## 5. The route, and who acts on it

**The route by which an Annex 11 expectation reaches anything Helix does is the EU-facing product route,
and its owner is Dr Anselm Ferrão, Qualified Person, EU-facing products.**

**Dr Ferrão is not a Helix role and this repository has never described him as one.** He acts for the
holder of the EU manufacturing and import authorisation and not for Helix; Helix does not have a Qualified
Person of its own and could not have one. **The Qualified Person's certification duties arise under EU law
and are owed within the EU framework**, and the Helix-side owner of the corresponding obligation `O12` is
Rebekah Sandquist.

**So the shape of the thing is precise.** Helix is a US drug manufacturer, inspected by FDA under Parts 210
and 211, with `21 CFR Part 11` reaching some of its records. **Some of what it makes is presented into the
EU through a route on which a Qualified Person acts, and it is that route — not FDA, and not Helix's
US-facing obligations — that carries the Annex 11 expectation.** The expectation belongs to the route. It
does not migrate to the rest of the estate because the estate is convenient.

## 6. And Helix cannot say which systems the route reaches

**This is the honest problem in the chapter and it is not softened.**

**No register this programme has built joins a system to a product, or a product to the EU-facing route.**
Phase 02's systems register carries systems; Phase 02's record-type register carries record types; Phase 03
carries record-type-and-system pairs; Phases 04 to 07 carry obligations, control positions, auditable event
classes and retention derivations. **Not one of them carries a product.** The enquiry that established it —
`EV-826`, made on 2027-04-01 — names the registers searched one by one and records the answer as none, and
**the enquiry is retained rather than only its answer**, so that the absence can be tested as a search
rather than taken as a conclusion.

**The consequence is that Helix cannot enumerate the subset of its estate that the EU-facing route
reaches.** Not approximately, not provisionally, not with a caveat. **The join does not exist to be made.**

**So the standard is applied to the whole estate, and what was decided and what was forced are recorded
separately.**

> **Three courses were available. Adopt nothing, because no source binding Helix requires a periodic review
> at all. Build the product-to-system mapping first and apply the standard to the subset it identifies. Or
> apply one standard across all sixty-one. The first was refused because the substance of the practice is
> worth having where the source is not; the second was refused because building it is a determination about
> products and this phase determines nothing about products, and it is registered as owed at `DP-44`; the
> third was adopted. What is forced is the impossibility of the middle course today: applying a standard to
> a subset nobody can enumerate is not available. What is chosen is that a standard is applied at all, and
> that it is applied now rather than after the mapping exists.**

**What is not the case is that Helix has decided every system deserves the treatment the EU-facing route
expects, or has raised a standard.** It has adopted a practice voluntarily and has been unable to narrow
it, and `IS-54` records the inability rather than the adoption.

**The reason is on the register's own first section as well as here**, because a reason recorded only in
a chapter is a reason that will not travel. **`IS-54`**, raised on 2027-04-01, owned by Rebekah Sandquist,
with `DP-44` as the route.

**And the reading this arrangement invites is refused in advance.** A firm that applies an EU-derived
expectation to its whole estate will be described inside the firm, within about a quarter, as having raised
its standard. **What has happened is that it could not narrow one**, and the difference
is the entire content of `IS-54`. `PR-62` carries the risk that the first description wins.

## 7. Annex 11's audit trail clause, and the argument this completes

**The programme has carried half a picture since Phase 05, and this is where the other half is stated.**

**EU GMP Annex 11, *Audit Trails* — clause 9, and 4.9 in the Health Canada adoption. EU-DERIVED. Quoted
whole:**

> *"Consideration should be given, based on a risk assessment, to building into the system the creation of
> a record of all GMP-relevant changes and deletions (a system generated 'audit trail'). For change or
> deletion of GMP-relevant data the reason should be documented. Audit trails need to be available and
> convertible to a generally intelligible form and regularly reviewed."*

**Set that beside what `21 CFR Part 11` requires, and the whole position can be stated in one place for the
first time.**

| Question | `21 CFR Part 11` | **EU GMP Annex 11 — EU-derived** |
|---|---|---|
| **Is a reason for change required?** | **No.** There is no textual requirement anywhere in Part 11 for a reason-for-change field. `21 CFR 11.10(e)`'s second sentence is a **no-overwrite** requirement — *"Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information."* — and nothing more | **The reason *should* be documented**, for change or deletion of GMP-relevant data. **A *should*, in a guideline that is not FDA's** |
| **Is a review frequency required?** | **No.** `21 CFR 11.10(e)` states none, and no other paragraph of Part 11 states one. **Part 11 imposes no audit trail review obligation at all** | **Audit trails *need to be* … *regularly reviewed*.** **No interval is named**, and the sentence sits in a guideline that is not FDA's |
| **Where does a US review obligation come from, then?** | **From the predicate rules**, and FDA's construction is that the audit trail is part of the record already being reviewed — `21 CFR 211.194(a)(8)` for laboratory records, `21 CFR 211.192` for production and control records. `../01-program-foundation-and-regulatory-scoping/01.04-the-predicate-rules-and-enforcement-discretion.md §7` owns that argument | — |
| **And its frequency?** | **Event-driven, tied to the underlying data.** FDA, verbatim: *"If the review frequency for the data is specified in CGMP regulations, adhere to that frequency for the audit trail review."* **Risk-based periodicity applies only where CGMP is silent about the underlying data** | — |

**Both Annex 11 sentences are *shoulds* in a guideline that is not FDA's. Helix's estate spans both
regimes. And the two are never merged.**

**What merging would look like, so that it can be recognised if it is ever attempted.** It would look like
a Helix procedure requiring a reason-for-change field *under `21 CFR Part 11`*; or a Helix procedure setting
a quarterly audit trail review *as a Part 11 requirement*; or an Annex 11 clause cited in a document about
Helix's US-facing route without the words *EU-derived* beside it. **Each of those is a real requirement
somewhere, attached to the wrong source**, and each would leave Helix defending against an FDA investigator
a rule FDA never made — or, worse, discharging an EU expectation in a form the EU route cannot recognise
because it was written to a US citation.

> **No FDA source names a stand-alone periodic audit trail review frequency. An EU-derived guideline says
> audit trails need to be regularly reviewed, without naming an interval. Neither statement is converted
> into the other, and no frequency for the review of any record is set anywhere in this phase.**

**Where Helix implements a reason-for-change field or a review interval, it is Helix's decision and this
repository says so** — `ADR-0036`. That is the same discipline `ADR-0005` applies to FDA's Computer
Software Assurance guidance, which is device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820` and does not apply to Helix:
**adopted voluntarily, labelled as adopted, every time.**

## 8. What adopting it voluntarily costs, stated rather than avoided

**A voluntarily adopted standard is enforceable against the firm that adopts it.** `21 CFR 211.22(d)`
requires the quality control unit's responsibilities and procedures to be in writing and provides that such
written procedures **shall be followed**; `21 CFR 211.100(b)` and `21 CFR 211.160(a)` do the same work for
procedures owned outside it. **Once Helix writes down that it reviews its systems on a stated cadence, an
FDA investigator may ask to see the reviews** — not because Annex 11 reaches Helix, which it does not, but
because Helix's own written procedure does, and a written procedure not followed is a CGMP matter under
Helix's own predicate rules.

**That cost was taken with the decision and not discovered afterwards.** It is the same trade `ADR-0003`
took over enforcement discretion and `ADR-0005` took over the CSA guidance: **the programme gets the
substance of a good practice and never claims a source that does not reach it, and it accepts that the
practice, once written, binds.** **A voluntary standard that the firm does not intend to be held to is not
a standard; it is a statement of aspiration in a controlled document.**

## 9. What this chapter does not establish

- **It reports no arrangement, no frequency, no band, no move and no first due date.** All of those are
  `08.09-the-review-established.md`'s and `logs/periodic-review-register.md`'s.
- **It reports the result of no periodic review**, because none has been performed — **`IS-57`**. **An
  arrangement with a first due date is not a review.**
- **It does not claim that `21 CFR 211.180(e)` supports a periodic review of a computerised system.** **It
  is a product review**, and it is named in this phase only in order to say so.
- **It does not claim that any Annex 11 obligation binds Helix's US-facing route.** **It does not**, and
  every citation of Annex 11 in this phase carries the label EU-derived.
- **It sets no audit trail review frequency, and no review frequency for any record.**
- **It builds no product-to-system mapping and determines nothing about any product** — **`IS-54`** records
  the absence and `DP-44` is the route.
- **It closes no observation, makes no representation to FDA and reaches no compliance or legal
  conclusion** — `ADR-0051`, `ADR-0057`.
- **It re-bands no Phase 04 obligation, re-determines no Phase 05 cell, re-scores no Phase 03 pair and
  re-derives no Phase 07 period.**
- **It bears on `IS-01` not at all**, and it names no candidate explanation of it, by number, by content,
  by paraphrase or by allusion — `08.11-is-01.md`, `ADR-0055`.
