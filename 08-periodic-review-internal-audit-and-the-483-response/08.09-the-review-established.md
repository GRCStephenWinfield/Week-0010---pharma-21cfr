*Sixty-one systems have a review frequency and a first due date, and not one of them has been reviewed.*

---

## 1. What this chapter reports, and what it is not permitted to report

**`RV-01` to `RV-61` — one row per system, with a frequency, a first due date, and a reason on every row
that departs from its default.** The register is `logs/periodic-review-register.md` and it is
authoritative; every figure below is a count of its rows.

**Nothing in `21 CFR Part 11` and nothing in Parts 210 and 211 requires a periodic review of a computerised
system, and `21 CFR 211.180(e)` is a product review and is not the basis for one.**
`08.08-nothing-requires-a-periodic-review.md` owns that argument, owns the EU-derived limb and owns the
route Dr Anselm Ferrão acts on, and this chapter cites it rather than restating it. **The review is adopted
voluntarily** — `ADR-0053`, `DEC-808`.

> **This chapter reports the result of no periodic review, because none has been performed.**

**`IS-57`** — the arrangement is established and has not been run. **An arrangement with a first due date is
not a review**, and `§7` sets out what that means in terms.

## 2. Why all sixty-one, and why that is not generosity

**EU GMP Annex 11 clause 11 asks for a periodic evaluation as a *should*, in a guideline that is not
FDA's.** It
reaches Helix through the EU-facing route on which Dr Anselm Ferrão acts for the holder of the EU
manufacturing and import authorisation — **not for Helix, and not as a US regulatory role.**

**Helix cannot say which of its sixty-one systems that route reaches.** Phase 02's systems register carries
systems; Phase 02's record-type register carries record types; **no register this programme has built joins
either of them to a product, and no register joins a product to the EU-facing route.** `EV-826` records the
enquiry, names the registers searched one by one, and records the answer as none. **`IS-54`**, raised on
2027-04-01, owned by Rebekah Sandquist, with `DP-44` as the route.

**So one standard is applied to all sixty-one, and the reason is recorded as the reason.** **Helix is not
extending an EU-derived expectation across the estate out of ambition. It is doing so because it cannot
identify the subset the expectation reaches**, and applying a standard to a subset you cannot enumerate is
not available. **`PR-62` carries the risk that this is reported inside Helix as a raised standard**, and
the register states the reason in its own first section so that the reading has to be made against the
document rather than around it.

## 3. The default, and where it comes from

**The default frequency is derived from Phase 04's complexity-and-reliability band and from nothing
else** — `ADR-0054`, `DEC-811`, taken by the Computerised System Validation Lead on 2027-04-05.

**`21 CFR 211.68(b)` authorises that band for one purpose only — the degree and frequency of input/output
verification — and this is not that purpose. Using it to set a periodic review interval is Helix's own
method choice, made under no US provision, and is labelled as Helix's here, in `ADR-0054`, and on the face
of `logs/periodic-review-register.md` at the column definition every row is built from**, on the rule
Phase 04 fixed at `GOV-18`.

| Phase 04 band | Systems | Default frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | **11** | **Annual** |
| **Medium** | **27** | **Two-yearly** |
| **Low** | **23** | **Three-yearly** |
| | **11 + 27 + 23 = 61** | |

**The band is Phase 04's, read across row by row, and it is not re-taken here.** It was determined against
three observable features — configuration, interfaces and failure history — on a method recorded on its own
form with its own basis, **before this phase existed and by people who could not have known what it would
be used for.** **That is the whole of why it is the default.** A frequency set system by system on the
judgement of the people who own the systems is a frequency those people can defend and nobody else can
check; **a default taken from a band determined at the Phase 04 vantage of 2026-11-30, more than four
months before this phase opened and against three features that have nothing to do with a review interval,
is a default nobody in this phase could tune.** `AS-56` records the assumption that no system has been
re-banded since that date, and no `ADR-0015` or `ADR-0022` trigger has fired against it.

**Only column four is consulted.** Phase 04's register carries two graduations in two columns that are
never merged, and **the data integrity position in column five is not read anywhere in
`logs/periodic-review-register.md`** — `PR-29`. Where the record position matters, it matters because
Phase 04's own `§4` named the five systems on which it governs, and those five appear in the reason column
of five moved rows rather than in a formula.

## 4. Twenty move, and every one of them moves upward

**Twenty of the sixty-one carry a frequency shorter than their band's default. Eleven Medium systems move
to Annual and nine Low systems move to Two-yearly. 11 + 9 = 20.**

**No system on the register moves to a longer frequency than its default. Not one.**

**That direction is fixed and it is the whole of `ADR-0054`'s force.** The case for allowing movement in
both directions was argued at length and lost narrowly: a High-band system of frozen configuration and
untroubled history plainly does not need looking at every year, and refusing to say so spends the review
day on the system that needs it least. **It lost because the reason offered for lengthening an interval is
always the same reason — nothing has gone wrong — and a control relaxed on the ground that it has never
fired is a control being switched off.** **A band that can be argued away in the same document that
publishes it is not a band.** `DEC-811` records the refusal and what it cost.

**Each of the twenty carries a reason specific to the system in its own row, and there is no rule that
generates them.** They fall into five groups when read together, and the register says explicitly that the
groups are a way of reading the twenty rather than a rule that produced them.

| Ground | Move | Systems |
|---|---|---|
| **The shared-credential class at `21 CFR 11.10(d)` — the subject matter of `OBS-4`** | Medium → **Annual** | `SYS-003`, `SYS-004`, `SYS-005`, `SYS-006`, `SYS-007` |
| | Low → **Two-yearly** | `SYS-009`, `SYS-010`, `SYS-031`, `SYS-032` |
| **The five at Phase 04's `system-rigour-register.md §4` on which the record is graver than the system** | Medium → **Annual** | `SYS-050` |
| | Low → **Two-yearly** | `SYS-029`, `SYS-030`, `SYS-031`, `SYS-032` |
| **The retention estate Phase 07 named** — `IS-47` | Medium → **Annual** | `SYS-059` |
| | Low → **Two-yearly** | `SYS-036`, `SYS-060`, `SYS-061` |
| **The vendor-hosted systems on which `21 CFR 11.10` was never assessed** — `CG-03`, `IS-09` | Medium → **Annual** | `SYS-040`, `SYS-056` |
| **The two systems carrying the stability record a marketed product's expiry is judged against** | Medium → **Annual** | `SYS-034`, `SYS-035` |

**`SYS-031` and `SYS-032` appear under two grounds and move once.** Either ground alone would have moved
them; **the move is one step and not two, because a frequency is a frequency and reasons do not
accumulate.**

**Four of the five groups move as complete sets, and the fifth is complete in its result rather than in its
moves. That is the check on the whole exercise.** Every system named in `CG-11` or `CG-13` moves one step
up from its own default, whatever that default was. Every one of the five at Phase 04's `§4` moves one step
up. **All four systems of the retention estate move upward, three of them from Low and one from Medium.**
**The fifth ground is the stability pair, and it is a named set of two: `SYS-034` and `SYS-035` are the only
systems in the estate whose records carry the stability data a marketed product's expiry is judged against,
both are Medium, and both move to Annual.** And all three vendor-hosted systems end at Annual — two by a
move and `SYS-041` because its band was already High. **A ground that moved some members of a named set and
not others would be a reason that was really a preference**, and the register would not survive being read
across.

## 5. The result

**Annual 22 · Two-yearly 25 · Three-yearly 14. 22 + 25 + 14 = 61.**

| Frequency | Systems | How it reconciles |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual** | **22** | 11 High at the default **+** 11 Medium moved |
| **Two-yearly** | **25** | 27 Medium **−** 11 moved to Annual **+** 9 Low moved up |
| **Three-yearly** | **14** | 23 Low **−** 9 moved to Two-yearly |

**A caution about a numeral, applied before the collision rather than after it.** **The twenty-two systems
reviewed annually and the twenty-two commitments the response of 2026-03-06 made are unrelated
populations.** One is a count of rows in `logs/periodic-review-register.md` computed from Phase 04's band;
the other is a count of rows in `logs/commitment-register.md` computed from `EV-106`. **They are joined by
no document in this phase, they are never printed in one sentence anywhere else, and neither is evidence
about the other.** `PR-52` is the standing discipline and this is its Phase 08 instance.

## 6. The first due dates, and the pass they belong to

**The first review of every system falls due after this vantage. The earliest is 2027-05-31 and the latest
is 2028-04-28.** **None of the sixty-one falls due on or before 2027-04-09.**

**The first pass runs from 2027-05 to 2028-04 and covers all sixty-one systems inside twelve months** —
five in each of the first eleven months and six in the twelfth, **5 × 11 + 6 = 61.** Each row's date is
the last weekday of the month the pass allocates to it. **The pass is ordered by adopted frequency and then
by identifier**: the twenty-two annual systems first, then the twenty-five two-yearly, then the fourteen
three-yearly.

**After the first pass, a system's next review falls due one interval from the review that was performed
and not from the month the pass allocated to it.** The frequency governs the interval between reviews; the
pass governs only the order in which the estate is first reached.

**And the dates carry a fact the chapter does not smooth.** **The programme closes on 2027-04-30.** **The
earliest first review falls due a month after that, and the latest falls due almost a year after it.**
**`CAL-07` is one of the seven calendar entries `IS-07` records as outliving the programme with no named
owner beyond close-out**, and `RV-01` to `RV-61` are sixty-one commitments of exactly that kind. **The
obvious answer — that the quality unit holds them under `21 CFR 211.22` — is probably right, is still not
written down, and this chapter is not going to write it down on the quality unit's behalf.**

## 7. `IS-57` — an arrangement is not a review

**Reviews performed against any of the sixty-one rows: 0 of 61.**

**`CAL-07` has stood in the programme calendar since 2026-05-29** with a cadence of annual per system, a
named owner and a first occurrence of **2027-02-01**. **That date has passed and nothing has been performed
under it.** `07.13 §7` recorded the position from the other direction: *"And no periodic review exists at
all."*

**What this phase has done is build the arrangement that entry described as missing.** **What it has not
done is run it, and it could not have: the earliest row falls due seven weeks after the vantage.**

**No reviewer is named on any of the sixty-one rows**, and that is a gap rather than an omission. **The
review of a system cannot be performed by the system's owner, because the owner is the person whose
arrangements the review examines.** Who performs each review is a determination nobody has made —
**`DP-45`**, open, and competing for the same quality unit capacity as `DP-07`, `DP-11`, `DP-22`, `DP-27`,
`DP-35` and `DP-38`. **The record each review would be written on —
`templates/periodic-review-record-template.md` — is an empty form and contains no result.**

> **`IS-57`. The periodic review is established and has not been run. Nothing in this phase reports the
> result of one.**

## 8. What the review examines

**Every row names the same base and adds what is specific to its system.** The base is **the nine items EU
GMP Annex 11 clause 11 names — labelled EU-derived on every row** — together with the Helix-side items each
review adds: **the eleven `21 CFR 11.10` positions Phase 05 published for that system, its retention
position and its backup position.**

**The nine EU-derived items are the guideline's own and are quoted rather than paraphrased at
`08.08-nothing-requires-a-periodic-review.md`**, which owns the citation. **They are named here as the
content of a review Helix has adopted voluntarily, and at no point as an obligation binding Helix's
US-facing route** — **no Annex 11 obligation binds it.**

**The system-specific additions are what make a row a row rather than a heading.** On the two chromatography
instances the review examines the auditable event configuration at `AE-01` to `AE-34`. On the twelve
systems named in a shared-credential gap it examines the `21 CFR 11.10(d)` account position. On the three
vendor-hosted systems it examines **the measures the vendor's qualification recorded against
`21 CFR 11.30` — a section that reaches open systems only and therefore reaches none of these three, which
Phase 02 determined closed — together with the `21 CFR 11.10` assessment `CG-03` records was never
performed**, and what the vendor's backup covers. On the four systems of the retention estate it examines
**whether a restore has been performed and
what it was compared with.** And on the systems that hold no validation package, **it examines the
validation status report Annex 11 clause 11 names — EU-derived — which does not exist for them** —
`IS-23`.

## 9. What this chapter does not establish

- **It reports no review result**, and no such result exists — `IS-57`.
- **It re-bands nothing.** Phase 04's `11 / 27 / 23` is read across row by row and no band is edited,
  re-derived or re-taken — `AS-56`.
- **It sets no retention period, re-determines no Phase 05 cell, re-scores no Phase 03 pair and re-derives
  no Phase 07 period.**
- **It sets no audit trail review frequency.** **A periodic review of a system is not a review of a
  record**, and `RV-01` to `RV-61` say nothing about when any record is reviewed. **No primary source names
  a stand-alone periodic audit trail review frequency and none is set anywhere in this phase.**
- **It does not claim that `21 CFR 211.180(e)` supports a periodic review of a computerised system.** **It
  is a product review**, and it is named in this phase only in order to say so.
- **It does not claim that any Annex 11 obligation binds Helix's US-facing route.** **It does not**, and the
  EU-derived limb is labelled EU-derived at every citation.
- **It states no CAPA, no deviation, no remediation plan and no remediation cost**, and it reaches no
  compliance conclusion and no legal conclusion.
