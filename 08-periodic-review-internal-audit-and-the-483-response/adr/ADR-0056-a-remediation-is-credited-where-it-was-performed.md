# ADR-0056 — A remediation the programme performed is credited to the programme and not to the commitment's owner, and the difference is recorded

*A commitment tracked in one place and discharged in another is a commitment nobody is tracking.*

## Context

**The response filed on 2026-03-06 stated everything it planned with an owner and a date, and the owners it
gave were individuals rather than functions** — `01.02 §6`, and `../08.03-the-commitments.md §2` for whom.
That was before `ADR-0008` existed and it anticipated it: **the regulation's unit of accountability is a
person**, and the response was written that way because a regulator reading *the quality organisation will*
has been told nobody will.

**What happened afterwards is the thing this decision is about, and it is not a scandal.** A programme
running for thirteen months across an estate in scope will, in the course of doing its own work, bring
about states of affairs that somebody else undertook to bring about. It does not take the work over. **The
artefact a commitment described — a determination, an enumeration, a characterisation, a register — is
often the same artefact the programme needed for its own purposes**, and once it exists, the commitment's
condition is satisfied by whoever caused it to exist.

**So when the commitments were read, a second question had to be asked of every one that had been
satisfied: who performed the act?** And a third, which nobody had thought to ask before: **what does the
named owner's own tracking say?**

**The temptation at the register-design stage is the whole of the problem.** A register with a *position*
column and no *discharged by* column is simpler, shorter and, at the level of the organisation, true: the
company undertook to do a thing, the thing is done, the company did it. **Every step of the smoothing is
defensible. It is only the result that is useless.**

**And there is a second smoothing available, which is worse.** Where a named owner's own tracker disagrees
with the programme's register, the tidy act is to update the tracker. **It would take an afternoon, it
would be done in good faith, and it would destroy the only evidence that the two ever disagreed.**

## Decision

**A remediation the programme performed is credited to the programme and not to the commitment's named
owner, and the difference is recorded.**

Four limbs.

**The register carries the named owner the response gave and the party that performed the act as two
separate columns.** The first is a fact about the response of 2026-03-06 and does not move. **The second is
a fact about what happened afterwards**, and there is no reason in logic why the two must agree.

**Where the act that satisfied a commitment was performed by this programme, the register says so.** It
does not report the commitment as discharged by the individual the response named.

**The owner's own tracking position is recorded as a third column, as it stood when it was read.** **No
tracker was edited, corrected or reconciled**, and the enquiry that established what each showed is
retained rather than only its answer.

**And where the two disagree, the disagreement stands in the register as the finding.** Any later act by an
owner that brings them into agreement is **a new act with its own record and its own date**, and never a
correction of history.

## Alternatives considered

**Record the commitment as met by its named owner, on the ground that the owner is accountable for the area,
the work was done on the owner's estate with the owner's cooperation, and the company as a whole has done
what it said it would do.**

**This is the strongest alternative and it has to be put properly, because it is not a fudge — it is a
coherent view of what accountability means in an organisation.**

**Accountability is not the same as personal performance, and this repository says so itself.**
`ADR-0008`'s own boundary is that **ownership does not mean performance**: the owner is accountable that the
control is defined, resourced, performed as defined and evidenced. **On that definition an owner who
ensured the work was resourced — by supporting a funded programme that did it — has discharged the
accountability**, and insisting on personal performance would be a standard the repository applies nowhere
else. Record review is performed across hundreds of people in QC and QA; nobody suggests the Head of
Quality Control personally reviews each record.

**The commitment was made by Helix, not by an individual.** The response is a company document. FDA was
told what Helix would do, and Helix did it. **A register that reports the commitment as not discharged by
its owner is, at the level that matters to the reader of the response, reporting a distinction without a
difference.**

**And there is a fairness point that is not trivial.** The named owners cooperated. They gave access, they
answered enquiries, they attended sittings, they released people. **Recording that they did not discharge
their own commitments reads as a criticism**, and a programme that criticises the people who cooperated
with it will find the next programme has less cooperation.

**Refused, on four grounds.**

**Accountability discharged by somebody else has not been exercised, and the difference is the whole
information content of the register.** `ADR-0008`'s four limbs are conjunctive: defined, resourced,
performed as defined, **and evidenced.** **Where another party performed the act, what the owner did was
not object** — which may be entirely reasonable, and is not the same as having discharged the commitment.
**A register that cannot tell those apart cannot tell anybody anything about how the organisation actually
runs**, which is the only reason to keep it.

**The programme ends and the accountability does not.** Close-out is 2027-04-30. **Every commitment
discharged by the programme rather than by its owner is a commitment whose recurrence, maintenance and
evidencing now sit with somebody who has not performed it once.** A register that recorded the owner as
having discharged it would have concealed precisely the thing the organisation needs to know before the
programme's people leave — and it would have concealed it in the phase after which nobody re-reads the
register.

**The owner's own tracking is the evidence, and it exists.** This is what turns the question from a
philosophical one into a factual one. **The tracking each named owner maintains was read**, and where it
disagrees with the register, that is not a matter of interpretation about what accountability means. **It
is two records of the same undertaking that say different things.** Reporting the commitment as discharged
by its owner would require the register to disagree with the owner's own record in the owner's favour,
which is an odd position for an assessment to take.

**And the fairness point is conceded and answered.** **The register records an act, not a characterisation
of a person.** Nothing in it says any owner failed, was negligent or should have done otherwise; **no
document in this repository assesses any individual's work**, and the phase says so in terms. **The finding
is about a tracking arrangement, not about the people inside it** — and the arrangement is the thing that
will still be there after everyone in this programme has moved on.

---

**Reconcile the owners' trackers to the register as part of the phase, so that the organisation ends with
one consistent account of what has been discharged.**

**Tidy, obviously well intentioned, and the thing most programme managers would do.** Two records of the
same undertaking saying different things is a defect; defects get fixed; and leaving an owner's tracker
wrong helps nobody. **A single consistent account is also what an inspector would expect to find.**

**Refused, on two grounds.**

**Two records that disagree are evidence; one record that has been reconciled is not.** The disagreement is
the finding. **Editing the trackers would remove the finding and leave the register asserting it**, which
is the worst of both — a claim in one document about a state of affairs that no longer exists in the other.
**"No entry is edited in place without a record of what it said before"**, and the reason the repository
gives is exactly this: the question a later reader asks is not what the register says, it is what it said
at the time somebody relied on it.

**And it is not the programme's record to edit.** Each tracker belongs to a named individual, maintained
under that individual's own arrangements. **A programme that reaches into somebody else's records and
corrects them has done to those records what the estate was criticised for doing to its own** — changed a
prior state without a record of the change — and it would have done so in the same phase in which it
records that criticism about its own registers. **The route is the owner's own act, with its own record and
its own date**, and `DP-43` is the route.

---

**Record only the act and drop the owner's tracking column, on the ground that the tracking position is a
fact about somebody's private spreadsheet rather than about the commitment.**

**A narrower proposal with a real point behind it.** The trackers are not controlled documents. They are
working instruments maintained to varying standards by busy people, and elevating them into a register
column gives them a status they were never designed to carry. **Reporting a discrepancy against an
uncontrolled record risks making a finding out of somebody's filing habits.**

**Refused.** **The tracking position is not being assessed for accuracy; it is being recorded as what the
owner believes.** That is the point of the column. **A commitment whose owner believes it is still open,
and which the programme's register reports as met, is a commitment about which the organisation holds two
incompatible beliefs** — and the fact that one of the two records is informal makes it more revealing,
not less, because the informal record is the one the owner actually works from.

**And the column is what makes the finding checkable.** Without it the register would assert an inversion
and offer nothing to test it against. **With it, the enquiry is retained, the reading date is stated, and
anybody may go and look.**

## Consequences

**Accepted — the register reports something uncomfortable about named individuals' arrangements**, and the
phase carries an express statement that it assesses no individual's work. **The statement is made before
the finding rather than after it.**

**Accepted — the organisation ends the phase with two records that disagree, deliberately**, and the
disagreement is handed forward as `DP-43` rather than resolved. **A reader will regard that as untidy. It
is untidy on purpose.**

**Accepted — the finding will be read as a criticism of the owners.** `PR-58` and the chapter's own opening
carry the risk. **The mitigation is a sentence, and a sentence is a weak mitigation**, which is why the
register records acts and dates rather than adjectives.

**Accepted — the phase reports a governance defect that its own existence produced.** The programme did the
work; the programme is the reason the inversion exists. **Recording it is the programme reporting on
itself**, which is the same posture the phase takes about its own registers elsewhere.

**Gained — the organisation knows, before the programme ends, which commitments have never been performed
by the person who will have to keep performing them.** That is a fact of the first importance at close-out
and it existed nowhere before this phase.

**Gained — a register that could have come out the other way.** Had the owners' trackers agreed with the
register, the columns would have shown it.

## Refused

**This decision forbids the following in Phase 08 and binds every later phase that cites its registers.**

- **Recording a commitment as discharged by its named owner where another party performed the act.**
- **Editing, correcting, reconciling or annotating any owner's own tracking to agree with a programme
  register** — in this phase or in any later one. **Any act that brings them into agreement is a new act
  with its own record and its own date.**
- **Dropping, merging or collapsing the named owner, the discharging party and the owner's tracking
  position into a single column.**
- **Characterising any individual's work, diligence or performance**, or naming an individual in an
  adverse sense. **The finding is about a tracking arrangement.**
- **Re-attributing a commitment's named owner.** The response of 2026-03-06 named them and **no commitment
  is rewritten** — `ADR-0052`.
- **Treating a commitment discharged by the programme as evidence that the condition an observation
  records has ceased** — those are different objects — `ADR-0051`.
- **Stating a CAPA, a deviation, a remediation plan or a remediation cost** arising from the divergence.
