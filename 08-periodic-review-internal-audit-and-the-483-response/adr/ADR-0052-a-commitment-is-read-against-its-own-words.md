# ADR-0052 — The commitments are assessed against their own words, and one with no completion criterion is recorded as having none

*A commitment written so that nobody can say whether it was met is a commitment nobody can be held to, and the programme records that rather than repairing it.*

---

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | Accepted |

## Context

**The commitments Helix filed with FDA on 2026-03-06 are the only document in which the company has told a
regulator what it intends to do about the observations this programme took, and this phase is the first
time they are read against what happened.**

They were drafted inside the company, reviewed by six named individuals, released through the quality unit
and countersigned by the Vice President, Quality. The review was not perfunctory: **two proposed
commitments were removed in review because they named dates the programme could not own.** The response
separated what had already been done from what was planned, described nothing as complete that was not, and
made no claim about compliance status.

**And every commitment in it was written under the pressure of a fifteen-business-day window, in the weeks
after an inspection, by people who had not yet begun the work they were describing.** That is the
circumstance in which the phrases that mean nothing get written — the reinforcements, the improvements, the
continuings — because those phrases are true of every possible future and therefore commit the drafter to
none of them. **They are not dishonest. They are what a competent person writes when the honest sentence
would require knowing something that will not be known for a year.**

**So the assessment has a specific hazard, and it is not that the assessor will be unfair.** It is that a
commitment with any elasticity in it can be read, at the far end, in a way that the outcome satisfies — and
a competent professional will find that reading honestly, because it is genuinely available. **The
elasticity is the finding, and it is invisible from the far end.**

**A second hazard runs the other way.** The programme now knows what each commitment should have said. **A
criterion written now, by the programme, would produce an assessment of Helix against a document that does
not exist**, and would be the firm marking a paper it set, after the work, knowing the answer.

**The method was therefore authorised on 2027-03-25 at `DEC-804`, and `EV-106` was opened on 2027-03-30.**
The order is on the record because it is the only thing that makes the reading worth anything.

## Decision

**The commitments are assessed against their own words, and a commitment whose words name no completion
criterion is recorded as having none rather than judged met or unmet.**

Five limbs.

**The words assessed are the words the response used.** Not the covering text, not the charter, not the
quality records that later attached to the same subject matter, not what the drafters would say if asked
now. **The commitment is the sentence Helix gave to a regulator and it is assessed as that sentence.**

**A completion criterion is a state of the world, named in the commitment's own words, that would satisfy
it** — observable in principle by somebody who was not party to the commitment, **and with a state of the
world in which it is false.** A date is not a criterion; an owner is not a criterion. **A commitment
satisfied by every state of the world is satisfied by the state of the world that obtained on the day the
Form FDA 483 was issued.**

**The reading precedes the description of practice.** For each commitment: read the words; ask whether they
name such a state of the world; record the answer or its absence. **Only then is what actually happened
described.**

**Where the words name none, the position recorded is *No completion criterion*.** It is a complete cell,
not an empty one, and it says something checkable about the commitment while saying nothing whatever about
whether Helix did the work.

**And no criterion is supplied now, for any commitment.** **No commitment is rewritten, extended, narrowed,
replaced or added to; no new commitment is made; and no date any commitment carried is extended, replaced
or proposed.**

## Alternatives considered

**Judge every commitment on the balance of the evidence, supplying the missing test from what a reasonable
reader would have understood the commitment to mean.**

**This is what most assessments of this kind actually do, and the case for it is strong enough that it has
to be set out properly.**

**A register with three values is more useful than a register with four.** *Met* and *Not met* are
actionable; *No completion criterion* is a comment about drafting, and an accountable owner reading it
learns nothing about what to do next. **A quality system exists to drive action, and a position that drives
none is a position that will be skipped.**

**The missing test is usually not really missing.** A commitment to *reinforce the importance of individual
accountability* plainly contemplates something — training, communication, a procedural change — and a
reader of ordinary competence can say what would and would not count. **Refusing to supply it looks less
like rigour than like pedantry**, and it declines to use knowledge the assessor actually has.

**And there is a fairness argument, which is the best one.** Several of these commitments describe work
that very likely happened. **Recording them as untestable, rather than as met, denies the organisation
credit for something it may well have done** — and a programme that will not credit good work when it sees
it will not be believed when it criticises.

**Refused, on four grounds.**

**Supplying the test would assess Helix against a commitment Helix never filed.** The sentence in the
register would be the programme's sentence and not the response's, and the position recorded against it
would be a position about a document that exists nowhere outside this repository. **What Helix owes a
regulator is what Helix said, not what the programme thinks it should have said.**

**It would be the firm marking its own homework twice.** Helix set the question, inside the company. If it
now also writes the mark scheme, after the work, knowing the answer, **the exercise closes on itself: the
assessment can only report that the company met a standard the company wrote for itself once it knew what
it had done.** *"An assurance opinion from the party that designed the thing being assured is not an
opinion, it is a restatement"* — and a criterion supplied after the fact is that restatement with an extra
step.

**It would convert a finding about drafting into a finding about delivery.** Those are different findings,
owned by different people. **The response's drafting belongs to the regulatory function; the remediation
belongs to the accountable owners.** Supplying a criterion and then recording *not met* against it would
take a defect in a document Helix filed with an agency and re-describe it as somebody else's delivery
shortfall — which is both wrong and, for the function performing the assessment, convenient.

**And the fairness argument is conceded and answered.** **The programme does not record these commitments
as not met, and says in terms that nothing suggests the work described did not occur.** Reinforcement,
awareness and improvement are real things organisations really do. **What is absent is not the work. It is
the test** — and *unassessed is not adverse*, a position this repository has held since Phase 02.

---

**Rewrite the three-value assessment as a two-value one plus a footnote: record the commitment as *Not
met*, with a note that the drafting made it untestable.**

**A tidier register**, and it has the merit of putting the defect where somebody will see it: an owner
scanning a *Not met* column will read the note; an owner scanning a *No completion criterion* column may
conclude the row does not concern them.

**Refused.** **It manufactures a failure out of a drafting defect.** Nothing establishes that the activity
did not happen, and recording *Not met* asserts that it did not. **The note does not repair this**, because
the note is the part that does not travel: a count of commitments not met will be reported, quoted and
carried into a management summary, **and the footnote explaining that three of them are not failures will
not go with it.**

**And it is the mirror image of the error the programme refuses in the other direction.** Recording an
untestable commitment as *met* would credit Helix with a change it cannot demonstrate; recording it as *not
met* debits Helix with a failure it cannot demonstrate. **Both are the same error — a position taken where
none is available — and choosing the unflattering one is not rigour, it is a different bias with better
optics.**

---

**Read the commitments alongside what the programme did, so that the assessment is efficient and the
correspondence between undertaking and delivery is visible on the same page.**

**A practical proposal with a real efficiency behind it.** The programme's registers already establish most
of what the commitments were about; reading the two together is one pass rather than two, and the
correspondence is precisely what the phase is trying to expose.

**Refused, and the refusal is about sequence rather than about content.** **A commitment read backwards
from what happened is a description of what happened.** Given a sentence with elasticity and a known
outcome, the reading under which the outcome satisfies the sentence will be found, and found in good faith.
**The elasticity — which is the entire finding — is invisible from that direction**, because every
commitment looks satisfiable once you know what satisfied it.

**The method therefore does the same work in the opposite order**, which costs a pass and buys the finding:
read the words first, in ignorance of the outcome, record the criterion or its absence, and only then
describe the practice. **The correspondence between undertaking and delivery is still exposed** — it is at
`../08.04-who-actually-did-it.md` — and it is exposed by a method that could have produced a different
answer.

## Consequences

**Accepted — the register carries a position that drives no action.** Some rows record a fact about how a
sentence was written and nothing about what anybody should do next, and an owner reading one of them may
reasonably ask what it is for. **The answer is that it is for the next response Helix files.**

**Accepted — the phase criticises a document produced by the function that owns this decision.** The
response was released through the quality unit and the regulatory function drafted it. **The decision to
record the defect rather than repair it was taken by the Head of Regulatory Affairs, about that function's
own document**, and that is recorded because it is the part of the decision that cost something.

**Accepted — the reading took a separate pass and a separate authorisation, and the phase is longer for
it.**

**Accepted — a reader will ask why the commitments were not simply reissued in better form.** They were
not, because reissuing them is a representation and this programme makes none — `ADR-0051`, `ADR-0057` —
and because **the commitments are read against their own words, not rewritten.**

**Gained — the next response Helix files can be tested before it is sent.** The completion-criterion test
is three questions long, it takes minutes per commitment, and **it is the only artefact of this phase that
would have prevented the finding it produced.** It is carried into the instrument at
`../templates/commitment-assessment-record-template.md`.

**Gained — a position that could have come out the other way.** Every row in the register was produced by a
reading made in ignorance of the outcome, which is the only arrangement in which *met* means anything.

## Refused

**This decision forbids the following in Phase 08 and binds every later phase that cites its registers.**

- **Supplying, drafting, proposing or implying a completion criterion for any commitment that carries
  none**, in a chapter, a register cell, a diagram, a governance minute or a worked example.
- **Rewriting, extending, narrowing, replacing or adding to any commitment the response of 2026-03-06
  made**, or making a new one.
- **Extending, replacing or proposing a date for any commitment.**
- **Recording an untestable commitment as met or as not met**, or reporting it inside either count.
- **Assessing any commitment that attaches to an observation outside this programme's scope.** **The
  exclusion is published on the register so that it can be seen rather than inferred** — `ADR-0006`.
- **Reading a commitment against what happened before reading it against its own words.**
- **Treating a commitment position as a position on a condition, or the reverse** — `ADR-0051`.
- **Stating a CAPA, a deviation, a remediation plan or a remediation cost** arising from any commitment
  position.
- **Characterising any individual's drafting, or any individual's work** — the defect is in a sentence, not
  in a person.
