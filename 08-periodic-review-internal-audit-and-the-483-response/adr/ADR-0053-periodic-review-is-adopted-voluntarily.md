# ADR-0053 — Periodic review is adopted voluntarily where FDA requires none, and its EU-derived limb is labelled EU-derived at every citation

*Helix establishes a review no FDA source requires, on a content an EU guideline supplies, and says which is which every single time.*

## Context

**Helix's estate has never had a periodic review of a computerised system, at any point, and nothing in the
regulations Helix is inspected against requires one.**

**Nothing in `21 CFR Part 11` requires it.** None of the eleven paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` is a recurring
evaluation of a system's continuing state, and none names an interval. `21 CFR 11.10(e)` requires audit
trails to be generated, not to obscure previously recorded information, to be retained and to be available
for agency review and copying — **and imposes no internal review obligation at all.**

**Nothing in Parts 210 and 211 requires it either.** `21 CFR 211.68` requires appropriate controls over
computerised systems and, at `(a)`, a written program designed to assure proper performance. `(b)` further
requires that *"[i]nput to and output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or
data shall be checked for accuracy"* and that *"[t]he degree and frequency of input/output verification
shall be based on the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system."* **That is the one
CGMP sentence that scales a computerised-system obligation to complexity and reliability — and its object
is input/output verification and nothing else under that provision, not the system's continuing validated
state.** `21 CFR 211.68` imposes no general computerised-system validation requirement and no periodic
system review.

**And `21 CFR 211.180(e)` is a product review.** Its object is *the quality standards of each drug product*
and its stated purpose is to determine the need for changes in drug product specifications or in
manufacturing or control procedures. **It says nothing whatever about computerised systems.** It is,
nonetheless, the citation that appears at the head of a very large number of periodic system review
procedures in this industry, **and a control performed for years against a mis-cited obligation is a
control nobody has ever been able to argue about, because the argument was foreclosed by the citation.**

**What does ask for one is EU GMP Annex 11**, at its *Periodic evaluation* clause, **as a *should*, in a
guideline, in a body of law FDA does not enforce.** It reaches anything Helix does only through the
EU-facing product route on which Dr Anselm Ferrão acts, **for the holder of the EU manufacturing and import
authorisation and not for Helix.**

**And Helix cannot say which of its systems that route reaches.** No register this programme has built
joins a system to a product, or a product to the EU-facing route. The enquiry that established it names the
registers searched one by one and records the answer as none — **`IS-54`**.

**So the decision is taken in an awkward position and the awkwardness is the point.** Helix is establishing
a control that no source binding it requires, on a content supplied by a source that does not bind it, over
a population it cannot narrow to the systems the non-binding source would reach.

## Decision

**Periodic review is adopted voluntarily where FDA requires none, and its EU-derived limb is labelled
EU-derived at every citation.**

Five limbs.

**The adoption is recorded as voluntary, on the face of the arrangement.** No document in this phase cites
any FDA source as requiring a periodic review of a computerised system, **and `21 CFR 211.180(e)` is never
cited as the basis for one.** It is named in this phase only in order to record that it is a product review.

**The content comes from EU GMP Annex 11's *Periodic evaluation* clause, quoted rather than paraphrased,
and labelled EU-derived wherever it appears** — in a chapter, a register column, a review record, a
governance minute or a diagram. **Only the January 2011 text is in force**, and the draft revision
consulted on to 2025-10-07 is watched at `CAL-12` and never cited as an obligation.

**No Annex 11 obligation is described as binding Helix's US-facing route.** **It does not. FDA does not
enforce EudraLex, an FDA inspection is not an Annex 11 inspection, and an Annex 11 expectation is not an
FDA requirement.**

**The standard applies to the whole estate, and the reason is recorded as the reason.** **Not as
generosity, and not as a raised standard, but because Helix cannot identify the subset the EU-facing route
reaches** — and applying a standard to a subset that cannot be enumerated is not available. **The reason is
carried on the register's own face as well as in the chapter**, because a reason recorded only in a chapter
does not travel.

**And the review is a review of a system, not of a record.** **No audit trail review frequency and no
review frequency for any record is set anywhere in this phase.**

## Alternatives considered

**Cite `21 CFR 211.180(e)` as the basis and run the periodic system review under it, as most of the
industry does.**

**This alternative has to be argued properly, because it is not a lazy option — it is the option a great
many careful, well-run firms have taken, and it produces a real control that catches real problems.**

**It gives the review a US anchor, which matters more than it sounds.** A control with a CGMP citation at
its head is a control that survives budget review, gets resourced, gets audited internally and gets shown
to an investigator without an argument about why it exists. **A control whose stated basis is *we chose
to* is the first control cut when the programme's people leave**, and every quality professional knows it.

**The two reviews genuinely share material.** An annual product quality evaluation looks at deviations,
investigations, complaints and trends; a periodic system evaluation looks at deviation records, incidents,
problems and performance. **Running them out of one procedure, against one citation, on one annual cycle,
is administratively coherent** and avoids two overlapping reviews performed by the same people a month
apart.

**And the paragraph is not wholly silent on the territory.** `21 CFR 211.180(e)` requires records to be
**maintained so that the data in them can be used** for the evaluation — a requirement about the condition
records are kept in, which a system that has degraded would fail. **A firm could argue, without
embarrassment, that keeping the systems that hold those records fit for that purpose is within the
paragraph's contemplation.**

**Refused, on three grounds.**

**The paragraph's object is a drug product and the review's object is a system, and no amount of overlap in
inputs makes those the same object.** The sentence evaluates *the quality standards of each drug product*
in order to determine the need for changes in specifications or in manufacturing or control procedures.
**An annual product quality evaluation could be performed perfectly for a decade without anybody ever
asking whether a computerised system remains in a validated state**, because that is not what the paragraph
asks anybody to look at. The word doing the work in the industry's reading is *annual*, **and it is doing it
in a sentence about a medicine.**

**A mis-cited basis is worse than no basis, because it cannot be corrected by argument.** A control that
says *we adopted this* invites the question *why*, and the answer is available and checkable. A control that
says *`21 CFR 211.180(e)` requires this* forecloses the question — **and when somebody eventually reads the
paragraph, what collapses is not the citation but the control's credibility, and with it every judgement
made under it.** **This repository has taken the same position three times: `ADR-0003` on enforcement
discretion, `ADR-0005` on the CSA guidance, and `ADR-0046` on records that leave Part 11.** **A programme
that claims a source that does not reach it has bought a year of comfort with its own defensibility.**

**And it would be a false statement to an investigator, made in a controlled document.** That is the
shortest form of the objection and it is sufficient alone.

---

**Do not establish a periodic review at all, on the ground that no source binding Helix requires one, and
spend the effort on obligations that are actually binding.**

**A serious and disciplined position**, and the one most consistent with a narrow reading of the
programme's own charter. The programme exists to discharge obligations Helix is under. **The registers
already record a substantial quantity of work that is genuinely owed under `21 CFR Part 11` and Parts 210
and 211 and has not been done**, and adding a voluntary control ahead of those is a form of displacement
activity. **A control adopted voluntarily also becomes enforceable against the firm** — `21 CFR 211.22(d)`
provides that the quality control unit's written procedures **shall be followed** — **so Helix would be
manufacturing an exposure it does not currently have.**

**Refused, on two grounds, and the second is the one that decided it.**

**The exposure argument proves too much.** Every written procedure a firm adopts becomes enforceable
against it; that is what a procedure is for. **A quality system that declined to write down anything it was
not compelled to write down would be a quality system consisting entirely of the regulations**, which is
not a quality system.

**And the estate's own record says why the review is needed, without any reference to Annex 11.** Seven
phases have established, on Helix's own registers, that the systems' validated state, control positions,
audit trail configurations, retention arrangements and backup positions were in many cases determined for
the first time by this programme — **and that nothing whatever existed to keep any of those determinations
true afterwards.** *"A determination nobody maintains is a determination that was true once"* — `ADR-0015`,
which fixed a review trigger for the record determination in Phase 02 for exactly this reason. **The
periodic review is the same instrument at estate scale, and its justification is Helix's own position
rather than a European guideline.** Annex 11 supplies the review's **content**; it is not the reason the
review exists.

---

**Adopt the review, but scope it to the systems that plausibly serve EU-facing products, so that the
EU-derived expectation is discharged where it reaches and no further.**

**The most intellectually honest of the alternatives, and the one that would have been chosen if the
information existed.** It would apply an EU-derived expectation exactly where the EU-derived route runs,
would not extend a non-binding standard across an estate it does not reach, and would be smaller, cheaper
and faster.

**Refused, because the join does not exist.** **No register this programme has built carries a product.**
Phase 02's registers carry systems and record types; Phases 03 to 07 carry pairs, obligations, control
positions, auditable event classes and retention derivations. **Not one of them joins a system to a product
or a product to a route**, and the enquiry that established that is retained so that the absence can be
tested as a search rather than taken as a conclusion.

**And *plausibly* is the word that disqualifies it.** A scope built on which systems plausibly serve
EU-facing products is a scope built on somebody's recollection, and **a scoping decision resting on an
unrecorded assumption is precisely the defect this programme was created to remove** — `ADR-0001`,
`IS-02`. **Building the mapping is a determination about products, and this phase determines nothing about
products**; the absence is recorded as `IS-54` with `DP-44` as the route, and a later phase may build it and
narrow the scope with a basis.

## Consequences

**Accepted — Helix has created an obligation for itself that it did not previously have**, and it is
enforceable against Helix through its own procedures. **That was taken with the decision, not discovered
after it.**

**Accepted — the review reaches systems that no EU-facing route touches**, and Helix cannot say which ones
those are. **The cost of the missing mapping is paid in scope**, and `IS-54` names it rather than absorbing
it.

**Accepted — the arrangement will be described inside Helix as a raised standard**, within about a quarter,
by somebody summarising it in good faith. **It is not a raised standard. It is a failure to be able to
narrow one**, and `PR-62` carries the risk that the first description wins. **The register states the
reason in its own first section so that the reading has to be made against the document rather than around
it.**

**Accepted — every citation of Annex 11 in this repository carries a label**, which is repetitive to write
and repetitive to read, and **the repetition is the control.** A single unlabelled citation, copied into a
procedure, becomes an obligation Helix does not have.

**Gained — a control whose basis can be checked.** Anybody may read the clause, read the paragraph Helix
declined to cite, and disagree with the decision. **A control that can be argued with is a control somebody
has thought about.**

**Gained — the estate acquires the instrument that keeps seven phases of determinations from becoming
determinations that were true once.**

## Refused

**This decision forbids the following in Phase 08 and binds every later phase that cites its registers.**

- **Citing `21 CFR 211.180(e)`, `21 CFR 211.68`, `21 CFR 11.10(k)` or any other FDA source as requiring a
  periodic review of a computerised system.** **`21 CFR 211.180(e)` is a product review.**
- **Citing any EU GMP Annex 11 clause without the label EU-derived**, in a chapter, a register, a review
  record, a template, a diagram, a minute or a workbook.
- **Describing any Annex 11 obligation as binding Helix's US-facing route**, or describing the Qualified
  Person as a US regulatory role.
- **Citing the draft revision of Annex 11, or any draft, as a requirement**, or assuming an adoption date.
- **Describing the estate-wide scope as generosity, as a raised standard, as best practice or as anything
  other than what it is** — an inability to identify the subset.
- **Building, inferring or assuming a product-to-system mapping**, or determining anything about any
  product.
- **Setting an audit trail review frequency, or a review frequency for any record**, anywhere in this
  phase. **A periodic review of a system is not a review of a record.**
- **Merging a Part 11 position with an Annex 11 expectation**, in either direction — including a
  reason-for-change field or a review interval described as a `21 CFR Part 11` requirement.
- **Reporting the result of any periodic review.** **None has been performed** — `IS-57`.
