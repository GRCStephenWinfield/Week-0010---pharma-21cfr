A 21 CFR Part 11 Data Integrity Programme, in Nine Phases

An end-to-end FDA 21 CFR Part 11 data integrity and computerised system validation programme, run for a US oral solid dose drug manufacturer from a Form FDA 483 through to close-out — four hundred and twenty-four days, nine published phases, and a determination made record type by record type rather than asserted once.

Helix Pharma, Inc. is fictional. Its sites, its people, its systems, its inspection, its Form FDA 483 and every figure here is illustrative. The regulations, guidances and standards are real, are cited exactly, and can be looked up — 21 CFR Parts 11, 210, 211 and 820, FDA’s August 2003 Scope and Application guidance, FDA’s December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity questions and answers, EU GMP Annex 11, GAMP 5, MHRA, PIC/S and NIST SP 800-53. The two kinds of statement are kept visibly apart throughout. Nothing here is advice.

The scenario

FDA conducted a routine CGMP surveillance inspection of the Ridgemont, New Jersey site from 2026-02-02 to 2026-02-13 — twelve calendar days — and at the close issued a Form FDA 483 carrying seven inspectional observations, of which three concern electronic records: audit trail review in the QC laboratory, attribution of entries on a packaging line system, and a change to an approved value in the electronic batch record. Helix filed a written response with FDA on 2026-03-06, twenty-one calendar days after issue and exactly fifteen business days. The programme was funded seventeen days after the 483, kicked off on 2026-03-02 and closed out on 2027-04-30. A Form FDA 483 is not a final agency determination; no Warning Letter, Untitled Letter or judicial action exists, and no document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of any kind.

What this repository argues

Seven positions hold across all nine phases. Each is argued at length in exactly one chapter and cited from everywhere else.

  • Part 11 applies to records, not to systems

    A system is in scope because of the records it holds, and no document here writes “a Part 11 system” without saying which record types make it one.

    21 CFR 11.1(b) — the part applies to records in electronic form created, modified, maintained, archived, retrieved or transmitted under any records requirements set forth in agency regulations. ADR-0001.

  • The predicate rule does the work

    Every one of the four categories FDA’s narrow interpretation reaches starts from a record some other regulation already required to exist. Remove the predicate rule and there is no Part 11 record.

    FDA’s August 2003 Scope and Application guidance — records not required to be retained under predicate rules, but nonetheless maintained electronically, are not part 11 records.

  • Enforcement discretion is a stated intention, not an exemption

    It is not a legal exemption and not a safe harbour, and it confers no rights. Every row that shows discretion on the Part 11 side shows an unmoved obligation on the predicate-rule side.

    The August 2003 guidance — persons must still comply with all applicable predicate rule requirements for validation — read against 21 CFR 211.68, over which no discretion has been announced. ADR-0003.

  • The printout route has three conditions and is not an escape hatch

    Two FDA states expressly and a third Helix derives; they are conjunctive, and for a dynamic record the first of them generally fails. The set of three is never attributed to FDA’s text.

    The August 2003 guidance’s printout passage, read against 21 CFR 211.194(a)(4). ADR-0010.

  • A determination is not a control, and a control is not validation

    Determining that Part 11 reaches a record says nothing about whether the record is any good; assessing that a control is present is not evidence that the system was validated.

    21 CFR 11.10(a) — validation of systems to ensure accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and the ability to discern invalid or altered records. ADR-0029, ADR-0034.

  • A disposition is not a closure

    An observation is disposed of by stating the condition’s present position. Closure is a representation about a matter between a firm and an agency, and the firm is one of two parties to it.

    FDA’s own statement — the FDA Form 483 does not constitute a final agency determination of whether any condition is in violation of the FD&C Act or any of its relevant regulations. ADR-0051.

  • Readiness is the ability to answer, not the quality of the answer

    In February 2026 Helix could not say which of its records were Part 11 records, which systems held them, or what its control position was. It can now answer all three, and many of the answers are not met.

    A readiness position is not a compliance position, and no compliance conclusion is stated anywhere in this repository. ADR-0059.

And one refusal, held for eight phases

Four internal audits between 2023-06 and 2025-09 covered the three systems the observations concern — each system falling inside the scope of at least two — and none raised a finding on the subject matter of any of them. The question was raised on 2026-05-19, carried open and unweighted for eight phases, and closed at Phase 08 on what the programme could establish, without any of the four candidate explanations being chosen. No candidate is named, numbered, ranked or alluded to in the phase in which it closes, or anywhere else. The refusal is permanent.
