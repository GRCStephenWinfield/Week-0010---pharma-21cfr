# 03.11 — The Flow the Unit Does Not See

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Document ID | `HLX-DI-2026-311` |

| Owner | Joachim Reuter — Head of Automation and Manufacturing IT |

| Classification | Confidential — Helix Pharma, Inc. Data Integrity Programme // Illustrative Portfolio Sample |

*This phase speaks as at 2026-09-30. All content about Helix Pharma, Inc. is fictional and illustrative; the regulations and guidances cited are real and are cited exactly.*

---

## 1. What this chapter settles

Every preceding chapter of this phase has argued for the method. This one argues against it.

**Helix's unit of assessment is a record type on a system. That is an information-technology-shaped unit,
and the most methodologically detailed regulator-authored source on this subject directs that a data
integrity risk assessment should not be shaped that way.** The direction is PIC/S PI 041-1's, it is one
sentence long, and Helix's method does not follow it.

**Thirty-one of the thirty-four in-scope record types are held on more than one system. The assessment
looks at the record on each of those systems, and it looks at the movement between them nowhere.**

This chapter states the criticism in the source's own words, says exactly what Helix's proxy buys and
exactly what it costs, sets out the four questions the unit cannot ask, and raises the issue. **It does not
resolve it, and it does not propose a replacement unit at this vantage.** A phase that finds the hole in
its own instrument and then patches it in the same document has usually patched it badly; the honest
sequence is to publish the hole, carry it, and let the phase that has to live with the consequence decide
what to do.

## 2. The sentence

From PIC/S PI 041-1, *Good Practices for Data Management and Integrity in Regulated GMP/GDP Environments*,
in force 1 July 2021, in its section on data risk — **§5.5.4**, verbatim:

> *"Risk assessments should focus on a business process (e.g. production, QC), evaluate data flows and the
> methods of generating and processing data, and not just consider information technology (IT) system
> functionality or complexity."*

**Read the last clause without softening it.** It is not a suggestion that IT be considered alongside
process; it is a statement that an assessment which considers only information technology has missed the
subject. **An assessment structured as a list of systems, with attributes scored against each, is the
structure that sentence is written about.**

> **The final clause is the one Helix has to answer, and it is sharper than the short form suggests.**
> PI 041-1 does not merely warn against an IT-shaped assessment; it warns specifically against framing it
> on *system functionality or complexity*. **That is the frame `21 CFR 211.68(b)` uses — *"the complexity
> and reliability of the computer or related system"* — and it is the frame this phase is anchored on.
> Helix therefore holds a regulation and a guidance pointing in opposite directions on the same question,
> and it has followed the regulation. That is a choice, and it is stated here as a choice.**

Its status is stated with it, as this repository states the status of every non-FDA source. PI 041-1 says
of itself:

> *"This guide is not mandatory or enforceable under law. It is not intended to be restrictive or to
> replace national legislation regarding data integrity requirements for manufacturers and distributors."*

**PIC/S is not a US regulator and PI 041-1 is not an FDA requirement.** FDA participates in PIC/S, and that
participation does not make the guide enforceable in the United States — the guide says so itself in the
paragraph above. **What FDA's participation does mean is that PI 041-1 reflects a body of expectation FDA
takes part in shaping**, which is why a departure from it is worth writing down rather than worth
ignoring.

The same source supplies the criteria Helix's consequence axis is built on — *"Which decision does the
data influence?"* and *"What is the impact of the data to product quality or safety?"* — adopted
voluntarily and labelled as adopted. **Helix cannot take the criteria and decline the caution in the same
document.**

## 3. What Helix's unit is, and why it is the shape it is

`03.02` fixed the unit as the record-type/system pair and argued for it. Nothing here withdraws that
argument. But the pair has a shape, and the shape is worth naming plainly: **the record type comes from a
register of record types, the system comes from a register of systems, and the join produces one row per
place a record sits. The rows are places. There are no rows for movements.**

That is not an accident of implementation. It follows from what Phase 02 handed over. **Phase 02 produced
two registers, and neither of them is a process map.** The record-type register carries predicate-rule
positions and the systems that hold each type; the systems register carries systems, owners, locations and
the record types on each. **There is nothing in either register that says a record leaves one system and
arrives at another**, because Phase 02 was not asked to establish that and did not.

**A method can only be built on the evidence that exists at the vantage.** Building a process-and-data-flow
unit in this phase would have meant establishing the flows first, and establishing the flows is a piece of
work nobody at Helix has done. `03.01 §8` gives the four reasons the assessment happened at all, and the
timetable — **`MS-07` on 2026-09-30, sixty-one days after `MS-06`, and `MS-08` sixty-one days after that**
— is a real constraint rather than an excuse. **The choice was a system-shaped assessment now or a
process-shaped assessment later than Phase 04 needed one.**

**Helix took the first and is saying so here.**

## 4. The number

**Thirty-one of the thirty-four in-scope record types are held on more than one system.** Three are held on
one system only.

The two most widely held record types sit on ten systems each — `03.06 §2` gives the spread. The two
chromatography data systems, one at Ridgemont and one at Cary, hold the same five record types as each
other, and a laboratory data archive and a backup and restore platform hold several of the same types
again. **Every one of those relationships is a place where a record moves, and every one of them is
assessed as two or more separate pairs with no row for what happens in between.**

The arithmetic of the assessment is unaffected: one hundred and twenty-one pairs, six hundred and five
determinations, and the count reconciles to the registers exactly as `03.02 §5` describes. **The
completeness of the population is not in question. What is in question is whether the population is a
population of the right objects.**

## 5. What the proxy buys

The case for the pair is not that it is a good model of a data flow. It is that it is a good model of
something else, and the something else has properties a process map at this vantage would not have had.

**It is countable, and the count reconciles.** One hundred and twenty-one pairs derive mechanically from
two published registers. A reader with those registers can re-run the join and get the same number. **A
process-based population at this vantage would have been a population somebody drew**, and its
completeness would have rested on the judgement of whoever drew it — which is precisely the failure
`ADR-0009` corrected in Phase 02, where a register that had never been reconciled to the estate was wrong
by twenty-six entries in both directions.

**It is auditable at the level of the row.** Every pair names a record type, a system, a predicate rule
and two owners. Each of those is a controlled identifier from a controlled register. A process step, at
this vantage, would name none of them.

**It inherits the determination rather than re-opening it.** Phase 02's constraint was that Phase 03 must
not re-open the applicability determination. A unit built from the determination's own outputs cannot
re-open it by accident. **A process-shaped unit would have had to decide, for every step, which record
type it produced — and that decision is a determination in everything but name.**

**It is re-runnable.** When a record type moves onto a new system, one pair appears. When a system leaves
the estate, its pairs go. `ADR-0022` makes that mechanical. A process population would have to be redrawn.

**And it can be handed to Phase 04 in the form Phase 04 needs.** Phase 04 sizes validation rigour against
assessed risk, and validation attaches to systems. **A risk statement expressed against a record type on a
system maps onto a validation package without an intervening translation.**

## 6. What it misses, exactly

> **The gap is not only a departure from good practice. It sits on a `shall`.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)`
> provides, in the sentence immediately before the one this phase is anchored on, that *"Input to and
> output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or data shall be checked for
> accuracy."* **The object of that requirement is exactly what the unit cannot see: the record entering a
> system and the record leaving it.** Helix's instrument assesses the record in each place and assesses
> neither the input nor the output, so **the assessment is anchored on the third sentence of
> `21 CFR 211.68(b)` and is blind to the second.** Nothing here states whether any such check exists at
> Helix, whether it works, or whether it is adequate — that is Phase 04's and Phase 05's, and this phase
> states no control position for anything. **The provision is named so that `IS-18` is carried as a gap
> against a regulation Helix is inspected against, and not only against guidance from a body that does not
> inspect it.**

Four questions the unit cannot ask. They are stated as questions rather than as gaps, because none of them
has been answered and this chapter is not answering them.

**One — does the record arrive intact?** Where a record type is held on two systems, something moved it.
The instrument asks whether the record on the source system is accurate and whether the record on the
destination system is accurate. **It does not ask whether the second is the first.** A transformation
between them — a rounding, a truncation, a field mapping, a unit conversion — would be invisible to both
determinations, because each is a determination about a record in a place.

**Two — which of the copies is the original?** This is the sharpest of the four, because it bears directly
on the attribute the phase spends the most time on. *Original or a true copy* is asked of a pair, so it is
asked separately of the same record type on each system that holds it. **The question of which of those
instances is the original is a question about the relationship between them, and the unit has no row for a
relationship.** `03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md` reports what the attribute produced; this chapter
records that the unit was not shaped to answer the version of the question that spans systems.

**Three — is anything lost at the boundary?** FDA's definition of metadata is *"the contextual information
required to understand data"*, and FDA treats the audit trail as metadata. **A transfer that carries the
values and not the metadata carries something that is no longer the record**, and the two pairs either side
of that transfer can both be determined Met without the loss appearing anywhere. Nothing in this phase
establishes that such a loss occurs anywhere at Helix. **Nothing in this phase would have found it if it
did.**

**Four — who owns the movement?** Every pair names two owners: the owner of the record type's
determination and the owner of the system. **The transfer between two systems with different owners is
owned by neither of them on the face of the register.** `ADR-0008` requires every control to have a named
individual owner, and this is a place where the method produces work with no owner rather than an owner
who has not been named — which is a worse condition and the reason this chapter exists.

**FDA's own definition of a system reaches further than the unit does, and the point is worth recording.**
The December 2018 guidance adopts the American National Standards Institute's definition — systems are
people, machines and methods organised to accomplish a set of specific functions — and adds that computer
or related systems can refer to hardware, software, peripheral devices, networks, cloud infrastructure,
**personnel** and associated documents including standard operating procedures. **An assessment whose
system half is a register entry has assessed a narrower object than FDA's own definition describes.**

## 7. What this chapter does not do

It does not propose the replacement, and the restraint is deliberate.

**A process-and-data-flow assessment is a different piece of work with a different input.** It needs the
flows established first: what is produced where, what moves, in what form, under whose hand, and what is
supposed to survive the move. **Nobody at Helix has established that**, and inventing it inside a chapter
that has just criticised the phase for building on what was available would be the same error committed
twice in one document.

**Nor does it withdraw the ratings.** The seventy-four scored pairs are determinations about records in
places, they were made on evidence, and they are not made wrong by the existence of a question the unit
did not ask. **They are made incomplete, and incomplete is what this chapter records.**

**And it draws no conclusion about any transfer at Helix.** No transfer has been examined. **Nothing here
says that any record has been altered, lost or degraded in moving between systems, and nothing here says
that any has not.** The position is unassessed, and unassessed is not adverse.

## 8. What is raised, and where it goes

**`IS-18`** is raised in this phase's RAID log: **the unit is IT-shaped and the transfer between systems is
assessed nowhere** — thirty-one of the thirty-four. It carries a named individual owner and a date in the
log, which is the authoritative record of its wording.

Three things about how it is carried.

**It is an issue and not a programme risk.** It is true now. It is not a thing that might become true, and
it is not a delivery concern. **An issue written as a worry is a risk in the wrong register.**

**It is not mitigated, because there is nothing to mitigate it with at this vantage.** It is carried open
with a reason, which is the treatment `01.12` fixed for an issue that cannot be closed on the evidence
available.

**And it is not scored.** The scale at `03.05 §7` applies to record-type/system pairs. **It is never
applied to a programme register entry**, and applying it to a criticism of the method itself would be a
category error twice over.

`03.13-phase-summary-and-transition.md` carries it out of the phase.

## 9. Why this chapter is in the phase at all

It would have been possible to publish the assessment without it. The population reconciles, the method is
internally consistent, and no reader would necessarily have noticed that the best available method source
recommends a different unit.

**A phase that finds the hole in its own instrument is worth more than one that does not look.** The
alternative is not a stronger phase; it is a phase whose weakest point is discovered by somebody else, at a
moment Helix does not choose, in a room where the answer *we knew and did not write it down* is worse than
the answer *we did not know*.

**And there is a specific value in publishing it now rather than in the phase that fixes it.** A limitation
disclosed by the phase that has just spent two months building on it is credible. The same limitation
disclosed later, by the phase that proposes to replace the method, reads as a business case. `ADR-0022`
already makes the assessment re-runnable; **whether the unit changes when it is re-run is a question this
phase leaves open and does not pre-empt.**

## 10. What this chapter does not establish

- **It states no result of any kind** — no determination, no count of determinations, no rating. `03.06` to
  `03.10`.
- **It establishes nothing about any transfer between any two systems at Helix.** None has been examined.
- **It does not re-open the applicability determination**, and the thirty-four and the sixty-one are
  unchanged.
- **It does not withdraw, qualify or re-score any pair.**
- **It states no validation status and no control position.** Phase 04 and Phase 05.
- **It cites PIC/S PI 041-1 as good practice and never as a source of obligation**, and it cites no draft
  as a requirement.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome.
