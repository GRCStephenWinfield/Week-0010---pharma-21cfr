## 1. What this chapter settles

`03.01` established that nothing required the assessment. `03.02` fixed the unit. `03.03` fixed the
vocabulary and `03.04` fixed the mapping. **This chapter is the instrument itself**: what is asked of each
of the one hundred and twenty-one pairs, what answers the question may receive, what happens to a pair
that cannot answer, how the pairs that can are scored, and what Helix has taken on by writing any of it
down.

Four decisions are recorded here or point here. **The five attributes are never averaged** — `ADR-0017`.
**Not determinable is a determination in its own right and a pair carrying one is not scored** —
`ADR-0018`. **The scale is Helix's own, is kept small, and is published** — `ADR-0020`. **Likelihood is of
the undetected alteration** — `ADR-0021`.

**It states no determination and no rating.** How the population divided is `03.06`'s, the unscorable
population is `03.08`'s, and the ratings are `03.10`'s. This chapter describes the instrument that produced
them and nothing that came out of it.

## 2. The instrument

Each pair is put to the same instrument, in the same order, and the order is not decorative.

| Step | What is done | Recorded on the pair |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The pair is identified** — one record type, one system, both by identifier | The two identifiers, and the two named owners |
| **2** | **The predicate-rule position is carried forward** from Phase 02's determination, unchanged | The provision that makes the record a required record |
| **3** | **The five attribute questions at `03.03 §4` are asked**, one at a time, of the record as held on that system | Five determinations, each **Met**, **Not met** or **Not determinable**, each with its basis |
| **4** | **The pair is routed.** A pair whose five determinations are all Met or Not met is scorable. **A pair carrying one or more Not determinable is not scored** | The register the pair goes to |
| **5** | **Consequence is assessed**, on the scored pairs, from what the record decides | One to five, **with its basis, on its own line** |
| **6** | **Likelihood is assessed**, on the scored pairs, of the **undetected** alteration | One to five, **with its basis, on its own line** |
| **7** | **The product is written out** and the band read off it | Likelihood, consequence, product, band |

**Steps five and six do not happen for a pair that fails step four.** That is the point of `ADR-0018` and
`§4` below argues it.

**Steps three and five are asked of different things and by different people.** The attribute questions are
questions of fact about a record on a system; the consequence question is a question about what the record
decides. Keeping them apart on the form is what allows the second to be reviewed by the quality unit
without re-opening the first.

## 3. Three determinations, and only three

| Determination | What it means | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Met** | **Helix examined the attribute for this record on this system and the attribute holds**, on evidence named on the form | Not an opinion that the attribute is likely to hold. **Not an inference from the attribute holding elsewhere for the same record type** |
| **Not met** | **Helix examined the attribute and it does not hold.** A fact about the present condition of the record | **Not a violation, not a deficiency and not a finding.** `03.12` owns that boundary. **And not a likelihood** — `§6` |
| **Not determinable** | **Helix cannot establish either way from anything available to it.** The evidence that would settle the attribute does not exist, or exists and cannot be got at, and the form records **what would be needed to determine it** | **Not "probably fine". Not "low risk". Not a soft Not met.** It is the absence of a determination, recorded as such |

**There is no fourth value, no partial, no percentage and no confidence figure.** An attribute either was
determined and holds, was determined and does not hold, or was not determinable. A scale with a middle
term invites the middle term, and the middle term is where the uncomfortable answers go to be filed.

**Every determination carries its basis.** A determination whose basis is not recorded cannot be reviewed,
cannot be challenged, and cannot be re-run when the facts change — and the facts will change, because
`ADR-0022` makes the assessment re-runnable on a trigger.

## 4. Not determinable is a determination, and it is not scored

**A pair carrying one or more Not determinable attributes is not scored. It receives no likelihood, no
consequence, no product and no band.** It goes to a separate register, it stays there until the thing that
made it undeterminable changes, and **it never converts into a scored entry by being reconsidered rather
than re-determined.**

This is the largest single act of the phase and it is a refusal, so the reasoning is set out at length.

**An absence of evidence scores nothing.** A score is a claim, and the claim it makes is that Helix has
looked and formed a view. Where Helix has not been able to look, there is nothing to form a view about. To
score such a pair is to convert *we do not know* into a number, and a number is exactly the form in which
*we do not know* stops being visible.

**Scoring an undeterminable pair low is the easy answer and it is the wrong one, in a specific way.**
Nothing is known about the attribute; therefore nothing adverse is known; therefore the pair looks
unremarkable in the register; therefore nobody goes back to it. **The pairs about which Helix knows least
would be the pairs the register drew the least attention to.** That is not a conservative error. It is an
inversion.

**Scoring it high is also wrong, and it is worth saying why, because it is the answer a cautious reviewer
proposes.** A high rating asserts a likelihood and a consequence, and the likelihood would have no basis.
It would also make the undeterminable population indistinguishable, in the register, from the population
Helix examined and found genuinely exposed — and those two groups need completely different work. **The
first needs evidence. The second needs a control.** A register that cannot tell them apart cannot direct
either.

**So the two populations are kept in two registers that never merge.** The scored pairs go to the `DR`
series; the unscorable pairs go to the `ND` series, one entry each, and every `ND` entry carries **what
would have to exist for the attribute to be determinable**. Neither register converts into the other. A
pair moves out of `ND` only by being determined, on the trigger at `ADR-0022`, and when it does it enters
`DR` as a new determination with its own date — **not as a conversion, and not by inheriting an
identifier.**

**And the refusal is what makes the scores worth reading.** A register in which every pair carries a
number is a register in which the reader cannot tell which numbers were earned. **Seventy-four scored
pairs and forty-seven refusals say more than one hundred and twenty-one numbers would**, because the
refusals are the evidence that the numbers were not manufactured. `03.08-what-cannot-be-determined.md`
reports the unscorable population and what it is handed to.

## 5. The five are never averaged

**A pair's position is its profile — five determinations, side by side, in FDA's order. It is not a mean,
a weighted mean, an index or a composite. The five are never traded off against one another.**

**A mean of five attributes is a number that describes nothing.** The attributes are not commensurable:
*legible* and *original or a true copy* are not two measurements of one underlying quantity, and averaging
them produces a figure that is neither. Worse, an average lets four comfortable determinations conceal one
uncomfortable one, which is precisely the arrangement an assessment exists to prevent.

**The pair's worst attribute governs the reading of the profile**, and where a pair carries a *Not met* the
register names which attributes those are in its own column. **Where all five hold, the pair is scored on
the same two axes and nothing is inferred from the clean profile** — likelihood is of the undetected
alteration and does not follow from an attribute holding. All five determinations remain on the form and
remain readable — they are not discarded, they are simply not averaged into anything.

That rule is `ADR-0017`, and it has one further effect worth stating. **Because the five are separate, the
register can be read down a column as well as across a row.** An attribute that is weak across many pairs
becomes visible as a pattern rather than being diluted into one hundred and twenty-one composite scores.
`03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md` is a chapter that only exists because the columns were kept.

## 6. Likelihood is of the undetected alteration

**Likelihood, in this method, is the likelihood that the record is altered, lost or misstated and that the
alteration is not detected before the record is relied on. It is not the likelihood of the alteration.**

That distinction is `ADR-0021` and it does three things.

**It makes likelihood assessable.** The probability that somebody makes an error, or that a system writes
a wrong value, is not something Helix can estimate honestly for one hundred and twenty-one populations of
records. The probability that such an event would go unnoticed is a question about arrangements — about
whether anything about the way the record is produced, held and read would surface the event — and that is
a question about facts Helix has.

**It keeps facts out of the likelihood column.** **An attribute recorded Not met is a fact, not a
likelihood.** If a record is not attributable, its non-attributability has a probability of one and
recording it as *likely* is a category error. What varies between two pairs both carrying a Not met is how
exposed each is to an undetected event given that condition, and that is what the likelihood expresses.

**It puts detection on the same page as prevention.** A method that scores only the chance of the event
rewards preventive arrangements and is blind to whether anything would ever notice. FDA's own framing in
the December 2018 guidance points the same way: risks to data include the potential to be deleted, amended
or excluded **without detection**.

**And it produces the movement rule.** **A rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence changed.**
Consequence is a property of what the record decides, which is a property of the record type — it does not
change because a system was reconfigured. Likelihood is a property of the arrangements around the record
on that system, and those change. **So when a rating moves and the consequence has not, the basis names
what changed about detection**, and where a rating moves because the consequence moved, the record type's
own position has changed and that is a matter for the determination's owner, not for the assessment.

## 7. The scale

**Likelihood one to five. Consequence one to five. The product read against three bands.** Both numbers,
and the basis for each, are recorded separately on every scored pair.

| Band | Product | What the band means |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | **15 and above** | **The band is a product of two axes and nothing more.** It is not a finding, not a deficiency, and **not an ordering: nothing in Helix's quality system yet says what any band obliges anybody to do** — `IS-21` |
| **Moderate** | **8 to 12** | Carried, with both bases, into Phase 04's input |
| **Low** | **6 and below** | Carried. **Low is a rating and not a clearance** |

**Likelihood one is reserved and unused.** No pair in this phase carries it. It is kept on the scale rather
than deleted from it, so that the scale has a floor that could be used if a future assessment found an
arrangement that genuinely warranted it — and so that its absence in this assessment is visible as an
absence rather than as a scale that never had one. **A method that quietly removes the value nobody
selected has removed the evidence that nobody selected it.**

**Eleven products are impossible on a five-by-five scale, and naming them is what makes the bands
defensible.** The only products two integers from one to five can produce are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10,
12, 15, 16, 20 and 25. **The impossible products are 7, 11, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 23 and 24, and no
pair carries one.**

**The band boundaries were then set in the gaps.** Low ends at 6 and Moderate begins at 8 because 7 cannot
occur; Moderate ends at 12 and High begins at 15 because 13 and 14 cannot occur. **No achievable product
falls between two bands, and no achievable product falls in two.** The bands are exhaustive and mutually
exclusive over the values the scale can actually produce, and that is a property of the scale rather than
a convention about rounding.

**What the scale is not.** It is not a maturity model. It is not a criticality tier. It is not comparable
with anything in the programme registers, and **it is never applied to `PR-01` to `PR-26` or to any other
programme entry** — `01.12 §4` fixed that rule and `01.12 §7`'s last row keeps it: nothing migrates from
the four programme registers into this one, in either direction. A scheduling concern and a threat to the
reliability of a batch record do not belong on one scale.

## 8. Why the scale is small

Helix could have built a larger instrument. Detectability as a third axis, weighting across the five
attributes, a ten-point consequence scale, a residual-risk calculation after assumed controls — all of
these exist in the wild and all of them look more rigorous on a slide.

**Three reasons the scale is two axes of five and no more.**

**Precision the inputs cannot support is not precision.** The consequence of a record being wrong is
assessed from what the record decides. That judgement is honest at five levels. It is not honest at ten,
and a ten-point scale would produce differences between adjacent values that no basis statement could
defend.

**A third axis would double-count.** Detectability is already inside the likelihood axis, by construction,
because likelihood here is of the **undetected** alteration — `§6`. Adding it again as a separate
multiplier would let the same fact reduce a rating twice.

**And the fewer moving parts a written procedure has, the fewer ways it can be departed from.** Which is
`§9`.

## 9. `21 CFR 211.22(d)` — the scale Helix writes is the scale Helix is held to

**No regulator prescribes a scale.** The words *maturity*, *score*, *scoring*, *rating* and *severity*
appear nowhere in FDA's December 2018 guidance. The words *risk* and *risk assessment* appear nowhere in
21 CFR Parts 210, 211 or 11. **Any scale — one to five, low/medium/high, colour bands, anything — is
entirely a company method choice, and no regulator will tell Helix its scale is wrong.**

**What a regulator will do is hold Helix to it.**

**`21 CFR 211.22(d)`, verbatim:**

> *"The responsibilities and procedures applicable to the quality control unit shall be in writing; such
> written procedures shall be followed."*

**Two clauses. In writing, and followed.** The provision does not require a risk assessment and does not
require a scale. It requires that whatever the quality control unit's procedures are, they be written down
and then actually applied.

> **The moment Helix writes this scale down, its own procedure becomes enforceable against it. The
> compliance question stops being whether the method is right and becomes whether Helix followed the
> method it wrote.**

**That exposure did not exist before this chapter and it exists now.** A firm that never wrote a scoring
procedure cannot depart from one. Helix has written one, published it, and put it inside the quality
unit's procedure set, and the exposure is the price of the assessment being auditable at all. It is
carried as an issue in this phase's RAID log rather than being noted and forgotten — **`IS-19`**.

**This is the argument for a small, defensible scale rather than an elaborate one, and it is the argument
`ADR-0020` records.** Every additional rule in a written method is an additional way to depart from it.
Weighting factors have to be applied consistently across one hundred and twenty-one pairs and then across
every re-run; a detectability axis has to be scored the same way by three assessors two years apart; a
residual-risk step has to assume controls whose position no phase has yet assessed. **An elaborate scale
is not more rigorous. It is more surface area.**

**And it is the argument for publishing it.** A scale kept in a spreadsheet is a scale nobody can check
against the register. Publishing it means a reader can take any scored pair, read its two bases, apply the
bands and see whether the answer follows. **A method that can be checked is a method that can be found
wrong, and that is the point.**

## 11. What this chapter does not do

- **It states no determination for any pair, no count and no rating.** `03.06` to `03.10`.
- **It does not name a single pair.** The register does.
- **It does not set the frequency of anything.** The re-assessment trigger is `ADR-0022`, and the review
  frequency of any record or audit trail is not this phase's to state.
- **It states no validation status and no control position.** Phase 04 and Phase 05.
- **It sets no retention period.**
- **A rating is not a finding, not a deficiency and not a violation.** `03.12` owns that statement in full.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion** and forecasts no inspection outcome.
