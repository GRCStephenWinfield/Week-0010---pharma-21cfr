## 1. What this chapter settles

Every assessment needs a vocabulary, and the vocabulary is where most of this industry's data integrity
work quietly goes wrong — not in the analysis, but in the words the analysis is written in. Attributes get
abbreviated, acronyms get extended, and both get attributed to bodies that never said them.

This chapter fixes the vocabulary the assessment uses. It quotes FDA's sentence verbatim, insists on the
two words that are routinely dropped from it, states what each of the five attributes asks of a
record-type/system pair, and then deals with the four additional attributes — **whose they are, what the
body that set them out actually says about them, and on what basis Helix uses any of them.**

**It does not map the attributes to anything.** `03.04-footnote-five.md` owns the mapping, and the mapping
is FDA's own. It states no determination and no result; the determinations are `03.06`'s and the analysis
of the attribute that fails most is `03.07`'s.

## 2. FDA's sentence, verbatim

**This section owns FDA's statement of the attributes for the repository.**

From FDA's *Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP: Questions and Answers*, December 2018, in answer
to the question *"What is 'data integrity'?"*, verbatim:

> *"For the purposes of this guidance, data integrity refers to the completeness, consistency, and
> accuracy of data. Complete, consistent, and accurate data should be attributable, legible,
> contemporaneously recorded, original or a true copy, and accurate (ALCOA)."*

Four things about that sentence, before anything is done with it.

**It is guidance and it says *should*.** The document carries **Contains Nonbinding Recommendations** on
every page, and FDA's own definition of the word is that *should* in agency guidance means something is
suggested or recommended but not required. **The attributes are not a requirement. The provisions they are
addressed through are** — `03.04`.

**It is one sentence in one guidance.** The acronym appears nowhere in 21 CFR Part 210, nowhere in Part
211 and nowhere in Part 11. The underlying obligations are in the regulation; the mnemonic is not. That is
a reason to use it as an assessment framework and not a reason to describe it as a rule.

**It is scoped.** *"For the purposes of this guidance"* is FDA's qualifier on the definition of data
integrity, and Helix keeps it. The definition is a guidance definition. **Part 11 does not define data
integrity, and it does not define audit trail, metadata, static record or dynamic record either** —
`01.03 §3` owns that argument.

**The sentence subordinates the acronym to the definition, and the usual industry framing has it the other
way round.** FDA says data integrity **is** completeness, consistency and accuracy; the five attributes are
how data becomes complete, consistent and accurate. The five are the means. The three are the thing. A
programme that treats the five as the definition has inverted the sentence it is quoting, and it will
eventually find itself arguing that a record is *contemporaneously recorded* as though that were the end
of the enquiry.

## 3. The two words the industry drops

**FDA's third attribute is *contemporaneously recorded*, not *contemporaneous*.** It is an adverb and a
participle, and it describes an act rather than a property. FDA glosses it in its own footnote as *(at the
time of performance)*. The bare adjective belongs to other bodies' formulations and is not FDA's word.

**FDA's fourth attribute is *original or a true copy*, not *original*.** This is not decoration. The
qualifier is the hook to `21 CFR 211.180(d)`, which permits records to be retained **either as original
records or as true copies**. Rendering the attribute as bare *original* drops the half of the phrase that
makes it satisfiable in an estate where records are copied, exported and archived as a matter of routine
— and it converts a question Helix can answer into one no manufacturer could.

**This repository writes both attributes out in full, every time, in every chapter, register and
template.** The cost is that sentences are longer. The benefit is that the assessment cannot be read as
having asked a narrower question than it asked.

## 4. What each attribute asks of a pair

The instrument asks one question per attribute per pair, and it asks the same question in the same words
of every pair, so that a determination on one can be compared with a determination on another. **The
question is asked of the record as held on that system**, which is what the unit at `03.02 §2` is for.

| # | FDA's attribute, in FDA's words | The question the instrument asks of the pair | What a determination therefore turns on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Attributable** | **Can each entry, change and approval in this record, on this system, be traced to one identified individual?** | Whether an individual can be identified through the mechanism that made the entry. Attribution is defeated by a shared credential even where access is properly controlled — `02.07 §6` |
| **2** | **Legible** | **Can this record, on this system, be read and understood — including the metadata needed to understand it — for as long as it has to exist?** | Readability of the content, and of the contextual information required to understand it. FDA's own definition of metadata is *"the contextual information required to understand data"* |
| **3** | **Contemporaneously recorded** | **Was this record, on this system, documented at the time of performance?** | When the record was captured relative to when the activity happened, and whether the system's own timing can show it |
| **4** | **Original or a true copy** | **Can Helix say which instance of this record, on this system, is the original — and, where it is a copy, that the copy is true?** | Whether an original is identifiable at all, and whether a copy preserves the content and meaning of the original, including the static or dynamic nature of what was copied |
| **5** | **Accurate** | **Is this record, on this system, a truthful representation of what happened?** | Whether the values in the record correspond to the activity, and whether anything about the way the record is produced can put that correspondence in doubt |

Two notes on the fourth row, because it is the attribute this phase spends the most time on.

**The question has two limbs and the first is often skipped.** Before asking whether a copy is true, the
instrument asks whether Helix can say **which instance is the original**. In an estate where a record type
is held on several systems, that is not a trivial question, and it is the point at which the pair as a
unit earns its keep: the same record type may have an identifiable original on one system and no
identifiable original on another.

**FDA has said what a true copy has to preserve**, and the phrase is used here as FDA uses it: a copy is
capable of being a true copy where it preserves the content and meaning of the original record, including
the metadata required to reconstruct the CGMP activity and **the static or dynamic nature of the original
record**. That last clause is where a printed rendering of an interactive record fails, and Phase 02
already applied the underlying distinction to seven record types at `02.06`.

## 5. Nothing in this chapter is a control

The five attributes describe **properties a record should have**. They do not describe controls, they do
not name mechanisms, and they do not correspond to anything in `21 CFR 11.10`.

**ALCOA is not a control set.** That is `ADR-0019`, and `03.04 §6` argues it. It is stated here only so
that the table at `§4` is not read as a control specification. A row in that table asks whether a property
holds. It does not say what would make it hold, who would operate the thing that made it hold, or whether
anything of the kind exists at Helix. **All three of those are Phase 04's and Phase 05's, and this phase
answers none of them.**

## 6. The four additional attributes, and whose they are

Four further attributes — **complete, consistent, enduring and available** — circulate widely, usually
under a name formed by adding a plus sign to the five-letter acronym.

**They are not FDA's.** They do not appear in FDA's December 2018 guidance. The extended acronym does not
appear in it either, and neither does the word *enduring*. Any document that presents the four as FDA's is
wrong on a point that can be checked in a minute.

**They are set out, with definitions, by MHRA**, in *'GXP' Data Integrity Guidance and Definitions,
Revision 1: March 2018*, and by **PIC/S PI 041-1** and **WHO TRS 1033 Annex 4**, both of 2021. This
repository states which body it is quoting, every time.

### What MHRA actually says, and the sentence that is routinely misread

MHRA says, at **§3.10**, verbatim:

> *"The guidance refers to the acronym ALCOA rather than 'ALCOA +'."*

and, in the same passage, verbatim:

> *"There is no difference in expectations regardless of which acronym is used since data governance
> measures should ensure that data is complete, consistent, enduring and available throughout the data
> lifecycle."*

**MHRA prefers the shorter acronym and treats the two as equivalent in expectation. It does not reject,
deprecate or disown the four attributes, and this repository never says that it does.** It defines all
four in its own definitions section, at **§6.1** — complete: *"the data must be whole; a complete set"*;
consistent: *"the data must be self-consistent"*; enduring: *"durable; lasting throughout the data lifecycle"*;
available: *"readily available for review or inspection purposes"*.

A reader who takes MHRA's first sentence without its second concludes that a regulator has ruled against
the four. **The second sentence says the opposite of that conclusion**, and it is in the same paragraph.

### And no originator is named

**This repository names no originator for either acronym, and the reasons differ.** For the five, the only
account of the acronym's origin is a **self-attribution published in a chapter newsletter by a former FDA
investigator** — one individual's account of his own work, not an agency statement and not a document FDA
has adopted. **The acronym's authority in this assessment comes from FDA quoting it in the December 2018
guidance and mapping it to the predicate rules, and from nothing else**, so who first assembled the letters
is immaterial to the instrument. For the four, **no source claiming authorship was found at all.** **An
assessment framework does not become more authoritative by being given a founder, and this repository does
not give it one.**

## 7. What Helix uses, and on what basis

| Set | Status in this phase | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **The five, in FDA's words** | **The assessment instrument. Five determinations per pair, six hundred and five in total** | FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP guidance — **the guidance a US drug manufacturer works to**, addressed to 21 CFR Parts 210, 211 and 212. **Nonbinding**, and the underlying obligations are the predicate rules at `03.04` |
| **The four additional attributes** | **Not scored, not determined, and not part of the instrument** | They are other bodies' formulations. MHRA's scope is UK industry regulated by MHRA and **it does not bind Helix**; PIC/S PI 041-1 states of itself that it *"is not mandatory or enforceable under law"*; WHO TRS 1033 Annex 4 is a WHO guideline addressed principally to prequalification and to authorities that adopt it |
| **MHRA's risk formulations** | **Adopted voluntarily, and labelled as adopted at every citation** | **§3.6**: *"The effort and resource applied to assure the integrity of the data should be commensurate with the risk and impact of a data integrity failure to the patient or environment"* · **§4.1**: *"Data criticality may be determined by considering how the data is used to influence the decisions made."* Helix finds these the best available statement of the method and adopts them **as a method, not as an obligation** |

**The labelling discipline is the one `ADR-0005` set for FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance and it
is applied here unchanged: where Helix adopts somebody else's formulation, the adoption is stated in the
same sentence or the one after it, in every phase, with no exceptions.** The repetition is the control.

**Why the four are not in the instrument, stated plainly.** Not because they are wrong — MHRA's own
position is that the expectations do not differ — but because **the instrument runs on the set FDA maps to
the predicate rules**, and footnote 5 maps five. Adding four attributes with no equivalent mapping would
have produced a register in which some determinations traced to a regulation Helix is inspected against
and others traced to guidance from a body that does not inspect it. **A register whose rows have different
kinds of authority is a register that will be read as though they had the same kind.**

## 8. What this chapter does not do

- **It does not map the attributes to any regulation.** `03.04`, and the mapping is FDA's.
- **It does not map any attribute onto `21 CFR 11.10(a)` to `(k)`**, and no document in this repository
  does. Where an attribute and a Part 11 paragraph are both relevant to the same record, **they are two
  assessments of the same record** — `03.04 §7`.
- **It states no determination, no count and no rating.** `03.06` to `03.10`.
- **It names no control, no configuration and no review frequency.** Phase 05.
- **It sets no retention period.** `IS-14` remains open and `21 CFR 211.180` governs the question.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion** and forecasts no inspection outcome.
