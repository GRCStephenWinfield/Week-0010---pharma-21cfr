## 1. What this chapter settles

There is exactly one published FDA mapping of the five data integrity attributes to regulation. It is a
footnote. It is attached to the sentence quoted at `03.03 §2`, it is four lines long, and almost nobody
reads it.

**It maps every one of the five attributes to the CGMP predicate rules for drugs, and it cites 21 CFR
Part 11 nowhere.**

That single fact reorganises the phase. It settles what the assessment is anchored on. It disposes of the
crosswalk that maps the attributes one-to-one onto the eleven paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10`, which is
everywhere in this industry's training material and is an invention. And it produces the consequence that
keeps Phase 05 on the plan: **an ALCOA assessment and a Part 11 control assessment are two different
assessments of the same records, and doing one does not do the other.**

This chapter quotes the footnote in full, works through what it maps, deals honestly with the three
citations in it that do not reach Helix, states what it does not cite, and draws the consequence. The
decision is `ADR-0019` / `DEC-305`.

## 2. Footnote 5, in full and exactly

**This section owns footnote 5 for the repository.** It is quoted whole, including the parts that do not
apply to Helix, because a quotation trimmed to the convenient sections is not a quotation.

From FDA's *Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP: Questions and Answers*, December 2018, footnote
5, attached to the answer defining data integrity — verbatim:

> *"These characteristics are important to ensuring data integrity and are addressed throughout the CGMP
> regulations for drugs. For attributable, see §§ 211.101(d), 211.122, 211.186, 211.188(b)(11), and
> 212.50(c)(10); for legible, see §§ 211.180(e) and 212.110(b); for contemporaneously recorded (at the
> time of performance), see §§ 211.100(b) and 211.160(a); for original or a true copy, see §§ 211.180 and
> 211.194(a); and for accurate, see §§ 211.22(a), 211.68, 211.188, and 212.60(g)."*

**Fifteen citations. Twelve to 21 CFR Part 211, three to 21 CFR Part 212, and none to 21 CFR Part 11.**

## 3. What it maps, attribute by attribute

| FDA's attribute | The sections footnote 5 names | Of those, the ones that reach Helix |
|---|---|---|
| **Attributable** | `21 CFR 211.101(d)` · `21 CFR 211.122` · `21 CFR 211.186` · `21 CFR 211.188(b)(11)` · `21 CFR 212.50(c)(10)` | The four Part 211 sections. **`21 CFR 212.50(c)(10)` does not** — `§4` |
| **Legible** | `21 CFR 211.180(e)` · `21 CFR 212.110(b)` | `21 CFR 211.180(e)`. **`21 CFR 212.110(b)` does not** |
| **Contemporaneously recorded (at the time of performance)** | `21 CFR 211.100(b)` · `21 CFR 211.160(a)` | **Both** |
| **Original or a true copy** | `21 CFR 211.180` · `21 CFR 211.194(a)` | **Both** |
| **Accurate** | `21 CFR 211.22(a)` · `21 CFR 211.68` · `21 CFR 211.188` · `21 CFR 212.60(g)` | The three Part 211 sections. **`21 CFR 212.60(g)` does not** |

Three observations, each of which does work later in the phase.

**FDA's own parenthesis defines the third attribute.** *"(at the time of performance)"* is FDA's gloss and
not a paraphrase, and it is the phrase `21 CFR 211.100(b)` and `21 CFR 211.160(a)` both use. The attribute
therefore has a textual test rather than an atmospheric one: **was the activity documented at the time it
was performed.**

**The fourth attribute is anchored on the retention provision and the laboratory record provision
together.** `21 CFR 211.180` is where the permission to retain either an original record or a true copy
comes from, and `21 CFR 211.194(a)` is what a laboratory record must contain — including, at `(a)(4)`,
*"a complete record of all data secured in the course of each test, including all graphs, charts, and
spectra from laboratory instrumentation, properly identified to show the specific component, drug product
container, closure, in-process material, or drug product, and lot tested"*. Phase 02 has already worked
with that provision, at `02.06`,
against seven record types. **The attribute that this phase spends the most time on is anchored on the
same text the previous phase found the most trouble in**, and `03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md` is
where that is reported.

**The fifth attribute is anchored partly on the quality unit's own authority.** `21 CFR 211.22(a)` is the
quality control unit's responsibility and authority, including the authority to review production records
to assure that no errors have occurred. **The accuracy attribute therefore runs partly through the
function performing the assessment**, which is one reason the method's governance sits where it does and
why `ADR-0002`'s separation of the data integrity function from the function that operates the systems
matters here rather than only in Phase 01.

## 4. The three citations that do not reach Helix, kept in the quotation

Three of footnote 5's fifteen citations are to **21 CFR Part 212**: `212.50(c)(10)`, `212.110(b)` and
`212.60(g)`.

**Part 212 is the current good manufacturing practice regulation for positron emission tomography drugs.
Helix Pharma manufactures oral solid dose pharmaceuticals and no PET drug. Part 212 imposes no obligation
on it, and those three citations do not reach it.**

**They are kept in the quotation anyway, and are then excluded by name.** The alternative — quoting the
footnote with the Part 212 citations silently removed — would produce a shorter and tidier passage that
is not what FDA wrote. A reader who then went to the source would find text the repository had edited
without saying so, and would be entitled to ask what else had been edited. **A quotation that has been
made convenient is evidence about the author, not about the source.**

This is the same discipline `ADR-0005` applies to FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance, which is
device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by the Quality Management System Regulation, and does
not apply to Helix: the document is described accurately and its inapplicability is stated, rather than
the document being left out of the account.

**The arithmetic that survives the exclusion:** twelve of footnote 5's fifteen citations reach Helix, they
are all in Part 211, and **every one of the five attributes still has at least one Part 211 anchor.** The
mapping does not collapse when Part 212 is removed; it thins in three places and holds in all five.

## 5. What it cites nowhere

**Footnote 5 does not cite 21 CFR Part 11. Not `21 CFR 11.10`, not any of its eleven paragraphs, not
`21 CFR 11.30`, not `21 CFR 11.50`, not `21 CFR 11.70`, and not any provision of Subpart C.**

That is not an oversight to be repaired by a helpful reader. It is consistent with everything else about
the two documents:

- **The words *ALCOA*, *attributable* and *data integrity* do not appear anywhere in 21 CFR Part 11.**
- **They do not appear in FDA's August 2003 Scope and Application guidance either.** FDA's Part 11 policy
  document predates the data integrity vocabulary entirely.
- **Part 11 does not define *data integrity*.** The definition Helix uses is a guidance definition, given
  *"for the purposes of this guidance"* in the December 2018 document — `01.03 §3`.

**The bridge between Part 11 and the five attributes is a 2018 CGMP guidance whose only mapping points at
Part 211.**

## 6. Why the crosswalk is an invention

The artefact this chapter is displacing is familiar: a table with five rows and eleven columns, or eleven
rows and five columns, in which *attributable* is said to be `21 CFR 11.10(d)` and `(g)`, *legible* is said
to be `21 CFR 11.10(b)`, *contemporaneous* is said to be `21 CFR 11.10(e)`, *original* is said to be
`21 CFR 11.10(c)`, and *accurate* is said to be `21 CFR 11.10(a)`. It appears in training decks, in audit
questionnaires and in a great many quality manuals.

**No FDA document contains it.** There is no published FDA mapping of the attributes onto
`21 CFR 11.10(a)` to `(k)`, and the only mapping FDA has published points at the predicate rule instead.
**FDA's own footnote refutes it.**

Three things are wrong with the crosswalk, in ascending order of seriousness.

**It has no source.** A mapping presented as regulatory that cannot be traced to a regulator is a
consultant's opinion with a citation column, and the citation column is what makes it dangerous rather
than merely unhelpful.

**It points at the wrong regulation.** The attributes describe properties of records that the CGMPs
require. `21 CFR Part 11` sets conditions under which FDA will treat an electronic version of a required
record as trustworthy and reliable. Those are different objects: the predicate rule says what the record
must be, and Part 11 says what the electronic form of it must be surrounded by. **A record can satisfy
every paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` and still fail `21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)`, because a complete audit trail on
an incomplete record is a complete audit trail on an incomplete record.**

**And it makes the second assessment look as though it has already been done.** This is the serious one.
If the attributes map onto `21 CFR 11.10`, then assessing the attributes has assessed the section, and a
Part 11 control assessment becomes a formality somebody will propose deferring. Helix has a live example
of what that costs: **Phase 02 found that measures deployed under `21 CFR 11.30` had been recorded as
though they discharged `21 CFR 11.10`, and `21 CFR 11.10` had accordingly never been assessed for three
systems at all** — `02.07`, `IS-09`. **A crosswalk is the same error in advance.**

## 7. The consequence: two assessments of the same records

**This is the load-bearing consequence of the whole chapter.**

> **An ALCOA assessment and a Part 11 control assessment are two different assessments of the same
> records. This phase does the first. Phase 05 does the second. Neither is a substitute for the other, and
> completing one does not reduce the other.**

| | **This phase — the ALCOA assessment** | **Phase 05 — the Part 11 control assessment** |
|---|---|---|
| **The question** | Are the records **attributable, legible, contemporaneously recorded, original or a true copy, and accurate**, on the systems that hold them? | Which of the controls at `21 CFR 11.10(a)` to `(k)`, and at `21 CFR 11.30` where it applies, are employed for the records those systems hold? |
| **What it is anchored on** | **The predicate rules named in footnote 5** — `21 CFR 211.22(a)`, `211.68`, `211.100(b)`, `211.101(d)`, `211.122`, `211.160(a)`, `211.180`, `211.180(e)`, `211.186`, `211.188`, `211.188(b)(11)`, `211.194(a)` | **21 CFR Part 11 itself**, read through the applicability determination Phase 02 published |
| **The unit** | The record-type/system pair — `03.02`, `ADR-0016` | Phase 05's to fix, against the same determination |
| **What it produces** | A profile per pair and, where the pair is fully determinable, a rating | A control position — **which this phase states for nothing and nobody** |
| **Milestone** | **`MS-07`, 2026-09-30** | **`MS-09`, 2027-01-29** |

**Where an attribute and a paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` are both relevant to the same record, this
repository says they are two assessments of the same record. It never says one maps onto the other.** That
rule binds every phase and every register, and it is `ADR-0019`.

**Saying this out loud is what stops Phase 05 being skipped.** A programme that has just published an
attribute assessment across one hundred and twenty-one pairs will be asked, in good faith, whether Part 11
is now covered. The answer is in this chapter and it is no, and the reason is that FDA's own footnote maps
the attributes somewhere else entirely.

## 8. What Helix does with the mapping

Three things, and no more.

**The mapping is the assessment's citation set.** Every attribute determination in the register names the
provision the attribute is addressed through, and the provisions come from footnote 5 rather than from
this programme's judgement. **The mapping is FDA's, and the repository says whose it is at every use.**

**The mapping is checked against the record type's own anchor.** Phase 02 recorded, for each of the
thirty-four in-scope record types, the predicate-rule provision that makes it a required record. Where
footnote 5's provision for an attribute and the record type's own anchor are the same provision, the
determination has two reasons to be about that text; where they differ, the determination records both.
**Neither replaces the other**, and Phase 02's own correction — that several record types' obligations run
through `21 CFR 211.100(b)` and `21 CFR 211.188(b)(5)` rather than through the condition rules they
evidence — is carried across unchanged.

**One gap in the correspondence is recorded rather than smoothed.** Of the **ten distinct Part 211
sections** footnote 5 names — twelve citations, of which two are paragraphs of sections cited elsewhere in
the same footnote — **nine appear in Phase 02's record-type register as, or within, the anchor for at
least one record type. `21 CFR 211.122` appears nowhere in it.**
That is a statement about Helix's register and not about the section. It is recorded here, handed to the
determination's owner, and left for the review the determination is subject to under `ADR-0015`. **No
conclusion is drawn from it in this phase, and no record type is added, removed or re-determined here.**

## 9. What this chapter does not do

- **It does not state any determination, count or rating.** `03.06` to `03.10`.
- **It does not state a control position for any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10`, for any system.** Phase 05,
  `MS-09`.
- **It does not map ALCOA onto `21 CFR 11.10`**, and it forbids any later phase doing so — `ADR-0019`.
- **It does not treat the attributes as a control set.** They describe properties of records — `03.03 §5`.
- **It states no validation status.** Phase 04.
- **It sets no retention period**, although `21 CFR 211.180` is named in the mapping. `IS-14` is open.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation, and forecasts no inspection
  outcome.
