## 1. What this chapter is about

Phase 02 tested eleven record types Helix had treated as outside Part 11 on the ground that they were
printed. **Seven failed the first condition of the paper-printout route — the record is dynamic and a static
printout cannot reproduce it — and became Part 11 records.** They are `RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15`, `RT-16`,
`RT-26`, `RT-27` and `RT-33`. **`IS-08`** carries the finding, `ADR-0010` carries the method, and
`02.06-the-paper-that-was-not-the-record.md` owns the argument. **None of that is re-argued here.**

**This chapter reports what happened when those seven were assessed.**

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Record types | **7** |
| Pairs they produce | **24** |
| Systems those pairs sit on | **18** |
| **Pairs Not determinable on at least one attribute** | **24 — all of them** |
| **Therefore inside the 47 that could not be scored** | **24 of the 47** |

**Every one of the 24 is Not determinable on at least one attribute, and not one of them carries a rating.**
`IS-17` carries that out of the phase.

**The provisions these twenty-four pairs run through are named here so that the chapter's central result
attaches to text.** *Original or a true copy* is anchored by footnote 5 on `21 CFR 211.180` and
`21 CFR 211.194(a)`; the operative sub-paragraph for retention is **`21 CFR 211.180(d)`**, which permits
records to be retained *"either as original records or as true copies such as photocopies, microfilm,
microfiche, or other accurate reproductions of the original records"*, and the operative sub-paragraph for
the laboratory record types among the seven is **`21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)`**, which requires *"a complete
record of all data secured in the course of each test, including all graphs, charts, and spectra from
laboratory instrumentation, properly identified to show the specific component, drug product container,
closure, in-process material, or drug product, and lot tested."* **Neither provision is asserted to be met
or not met for any of the twenty-four.** They are named because they are the text a determination of
*original or a true copy* would eventually have to be made against, and **the reason that determination
could not be made is that Helix has never stated which instance the provision is to be applied to.**

chromatographic integration and reprocessing record on the two chromatography data systems. **There is
nothing about either pair that Helix can currently determine.**

## 4. Why there is no assessment history to read

**The reason is structural and it is the point of the chapter.**

Helix treated these seven record types as outside Part 11 for the whole of the period in which they were
Part 11 records. **A record type nobody has determined to be in scope is a record type nobody retains
anything about**, because the retaining is done by the procedures that attach once the determination exists,
and the determination did not exist — `IS-02`, closed in Phase 02, and the search at `EV-226` that
established it.

So when the assessment arrived and asked *what does the system retain of when this entry was captured*, or
*which instance of this record does Helix rely on*, or *what would show this copy to be true*, the answer was
not that the position was poor. **The answer was that nobody had ever asked, so nothing had ever been
written down, so there was nothing for an assessment to read.**

**This is why the determinations cluster on three attributes and not on five.** *Legible* is determinable for
22 of the 24, because rendering a record is something a system does whether or not anybody determined its
scope. *Attributable* is determinable for 16, because account administration is a function of the system
rather than of the record's status. **The attributes that could not be determined are precisely the ones that
require somebody to have decided something and written it down** — which instance is the original, when
capture happens relative to the act, and what the record is to be compared against.

## 5. `AS-17` was tested here, and it failed

**Phase 02 opened `AS-17` in exactly this anticipation:** that the vendors of the chromatography data
system, the dissolution workstations and the environmental and warehouse monitoring systems would supply
technical documentation sufficient to describe how these seven record types are created, retained and
exported. Phase 02 recorded what would follow if it failed — *the seven have to be characterised by testing
rather than from documentation, which Phase 05 does not size for.*

**It was tested on 2026-09-17 and it failed.** `DEC-315`, evidence `EV-327`, and the assumption is closed
as failed rather than reworded.

**What arrived was not nothing.** Vendor documentation exists and was read. **It describes what the products
do.** It does not describe what these seven record types do at Helix — which instance the configuration in
use retains, what the retained object is relative to the acquisition, what is reconstructed on demand and
what is stored. **A product description answers a procurement question. The assessment was asking an
evidential one**, and the two are not the same question however well the first is answered.

**`PR-07` is the standing programme risk that a vendor is treated as supplying compliance rather than
features that support it**, and this is its concrete form: the documentation is genuine, complete for its
purpose, and does not reach the question. `DP-10` is escalated in consequence and `DP-16` is opened
alongside it.

**Recording the failure rather than widening the assumption is the house rule.** *A tested assumption becomes
either a fact or an issue and does not stay an assumption* — `01.12 §7`. `AS-17` became `IS-17`. Phase 02's
`AS-03` set the precedent with its split verdict, and the precedent is that an assumption that fails is
closed as failed.

## 6. Unassessed is not low risk

**This is the sentence the chapter exists to make available**, because the alternative reading is the one
that will be reached for.

**The 24 carry no rating.** They appear in no ranked list, no heat map — there is none — and no extract of
the highest-rated pairs. A reader scanning `logs/risk-register.md §2` will not find them. **A reader who
concludes from that absence that they are the safe part of the estate has read the register backwards.**

> **They are not low risk. They are unassessed, and unassessed is not the same thing as assessed and
> satisfactory.**

**And unassessed is also not adverse.** The repository has used that sentence twice already, at `02.07 §7`
and `02.12 §7`, and it holds here in both directions. Nothing in this chapter says the chromatography data
systems, the dissolution workstations or the monitoring systems hold unreliable records. **It says Helix
cannot currently say either way, for 24 pairs, and it says exactly what would let it.**

`PR-19` carries the risk that the unscorable population is read as low risk. The responses are that the `ND`
register is published in the same file as the scored one with the refusal stated at its head, that every row
carries what would be needed to determine it, and that `GOV-14` accepts the population expressly rather than
leaving it as a residue.

## 7. The `OBS-2` link, recorded and left alone

**Three of the seven — `RT-13`, `RT-14` and `RT-15` — are chromatography data system record types.
`OBS-2` concerns audit trail review in the QC laboratory. The link is recorded. No conclusion is drawn from
it, in either direction, and none may be drawn here.**

Phase 02 recorded the same link at `02.06 §9` and `DEC-211`, and `GOV-10 §6` directed that no document draw
a conclusion from it before **Phase 06**, which takes the chromatography data system at `MS-10`, and **Phase
08**, which takes the closure position on the observations. **That direction binds this chapter and this
chapter keeps it.**

**Why the discipline is harder here than in Phase 02.** Phase 02 could record the link as a coincidence of
scope. This chapter has to record it while also reporting that the two pairs on which nothing at all could
be determined are on the two chromatography data systems. **The sentence that joins those facts into a claim
is one line long and this phase has no basis for it.** What would be needed is an assessment of what the
audit trails on those systems actually record — Phase 05, with the chromatography data system in Phase 06 —
and an examination of the audit programmes against it, which is Phase 08. **Two of the three do not exist at
this vantage.**

**A Form FDA 483 is not a final agency determination of whether any condition is in violation.** No
observation has been closed, no closure position has been reached, and nothing in this chapter characterises
any past practice at Helix as a violation of anything.

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **It does not establish that any of the 24 pairs holds an unreliable record.** It establishes that Helix
  cannot determine at least one attribute of each, and names what would determine it.
- **It does not establish a control position for any of the 18 systems** — `IS-20`, and Phase 05 owns it.
  **It states no audit trail configuration and no review frequency**, and where an audit trail could not be
  inspected the consequence is a determination and not a requirement.
- **It does not re-open Phase 02's determination.** The seven are Part 11 records because `ADR-0010`'s test
  put them there, and nothing in this assessment disagrees or could.
- **It does not draw any conclusion from the `OBS-2` link** — §7.
- **It does not state a validation status for any of the 18 systems**, several of which are among the
  seventeen with no validation lifecycle behind them — `IS-13`.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**
