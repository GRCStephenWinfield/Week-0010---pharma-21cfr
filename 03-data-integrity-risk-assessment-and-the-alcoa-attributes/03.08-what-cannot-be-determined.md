## 1. One idea

**Forty-seven of the 121 pairs carry at least one attribute Helix cannot determine at all, and those 47 are
not scored.**

**An absence of evidence scores nothing.** It is a gap, not a low number. **A pair Helix cannot assess is not
a pair Helix has assessed favourably**, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of this
chapter.

`ADR-0018` and `DEC-310` record the decision. `GOV-14` accepts the population. `IS-16` carries it out of the
phase. The rows are at `logs/risk-register.md §4`, each with the attribute that could not be determined and
what would have to exist for it to be determined.

**This is the largest single act of the phase, and it is a refusal to produce a number.**

## 2. The two easy answers, argued fairly, and why both were refused

Two easy answers were available and `ADR-0018` refused them separately, because they are two different
proposals and a rebuttal of one is not a rebuttal of the other. **Score an undeterminable attribute at the
bottom of the scale and call it conservative**, which is the industry's usual practice; or **score it at
the top of the scale and call that caution instead.** Neither case is stupid and both deserve to be stated
properly before they are refused.

> **The case for scoring the 47 low.** A risk assessment exists to direct attention. A pair with an
> undeterminable attribute is, if anything, in worse shape than a pair with a known failure, because nobody
> even knows what is happening. Scoring it at the bottom of the scale puts it at the top of the
> remediation queue, which is where it belongs. It keeps the register on one page and one scale, so that a
> reader can rank everything against everything. And it errs towards caution, which is the direction a
> quality organisation is supposed to err in.

> **The case for scoring the 47 high.** If Helix cannot see, assume the worst. Put the pair at the top of
> the register, let evidence bring it down, and no reader can accuse the assessment of complacency about
> the part of the estate it understands least. It too keeps the whole population on one scale.

**Every clause of the first is true except the last, and the last is the one both cases rest on.**

**Scoring an undeterminable attribute low is not cautious. It is an inversion.** Nothing is known;
therefore nothing adverse is known; therefore the pair looks unremarkable and nobody returns to it. The
pairs about which Helix knows least would be the pairs the register drew the least attention to.

**And scoring it high is not caution either: a high rating asserts a likelihood with no basis, and makes
the pairs Helix could not see indistinguishable from the pairs it saw and found exposed.** Those two groups
need different work — the first needs evidence and the second needs a control — and a register that renders
them identically has destroyed the information that distinguishes them. **The conservative act is to say
you cannot see. Assigning a number you cannot support is not caution; it is a guess wearing the clothes of
a conclusion.**

**And a scored pair looks assessed.** This is the practical objection and it is the heavier one. Once a
rating exists, the pair enters every extract, every summary and every ranked list as a pair with an answer.
The question *why is there no evidence here* stops being asked, because the column that would have asked it
is full. **A gap that has been scored is a gap that has been closed by arithmetic.**

**The refusal has a cost and it was accepted.** Forty-seven pairs carry no rating and cannot be ranked
against the 74. A second register has to be maintained. Somebody will ask why the assessment has a hole in
it, and the answer — *because Helix has a hole in it* — is a harder thing to present than a number.
`DEC-310` records the cost; `GOV-12 §4` records that both alternatives were argued and lost.

## 3. Six conditions produce it

**Not determinable is not "difficult", "disputed" or "not yet done".** It is a specific state: **the
evidence that would settle the question does not exist, and somebody has looked for it in the places it
would be.** `AS-23` carries that as an assumption and `EV-316` is the record — every one of the 47 names
what was looked for, where, and by whom.

Six conditions produce it, and they are not interchangeable.

**There is no audit trail to inspect.** *Contemporaneously recorded* asks when the entry was captured
against when the act it records was performed. Answering it requires something that records both. Where the
system does not retain that, or where what it retains has never been characterised, the question has no
evidence to be answered from. **This is the condition behind 24 Not determinable determinations.** It is not
a statement about whether an audit trail should exist, how it should be configured or how often it should be
read — none of which this phase states, and the last of which no primary source names an interval for.

**There is no retention rule to test a copy against.** *Original or a true copy* asks whether a copy
preserves what it must preserve. There is no statement of what a copy must preserve, because no retention
period is recorded against any of the 34 in-scope record types — `IS-14`, open since 2026-07-24. **A trueness
test with nothing to test against is not a test.** This is the largest single condition, behind **41** Not
determinable determinations.

**There is no record of who held the account.** *Attributable* asks which named individual made the entry.
Where the account administration record does not reach back to the entry, the question cannot be answered
from anything Helix holds — 15 determinations. **This is not the same finding as a shared account.** A shared
account is a known state of affairs and produces *Not met*; an absent administration history produces *Not
determinable*, because Helix cannot say what the state of affairs was.

**There is nothing to compare the record against.** *Accurate* asks whether the record corresponds to the
event or measurement it records. Answering it requires a second thing — an independent instance, a physical
observation, a recalculation. Where the record is the only surviving trace of the event, there is nothing to
hold it against — 32 determinations.

**Nobody has tried to render the record from the store that holds it.** *Legible* asks whether the record
can be produced in complete and readable form with the context needed to understand it. Where a record sits
in an archive or a backup platform that has never been asked to give it back, neither answer can be
established from anything Helix holds — **9 determinations**, seven of them on the four archive, backup and
retention stores, and two on the pairs at `§4` on which no attribute at all could be determined.

**The record's relationship to its source is a function nobody wrote down.** Where an instance is produced
on demand from underlying data, rather than retrieved, its correspondence to that data is a property of the
query or the parameters and not of the record. **Nobody recorded the function, so nothing about the output
can be settled by inspecting the output.** This condition cuts across *original or a true copy*, *accurate*
and *contemporaneously recorded* at once, which is why the pairs it affects carry three and four
undeterminable attributes rather than one.

**Five of the six carry a count and the sixth cuts across them: 24 + 41 + 15 + 32 + 9 = 121.**

**The line between Not met and Not determinable, stated once.** If the facts can be established and the
attribute does not hold, the determination is **Not met**. If the facts themselves cannot be established,
the determination is **Not determinable**. `../03.05 §3` owns the rule. The distinction is a distinction
about Helix's knowledge and not about the record's condition, and drawing it is the reason the two
populations can be handed to different places.

**Two pairs carry all five attributes undeterminable**, and they are the chromatographic integration and
reprocessing record on the two chromatography data systems — `RT-13` on `SYS-023` and on `SYS-024`. **There
is nothing about either that Helix can currently determine.** Not who made the entry, not whether the record
can be rendered completely, not when it was captured, not which instance is the original, and not whether it
corresponds to anything.

**Nine record types could not be scored on any system that holds them** — `RT-12`, `RT-13`, `RT-14`,
`RT-15`, `RT-16`, `RT-17`, `RT-26`, `RT-27` and `RT-33`. **Seven of those nine are Phase 02's seven**, and
`../03.09-the-seven-assessed-for-the-first-time.md` owns them. **Two are not**: `RT-12`, the chromatographic
raw data file and acquisition metadata, and `RT-17`, the system suitability test record, were in scope
throughout and are still pairs on which no instance anywhere could be scored. **That is the cut this
chapter exists to publish**, because a reader told only about the seven would conclude that the unscorable
population is a consequence of a Phase 02 correction. It is not. It is wider than that.

**The owner distribution is a fact about where records are held and is not a statement about any
individual.** It is published rather than suppressed because leaving it out would make the population look
evenly spread when it is not, and because the closure routes run through different functions depending on
where the pairs sit.

## 5. What would be needed, by class

**Every one of the 47 rows carries a closure condition.** That is the register's design and it is not
decoration: **a gap with a stated closure condition is a gap somebody can close, and a gap without one is a
complaint.** `PR-26` carries the risk that a closure condition is read as a document to be written rather
than as evidence to be produced.

| The attribute that could not be determined | Determinations | What would determine it | Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Original or a true copy** | **41** | A statement, per record type, of which instance Helix relies on as the original, and a demonstrated method by which a copy of it is shown to be a true copy — supported by a retention rule the copy can be tested against | **`DP-17`**, `DP-18` |
| **Accurate** | **32** | A comparison of the record as held against the event or measurement it records, or against an independent instance of the same content — which for several pairs requires the second instance to be identified first | `DP-17`, `DP-16` |
| **Contemporaneously recorded** | **24** | A record of when the entry was captured as against when the act or measurement it records was performed, and a statement of what the system retains of both | **`DP-16`** |
| **Attributable** | **15** | An account administration record showing which named individual held the credential in use at the time of each entry | **`DP-20`** |
| **Legible** | **9** | A demonstration that the record can be rendered from the store that holds it in complete and readable form, with the context needed to understand it | `DP-16`, `DP-17` |
| **Total** | **121** | | |

**Three of those five closure routes are documents and two are technical.** `DP-17` and `DP-18` need a
decision written down. `DP-16` and `DP-20` need facts from vendors and from the IT organisation's access
administration function — and `ADR-0002` and `01.07 §6` keep the Data Integrity Lead out of account
administration, so `DP-20` is a request the role cannot itself fulfil. **`PR-05` is the standing risk that
determinations need cooperation the determiner cannot compel, and it was exercised again in this phase.**

## 6. What the 47 are handed forward as

**As a population. Not as a score, not as a queue, and not as a backlog.**

| Handed to | What it gets | What it must not do |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 04**, `MS-08`, 2026-11-30 | The 47 alongside the 74, with their closure conditions, as an input to the validation framework's rigour. **The risk-basing of validation extent is FDA's guidance, not FDA's regulation** — December 2018 Q&A, **Q3**, a nonbinding *should*: *"The extent of validation studies should be commensurate with the risk posed by the automated system."* `21 CFR 211.68(b)`'s *shall* graduates **input/output verification** and is cited only for that — `../03.01-nothing-required-this.md §5` — and **neither axis of this scale is the axis that regulation names**: it grades on *"the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system"*, and this assessment grades on what the record decides and on whether an alteration would be noticed. `../03.13-phase-summary-and-transition.md §6` carries what Phase 04 must state before relying on it | **Size rigour from the 74 alone.** `PR-24` carries exactly that risk, because the 47 carry no number to size against |
| **Phase 05**, `MS-09` | The attribution and contemporaneity closure routes — 15 and 24 determinations — which run through access administration and audit trail behaviour | Read a Not determinable determination as a control finding. **It is a statement about evidence, not about a control** — `IS-20` |
| **Phase 06**, `MS-10` | The chromatography data system pairs, including the two on which nothing at all could be determined | Draw a conclusion connecting them to `OBS-2` before it and Phase 08 have the evidence — `GOV-10 §6` |
| **Phase 07**, `MS-11` | The archive, retention and copy questions — `DP-18`, and the record types at `SYS-036`, `SYS-059`, `SYS-060` and `SYS-061` | Treat the archive's contents as adverse. **Unassessed is not adverse** |

**And the population stays a population.** An `ND` row that becomes determinable is re-assessed on the
trigger at `ADR-0022` and enters the scored register as a new entry with a new identifier and a date. **The
`ND` identifier closes against the evidence that determined it and is never reused, and no `ND` identifier
is ever converted into a `DR` identifier.** The two registers do not merge, are not summed and are not two
halves of one scale — `logs/risk-register.md §0`.

## 7. Why this is the phase's central argument

**Because it is the one place where the assessment could have made itself look complete and refused.**

A register of 121 rows all carrying a rating would have been shorter, easier to present, easier to rank and
easier to report to a steering committee. It would also have asserted, 47 times, a state of knowledge Helix
does not have. **The 74 scores are worth reading precisely because the 47 are not there** — a reader can
trust that a rating means somebody could see, because the rows where nobody could see are somewhere else
with their reasons written out.

**This is the same discipline the repository has kept twice before, and it is worth naming the pattern.**
Phase 01 published four candidate explanations for its audit history and chose none, because choosing one
would have been comfortable and unsupported — `ADR-0007`. Phase 02 established the premise of one of those
candidates and refused to promote it — `ADR-0012`, and *"establishing a candidate explanation's premise
does not promote the candidate to the answer."* **Phase 03 refuses to convert an absence of evidence into a
number.** Three phases, three refusals, and each of them was the cheapest thing in the phase to give away.

**The refusal is also what makes the phase auditable.** A third party can test 47 claims of the form *this
could not be determined and here is what was looked for* — `EV-316`. It cannot test 47 ratings that were
assigned to fill a column.

## 8. What this chapter does not establish

- **It does not establish that the 47 are worse than the 74**, or better, or comparable. **They are not on
  the scale.** Any statement ordering an `ND` row against a `DR` row is a statement this phase does not
  support — `PR-19`.
- **It does not establish that any record in the 47 is unreliable, incomplete or inaccurate.** It establishes
  that Helix cannot currently say. **Unassessed is not adverse**, and neither is undeterminable.
- **It does not state a control position, a validation status, an audit trail configuration, a review
  frequency or a retention period.** Where the absence of one of those is why an attribute could not be
  determined, the absence is recorded as a fact about evidence and not as a requirement — `IS-20`.
- **It does not say what Helix must do about any of it.** The assessment says where the data is weak;
  nothing in the quality system yet says what Helix must do about it. **`IS-21`**, and Phase 04 owns it.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**
