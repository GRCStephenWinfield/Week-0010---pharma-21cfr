## 1. What this chapter settles

This is the first phase in the programme that scores anything, and the first thing it has to say is that
nothing obliged it to.

**No FDA regulation and no FDA guidance requires a US drug manufacturer to produce, maintain or
periodically update a document called a data integrity risk assessment.** The phrase appears in neither.
It is not in 21 CFR Parts 210, 211 or 11, and it is not in FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity
guidance. A reader who has been told otherwise has been told it by somebody who was not reading the
sources.

That is an awkward opening for a phase whose entire output is an assessment, and it is deliberate. A
method presented as compelled is a method nobody has to defend. A method presented as chosen has to say
what it was chosen for, what it was chosen instead of, and what it will be measured against — and that is
the difference between an artefact that survives a question and an artefact that survives until the
question is asked.

This chapter owns three things for the repository: **the negative** — that nothing requires this document;
**where FDA does invoke risk — one general statement in the guidance's Introduction and three bounded
invocations in its answers**; and **the one regulation that does say *shall*
about graduating a control by system**, which is `21 CFR 211.68(b)` and which is where Helix's method is
anchored.

It does not describe the method. `03.05-the-method-and-the-scale.md` owns the instrument, the three
determinations and the scale, and `03.02-the-unit-of-assessment.md` owns the unit the method is applied
to.

## 2. What a data integrity risk assessment is for

Strip the name off it and the artefact is doing one thing: **deciding where to look hardest.**

Helix holds thirty-four Part 11 record types across sixty-one systems — the determination Phase 02
published at `02.05` and `02.08`. Everything in that population is subject to the same predicate rules.
Nothing in it is exempt from anything. But the effort available to assure those records is finite, and
distributing it evenly across the population is a decision, not a neutrality: it means the record that
determines whether a batch is released is examined with the same care as the record that logs a warehouse
temperature, and one of those two decisions is wrong.

The assessment is how Helix decides which is which, in writing, before the decision has to be made under
pressure. It produces three things the programme did not have on 2026-07-31: **a population that can be
counted**, **a determination per attribute per pair that somebody put their name to**, and **an ordered
statement of where the data is weakest**, which Phase 04 needs before it can size a validation framework
and Phase 05 needs before it can select a control.

**What it does not produce is a compliance position.** It is not a statement that any record is
compliant, that any control works, or that any system is validated. `03.12-what-this-does-not-establish.md`
carries that boundary in full.

## 3. The negative, stated exactly

**This section owns the negative for the repository.** It is stated at the level of the source, because a
negative claim of this kind is only worth making if it can be checked.

| Source | What it says about a data integrity risk assessment |
|---|---|
| **21 CFR Part 210** | The words *risk* and *risk assessment* do not appear. Neither do *data integrity*, *audit trail*, *metadata* or *ALCOA* |
| **21 CFR Part 211** | The same. Part 211 imposes obligations on records, controls, procedures and review. **It imposes no obligation to perform, document or maintain a risk assessment of any kind** |
| **21 CFR Part 11** | The words *risk* and *risk assessment* do not appear in the rule text. Risk-based decision-making about Part 11 arrives through the August 2003 Scope and Application guidance, **not through the rule** |
| **FDA, December 2018, *Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP: Questions and Answers*** | **The exact phrase "data integrity risk assessment" does not appear.** Risk is invoked in the general statement in the Introduction and in the three bounded places at `§4` below, and in none of them is the invocation an instruction to produce a standing assessment document |

Two consequences follow and the phase holds both.

**The first is that the assessment is Helix's decision.** It was taken as a programme decision and
authorised at `GOV-11`, and it could have been taken differently. Nothing in this repository
presents the assessment as compelled, and no chapter of this phase writes *FDA requires* about it.

**The second is that the guidance which does discuss risk is nonbinding, and the phase says so at every
citation.** FDA's own words, on the face of the December 2018 guidance:

> *"In general, FDA's guidance documents do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities. Instead,
> guidances describe the Agency's current thinking on a topic and should be viewed only as
> recommendations, unless specific regulatory or statutory requirements are cited. The use of the word
> should in Agency guidances means that something is suggested or recommended, but not required."*

Every page of it carries the header **Contains Nonbinding Recommendations**. That sentence governs the
reading of everything in `§4`.

## 4. Where FDA does invoke risk — one general statement and three bounded ones

**The broadest thing FDA says about risk in this guidance is in its Introduction**, and it is quoted first
because an inventory that leaves it out is not an inventory. Verbatim:

> *"CGMP regulations and guidance allow for flexible and risk-based strategies to prevent and detect data
> integrity issues. Firms should implement meaningful and effective strategies to manage their data
> integrity risks based on their process understanding and knowledge management of technologies and
> business models. Meaningful and effective strategies should consider the design, operation, and
> monitoring of systems and controls based on risk to patient, process, and product."*
>
> **It is three sentences of *should*, it names no artefact, and it is the passage carrying footnote 3's
> pointer to ICH Q9.**

**Then the three bounded invocations in the answers.** Each is bounded to a particular determination, and
none of them is a general mandate.

| # | Where | What is being decided | FDA's words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Q3 — validation** | **The extent of validation studies** | *"The extent of validation studies should be commensurate with the risk posed by the automated system."* |
| **2** | **Q8 — audit trail review frequency, and only where the regulation is silent** | **The frequency of audit trail review, where CGMP specifies no frequency for the underlying data** | *"If the review frequency for the data is not specified in CGMP regulations, you should determine the review frequency for the audit trail using knowledge of your processes and risk assessment tools. The risk assessment should include evaluation of data criticality, control mechanisms, and impact on product quality."* |
| **3** | **Q18 — remediation after a failure** | **The scope and effect of a data integrity failure that has already occurred** | Firms should demonstrate effective remediation by investigating to determine the scope of the problem — *"including impact on data used to support submissions to FDA"* — and its root causes, and by *"conducting a scientifically sound risk assessment of its potential effects"* |

**Read the second row twice, because it is the one most often quoted with its condition removed.** FDA's
rule on audit trail review frequency is **inheritance, not periodicity**: where CGMP specifies how often
the underlying data is reviewed, the audit trail review takes that frequency. Only where the regulation is
silent does the firm set a frequency, and only there does risk enter. **This phase sets no review
frequency for anything** — that is Phase 05's, and `01.04 §7` owns the argument about where the obligation
comes from.

There is a further invocation, older and often overlooked, and it is worth separating from the three
because it is doing something different. FDA's August 2003 Scope and Application guidance uses the phrase
*"a justified and documented risk assessment"* three times — about whether to apply audit trails, about
the approach to validation, and about how records are maintained. **That is a control-selection risk
assessment, it is a recommendation rather than a requirement, and it is not a data integrity assessment of
a record population.** It sits closer to Phase 05's work than to this phase's, and it is recorded here so
that nobody later cites it as the authority for what Phase 03 did.

**The methodological reference FDA itself points to is ICH Q9.** Footnote 3 reads, in full: *"See ICH
guidance for industry Q9 Quality Risk Management."* **The version currently adopted by FDA is Q9(R1),
issued as guidance in May 2023, which post-dates the 2018 footnote and revised the document it pointed
to.** Helix reads footnote 3 as a pointer to the current Q9(R1). It is guidance, it is nonbinding, and its
most useful sentence for a programme of this size is a refusal to prescribe:
*"Formality in quality risk management is not a binary concept (i.e., formal/informal); varying degrees of
formality may be applied during quality risk management activities."* Helix adopts that principle
voluntarily and labels it as adopted, in the same discipline `ADR-0005` applies to FDA's Computer Software
Assurance guidance — which is device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by the Quality
Management System Regulation, and does not apply to Helix.

## 5. The one `shall` — `21 CFR 211.68(b)`

There is a regulation that graduates a control by the properties of the system it runs on, it is in the
drug CGMPs, it says *shall*, and it is cited far less often than it deserves.

**`21 CFR 211.68(b)`, verbatim, in full:**

> *"Appropriate controls shall be exercised over computer or related systems to assure that changes in
> master production and control records or other records are instituted only by authorized personnel.
> Input to and output from the computer or related system of formulas or other records or data shall be
> checked for accuracy. The degree and frequency of input/output verification shall be based on the
> complexity and reliability of the computer or related system. A backup file of data entered into the
> computer or related system shall be maintained except where certain data, such as calculations performed
> in connection with laboratory analysis, are eliminated by computerization or other automated processes.
> In such instances a written record of the program shall be maintained along with appropriate validation
> data. Hard copy or alternative systems, such as duplicates, tapes, or microfilm, designed to assure that
> backup data are exact and complete and that it is secure from alteration, inadvertent erasures, or loss
> shall be maintained."*

**The third sentence is the anchor: *"The degree and frequency of input/output verification shall be based
on the complexity and reliability of the computer or related system."***

That is the only place in Part 211 where the intensity of a control is expressly made a function of the
properties of the system it is applied to, and it is a `shall` rather than a `should`. Helix's method is
anchored there — **on a regulation, and not on a guidance** — and the anchoring is what `ADR-0016` and
`ADR-0020` both rest on.

**Two qualifications the phase states rather than hides.**

**Its scope is narrow.** `21 CFR 211.68(b)` graduates **input/output verification** and nothing else. It
does not, by its terms, authorise graduating audit trails, access controls, review or retention. Helix's
assessment produces a graduated statement about the reliability of records, and where a later phase uses
that statement to size a control other than input/output verification, the justification is Helix's own
and must be labelled as Helix's. **A regulation cited beyond its own subject matter stops being a
citation.**

**The clause does not require an assessment either.** It requires that the degree and frequency of
input/output verification **be based on** complexity and reliability. It does not say how the firm is to
know what those are, and it names no instrument. **The assessment is how Helix proposes to know. It is not
the thing the regulation asks for; it is the evidence that the thing the regulation asks for was done on a
basis rather than on a habit.**

## 6. Two provisions that are not the hook, and are regularly said to be

**`21 CFR 211.110(a)`** requires written procedures describing the in-process controls and tests to be
conducted, and provides that *"Such control procedures shall include, but are not limited to, the
following, where appropriate"*, followed by an enumerated list — tablet or capsule weight variation,
disintegration time, adequacy of mixing, dissolution time and rate, clarity, completeness or pH of
solutions, and bioburden testing. **The words *where appropriate* qualify that list of in-process tests.**
They mean apply the ones that suit the dosage form. They are not a general licence to apply CGMP controls
only where a risk assessment says so, and reading them that way is an error visible to anybody who reads
the sentence to its end. Phase 02 records that `21 CFR 211.110(a)` specifies no record content at all.

**`21 CFR 211.180(e)`** requires that written records be maintained so that the data in them can be used
for evaluating, at least annually, the quality standards of each drug product, to determine the need for
changes in specifications or in manufacturing or control procedures. **Its object is the quality standards
of the product and its stated purpose is that determination.** It says nothing about data integrity, and
it does not use the phrase *annual product review*. A programme that cites it as the requirement to review
data integrity annually is citing a provision that does not say that. Helix cites it for what it does say
— it is one of the two sections FDA's own footnote 5 maps *legible* to, and
`03.04-footnote-five.md` owns that mapping.

**And the register uses the industry term, so the collision is reconciled here rather than left to be
found.** **`RT-40` is named *Annual product review record* because that is what Helix's quality system
calls it. `21 CFR 211.180(e)` does not use that phrase**; it requires records to be maintained so the data
in them can be used for *"evaluating, at least annually, the quality standards of each drug product."*
**The record type name is Helix's; the obligation is the regulation's, and the two are not the same
thing.**

## 7. What the international sources say, and what they bind

Four bodies of material are read as reference. **None of them binds Helix, and each is labelled at every
citation.**

| Source | Status | What it says about a data integrity risk assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **WHO Technical Report Series No. 1033, Annex 4, *Guideline on data integrity*, 2021** | **A WHO guideline.** Addressed principally to WHO prequalification and to national authorities that adopt WHO guidance. **It does not bind a CDER-inspected US site** | **The only primary source that names a data integrity risk assessment and expects one.** It expects the assessment to be risk-based, to cover the data life cycle, to consider data criticality, and to record the prioritisation of actions and the acceptance of residual risk. **It prescribes no scale** |
| **PIC/S PI 041-1, *Good Practices for Data Management and Integrity in Regulated GMP/GDP Environments*, in force 1 July 2021** | **Not mandatory or enforceable under law**, in its own words. Developed primarily for inspectors. FDA's participation in PIC/S does not make it enforceable in the United States | Expects assessment of data criticality and data risk and gives criteria to consider. **Prescribes no scale.** It also directs, at `§5.5.4`, that assessments focus on business processes and data flows and *"not just consider information technology (IT) system functionality or complexity"* — a direction Helix's unit does not follow, and `03.11-the-flow-the-unit-does-not-see.md` owns that tension rather than hiding it |
| **MHRA, *'GXP' Data Integrity Guidance and Definitions*, Revision 1: March 2018** | **UK scope**, in its own words at **§2.1**: *"This document provides guidance for UK industry and public bodies regulated by the UK MHRA"*. Adopted by Helix **voluntarily** where adopted at all | Expects a documented system providing an acceptable state of control based on data integrity risk, with effort *"commensurate with the risk and impact of a data integrity failure to the patient or environment"* — **§3.6**. **Prescribes no scale** |
| **EU GMP Annex 11, revision 1, in operation 30 June 2011** | **EU guidance. FDA does not enforce EudraLex**, and an Annex 11 expectation is not an FDA requirement | Clause 1 provides that decisions on the extent of validation and data integrity controls should be based on a justified and documented risk assessment of the computerised system. **A draft revision was consulted on between 7 July and 7 October 2025 and is not in force; nothing here cites a draft as a requirement** |

**One dating point, because it is the trap that catches most industry material.** The WHO document above is
**TRS 1033 Annex 4 (2021)**, and it states in terms that it **replaces** the WHO guidance on good data and
record management practices at **TRS 996 Annex 5 (2016)**. A great deal of published material still cites
the superseded one as current. **Helix cites TRS 1033 Annex 4 and does not cite TRS 996 Annex 5 at all.**

## 8. So why did Helix do it

Four reasons, stated in the order of their weight, and each is Helix's own.

**One — because `21 CFR 211.68(b)` requires the degree and frequency of input/output verification to be
based on the complexity and reliability of the system, and the assessment is the basis.** Without it the
graduation exists but its basis does not, and a `shall` discharged by an undocumented judgement is a
`shall` discharged in a way nobody can examine.

**Two — because FDA's guidance directs a risk-based determination in the two places Phase 04 and Phase 05
have to make one:** the extent of validation, and the frequency of audit trail review where CGMP is
silent. Those determinations are due at `MS-08` and `MS-09`. **A determination due in November cannot rest
on an assessment nobody made in September.**

**Three — because the December 2018 guidance's ALCOA sentence and its footnote 5 give the assessment a
vocabulary and a mapping that are FDA's rather than a consultancy's** — `03.03` and `03.04`.

**Four — because the quality unit has to be able to say, in writing, how it decided.** `21 CFR 211.22(d)`
requires the quality control unit's responsibilities and procedures to be in writing and provides that
*"such written procedures shall be followed."* That provision cuts both ways and the phase says so out
loud: it is the reason to write the method down, and it is the reason to keep the method small. **The
moment Helix writes a scale, its own procedure is enforceable against it.**
`03.05-the-method-and-the-scale.md §9` owns that argument, and `ADR-0020` records the decision.

## 9. What this chapter does not do

- **It does not describe the instrument, the three determinations or the scale.** `03.05`.
- **It does not define the unit of assessment.** `03.02`.
- **It states no result of any kind** — no determination, no rating, no count of anything assessed. The
  results are `03.06` to `03.10`.
- **It sets no audit trail review frequency and states none as a requirement.** The frequency is inherited
  from the review of the underlying data, and no primary source names one.
- **It states no validation status and no control position.** Phase 04 and Phase 05.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion**, characterises nothing as a violation of anything, and forecasts no
  inspection outcome.
