## 1. What this chapter does

`../03.05-the-method-and-the-scale.md` built the instrument. This chapter runs it, once, across the whole
population, and reports what came out. **It argues no part of the method**; where a rule of the method
explains a result, the rule is cited and not restated.

**One instrument, 121 pairs, five attributes each, 605 determinations, and no exceptions.** No pair was
skipped because it looked obvious, and no pair was assessed twice because its first answer was
uncomfortable. **A pair whose five attributes are all Met is recorded with the evidence examined for each of
the five**, on the same form and to the same standard as a pair that failed, because a *Met* nobody can
trace to an examination is an opinion in a register that looks like a determination.

The results are at `logs/attribute-determination-register.md`, which is the authoritative record. This
chapter is the argument the register cannot make about itself: what the numbers are, how the population
divides, and what the division means.

## 2. The population, restated once and not re-derived

The unit is the record-type/system pair — `ADR-0016`, `DEC-303`, argued at `../03.02-the-unit-of-assessment.md`.
The population is the join of Phase 02's 34 in-scope record types to the 61 systems that hold at least one
of them, taken from `../02-the-system-inventory-and-part-11-applicability/logs/record-type-register.md` and
`../02-the-system-inventory-and-part-11-applicability/logs/systems-register.md` and derived rather
than typed — `DEC-304`, `EV-304`.

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| In-scope record types | **34** |
| Systems holding at least one | **61** |
| **Assessment pairs** | **121** |
| Attribute determinations | **121 × 5 = 605** |

**The shape of the population matters to how the results read**, and it is not even.

| Cut | Position |
|---|---|
| Widest spread | **`RT-04`** — the in-process control result record — and **`RT-11`** — the analytical result record — are each held on **10** systems. **`RT-27`**, the differential pressure, temperature and humidity trend record, is on **8** |
| Densest system | **`SYS-041`**, the electronic quality management system, carries **7** pairs. **`SYS-001`, `SYS-023`, `SYS-024` and `SYS-043` carry 5 each** |
| The long tail | **30 of the 61 systems carry exactly one pair** |
| Chromatography | **`SYS-023` and `SYS-024` carry 10 pairs between them** |
| By owner | Joachim Reuter **45** · Dr Sunita Raghunathan **37** · Bernard Kwiatkowski **15** · Amara Sissoko **13** · Terrence Abbatiello **11**. **45 + 37 + 15 + 13 + 11 = 121** |
| By location | Ridgemont **78** · Cary **17** · Ridgemont data centre **17** · Vendor-hosted **9**. **78 + 17 + 17 + 9 = 121** |
| **What the unit does not see** | **31 of the 34 in-scope record types are held on more than one system.** The pair assesses the record on each system and assesses the transfer between them nowhere — `IS-18`, and `../03.11-the-flow-the-unit-does-not-see.md` owns it |

**The owner and location cuts are facts about where records are held.** They are not statements about any
individual and no document in this repository assesses any individual's work.

## 3. The 605, in one table

Counted from the rows of `logs/attribute-determination-register.md` and from nothing else.

| Determination | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Met** | **388** | The attribute holds for this pair and the evidence for it was examined |
| **Not met** | **96** | The attribute does not hold. **A fact established at this vantage, not a likelihood** |
| **Not determinable** | **121** | Helix cannot establish either answer from anything it holds |
| **Total** | **605** | **388 + 96 + 121 = 605** |

**Read by attribute, which is the only cut that carries meaning**, because the five are never averaged —
`ADR-0017`:

| Attribute | Met | Not met | Not determinable | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attributable | 83 | **23** | **15** | 121 |
| Legible | 103 | **9** | **9** | 121 |
| Contemporaneously recorded | 80 | **17** | **24** | 121 |
| **Original or a true copy** | **46** | **34** | **41** | **121** |
| Accurate | 76 | **13** | **32** | 121 |
| **Total** | **388** | **96** | **121** | **605** |

**Three things in that table are worth stopping on.**

**The first is the fourth row.** *Original or a true copy* is the only attribute on which fewer than half
the population is recorded Met — 46 of 121 — and it is simultaneously the most-failed attribute and the
most-undeterminable one. **34 + 41 = 75 of the 121 pairs are either known not to meet it or cannot be shown
to meet it.** `../03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md` owns that result and `IS-15` carries it.

**The second is that legible is the quiet one.** Nine Not met and nine Not determinable, and 103 Met. That
is not a reason to be pleased. It reflects the fact that legibility is the attribute a system is most likely
to satisfy incidentally, by rendering what it holds, and the attribute whose failure is most obvious when
it occurs. **An attribute that rarely fails is an attribute that tells you least when it holds.**

**The third is that the Not determinable column does not distribute like the Not met column.** *Accurate*
has 13 Not met and 32 Not determinable. Accuracy is not commonly untrue at Helix; it is commonly
unverifiable, because verifying it requires a second thing to compare the record against and for many pairs
there is not one. **Not met and Not determinable are not two grades of the same answer. They are answers to
two different questions**, and `../03.05 §3` owns the distinction.

## 4. How the population divides, and it divides once

**The division is on Not determinable and on nothing else.** A pair on which all five attributes are
determinable is scored, whatever the determinations are. A pair carrying one or more Not determinable
attributes is not scored at all — `ADR-0018`, `DEC-310`.

| Population | Count | Register | Carries a rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fully determinable | **74** | `DR-01` to `DR-74` | **Yes** |
| Carrying at least one Not determinable attribute | **47** | `ND-01` to `ND-47` | **No** |
| **Total** | **121** | | **74 + 47 = 121** |

**The 121 Not determinable determinations sit on 47 pairs, not on 121.** Nine of the 47 carry one
undeterminable attribute, eight carry two, twenty-six carry three, two carry four and two carry all five —
**9 + 8 + 26 + 2 + 2 = 47**. The two carrying all five are the chromatographic integration and reprocessing
record on the two chromatography data systems, `RT-13` on `SYS-023` and on `SYS-024`, and there is nothing
about either that Helix can currently determine.

**Neither register converts into the other.** `DR` and `ND` are not two halves of a scale and are not two
ends of one. A pair whose undeterminable attribute is later determined does not become a `DR` row by
relabelling; it is re-assessed under `ADR-0022` and enters the scored register with a new identifier and a
date. `logs/risk-register.md §0` states the rule and `../03.08-what-cannot-be-determined.md` argues it.

## 5. The 74, in outline only

The ratings, the nine High and their two bases are `../03.10-the-scored-risks.md`'s and are not previewed
here. Two facts about the 74 belong in this chapter because they are facts about the assessment rather than
about the ratings.

**The first: 14 of the 74 carry five Met determinations and no Not met.** Fourteen pairs came through the
instrument clean. Several of them are still not rated Low, and that is the method working rather than
failing: **likelihood is of the undetected alteration, and a pair whose five attributes all hold can still
be a pair that nothing in the ordinary course compares against** — `ADR-0021`, `../03.05 §6`.

**The second: 63 of the 96 Not met determinations sit on scored pairs and 33 sit on unscored ones.** A pair
can be Not met on one attribute and Not determinable on another, and neither determination cancels the
other. **The 33 Not met determinations on unscored pairs are as much a fact as the 63 on scored ones** —
they simply cannot be turned into a rating, because a rating is taken across five attributes and one of them
is not there.

## 6. What "assessed" means here, and what it does not

**Every one of the 121 pairs was assessed.** That sentence is exact and it is the reason `IS-11` closes.
Phase 02 left the programme with an open question in its own words: *the determination exists and says 34
record types across 61 systems are in scope; nobody has assessed whether the data in any of them is any
good.* **Somebody has now.** Every pair has five determinations, each with a named determiner, a date, a
quality unit reviewer and the evidence examined — `EV-314`, `EV-315`.

**A determination of Not determinable is a result of assessment and not an absence of one.** That is the
proposition `DEC-317` turns on and it is worth stating plainly, because the opposite reading is available
and is wrong. To record that an attribute cannot be determined, somebody has to establish what evidence
would determine it, look for that evidence in the places it would be, and record that it is not there —
`EV-316`, `AS-23`. **A pair nobody looked at and a pair somebody looked at and could not resolve are
different objects, and only the second has a closure condition.**

**What the assessment therefore did not do.** It did not establish that any record is reliable — a *Met* on
five attributes is a statement about five specific properties and not a general endorsement. It did not
establish that any control works; **`IS-20`** carries that and Phase 05 owns it. It did not establish
anything about the transfer of a record between systems; **`IS-18`** carries that. It did not re-open the
Part 11 determination, and no row in either register disagrees with Phase 02 about what is in scope —
`DEC-304`.

## 7. The one place the assessment surprised the people who ran it

**The assessment expected *attributable* to be the attribute that failed most.** The expectation was
reasonable, it was written down before the work started, and it was wrong. `EV-325` is the record of both
halves, and the argument is `../03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md §5`'s rather than this chapter's.

**It is recorded here because a phase that quietly drops an expectation once it fails leaves a reader unable
to tell whether it ever had one.** The same discipline produced Phase 02's split verdict on `AS-03` —
held on its date, failed on its method — and it produces this phase's closure of `AS-17` as failed rather
than as reworded.

## 8. What was examined, and what was taken on description

**A determination rests on something, and the register says what.** `EV-315` is the schedule of what was
inspected for each determination recorded *Met*, and it exists because the alternative — a *Met* with no
traceable examination behind it — is the defect this phase would be most easily criticised for.

**One assumption is load-bearing on the whole exercise and is recorded rather than buried.** `AS-22`: the
individuals who supplied the facts each determination rests on — record owners, system owners and
administrators — described the position as it is rather than as procedure says it should be. **If `AS-22`
fails, determinations recorded Met rest on a description rather than on the position**, and the register
becomes a record of what people believe. It is carried open, with its owner named, and it is the reason
`EV-315` names the artefact examined and not the person who described it.

**Two further assumptions bound the exercise.** `AS-20`: the 121 pairs remain the population to `MS-08`, so
that Phase 04 sets rigour against a register that still describes the estate. `AS-24`: FDA's five attributes
are sufficient to describe the integrity position of a Helix record type, so that a pair with five *Met*
determinations has no material integrity question left unasked by this instrument. **Neither is proved and
both are written down.**

## 9. What this chapter does not state

- **No validation status for any system.** Phase 04, `MS-08`. Eleven of the seventeen systems that had never
  entered a validation lifecycle — `IS-13` — carry pairs in this assessment, and no rating says anything
  about their validation state.
- **No control position.** Which paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` are met, for any system, is not stated here
  and is Phase 05's — `IS-20`, `IS-09`.
- **No audit trail configuration and no review frequency.** Where an audit trail could not be inspected, the
  consequence is a Not determinable determination, not a statement about what an audit trail should do or
  how often it should be read. **No primary source names a frequency and this phase names none** — `IS-04`.
- **No retention period** — `IS-14`, `DP-18`.
- **No mapping of an attribute onto a paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10`.** FDA's footnote 5 maps these attributes
  to the predicate rules and cites Part 11 nowhere — `../03.04-footnote-five.md`, `ADR-0019`. **Where an
  attribute and a Part 11 paragraph both bear on the same record, they are two assessments of that record.**
- **No finding, no deficiency, no violation and no legal conclusion of any kind.**
- **No forecast of what any inspection or audit will find.**
