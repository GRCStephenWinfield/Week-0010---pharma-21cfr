## 1. The 74

**Seventy-four of the 121 pairs are determinable on all five attributes and were therefore scored.** They
are `DR-01` to `DR-74` at `logs/risk-register.md §2`, one row each, and every row carries a likelihood with
its basis and a consequence with its basis, recorded separately.

| Rating | Count |
|---|---|
| **High** — product 15 or above | **9** |
| **Moderate** — product 8 to 12 | **31** |
| **Low** — product 6 or below | **34** |
| **Total** | **74** — **9 + 31 + 34 = 74** |

The scale is `../03.05-the-method-and-the-scale.md`'s and is not re-argued here. Three of its properties
govern how this chapter reads and each is cited rather than restated: **likelihood is of the *undetected*
alteration** (`ADR-0021`); **a rating moves on likelihood unless the consequence changed** (`ADR-0021`);
and **the five attributes are never averaged, so a rating is not a summary of the profile** (`ADR-0017`).

## 2. What the ratings are made of

**Consequence is a property of the record type. Likelihood is a property of the pair.** What is lost if the
record is wrong travels with the class of record; whether an alteration would be noticed depends on the
system holding it. That is why the same record type carries the same consequence on most of its rows and
different likelihoods, and it is why **a rating that moves between two systems has moved on likelihood**.

**Where consequence does differ between two instances of the same record type, the difference is a
difference of role** — a secondary or derived holding of a record type has its consequence recorded lower,
and **the band the basis's closing clause names is what records the difference**. `AS-21` carries the assumption that
consequence is otherwise stable across the systems holding a type, and it is load-bearing on every
comparison in this chapter.

**Seven of the 74 take their likelihood from a comparison with an instance that is itself in the unscored
register.** The comparison is an act somebody performs and its existence is a fact, but **the trueness of
the instance compared against is undetermined**, and those seven ratings would move if that changed. The
rows are `DR-01`, `DR-02`, `DR-62`, `DR-64`, `DR-65`, `DR-71` and `DR-72`. **This is disclosed and not
rescored**: `../03.08-what-cannot-be-determined.md §1`'s rule cuts both ways, and a comparison whose other
half Helix cannot see is still a comparison somebody performs. `ADR-0022`'s `R2` is the route by which any
of the seven would be re-run.

**The distributions, counted from the rows:**

| Likelihood | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | | Consequence | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rows | **54** | 13 | 5 | 2 | | Rows | 5 | **33** | **29** | 7 |

**Fifty-four of the 74 sit at likelihood 2**, and the reason is stated on each of those rows: an instance of
the same content exists elsewhere and the two are brought together in the ordinary course of the work, or a
second person reads the record against the act it records. **That is a statement about what would notice,
which is what the axis is for.** It is not a statement that the record is well controlled, that a control
has been tested, or that anything has been validated.

**Likelihood 1 is reserved and no row uses it.** A likelihood of 1 asserts that an undetected alteration is
not credible, and Helix has assessed no pair for which it can say that.

**The products actually used are 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15 and 16.** **No row carries 7, 11, 13, 14, 17, 18,
19, 21, 22, 23 or 24**, and none can: those numbers are not products of two integers between 1 and 5. **The
bands therefore do not touch.** There is no product between 6 and 8 and none between 12 and 15, so **no row
sits on a boundary and no row was moved to get off one** — which is a property of the arithmetic rather
than a virtue of the assessors.

## 3. Fourteen of the 74 have nothing recorded against them, and several are not Low

**Fourteen pairs carry five *Met* determinations and no *Not met*.** They came through the instrument clean.
Ten of them are rated Moderate and four are rated Low, and **not one is rated High.**

**That ten pairs with a clean profile are rated Moderate is the method working rather than failing.** The
electronic batch production and control record on the manufacturing execution system, `DR-01`, is one of
them: all five attributes hold, and it is rated Moderate at likelihood 2 × consequence 5 because the
consequence of an undetected alteration to a batch production and control record is what it is whatever the
attributes say. **An attribute assessment describes properties of a record. A rating describes what would
happen if the record were wrong and nothing noticed.** They are different questions and the register keeps
them in different columns.

**Conversely, a pair with a *Not met* determination is not thereby High.** Thirty of the 74 are recorded
*Not met* on "original or a true copy" and seventeen of those thirty are rated Low, because on those pairs a second
instance exists and is compared. **A Not met is a fact, not a likelihood** — `ADR-0021` — and the register
would be dishonest if it turned every fact into a rating.

## 4. The nine High, with their two bases stated separately

**Each of the nine is set out below with its likelihood basis and its consequence basis as two separate
statements**, because a rating whose two halves cannot be inspected separately cannot be argued with —
`DEC-312`. The rows themselves are at `logs/risk-register.md §2` and the completed scoring records are at
`EV-317`; the extract taken to the Steering Committee is `EV-323`.

### `DR-05` — `RT-05` equipment cleaning and use log, on `SYS-001`, the manufacturing execution system

**Owner Joachim Reuter. Likelihood 5 × consequence 3 = 15.**

**Likelihood basis.** Nothing in the ordinary course compares this record with another instance or with the
event it records; there is no act an alteration would have to survive. The cleaning happened, the log says
it happened, and nothing independent records the same event.

**Consequence basis.** The cleaning and use log is the evidence that the equipment was clean and that its
use is traceable to a batch. An undetected alteration would change what a reviewer sees of a controlled step
without by itself changing a disposition.

**Attributes recorded Not met:** contemporaneously recorded, original or a true copy.

### `DR-06` — `RT-04` in-process control result record, on `SYS-002`, the process historian

**Owner Joachim Reuter. Likelihood 4 × consequence 4 = 16.**

it as a matter of course; an alteration would wait on whatever prompted somebody to look. The historian's
copy is consulted when something else raises a question, not as part of batch record review.

**Consequence basis.** The in-process control result is one of the acceptance criteria the batch is judged
against at review. An undetected alteration would change the evidence a quality decision rests on.

## 5. What the nine have in common, and what they do not

**They are not the nine most consequential records at Helix.** Two of the nine carry consequence 3 and reach
High on likelihood 5. Several pairs with consequence 5 are rated Moderate because a comparison exists.
**The rating is a product, and a product is not a ranking of importance.**

**They do not cluster in one function.** Three are on systems owned by Joachim Reuter, three by Amara
Sissoko, two by Terrence Abbatiello and one by Bernard Kwiatkowski. **Four of the nine are on systems that
are nobody's idea of a laboratory or a shop floor** — an enterprise resource planning system and a
computerised maintenance management system — and that is the most useful thing about the list. The records
that nothing compares against are frequently the records held in the systems that everybody thinks of as
administrative.

**What they do have in common is the likelihood axis.** Seven of the nine sit at likelihood 4 or 5, and in
every one of those cases the basis is the same shape: **no routine act brings this record together with
anything else.** That is a statement about the ordinary course of work at Helix and not about any control,
and **the remedy it points at is not necessarily technical** — a comparison somebody performs is a
comparison, and this phase does not say which kind Helix should have.

## 6. What a rating does not authorise

**Stated as refusals, because a caveat in a footnote is a caveat nobody reads.** `IS-20` carries this out of
the phase and `PR-20` carries the risk that it is ignored.

| A rating is not | Why |
|---|---|
| **A finding** | A finding is made by somebody with the authority to make one, against a standard, in a process that produces findings. **This is Helix's own assessment of its own position at one vantage** |
| **A deficiency** | A deficiency is a shortfall against a stated requirement. This assessment states no requirement and tests nothing against one |
| **A violation** | **No document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of any kind**, and none characterises any past practice at Helix as a violation of anything |
| **An inspectional observation** | `OBS-1` to `OBS-7` are the 483's, in the 483's own numbering, and are never renumbered or extended — `ADR-0006`. **No `DR` identifier is an observation and no observation is a `DR` identifier** |
| **A control position** | **Nothing here says whether any control works.** Which paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` are met, for any system, is Phase 05's — `IS-20`, `IS-09` |
| **A validation status** | Phase 04, `MS-08`. Several of the 74 sit on systems that had never entered a validation lifecycle — `IS-13` — and no rating says anything about that |
| **A statement about a person** | **No document in this repository assesses any individual's work.** The owner column names who owns the system, not who is responsible for a rating |
| **An ordering** | **A band is a product of two axes and nothing more.** No pair is placed in front of Phase 04 first because it is rated High, and no pair waits because it is rated Low — `../03.05-the-method-and-the-scale.md §7` |
| **A remediation priority** | **The assessment says where the data is weak. Nothing in Helix's quality system yet says what any band obliges anybody to do** — `IS-21`, and Phase 04 owns it |

**The rating most likely to be misread is Low, not High.** A Low rating says that on this pair, at this
vantage, on this scale, an undetected alteration would meet something that would show it, and the
consequence if it did not is bounded. **It does not say the pair is compliant, controlled, validated or
safe.** Thirty-four pairs are rated Low and thirty of them carry at least one attribute recorded *Not met*.

**And a rating says nothing about the 47 that are not rated.** They are not below Low. **They are not on the
scale** — `../03.08-what-cannot-be-determined.md`, `PR-19`.

## 7. The scale is now something Helix will be held to

**`21 CFR 211.22(d)` requires the quality control unit's responsibilities and procedures to be in writing
and provides that *"such written procedures shall be followed."*** No regulator prescribes a risk scale.
**The moment Helix writes one, its own procedure is enforceable against it** — `ADR-0020`, `DEC-313`, and
`../03.05 §9` owns the argument.

**Two consequences are recorded rather than assumed.** **`IS-19`**: the scale is now a written procedure and
nothing in Helix's quality system yet requires it to be applied or maintained. **`AS-25`**: the scale is
applied unchanged to `MS-13`, so that a rating taken in Phase 03 remains comparable with one taken later —
and **a scale varied after publication is a written procedure that was not followed.** `PR-22` carries the
pressure to elaborate it.
