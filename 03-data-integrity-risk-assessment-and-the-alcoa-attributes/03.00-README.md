*Phase 03 speaks as at **2026-09-30**. Helix Pharma, Inc., its sites, its people, its systems, its inspection and every figure in this repository are fictional and illustrative. Where a statement is made about a real regulation or guidance — 21 CFR Parts 11, 210 and 211, FDA's August 2003 Scope and Application guidance, FDA's December 2018 drug CGMP data integrity guidance — it is real and is cited exactly, and the two kinds of statement are kept visibly apart throughout.*

---

## 1. What this phase is

Phase 02 ended on a sentence: *the determination exists, it says thirty-four record types across
sixty-one systems are in scope, and nobody has assessed whether the data in any of them is any good.*
**Applicability is not integrity.** `IS-11` carried the difference forward and this is the phase where it
gets measured.

**This is the first phase in the programme that scores anything, and the first thing it says is that
nothing required it to.** No FDA regulation and no FDA guidance requires a data integrity risk assessment;
the phrase appears in neither. The assessment is Helix's decision, authorised at `GOV-11`, and anchored on a
regulation rather than on a guidance — **`21 CFR 211.68(b)`**, which provides
that *"The degree and frequency of input/output verification shall be based on the complexity and
reliability of the computer or related system."*

The phase joins Phase 02's two registers to produce a population, asks five questions of each member of it
in FDA's own words, refuses to score the members that cannot answer, scores the ones that can on a scale
Helix wrote and published, and then criticises the unit it used to do all of that.

## 2. Read this first

Four things, and the rest of the phase follows from them.

**Nothing required this.** The phrase *data integrity risk assessment* appears in no FDA regulation and no
FDA guidance. What FDA does say about risk in the December 2018 drug CGMP Q&A is **one general statement in
its Introduction** — that CGMP regulations and guidance allow for flexible and risk-based strategies, and
that firms *should* implement meaningful and effective ones — and **three bounded invocations in its
answers**: the extent of validation, the frequency of audit trail review **where the regulation is
silent**, and post-failure remediation. **None of them names an artefact.**
`03.01-nothing-required-this.md` owns the argument and the anchoring on `21 CFR 211.68(b)`.

**FDA's own ALCOA mapping goes to the predicate rules and cites Part 11 nowhere.** Footnote 5 of the 2018
guidance maps each of the five attributes to sections of 21 CFR Parts 211 and 212. **It cites 21 CFR
Part 11 nowhere at all.** The industry crosswalk that maps the attributes one-to-one onto
`21 CFR 11.10(a)` to `(k)` **is an invention, and FDA's own footnote refutes it.** The consequence Helix
draws is the reason Phase 05 stays on the plan: **an ALCOA assessment and a Part 11 control assessment are
two different assessments of the same records.** `03.04-footnote-five.md` is the centre of the phase.

**The unit is the record-type/system pair, and the five attributes are never averaged.** The same record
type is not at the same risk on every system that holds it — `ADR-0016`. Each pair carries five
determinations side by side, and **a mean of five attributes is a number that describes nothing** —
`ADR-0017`.

**And the largest single act of the phase is a refusal to score.** A pair carrying an attribute Helix
cannot determine at all is **not scored**. An absence of evidence scores nothing; it is a gap, not a low
number — `ADR-0018`. **Seventy-four pairs are scored and forty-seven are not, and the forty-seven are what
make the seventy-four worth reading.**

## 3. What Phase 03 produced, said first

Six things. Each has exactly one owning chapter, and no other chapter restates it.

- **A population, derived rather than designed.** Phase 02's thirty-four in-scope record types joined to
  the sixty-one systems that hold them produce **one hundred and twenty-one record-type/system pairs**, and
  the join is mechanical and re-runnable from the two published registers.
  **`03.02-the-unit-of-assessment.md`**, with **`ADR-0016`**.
- **A vocabulary that is FDA's rather than the industry's.** FDA's sentence quoted verbatim, with
  **"contemporaneously recorded" and not "contemporaneous"**, and **"original or a true copy" and not
  "original"**. The four additional attributes are named, attributed to the bodies that set them out, and
  kept out of the instrument. **`03.03-the-five-attributes-in-fdas-words.md`**.
- **A mapping that is FDA's own, and the crosswalk it displaces.**
  **`03.04-footnote-five.md`**, with **`ADR-0019`**.
- **An instrument, a refusal and a scale.** Three determinations and only three; likelihood **of the
  undetected alteration**; two axes of five; bands set in the gaps of the product set; and
  `21 CFR 211.22(d)`, which makes Helix's own written method enforceable against it.
  **`03.05-the-method-and-the-scale.md`**, with **`ADR-0017`**, **`ADR-0018`**, **`ADR-0020`**,
  **`ADR-0021`** and **`ADR-0022`**.
- **The assessment itself, and what it found.** **`03.06-the-assessment.md`** owns the six hundred and five
  determinations and how the population divides; **`03.07-the-attribute-that-fails-most.md`** owns the
  attribute that produced the phase's headline; **`03.08-what-cannot-be-determined.md`** owns the
  unscorable population; **`03.09-the-seven-assessed-for-the-first-time.md`** owns the pairs generated by
  Phase 02's seven; **`03.10-the-scored-risks.md`** owns the ratings. **Only the 74/47 division appears in
  this README, because it is the shape of the phase; every other result — the determination counts, the
  attribute cuts, the ratings and the population's distribution by owner and location — appears first in
  its owning chapter and is cited from here.**
- **And the criticism the phase makes of its own method.** PIC/S PI 041-1, at `§5.5.4`, directs that a risk
  assessment focus on business processes and data flows and *"not just consider information technology (IT)
  system functionality or complexity"*. **Helix's unit is
  IT-shaped. Thirty-one of the thirty-four in-scope record types are held on more than one system, and the
  transfer between them is assessed nowhere.** **`03.11-the-flow-the-unit-does-not-see.md`**, with
  **`IS-18`**.

**The sixth is the one to read twice.** A phase that has just published one hundred and twenty-one
assessed pairs is under no obligation to point out the shape of what it did not assess, and the discipline
is to do it anyway.

## 4. What Phase 03 deliberately does not establish

None of the following exists at the vantage, and nothing in this phase states, implies or forecasts any of
it.

- **No validation status for any system.** Phase 04, `MS-08`. **A rating is not a validation status.**
- **No control position.** **Which paragraphs of `21 CFR 11.10` are met, for any system, is not stated
  anywhere in this phase.** Phase 05, `MS-09`. `IS-09` remains open.
- **No audit trail configuration, and no review frequency stated as a requirement.** The frequency of
  audit trail review is inherited from the review frequency CGMP specifies for the underlying data;
  **where CGMP specifies none, the firm determines it, and only there does risk enter** — `03.01 §4`.
  **No primary source names a frequency**, and this phase names none.
- **No retention period.** `IS-14` remains open and `21 CFR 211.180` governs the question.
- **No electronic signature deployment.**
- **No remediation plan, no remediation action and no remediation cost.** **The assessment says where the
  data is weak. Nothing in the quality system yet says what Helix must do about it** — that is Phase 04's,
  and `03.12` states it as the question the phase leaves open.
- **No periodic review, no internal audit result, no third-party audit result and no further inspection
  outcome of any kind.**
- **No answer to `IS-01`**, and **no promotion of its fourth candidate explanation** by statement, by
  ordering or by tone — `ADR-0012`, `02.11`. `03.12` owns the refusal.
- **No re-opening of the Part 11 applicability determination.** The thirty-four and the sixty-one are
  Phase 02's and are unchanged.
- **No legal conclusion of any kind**, and no forecast of what any future inspection will find.

**A rating is not a finding, not a deficiency and not a violation.**

## 10. Standing framing

These positions hold across all nine phases and are restated here so that a reader arriving at Phase 03
has them.

- **There is no Part 11 certification, no Part 11 certificate and no FDA-approved or FDA-validated
  software.** A product has **features that support** compliance; compliance is a property of Helix's
  validated system in its intended use.
- **Part 11 applies to records, not to systems**, and this repository never says "a Part 11 system"
  without saying which records make it one — `ADR-0001`.
- **Enforcement discretion is FDA's stated intention. It is not a legal exemption and not a safe
  harbour**, and **`21 CFR 211.68` requires appropriate controls over Helix's computerised systems — and,
  at `(a)`, a written program designed to assure their proper performance — whatever FDA does about
  Part 11** — `ADR-0003`.
- **FDA's Computer Software Assurance guidance is device guidance under `21 CFR Part 820`, as amended by
  the Quality Management System Regulation, and does not apply to Helix.** Adopted voluntarily as good
  practice, labelled as adopted every time — `ADR-0005`.
- **GAMP 5 is ISPE good-practice guidance, never a requirement**, and no software-category count is
  published in this repository.
- **FDA uses plain ALCOA**, and writes it *"attributable, legible, contemporaneously recorded, original or
  a true copy, and accurate"*. **The four additional attributes are other bodies', MHRA itself prefers the
  plain acronym and does not reject the four, and this repository names no originator for either set.**
- **ALCOA does not map onto `21 CFR 11.10`.** FDA's only published mapping is footnote 5, and it cites the
  predicate rules — `ADR-0019`. Where an attribute and a Part 11 paragraph are both relevant to the same
  record, **they are two assessments of the same record**.
- **Part 11 requires no reason-for-change field, no read-event logging, no cryptographic hashing under
  `21 CFR 11.70` and no password interval under `21 CFR 11.300(b)`. It requires no multi-factor
  authentication in the modern sense — `21 CFR 11.200(a)(1)` requires two distinct identification
  components, which is not the same thing.**
- **A closed system is defined by who controls access** — `21 CFR 11.3(b)(4)`. Hosting is not the test.
- **`21 CFR 11.3(b)` contains nine definitions and Part 11 contains ten sections.** Citations in this
  repository are exact and can be looked up.
- **A Form FDA 483 is not a legal finding.** No document in this repository reaches a legal conclusion of
  any kind, and **never forecast what a future inspection will find.**
- **An open gap is Helix's own assessment of its own position.** It is not a violation, not a breach, not a
  non-compliance, not a finding and not an inspectional observation.
- **Applicability is not integrity**, and **a rating is not a finding.**

## 11. How to read this phase

Read **`03.04`** if you read only one chapter. Four lines of an FDA footnote settle what the assessment is
anchored on, dispose of the industry's most widely reproduced data integrity artefact, and produce the
consequence that keeps a whole later phase on the plan.

Read **`03.01`** for why the phase exists at all when nothing required it, and **`03.05`** for the
instrument and for the sentence in `21 CFR 211.22(d)` that makes Helix's own procedure enforceable against
it. Read **`03.02`** for the unit and **`03.03`** for the vocabulary; between them they explain what is
being assessed and in whose words.

Read **`03.06`** to **`03.10`** for what the instrument produced, and read **`03.08`** with particular
attention, because the refusal it reports is the largest single act of the phase.

Read **`03.11`** last of the argued chapters. It is the phase's criticism of its own method, and it is
where a reader who has been persuaded by everything before it should stop and reconsider.

Read for the arguments rather than the citations. Where a chapter cites `21 CFR 211.68(b)` or
`21 CFR 211.194(a)`, it is locating a requirement in the text — **not** asserting that Helix meets it.
