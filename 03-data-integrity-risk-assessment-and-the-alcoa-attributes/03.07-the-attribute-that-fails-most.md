## 1. The result

**"Original or a true copy" fails or cannot be determined on 75 of the 121 pairs.**

| Determination | Pairs |
|---|---|
| **Not met** | **34** |
| **Not determinable** | **41** |
| **Either** | **75** — **34 + 41 = 75** |
| Met | **46** |
| **Total** | **121** |

It is **both** the most-failed attribute and the most-undeterminable one, and it is the only one of the five
on which fewer than half the population is recorded *Met*. No other attribute comes close on either count:
the next most-failed is *attributable* at 23 Not met, and the next most-undeterminable is *accurate* at 32
Not determinable.

**`IS-15`** carries the result. `logs/attribute-determination-register.md §3` is the count and
`logs/risk-register.md` is where each affected pair sits. This chapter is the argument for why.

## 2. What the attribute asks

FDA's sentence, which `../03.03-the-five-attributes-in-fdas-words.md` owns and quotes in full, says
**"original or a true copy"** and not *original*. The distinction is load-bearing and this chapter turns on
it. **The attribute is satisfied by an original or by a true copy, and to claim either you must be able to
say which one you are holding.**

FDA's footnote 5 maps this attribute to **`21 CFR 211.180`** and **`21 CFR 211.194(a)`** — the retention and
maintenance of records, and the content required of a complete record of laboratory data. That mapping is
to the predicate rules; **it cites Part 11 nowhere**, and `../03.04-footnote-five.md` owns the consequence.

So the attribute asks two questions of every pair, and a pair has to answer one of them:

> **Is this instance the original record — the first capture, from which everything else derives?**
>
> **Or is it a copy, and can Helix demonstrate that it is a true one?**

**A record that answers neither does not fail the attribute because it is inaccurate.** It fails, or cannot
be determined, because nobody at Helix can say which of the two it is. That is a documentary condition
rather than a technical one, and it is why the result is spread across every owner and every location rather
than concentrated in one function.

## 3. Why it fails: four routes, and each is a different problem

### 3.1 A record that can be reprocessed has no single original in the way a weighing ticket does

**A weighing ticket has an obvious original.** The balance produced one, at one moment, and any later paper
is either it or a photocopy of it. **A chromatogram is not like that**, and the difference is not a matter
of degree.

A chromatographic acquisition produces a data file. From that file a result is produced by integration —
where the peak starts, where it ends, what the baseline is — and the integration is a set of parameters
applied to the data, not a property of it. Change the parameters and the same data yields a different
number. **`RT-13`, the chromatographic integration and reprocessing record, is the record of that
calculation**, and `21 CFR 211.194(a)(5)` requires a record of the calculation.

**Which instance of `RT-13` is the original?** The first integration, the one the analyst reported, or the
one the system holds now? A defensible answer exists for each, and Helix has never written one down. **The
attribute cannot be determined on `RT-13` on any of the five systems that hold it**, and on two of them —
`SYS-023` and `SYS-024` — no attribute of the five can be determined at all.

The same shape recurs wherever a record is a view over an underlying data set rather than a capture of a
moment: `RT-14` and `RT-15` on the chromatography data systems, `RT-16` the dissolution profile across
vessels and time points, `RT-26` a viable-count trend that can be re-ranged and re-grouped, and `RT-27` the
environmental trend record. **These are exactly Phase 02's seven**, which `IS-08` put into scope because a
static printout cannot reproduce a dynamic record. **Phase 02 established that the paper was not the record.
Phase 03 establishes that Helix cannot say what is** — and `../03.09-the-seven-assessed-for-the-first-time.md`
owns the population that follows from it.

### 3.2 The archive holds copies whose trueness nobody has ever demonstrated

**`SYS-036` is the laboratory data archive and long-term retention store.** It holds `RT-11`, `RT-12` and
`RT-13`. Every instance in it arrived from somewhere else — from a chromatography data system, from a LIMS,
from an instrument workstation. **Every instance in it is therefore a copy**, which is not a criticism: an
archive holding copies is what an archive is for, and `21 CFR 211.180` is about maintaining records rather
than about where they were first written.

**The attribute does not ask whether the archive holds copies. It asks whether they are true copies, and
whether Helix can demonstrate it.** To demonstrate a copy is true you need two things: a statement of what
the copy must preserve, and a method that shows this copy preserved it. Helix has neither, for this store or
for the three others in the same position — `SYS-059` the batch record archive, `SYS-060` the analytical data
backup and restore platform, and `SYS-061` the Cary laboratory archive.

**Nobody has ever demonstrated that a copy in `SYS-036` is a true copy of what it came from.** That sentence
is the finding, and it is a statement about an absence of demonstration rather than about the archive's
contents. The archive may be perfect. **Unassessed is not adverse** — and unassessed is also not
demonstrated, which is what the attribute asks for.

**One reason the demonstration has never been attempted is recorded elsewhere and belongs here.** There is
no retention period recorded against any of the 34 in-scope record types — `IS-14`, raised in Phase 02 and
still open, with `DP-14` and now `DP-18` carrying it. **Without a retention rule there is no statement of
what a copy must survive, for how long, and in what form**, so there is nothing for a trueness test to test
against. The archive question and the retention question are the same question approached from two ends.

### 3.3 A record rendered from a query is not the record

**`RT-33` is the warehouse temperature excursion search result.** Somebody asks the monitoring system
whether a stored material was exposed to an out-of-limit condition, over a window, at a location, against a
limit. The system answers. The answer is relied on.

**The answer depends on the query, and the query is not on the page.** Change the window by an hour, the
location by a zone or the limit by a degree and the same underlying data returns a different result. Phase
02 recorded exactly this when it put `RT-33` into scope: the result depends on the query, which is not
printed on the page — `02.06 §6.7`.

**A rendering is not an original and it is not obviously a copy of one either.** It is a new object,
produced on demand, whose relationship to the retained data is a function nobody wrote down. The attribute
cannot be determined for `RT-33` on either of the systems that hold it, and the same reasoning reaches every
record type whose instance is produced rather than retrieved.

### 3.4 The same record exists in several places and no rule says which one Helix relies on

This is the quietest of the four and it accounts for most of the 34 recorded **Not met**.

**`RT-04`, the in-process control result record, is held on 10 systems.** A tablet press writes it; the
manufacturing execution system holds it; the process historian holds it. Three instances, and no document in
Helix's quality system says which of them is the record Helix relies on. **`RT-07` is on 7 systems, `RT-08`
on 7, `RT-35` on 5**, and the attribute is recorded Not met on 7, 6 and 4 of those respectively. In each
case the same content exists in more than one place and the question *which one is the original* has never
been asked, let alone answered.

**Why this is Not met rather than Not determinable, and the distinction is deliberate.** For these pairs
Helix can establish the facts: it knows the instances exist, it knows how each arrived, and it knows that no
statement of reliance exists. The attribute is therefore determinable and it does not hold. **Where the
facts themselves cannot be established — because the record's relationship to its source is a function
nobody recorded — the determination is Not determinable instead.** `../03.05 §3` owns the rule and
`../03.08-what-cannot-be-determined.md §3` owns what makes the difference.

**`DP-17` is the closure route for both**, and it is the single largest closure route in the phase: a
statement, per record type, of which instance Helix relies on as the original, and of the method by which a
copy of it is shown to be a true copy. **It does not require a system change. It requires a decision
somebody has to write down.**

## 4. Where the 75 sit

**The result is not a laboratory result, and reading it as one would be the most available mistake.**
`PR-25` carries that risk.

| Route | Where it operates | Determination it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Reprocessing and re-rendering — §3.1 | Chromatography data systems, dissolution workstations, environmental and warehouse monitoring | Mostly **Not determinable** |
| The archive — §3.2 | `SYS-036`, `SYS-059`, `SYS-060`, `SYS-061` | **Not determinable** |
| The query — §3.3 | Warehouse environmental and cold-chain monitoring | **Not determinable** |
| Multiple instances, no statement of reliance — §3.4 | Manufacturing execution, packaging lines, equipment controllers, the enterprise resource planning system, warehouse and distribution | Mostly **Not met** |

The 34 Not met determinations fall overwhelmingly on manufacturing and packaging record types — the
in-process control result, the labelling issuance and reconciliation record, the automated packaging
inspection record, the finished product distribution record, the equipment cleaning and use log and the
component receipt and release status record. **Of the five system owners, the one carrying most of the Not
met determinations on this attribute is not the one carrying most of the Not determinable ones.** The
attribute fails in production and cannot be determined in the laboratory and the archive, for different
reasons, and neither half is a comment on the people who hold those systems.

## 5. The expected answer was "attributable", and the expectation was wrong

**Before the assessment began, the attribute expected to fail most was *attributable*.** The expectation was
written down, it is at `EV-325`, and it is recorded here because a phase that drops an expectation once it
fails leaves a reader unable to tell whether it ever had one.

**The reason for the expectation was `OBS-4`.** The Form FDA 483 issued at Ridgemont on 2026-02-13 recorded,
in Helix's own summary of it, that a user account shared by more than one operator was in use on a packaging
line system, with the consequence that entries made through it could not be attributed to a named
individual. That is an attribution problem, it is on the record, and it was the most vivid data integrity
fact anybody at Helix could name. **It was reasonable to expect the assessment to find its shape repeated
across the estate.**

**It did not.** *Attributable* is recorded Not met on 23 pairs and Not determinable on 15 — 38 of 121, which
is a real result and is half the size of the one at the top. **"Original or a true copy" beat it on both
counts, and nobody predicted it.**

**Three things follow, and the third is the one that matters.**

**The expectation was anchored on the most recent evidence rather than on the widest.** `OBS-4` is one
observation about one class of system. An inspection reveals a state of affairs at the place and time it
looked; it does not survey an estate. Building an expectation from it is building from a sample of one.

**The attribute that fails most is the one nobody had a vocabulary for.** Everybody at Helix could describe
a shared account. Very few could describe what makes a copy true, because nothing in the quality system had
ever required anybody to. **An attribute nobody has language for is an attribute nobody notices failing.**

**And an instrument that only finds what its designers expected is not an instrument.** The value of running
all five attributes across all 121 pairs, separately and without averaging, is precisely that it can produce
an answer its designers did not want. **This is the phase's evidence that the method is doing work rather
than confirming a prior**, and it is the argument for `ADR-0017` that no amount of reasoning about
averaging would have supplied.

## 6. `OBS-4` and `OBS-2`, recorded and left alone

**Both links are recorded. No conclusion is drawn from either, and none may be drawn here.**

| Observation | The link, stated exactly | Whose it is |
|---|---|---|
| **`OBS-4`** | The expectation that *attributable* would be the attribute that failed most was formed from `OBS-4`, and the assessment did not bear it out. **The assessment reaches no conclusion about `OBS-4`, about the packaging line systems, or about whether the observation's subject matter is present anywhere else** | The account and authority position is **Phase 05**, `MS-09`. The closure position on any observation is **Phase 08** |
| **`OBS-2`** | Three of the record types on which this attribute cannot be determined — `RT-13`, `RT-14`, `RT-15` — are chromatography data system record types, and `OBS-2` concerns audit trail review in the QC laboratory. **The link is recorded. Nothing is inferred from it in either direction** | **Phase 06** for the chromatography data system, `MS-10`; **Phase 08** for the closure position |

**Why the discipline matters more here than usual.** This chapter has produced a headline result about
laboratory and archive record types, and `OBS-2` is a laboratory observation. The sentence that connects
them costs nothing to write and would be a conclusion this phase has no basis for. Phase 02 refused the same
sentence at `02.06 §9` and `GOV-10 §6` directed that no document draw it before Phase 06 and Phase 08.
**That direction binds this chapter.**

**A Form FDA 483 is not a final agency determination of whether any condition is in violation**, no
observation has been closed, and no document in this repository characterises any past practice at Helix as
a violation of anything.

## 7. What this result does not establish

- **It does not establish that any record at Helix is not what it purports to be.** The attribute asks
  whether Helix can say which instance is the original and whether a copy is true. **Not met means Helix
  cannot say so.** It does not mean the record is wrong, altered, incomplete or unreliable.
- **It does not establish a control position.** Nothing here says whether any paragraph of `21 CFR 11.10` is
  met for any system — `IS-20`, and Phase 05 owns it. **An attribute assessment and a Part 11 control
  assessment are two assessments of the same record**, and this is the first.
- **It does not state a retention period, and the absence of one is part of the finding rather than a gap in
  the finding** — `IS-14`, `DP-18`.
- **It does not state a validation status for any system**, including the archives.
- **It reaches no legal conclusion and forecasts no inspection outcome.**

## 8. What would close it

| Route | What is needed | From | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`DP-17`** | **A statement, per record type, of which instance Helix relies on as the original**, and the method by which a copy of it is shown to be a true copy | The quality unit, with the record owners | Priyanka Venkataraman |
| **`DP-18`** | A retention period for each of the 34 in-scope record types, so that a copy has a rule to be tested against. Extends `DP-14` | The quality unit's records retention schedule | Priyanka Venkataraman |
| `DP-16` | For the reprocessed and re-rendered record types, a statement of what the system retains and what it reconstructs | Vendors, IT and Automation | Joachim Reuter |
| **What none of these needs** | **A system change, a purchase or a project.** The largest single closure route in this phase is a decision written down | | |

**That last row is the reason this chapter reads the way it does.** The attribute that fails most at Helix
fails for want of a document, and it has failed quietly for as long as nobody was required to write one.
**Whether Helix must now write it is not this phase's to say** — `IS-21`, and Phase 04 owns it.
